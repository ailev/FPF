# IS.Readme - Find the first useful contribution

Use implementation facilitation when a participant needs help making an evidence-informed practice usable in its setting. The participant may also want to develop the ability to conduct later changes. Enter at the missing contribution. The examples below and the [pattern index](#table-of-contents) identify starting points.

**A tool cannot open the older of two source editions.** Use IS.2 to establish what the comparison requires. Try an available, permitted copy that contains the needed material. If access depends on the source owner, obtain that access or report the limit. When the source does not exist, the practice responsible for the comparison can consider a qualified substitute or state which conclusion remains open.

**A sponsor asks for training while ordinary work makes the practice costly.** Use IS.1 to establish what the recipient needs. With IS.2, examine an episode for the missing operation or conditions such as time, permission or a workable response to findings. Use HCD if the person needs to learn the operation. If an organizational decision is needed, obtain it from the authority responsible for it, using OCE where appropriate.

**Participants disagree about a practice that works for some and excludes others.** Start with IS.6 when the people who experience the difficulty cannot affect the decision. Use IS.7 to act on the interaction between the practice, those using or receiving it, and its setting. This may require a change in the proposed practice, in the facilitating help, or in a condition controlled elsewhere. If recipients are expected to conduct later changes themselves, IS.8 develops that ability through actual facilitation work and evidence of what they can subsequently do.

**Temporary help is ending.** Start with IS.5 using what the work already shows. Establish whether any contribution must continue, who could supply it and what work remains. Retain adequate current provision, arrange an actual transfer or end the contribution responsibly. Reopen strategy construction through IS.3 only where the continuation needs it.

The [Architectural Reference](#isreference1---implementation-facilitation-and-the-language-that-describes-it) explains the connected practice and its distinctions. The pattern bodies develop the Methods and worked cases. [Framework relations and sources](#isreference8---obtain-the-external-contribution) locate professional suppliers and explain when their conditions must be reconsidered.

# Implementation Facilitation Principles Framework Architectural Reference

## IS.Reference:1 - Implementation facilitation and the language that describes it

Implementation facilitation helps the participants in a practice make it usable in their circumstances, examine consequences or develop the ability to conduct later changes. The facilitator helps with the requested change. The performer carries out the target practice. Those affected can report benefits, burdens or exclusion. Each authority decides matters within its remit. One participant can hold several roles. Establish any qualification or authority needed for the particular contribution.

This reference addresses those providing, receiving or deciding on implementation support. It explains what the Methods provide and which contributions come from another practice. The principal sources concern human and social services. To transfer their findings to a professional community, independent practice or a setting using AI, compare the recipients, intervention, authority and evidence conditions, and establish whether material differences limit the transfer.

Implementation facilitation combines knowledge of how a practice works in its setting with ways to help participants change it. Research, professional judgement and the experience of people affected can support different claims. When they disagree, examine what each source establishes. The facilitator can select or adapt help. Those responsible for the target work carry it out. To judge the change, recover why it was selected, what participants did and what happened in the receiving work. Use those observations to reconsider the practice and its support where warranted.

Different evidence models, facilitation strategies and providers can serve this field while preserving the relation between knowledge of the receiving practice, help and observed consequences. A profession can change tools or organizational home and still need that same help. Research, direct service delivery, organizational change, human development and community management contribute under their own purposes and criteria. Implementation research can also receive qualified observations from facilitation.

Implementation science and practice is one possible wider field. Evidence synthesis or translation makes the relied-on knowledge and its limits usable. Implementation research investigates delivery, mechanisms and context. The target profession supplies delivery and technical judgement. Facilitation connects those contributions to a change that participants can make in the receiving setting. Together they can support implementation and use evidence from its consequences to revise the grounds for further work.

Within that wider field, a facilitator uses relevant evidence about the practice, its recipients and their setting to adapt the help and make it available. Observations can establish what was delivered, used or prevented. The research or synthesis practice judges what those observations support beyond that setting. The provider or profession may change while this relation between knowledge, help and observations continues. Select the contributions the undertaking needs.

IS.1–IS.3 and IS.5 explain agreement, diagnosis, strategy and continuation of help. Use [FPF A.3.1.MI](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#a31mi---introduce-a-method-into-practice-and-decide-what-to-keep-or-change) to introduce a Method and interpret later use. An implementer can do this independently. IS.6–IS.8 address three further facilitation tasks. Participants with unequal influence may need a way to make their knowledge count. A change may depend on the interaction of practice, participants and setting. Recipients may want to facilitate a later change themselves, using relevant human-development or organizational-capability Methods.

For an uncomplicated access failure, use IS.2 or [FPF A.15.8.MS](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#a158ms---configure-methodological-support-for-work) and stop when the missing contribution is supplied. For a disagreement whose resolution depends on who can affect the practice, use IS.6. For a practice that must change with its setting, use IS.7. For recipients expected to lead a later change, use IS.8. Existing adequate results can enter any of these Methods directly.

A discussion can supply the whole contribution when its participants need to resolve a question together. An undertaking that also depends on evidence interpretation, changed provision, protected participation or later capability needs those contributions. Use [FPF C.32.MWA.PP](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c32mwapp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-provision-arrangements) to compare whole arrangements and [OCE.6](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce6---establish-holder-assignments-and-enabling-relations-for-organization-change) to establish needed organizational relations. Obtain technical judgement from the target profession. Internal, external and distributed provision are possible.

Implementation can finish while development continues. A useful result may expose a more demanding attainable problem, a changed aim or a missing Method. Use [FPF C.40.OD](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c40od---construct-and-continue-open-ended-development) to construct a development opportunity, [FPF C.40.JD](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c40jd---develop-a-way-its-holder-and-its-provision-together) to relate changes in the way, its holder and its provision, and [FPF C.40.NP](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c40np---continue-from-a-result-to-new-attainable-problems) to continue to another worthwhile problem when one arises.

## IS.Reference:2 - Make useful practice obtainable in real work

Use these helping Methods when a person or system is expected to use a practice and a description, invitation, tool or training has left an important contribution unavailable. The practice may be new, difficult to continue or still being evaluated for local use. The support practitioner can help obtain the contribution, examine what happened or revise or end the help, according to the undertaking.

Start with the result the receiving work needs and the occasion when it needs it. Use an available account, observe the work or ask its performer when their contribution is needed. Establish which requirement, if any, is unmet. A decision may supply permission, an expert may settle a difficult judgement, and practice with feedback may develop personal ability. A method unsuitable for the work may need revision or replacement. One restored contribution can be sufficient.

Implementation facilitation helps establish, restore, adapt or continue a recipient’s practice. When an existing service supplies the result the recipient needs, use it directly. A support practitioner may arrange for a qualified provider to perform the whole target operation. Delivery can complete the provider’s task. If the recipient is to learn that operation, arrange the recipient’s own practice and feedback. Those responsible for the target practice, organizational design or the recipient’s wider development strategy retain decisions within their respective remits.

Understand the proposed support and establish what the receiving work requires. Use the participants’ knowledge where needed. The target judgement may come from the practitioner’s own expertise, an applicable result or a qualified provider, including a capable AI system. For a proposed arrangement, establish that the contribution will be available, suitable and permitted under the required conditions.

## IS.Reference:3 - Keep the target, the help and their results distinct

The **target practice** is what the receiving performers are trying to do. The **support strategy** is the proposed way of helping them establish, use or revise it. The **support arrangement** supplies the people, systems, time, tools, access and decisions needed for that strategy. A **support undertaking** is the bounded work and commitments through which that help is provided.

These distinctions change action. A different explanation of a target method may repair its description without changing the method. Replacing a classroom session with rehearsal changes the support strategy. Repairing an unavailable reference changes the arrangement. Changing the target's decision criterion can change the method itself. To attribute a successful result to one of these changes, establish its causal contribution.

Establish which of the following results the recipient needs. An undertaking can require more than one.

| Wanted result | What must actually be obtained |
| --- | --- |
| A useful result from the target practice | The result and conditions the receiver needs, including the result of qualified external performance when that route is used. |
| A person's independent or appropriately supported ability | That person's contribution in representative work under the promised support conditions, using the applicable human-development and assessment methods. |
| A continuing organizational contribution | The combined work, effective relations, means and ordinary provision conditions that allow the organization to deliver it. |
| Ability to arrange later implementation | The intended holder's performance of noticing a difficulty, obtaining suitable help, making permitted decisions and using the result, at the promised scope. |
| Continuation among other carriers | Evidence of access, use and the particular cultural relation claimed, at the scale of the claim. A record of one material handover can establish who received the material. Evidence of receiving use supports a claim about the practice itself. |

Selection, construction and adaptation of the target practice can remain open. For a particular attempt, recover the intended contribution, the intended operations, their conditions and the result the receiving work needs. To report a completed attempt, establish which operations took place. Preserve the status of a proposed method account. A.3.1.MI explains the stronger Method, Work and evidence distinctions when the use relies on them.

If the target question remains open, develop it before committing to an implementation plan. B.5.PI and C.40.CD help form and revise questions. C.40.OD helps construct and compare development opportunities. Use C.39 and the subject practice to develop a way to obtain the wanted result. Seek implementation support where its contribution would help. Implementation can also finish with a new problem worth pursuing.

## IS.Reference:4 - Use the connected Methods from the contribution that is missing

Use IS.1 to establish a workable support undertaking, IS.2 to investigate a consequential difficulty and obtain a needed contribution, and IS.3 to design or adapt support and its provision. Use A.3.1.MI to introduce a practice and examine later use, distinguishing changes to the target and to support. IS.5 addresses continued use, transfer, extension or closure. Use IS.6 when unequal influence prevents relevant knowledge from affecting a change, IS.7 for the interaction of practice, participants and setting, and IS.8 to develop the recipients’ ability to facilitate another change. Enter with an existing result wherever it meets the Method’s conditions.

A sufficient current arrangement can enter at A.3.1.MI. An inherited service can require only an IS.5 handover. A failed attempt can return directly to IS.2 or to the subject method's owner. Existing evidence can settle a question without another trial. Follow an ordinary path only while its next contribution is needed.

**Long working mantra**

> Establish what the recipient needs and what help is wanted and permitted. A sufficient service can supply a complete result. If a difficulty remains, follow the work to it and distinguish the explanations that matter to the answer. Obtain the missing contribution or construct suitable help with those whose knowledge or decisions it requires. For an introduction, examine what happened under the target, support and conditions used. Use the evidence to decide what can continue, change, transfer or end. Take a changed aim or a missing subject method to the practice that can settle it.

Diagnosis may reveal that the original request promised the wrong result. A good target can require different support. A useful local adaptation can become a method-development question. A successful episode can reveal an unsustainable dependence. The practitioner follows the affected contribution rather than restarting every activity.

## IS.Reference:5 - One performer, several practices; several performers, one result

Establish who supplies the implementation-support contribution and whether they have the necessary expertise, access and authority. Allocate any still-unassigned work when it needs to be performed. A single performer may provide the whole service. Several parties can also contribute under their own organizational arrangements and decision authority.

One residency coordinator, for example, can develop a method during a development project and later organize programmes and cohorts as ordinary provision. Project membership, method of work, capability and organizational assignment are different relations.

Establish the expertise needed for the target operation. A facilitator may help two professions investigate a difficulty while specialists in each profession settle its technical questions. A qualified AI system may supply a previously scarce result. Check physical access, permissions and evidence where the work depends on them. If someone is to develop or demonstrate an ability, establish that performer’s contribution.

Use C.32.MWA.PP when the question requires a comparison of materially different ways to perform the Methods and provide the necessary support. Use a qualified choice in the support strategy. Where proposed relations are needed in practice, establish that they operate. A.15.8.MS addresses method-use support, and C.32.MWA distinguishes interacting structures when that affects the comparison. Use suitable OCE or Operations results for organization, allocation and continuing service. An existing result can settle its own question.

## IS.Reference:6 - Recognize a useful local result before claiming a larger one

A plan, delivered workshop, configured tool, attempted use and successful receiving result are different findings. A record of activity can be complete while a required condition is missing. Report the result the evidence establishes for the question asked.

For ordinary help, identify the difficulty, the contribution supplied and what it made possible, at the level the question requires. Report remaining conditions or further action when relevant. Use stronger models or evidence where the requested conclusion needs them. For capability, identify the holder and work conditions. For cultural continuation, establish the carriers and relations claimed. For causal attribution, use C.28.

Use C.11.DUA to judge whether the value of an attainable answer warrants the work, delay and interruption of obtaining it. An existing bounded answer can suffice when the competing explanations leave its conclusion and grounds intact. Mandatory protections stay in force unless an effective authorized change applies.

## IS.Reference:7 - Sources, alternatives and limits

A catalogue of barriers or strategies can suggest where to look. Establish the operation, provider, timing and result that matter to the present work. Use an applicable rollout procedure when it already supplies the whole contribution. Reconsider support when a change in the conditions, recipients or target affects its suitability.

The [2020 conceptual account](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-020-05145-1) and [Practice Guide to Supporting Implementation](https://implementation.fpg.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/ISP-Practice-Guide.pdf) describe tailored implementation help and the development of recipients’ implementation capacity in service systems. They distinguish facilitation from taking over the target service. Their accounts inform participation, contextual judgement, use and feedback here. Continued expert or AI provision is another option when the receiving work calls for a specialist result. Its suitability depends on the provider’s capability, availability, permission and fit with the receiving use. A claim of effectiveness in that setting, or of a change in an AI system’s capability, needs evidence for that claim.

An implementer can use A.3.1.MI to introduce a practice without a separate helping relationship. Use it to distinguish observations of implementation, adaptation of the target, changes to the support strategy and causal claims. HCD, OCE, CB and the Core Method-development family supply their own Methods. The constructed cases illustrate reasoning under their stated conditions.

Obtain the relevant technical Method, learning contribution, organizational arrangement or development direction from the practice that can provide it. Preserve its conditions of use and identify any gap for the recipient. Reduce a service promise when it depends on expertise that cannot be supplied.

The short entries identify the receiving work and a contribution that could help. Use the relevant Method or the supplying practice in IS.Reference:1 when an operation, condition or evidence claim remains unresolved. The examples show selected combinations.

## IS.Reference:9 - Reconsider only the contribution whose basis has changed

When a basis changes, use the table to find which contribution may need reconsideration.

| Changed basis | Contribution to reconsider |
| --- | --- |
| The recipient's wanted result, authority or service terms change. | The IS.1 undertaking and any dependent support strategy. |
| An explanation no longer distinguishes cases that matter to the requested finding, its grounds or a decision. | The IS.2 diagnosis and its subject or research supplier. |
| A change to a provider, tool, source, permission, workload or recipient condition affects provision. | IS.3 provision and adaptation; A.15.8.MS, HCD or OCE where the needed contribution belongs. |
| Later work contradicts a target, strategy, fit or causal claim. | A.3.1.MI's exact maintained claim and the professional return that can revise it. |
| Continued use depends on help that is ending or on a new receiving setting. | The IS.5 continuation, transfer or extension. |
| Better implementation-support knowledge changes the explanation or useful operation. | The affected helping Method and its examples, source bounds and related returns. |
| A relied-on FPF or DPF meaning changes. | The affected dependency and the IS instructions that consume it, before relying on unchanged wording. |

Identify which claim the change affects and whether the available evidence still supports it. Reuse unaffected knowledge and qualified results. If a contribution is missing, identify what would restore the receiving use. Return a larger development question at its own scope.

Use [FPF E.21](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#e21---fpf-pattern-quality-evaluation-characteristicspace) to judge a pattern’s contribution and [FPF E.4.DPF.DA](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#e4dpfda---evaluate-a-dpf-or-lpf-package-for-its-declared-use-package-adequacy-characteristicspace) to evaluate a framework for its declared practice. [FPF F.19](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#f19---ontology-first-plain-technical-rewriting) addresses wording that obscures an action, participant or condition. Apply each judgement to the question it examined.

To examine a local support strategy, use [FPF A.3.1.MI](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#a31mi---introduce-a-method-into-practice-and-decide-what-to-keep-or-change) and the relevant professional practice to evaluate the target, the help and the receiving result.

Use a supplier’s existing result when it is available and applicable. If the work still needs a contribution that the supplier cannot provide, consider another qualified way to obtain it or state the limit. Changed evidence can affect the target practice, the support or the developmental arrangement independently.

For source qualifications, citation and edition information, see the [Sources and Edition Notes](#implementation-facilitation-principles-framework-sources-and-edition-notes).

# Patterns

## IS.1 - Agree a Useful Implementation-Support Undertaking

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable
>
> **Primary working result:** a bounded undertaking in which the parties can use the agreed help, responsibilities and conditions to obtain the receiving result or decide when to change or end the work.

### IS.1:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** a request for help, a service offer or a proposed change leaves it unclear what the help must make possible or what it can provide. Also use it when a previously sensible agreement no longer fits the target, recipient, provider or available means.

Identify one receiving use. What result is required, on what occasion, and does the offered help supply it? A request for a course may reflect a learning need, unavailable time, inaccessible material or a target that does not fit the work.

A support undertaking comprises the bounded work and commitments through which help is provided. A conversation, work note or service specification can establish what the parties agreed when it records an effective commitment.

Use an adequate existing agreement directly. A routine request covered by a current service can end with its usable response. Use an applicable contract, employment decision or professional authorization within its scope. A new commitment or decision requires the authority and process that govern it.

### IS.1:2 - Problem

A sponsor can ask for adoption, a learner expect instruction, a manager expect productivity and a provider promise attendance. Each can complete what they understood while the receiving practice remains impossible.

Unclear terms can create dependence or mistrust. A person may reveal mistakes to obtain assistance while their helper uses the same information for an undisclosed performance decision. An external provider may promise a result that depends on time the customer has not allocated. A recipient can assume that the provider will make technical decisions the provider is neither qualified nor authorized to make.

The practical problem is to secure useful help on workable terms and identify a request that needs revision before the parties commit more work.

### IS.1:3 - Forces

| Force | Consequential tension |
| --- | --- |
| Useful ambition and current means | The requested benefit can be valuable while a required contribution is unavailable. |
| Several recipients | The sponsor, performer and beneficiary may need different results and hold different rights. |
| Trust and accountability | Candid discussion can improve help. Undisclosed reporting can make the discussion unsafe or mislead its participants. |
| Expert help and retained responsibility | A provider may supply a difficult contribution without acquiring the recipient's decision authority. |
| Clarity and discovery | Agree what is needed to begin. Some questions about the target or the help require evidence from its use. |
| Simple entry and consequential commitments | A short conversation can suffice for ordinary help, while some undertakings require formal protection or contract work. |

### IS.1:4 - Solution

Start with the receiving result. Identify the help, contributions and decisions on which it depends. Use an adequate existing undertaking or agree one that can supply the required work. State the condition that could change its promise.

#### IS.1:4.1 - Recover the request through the receiving work

Use a recent case or the next intended use to establish the input, action, receiver and consequence. Ask the requester to show or describe the case when their knowledge is needed. For example, a reviewer may need to find which source edition supports a claim before recommending release.

Establish what the performer currently does, where the proposed contribution would enter and what makes its result usable by the receiver. Use the available work and accounts. When the performer or receiver holds missing information, obtain it from them where possible. A single practitioner may both perform the work and use its result.

Compare the request with the evidence. Keep the proposed training when the recipient needs to learn the operation and the training can support that learning. If a capable performer lacks access to the source, repairing access can meet the immediate need. If the benefit is disputed, use an applicable target decision, seek a new decision from its holder if needed, or use IS.2 to investigate the disputed claim.

Keep the detail that affects the agreement, the proposed work or the grounds for the promised result.

#### IS.1:4.2 - Distinguish the promised result from the help

Establish what counts as completion of this undertaking. A provider may deliver the whole target result. If the promise includes the recipient’s ability, examine the recipient’s own work under the agreed support conditions. Continuing provision and continuation among other carriers each require evidence at their own scope. A service can succeed even when a concurrent learning promise remains unmet.

Specify the provider’s contribution. The following requests illustrate different promises.

| Requested help | Contribution to make explicit |
| --- | --- |
| “Coach us.” | Which action or judgement the coach helps the performer construct, when help is available and what remains the performer's work. |
| “Train the team.” | Which later work each intended learner must become able to perform, with what practice, feedback and assessment. |
| “Implement the tool.” | Which configuration, integration, access and receiving-use conditions the provider establishes, and who holds any required technical decisions. |
| “Help us introduce the practice.” | Which local conditions, explanation, participation, trial, observation or revision work the helper supplies. |
| “Make us independent.” | Which contributions the recipient must perform or arrange without this particular helper, and which continuing services remain legitimate dependencies. |

One undertaking can combine several of these or other contributions. Explain how they work together to obtain the promised result.

Establish that the decisive operation can be supplied with the required competence, access and evidence. The helper can perform it, use an applicable existing result or obtain it from a qualified provider. If useful delivery still depends on a provider’s unconfirmed commitment or unavailable evidence, state that condition.

#### IS.1:4.3 - Agree decisions and information use with the relevant parties

Determine which decisions the undertaking requires and who can make them. The parties may need to select the target, authorize time, permit access, accept a result, change ordinary work, release observations or stop an attempt. Establish the applicable authority and any decision already in force.

A practical way to expose ambiguity is to ask: “If this attempt reveals that the proposed criterion is wrong, who can change it? If the participant cannot attend without missing current work, who can change the allocation? If the helper sees a harmful condition, where can that concern go?” Use the governing rule or decision to resolve a material ambiguity, or state the limit that remains.

Agree what information the helper receives, how it will be used and who will receive the result. Where support and evaluation coincide, explain that relationship before inviting candour. A useful arrangement may separate confidential problem-solving from a formal assessment, share only agreed task evidence, or use another qualified performer. Explain what can remain confidential and what the applicable rules require you to disclose.

For voluntary participation, establish consent from the participant’s applicable agreement. Membership and the sponsor’s enthusiasm do not establish it. For an employee or contracted provider, distinguish an existing obligation from optional participation. Use the governing rule to settle a question of rights, or obtain the missing decision from its holder.

#### IS.1:4.4 - Construct the smallest useful undertaking

Choose an initial result the receiver can use. For a failed source lookup, this might mean establishing where it failed, agreeing on a permitted repair and making the repaired route available to the reviewer on the next case. A meeting is useful where it supplies the needed contribution.

Name the minimum inputs and contributions needed for that result. Check that they can be supplied together at the relevant time. If they cannot, compare changing the first result, rescheduling, obtaining another supplier, using a qualified incumbent or stopping.

Agree the scope of the first attempt and the conditions that could change it. A short engagement can end with a repaired condition. An inquiry can return a qualified question or an established finding, with a decision available when needed. A larger implementation promise needs a workable support strategy and provision. Use IS.3 where either remains open.

Put the agreement where the parties can use it, such as their ordinary work note or service request. Identify the receiving result, the provider’s contribution, any contribution required of the recipient, applicable decisions and protections, and the conditions for revising the promise.

#### IS.1:4.5 - Reopen the agreement when its working basis changes

When the target, provider, workload or recipient changes, establish whether a promise or its grounds are affected. Preserve unaffected work. A new provider can change access and confidentiality without changing the target Method; a revised target can leave the same support relationship useful but require different expertise.

Return a workable undertaking, adequate direct help, a revised request or the specific condition that remains unresolved. If the required contribution cannot be supplied on acceptable terms, say which result remains unavailable and what would permit reconsideration.

### IS.1:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.1:5.1 - A workshop request becomes a bounded implementation undertaking

This constructed case concerns Harbor Design, where six analysts review technical evidence packages. A manager requests a two-hour workshop on checking source editions. The expressed purpose is fewer late corrections.

The support practitioner asks for one recently corrected package. The analyst shows that the correct source was known but unavailable through the shared lookup path. A second analyst describes avoiding an early challenge because it had previously been treated as a failure to meet the release date. These accounts identify difficulties worth addressing in the first two packages. Their prevalence remains open.

The parties rewrite the request:

> For the next two eligible packages, make the current source available, make the challenge recipient and response clear, and observe whether the analysts can complete the check under those conditions. The support practitioner arranges and examines this attempt. The source owner repairs access. The manager authorizes the protected review interval. The technical lead retains the source-validity judgement and release authority.

If the attempt identifies a missing recognition or comparison operation that the analyst needs to learn, the practitioner gives that later-work requirement to a qualified learning provider. For these two packages, examine whether the analysts have access and time for the check, whether a challenge reaches the technical lead, and how the lead responds.

The manager also asks for individual rankings from these observations. The helper explains what the two-package attempt can establish about support conditions. Comparing analysts would require a suitable assessment and legitimate use of the information. The manager can obtain those or retain the original purpose.

#### IS.1:5.2 - Make a review practice workable without acquiring the specialty

A self-employed translator is introducing a workflow that checks consequential technical terms before delivery. They can compare the source and translation but cannot decide several specialized meanings. The workflow needs a qualified judgement at the review point.

The translator and a specialist agree which questions need review, the source material, confidentiality conditions, response time and how an unresolved term returns before delivery. The specialist supplies the subject judgements. The translator uses them and remains responsible for the translation.

The bounded support undertaking can end once those contributions can be obtained and used on the required project. If an adequate existing review service already supplies all of them, the translator can commission that result. Repeated later work can justify reconsidering continued provision or personal acquisition, but does not decide that choice in advance.

#### IS.1:5.3 - A capable AI lacks authority for one required contribution

An AI assistant can compare descriptions, propose a support strategy and prepare permitted practice material. Its user asks it to arrange a colleague's participation. The assistant has no current authority to commit that colleague.

The assistant can prepare the request or identify an available alternative. The colleague’s contribution remains unconfirmed until an effective agreement or assignment supplies it.

### IS.1:6 - Bias-Annotation

A provider can interpret every request as demand for its standard service. A sponsor can speak as though their own benefit represents every participant's interest. Compare the proposed help with the requirements and evidence from the receiving work. Obtain the accounts of performers or recipients where the judgement depends on their knowledge or experience.

The examples emphasize professional services. In a personal use, the same questions can be settled by one person.

### IS.1:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The first receiving use and useful result are recognizable without knowing the provider's service catalogue.
- The provider’s contribution and any work required of the recipient are clear enough to act.
- Any claimed decision or information use has an established basis. If an authority remains unknown or a permission is missing, the limit on the undertaking is clear.
- The required contributions can be obtained together, or the promise retains the exact missing condition.
- A changed aim, provider, burden or protection condition can revise or end the undertaking.
- If delivery is claimed, evidence shows that the result reached the recipient or was made available as promised. Any claim of mastery rests on the recipient’s own performance under the applicable conditions.

### IS.1:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Sell the standard workshop before recovering the receiving work. | Inspect one intended use and compare what the workshop would supply. |
| Promise that the client will become independent without naming independent work. | State which contribution will transfer and which support remains. |
| Treat a sponsor's request as every participant's consent. | Recover the actual right or agreement for the contribution. |
| Gather candid failures while leaving their assessment use undisclosed. | Agree information use and resolve incompatible support/evaluation roles before collection. |
| Keep the broad promise after a necessary provider becomes unavailable. | Narrow the undertaking, obtain a substitute or stop the dependent result. |

### IS.1:9 - Consequences

The parties can begin useful help before every uncertainty is settled. A smaller undertaking can reveal whether the larger request is appropriate, and a sufficient ordinary service can close the work without a programme.

Early inquiry and negotiation take effort where the request or terms are open. Handle a routine request under an adequate existing agreement.

### IS.1:10 - Architectural Rationale

A support undertaking is the working connection between a recipient's need and the help actually obtainable. Starting from it prevents a service label from selecting the intervention in advance.

The same performer can hold several responsibilities, and several providers can jointly supply a result. Check that the required contributions and decisions are effective under the relevant conditions.

### IS.1:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The practice question is how to start useful help when expectations and responsibilities differ. The implementation-support guide's co-design and role-tension material informs involving recipients and making support conditions explicit.

Use [LDR.4](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/LEADERSHIP-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#ldr4---prepare-a-proposal-for-decision-and-first-use) when joint work needs preparation from discussion and objections through an effective decision to first use. IS.1 connects the intended help to the recipient’s undertaking. The recipient may need a completed expert result, an opportunity to learn or both.

A stable standard service with agreed rights and inputs can answer the request. Use it directly. Reopen the undertaking only when its actual promise or conditions no longer fit.

### IS.1:12 - Relations

Use IS.2 where a receiving difficulty remains unexplained and IS.3 when support still needs to be designed or adapted. A.3.1.MI helps establish what happened during introduction or later use and which conclusions follow. Use IS.5 for continued provision, transfer or closure.

Use MKT.11 to establish whether a commercial service promise can be met, [OCE.6 (OCE.6:4)](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce64---solution) for organizational assignments and enabling relations, and [LDR.4 (LDR.4:4)](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/LEADERSHIP-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#ldr44---solution) when a decision or first use requires preparation. Use HCD when personal acquisition is promised. Use C.39 when construction or qualification of a target or support Method remains open.

### IS.1:End

## IS.2 - Find the Implementation Difficulty and Obtain the Next Useful Contribution

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable
>
> **Primary working result:** a supported account of the implementation difficulty or an appropriate response, with the conditions on which the conclusion or use depends.

### IS.2:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** a practice cannot begin, a later use fails or the help appears inadequate, and you need to establish what happened, why it matters or what response is warranted. The target can be established or still under examination. Start from a concrete intended or observed use.

Examine one episode. What result was needed? What was available, what was attempted and where did the difficulty arise? Use a suitable record or observation, or ask the performer to show the difficult point when their knowledge is needed. An inaccessible input, unavailable time, a missing operation and an unsuitable target can call for different responses.

Investigate the implementation difficulty and any response the task requires. Use a qualified current finding directly when it settles the question. An interview, observation or small comparison can resolve a remaining uncertainty. The result may explain the episode, supply useful help, specify an obtainable contribution or identify a limit.

Use an adequate known response directly. When an unexpected result after an intervention needs explanation before adaptation, A.3.1.MI:4.5 supplies that developed comparison. When the question is already a human capability diagnosis or an organizational participation difficulty, use HCD.3 or OCE.10, or apply a sufficient diagnosis already available from that practice.

### IS.2:2 - Problem

Labels such as resistance, low readiness and missing capability can conceal different failures. A person may know the Method but lack permission, begin correctly but lose access, or complete the prescribed work without receiving any useful result. A capable provider may answer too late to help.

A broad inventory takes participant time and can delay an available response without identifying an obtainable repair. A quick fix can also move a difficulty downstream if it treats a symptom and overlooks the receiving work it affects.

Distinguish the explanations that matter to the requested finding, the grounds for relying on it or the next useful action. Obtain a missing contribution when that result requires one.

### IS.2:3 - Forces

| Force | Consequential tension |
| --- | --- |
| Several plausible explanations | The same missed action can have different causes and several can operate together. |
| Local knowledge and authority | Participants know important conditions, but their explanations do not alone settle empirical claims or commitments. |
| Timely help and uncertainty | A useful response may be justified before one cause is established. |
| Diagnostic depth and interruption | Further evidence can improve a finding or decision while consuming the time available for useful help. |
| Target integrity and local adaptation | A local repair can enable the intended practice or silently replace a decisive operation. |
| Adequate expertise and scarce supply | The needed answer may exist while no qualified provider is available at the required moment. |

### IS.2:4 - Solution

Reconstruct the receiving episode. Compare plausible explanations by what should differ in the evidence or in the response. Report what they support and where uncertainty limits the conclusion. If the task requires further help, obtain the smallest contribution that makes the needed use possible.

#### IS.2:4.1 - Follow the episode through the intended result

Establish the occasion, relevant input, interpretation, action, output and receiver as far as the evidence allows. If the question depends on what a participant understood or expected, use an appropriate account from them. Compare it with permitted evidence from the work, such as an input, tool response, product or receiving decision.

Trace the actual or proposed work to the result the receiver needs. Locate a blocked step or a completed step whose output fails to meet that need. Use suitable records, observations or the performer’s explanation. Trace the relevant dependency back to its inputs and forward to its consequences. Use B.1.5.EW when the action’s contribution to the encompassing work remains unclear.

Finding a source edition can support a judgement about a claim which, in turn, informs a release recommendation. Check whether the retrieved passage supports the claim. If the source is unavailable, identify which part of the recommendation depended on it. Use any independent evidence that supports the claim, and qualify the remainder.

Include a successful contrast when it can distinguish the failure. The same performer using a different source route may reveal an access problem; success with an expert present may instead reveal the expert's missing contribution. Report the assistance supplied in each episode when interpreting the result.

If the intended event has not occurred, use an explicit prospective case and keep its status clear. A walkthrough can identify a contradiction or unavailable input without proving that later work will succeed.

#### IS.2:4.2 - Distinguish explanations that matter to the finding or response

State explanations as working claims about the episode. “The lookup route cannot retrieve this edition” is more useful than “technology barrier”. State what evidence would distinguish the claim and which response, if any, would depend on it.

| Possible explanation | Observation that could distinguish the explanation | Different response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The needed source or tool cannot be used. | Can the qualified performer proceed when effective access is supplied? | Repair access or select another qualified obtaining way. |
| An operation or judgement is missing. | What contribution does an expert make that is absent from the ordinary attempt? | Use an adequate judgement or obtain it from a qualified provider. If the performer must acquire the operation, arrange suitable explanation, practice and feedback. |
| The target does not fit the receiving case. | Which relied-on condition differs, and does the known alternative address it? | Reconsider the target or qualify a variant through the subject practice and A.3.1.MV. |
| Correct work conflicts with current commitments. | What occupies the necessary interval, and who can change the allocation? | Establish the conflict. If it must be resolved, obtain a feasible arrangement under the applicable authority. |
| Correct participation brings adverse consequences. | What actually follows the action, compared with the stated rule? | Establish the adverse consequence. If intervention is required, use or obtain a change that can alter it. |
| A completed action returns no wanted value. | What did the receiver need, and what remained unresolved after completion? | Identify the mismatch. Repair the contribution or reconsider the desired action if the undertaking calls for change. |

The table is illustrative. Form another explanation when the case requires it. Several can survive: an unclear instruction and unavailable time can both matter. An available determinant inventory can prompt questions about the relevant operation and its grounds.

Silence or non-use can have several explanations. When the question concerns a person’s experience, reason or decision, use their applicable account or seek it where appropriate. A refusal can reflect a burden, an objection or the person’s choice. Obtain needed clinical or other specialist judgement from a qualified professional, or state the limit if it is unavailable.

#### IS.2:4.3 - Decide whether another observation is worth obtaining

First establish what the existing evidence supports. If all live explanations warrant restoring the same unavailable input, that response can proceed with its cause open. If the question asks which explanation accounts for past work, or if training and a change in sanctions are competing responses, a discriminating observation may matter.

Before commissioning an inquiry, identify an attainable answer and how it could affect the finding, its grounds or the next action. Establish the necessary work, access, provider and timing. With C.11.DUA, compare the full burden with using the current evidence, a reversible start, narrowing the promise or stopping.

Useful contrasts include testing an authorized lookup route, inspecting the same task with and without an available aid, or asking the receiver what made a result unusable. Keep the relevant conditions and limits. A successful assisted attempt establishes what was achieved with the observed help. Assess independent performance or the isolated effect of the assistance using evidence appropriate to each claim.

Where causal reliance exceeds what the local contrast supports, use A.3.1.MI:4.5, C.28 or the relevant RMP design for the unresolved question. A before-and-after count may establish an observed change while leaving its cause open. If repair is part of the undertaking, carry out one that the available evidence and authority warrant.

#### IS.2:4.4 - Obtain the response through its actual supplier

If the response requires another contribution, specify the case, the question to answer or condition to change, the receiving result, timing and necessary qualification. First establish whether a suitable result is already available.

For a missing judgement, apply a qualified result that covers the case or obtain it from a competent performer. A demonstration can help when the task requires understanding how the comparison works. For a learning need, give the provider the later-work action and evidence of the difficulty. For an allocation conflict, establish the overlapping commitments and identify any decision needed from their holders.

Use the supplying practice whose contribution the question requires.

- Use HCD.3 to qualify a possible human learning target, and a suitable HCD Method to address an established learning need.
- Use OCE.10 for an organizational participation difficulty and OCE.11 for coexistence with continuing service.
- Use A.15.8.MS for method-use support, A.3.1.FT to qualify fit or transfer, A.3.1.MV for a variant and C.39 where the target needs construction.
- Use CB.12 where repeated participation is needed and CB.14 for a shared repertoire.
- Use RMP when an unresolved knowledge question warrants an attainable inquiry.

A qualified professional result can complete the bounded response. Apply it within its conditions and return it to the intended receiver when delivery is part of the task.

#### IS.2:4.5 - Make the returned contribution usable in the next episode

Inspect the returned result against the question that required it. A claim that access has been repaired needs an actually usable route. An explanation needs to settle the disputed interpretation or preserve the exact unresolved question. An agreed allocation needs to be effective when the work occurs.

Where the undertaking includes a next use, make the result available where and when it is needed. This may mean placing a corrected reference at the point of use, obtaining an authorized case, delivering the expert answer or providing protected time. Use IS.3 when the strategy or its provision still needs design.

Observe a subsequent use when the task or proposed claim calls for it. Distinguish failed entry, missing observation and justified non-use. If a promised contribution failed, repair it or take the gap to its provider within the undertaking. If it was delivered and the larger result failed, investigate the next relevant difficulty when that question remains in scope.

#### IS.2:4.6 - Reconsider the target when support is the wrong repair

A support investigation can reveal that the target is no longer appropriate, that a new possible result is more valuable, or that a needed Method does not exist. State the discovery and the grounds for reconsidering the target.

Where a target decision is needed, use the evidence, existing results and receiving consequence to decide within your authority or return the question to its holder. C.39 and the subject practice can construct or qualify a way. C.40.OD can help compare development directions; B.5.PI/C.40.CD can help form a question when the initial interpretation fails. Implementation support can continue independent useful work while the dependent branch remains open or stopped.

Return the supported finding or response. State its conditions, any unresolved question and the next action when the receiving work requires one. If no permissible contribution can meet the current promise, identify that limit.

### IS.2:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.2:5.1 - Distinguish access, capability and consequences

Continue the constructed Harbor Design case. The helper reconstructs three episodes:

| Episode | What happened | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A source check with a working direct link | The analyst identified the relevant revision and explained its effect. | The episode establishes this analyst’s performance with that access. A claim about other analysts or general capability needs further evidence. |
| A check through the usual shared portal | The needed revision was unavailable, and the analyst used an older one. | The portal condition is a concrete repair target. |
| An early challenge with the correct revision | The manager deferred the challenge to preserve the release date; the analyst received an adverse response for delay. | Correct detection is insufficient while the receiving consequence defeats its use. |

The source owner repairs the portal. The responsible manager and technical lead use OCE.10 to establish how a legitimate concern reaches the release decision and provide time for that work.

Suppose the next supported episode succeeds with one analyst, while a second still cannot compare the two revisions. That different finding opens a bounded HCD question about the second analyst's operation. It does not retrospectively turn the portal failure into a learning defect.

If the manager refuses the needed change, the helper reports that the promised early-challenge practice remains unsupported. The source repair can still be useful.

#### IS.2:5.2 - An independent carrier needs a different input, not a community

A freelance repairer obtains a diagnostic procedure from a publisher. The new instrument exports a different identifier format, and the procedure's lookup fails.

The support practitioner asks for an allowed sample and follows it through the lookup. A subject expert can map the new identifier unambiguously. The existing instruction assumed the older format. The immediate response is a qualified input adapter and an explanation of its boundary.

If two new identifiers map to the same older code, the mapping is no longer sufficient. That changed condition returns to the target Method and subject expert. Keep the ambiguous mapping open until there is a qualified way to distinguish the identifiers.

#### IS.2:5.3 - Preserve uncertainty when the response does not depend on it

A service's help link has moved, and users cannot reach the support page. Records do not distinguish whether each user forgot the old route or followed a broken bookmark. Restoring the correct route and communicating it is supported under both explanations.

The local response can proceed on this basis. If a later decision concerns reducing reminders, the memory and cue question becomes material then.

### IS.2:6 - Bias-Annotation

The helper may favor explanations that match their own service: trainers see learning gaps, tool providers see configuration gaps and managers see motivation. Use the episode, recipient account and actual contribution to challenge that preference.

A successful contrast can also mislead when it changes several conditions. Keep expert assistance, case difficulty and effective access visible; their combined result does not isolate one cause.

### IS.2:7 - Conformance Checklist

- A concrete intended or actual receiving use grounds the difficulty.
- Live explanations differ in the action or warranted conclusion they imply.
- An adequate current response can end diagnosis without another observation.
- A selected inquiry can answer a consequential question about the finding, its grounds or a decision, and warrants its burden.
- The finding answers the receiving question within its evidence. When a further contribution or action is needed, its scope and availability are clear.
- The explanation distinguishes capability, organizational conditions, target fitness and support wherever they call for different conclusions.
- The target can be reconsidered when help would preserve the wrong practice.

### IS.2:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Score a barrier inventory and call the highest score the cause. | Recover the case and compare explanations through their different expected observations and actions. |
| Diagnose a missing skill whenever a person does not perform. | Inspect access, task conditions, consequences and the actual operation. |
| Ask for more evidence without saying what it changes. | State what the answer could change about the finding, its grounds or the subsequent work. |
| Forward a problem to “an expert” without a usable request. | Supply the case, needed result, timing and receiving condition. |
| Repeat an adequate OCE or HCD diagnosis under a new label. | Use the existing result if it fits. Act on a remaining question when there is one. |

### IS.2:9 - Consequences

The next intervention can address the contribution that actually limits use. Some requests become smaller repairs; others correctly return to a target decision or a professional supplier.

The conclusion depends on the relevant task evidence and, where needed, participants’ knowledge of the episode. State what remains open if these are unavailable. A narrow response can be justified with the cause still uncertain.

### IS.2:10 - Architectural Rationale

Examine a receiving episode to see how the occasion, action and means affected the result. For a prospective difficulty, specify the corresponding intended use. A label about an individual or organization can hide those connections.

The Method separates explanation from response selection. More than one explanation can support the same response, while one generic symptom can require very different actions. That distinction makes the work useful under uncertainty and limits unnecessary inquiry.

### IS.2:11 - SoTA-Echoing

OCE.10 explains how to reconstruct a consequential organizational gap, distinguish explanations, obtain an intervention and examine receiving use and burden. Its distinctions about sanctions and workload apply here. Use HCD.3 for human diagnosis and A.3.1.MI:4.5 for the fuller explanatory comparison after an implementation result.

Use these Methods where their subject fits. IS.2 connects an implementation-help request to a supported diagnosis or obtainable contribution in the receiving setting.

The simpler alternative is a known adequate response. Retaining it avoids spending participant time on uncertainty that cannot change the current finding or action. Reopen when another episode defeats its premise or a stronger claim makes the unresolved distinction material.

### IS.2:12 - Relations

Use the intended benefit and help boundary established under IS.1 or an equivalent undertaking. Use IS.3 when a support strategy remains to be constructed. A.3.1.MI can supply observations about failed entry, adaptations and later work. If transfer or continuation loses a necessary contribution, IS.5 can lead back to this diagnosis.

B.1.5.EW explains the constituent contribution in its encompassing work. C.11.DUA governs the worth of further inquiry. C.28 and RMP supply stronger causal or research work.

### IS.2:End

## IS.3 - Construct and Adapt a Support Strategy with Its Recipients

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable
>
> **Primary working result:** an assessment, comparison or design of support for the receiving task, with its working conditions and any limitation. Where a start is requested, the necessary contributions and permissions are in place or the remaining gap is explicit.

### IS.3:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** you know what contribution the receiving work needs and must determine whether a support strategy can supply it, compare alternatives or adapt a strategy to changed conditions.

Start with the result the receiving work needs. Establish what the performer would do, which conditions matter, what the receiver would obtain and whether the contribution can reach them in time. Use an adequate existing arrangement directly. Compare materially different ways of supplying the result where a choice or comparison remains open.

A support strategy specifies how to help establish, use or revise the target practice. A description explains the strategy and a schedule assigns time. Establish separately which contributions are available. The strategy might use teaching, expert performance, facilitation or a change to the conditions of work. A proposed change to the target may require a decision from its holder.

Use a sufficient strategy or routine access repair as it stands. If a decision depends on the technical validity of a Method, the organization’s design or the recipient’s development direction, use the appropriate professional result or state which conclusion remains conditional.

### IS.3:2 - Problem

A selected strategy can be plausible in the abstract and unworkable at the moment of use. Training can occur without the inputs needed for later practice. An expert can be available after the receiving decision. A common service can save preparation while creating an excessive queue. A local adaptation can improve convenience by deleting the very operation the target needs.

A difficulty can lie in introduction, in the target itself or in both. A failed briefing gives grounds to examine the briefing. Replacing a technical criterion can change the target. Identify which operation or condition changed before deciding what to preserve or revise.

Establish whether the proposed way can supply the requested contribution. If the task is to construct support, specify a workable arrangement or identify the condition that prevents one. Where a choice is needed, compare the alternatives at their full cost. Identify what would warrant revising the target, the help or the conditions of provision.

### IS.3:3 - Forces

| Force | Consequential tension |
| --- | --- |
| Subject integrity and local fit | The decisive operation must remain, while inputs, timing or permissible forms can vary. |
| Personal acquisition and provided results | Learning can improve future autonomy; expert provision can meet an immediate need without that acquisition. |
| Common support and local knowledge | Reuse can save effort, while context reconstruction and queues can consume the saving. |
| Support intensity and burden | Too little help can defeat use. Excess help can consume capacity or obscure what the intended performer did. |
| Current service and change | The same qualified time may be needed for ordinary obligations and for development or implementation. |
| Comparability and different promises | Cheap alternatives may supply different results or move work to a less visible participant. |
| Action and inquiry | The strategy must support useful work. Observations about delivery, use or effect take time and can require access or consent. Seek those needed for the promised finding or subsequent use. |

### IS.3:4 - Solution

Establish the contribution the receiver needs and whether existing provision meets it. If a different arrangement is needed, specify its operations, inputs, handoff and support. Work with the parties whose knowledge, action or agreement is required. Where comparison is needed, assess what each proposal can supply, which conditions it meets and the full work it requires. Obtain the necessary conditions before starting the selected work.

#### IS.3:4.1 - Recover the contribution and preserve a sufficient incumbent

Use the IS.1 undertaking and IS.2 response or equivalent inputs when available. Establish the target practice, receiving result, intended performer, starting conditions, protection and material uncertainty. A qualified result about an existing strategy or service can settle the present question.

Establish whether the next use requires a change. If a reference has moved, restoring the route may suffice. When a person needs to acquire an operation, arrange appropriate demonstration, practice and feedback on that person’s work. When the task needs the result, use qualified provision that meets the conditions, or compare available ways if selection remains open. An explanation may help someone use the delivered result. Reconsider an unsuitable target with the practice responsible for it.

Specify the receiving result and horizon for the comparison. A completed task and a promise of later mastery can have different value to the recipient. Compare them against the declared requirements and preferences, and use the appropriate decision authority if a trade-off must be settled.

#### IS.3:4.2 - Generate alternatives by changing the obtaining way

Establish the baseline from what the current arrangement can provide, what it leaves unmet and the work it requires. If another way is needed, vary how the contribution would be obtained. Possibilities include the following.

- make an existing ability usable by repairing access, time, equipment or authority;
- obtain the decisive contribution from a qualified expert, service or AI;
- develop a person’s ability through an appropriate HCD arrangement when the undertaking calls for human acquisition;
- make a tacit operation recoverable through an explanation, demonstration or usable tool;
- solve an open problem together when the required knowledge or decisions are distributed;
- choose a different target or reduce the promised use when the needed contribution is unavailable.

For an option whose operation is unclear, follow a relevant input to the output the receiver needs. For example, a coach may ask a reviewer to compare two source editions, identify a changed claim and explain its effect before giving feedback. An adequate instruction or qualified service account can already identify the operation.

Identify which subject operations the support must preserve. A learning designer can choose practice and feedback. An engineering criterion requires engineering grounds. A facilitator can organize discussion while the qualified participants supply the technical answer. If a Method is missing, use C.39 and the relevant profession to construct it, or state the dependency if the present work ends at design.

One adequate way can suffice. Consider another alternative if it could reveal a material difference in result, dependence, resources or protection.

#### IS.3:4.3 - Explain why each contribution could help

Connect the intended benefit to the action that could deliver it and the conditions it needs. Rehearsing a stop decision can help a person learn to recognize when to stop. An expert can instead inspect the work and supply the judgement in time for this case. Both may help the immediate work. The learning claim requires evidence of what the person can do.

Identify what would show that the promised contribution was supplied and, if relevant, used. Consider what the evidence would show if it were unavailable, late, misunderstood or unsuitable. Qualify a proposed mechanism at the strength its evidence supports. Existing professional knowledge can justify an attempt while its local effect remains open.

Use A.3.1.MI:4.5 when the proposed adaptation requires the fuller delivery–mechanism–context comparison. That Method connects rival explanations, attainable inquiry and return to the affected result.

Distinguish the support strategy from the target. Changing the time of a briefing or the availability of help can leave the target unchanged. Replacing its decision criterion, accepting a different input interpretation or removing a decisive comparison can change it. State the intended function and use evidence from performance to establish whether it was fulfilled. To claim the Method stayed the same, check whether its defining operations and conditions were preserved.

#### IS.3:4.4 - Construct the local way with its recipients and providers

Trace the contribution from the input the provider needs to the result the receiver can use. An existing instruction, service result or observation may establish that connection. If you are designing an unfamiliar arrangement and the handoff or operation remains unclear, work through a representative case with those who hold the needed knowledge. Establish where the input, explanation, tool or help enters and what must reach the receiver.

Revise the arrangement where the evidence exposes a problem. For an inaccessible input, obtain it, use a compatible operation or identify the unresolved target condition. If the result would arrive too late, examine another time or way of obtaining it. If a participant identifies a harmful consequence, preserve the objection and consider an arrangement that addresses it.

Establish what each party undertakes. For a service, the provider performs the agreed operation or arranges it and makes the result available as promised. The recipient can use the result in full. Specify any input, decision or action that genuinely falls to the recipient. If the undertaking is to develop the recipient’s ability, the recipient needs an opportunity to perform the intended operation and receive feedback. A sponsor may provide resources or hold a decision. Establish commitments, permission to use information and any required consent under their actual terms.

Choose the interaction to fit the result. Expert provision can deliver current work. Joint work can connect distributed interpretations or reveal a consequential operation. Teaching and coached practice can develop the recipient’s capability. Facilitation can help participants coordinate choices within their remits. Leadership can secure a contribution, and repairing conditions can make an existing ability usable. Combine approaches when the undertaking needs their distinct contributions.

A forecast review can supply a corrected forecast, help its author learn to judge later forecasts or reveal a defect in the forecasting Method. For today’s forecast, a competent reviewer can supply the complete result. For learning, have the author make the relevant judgement and obtain feedback. If the method is prescribed, the author can learn to use it while its selection remains with another decision holder. A defect in the method can be reported to the holder or subject practice responsible for it.

When an instruction is needed, make it usable where the action occurs. Explain how to recognize the situation, which input the performer needs, what to do, how to inspect the result and when to seek help or stop. Use A.3.2.DE if what is missing is a reusable explanation.

Check that each party has the information it needs for its work. The receiver may need to know how to obtain, use or question the result. The provider needs the relevant inputs, conditions, responsibilities and route for a disagreement. Match them to the capabilities and authority of the actual participants.

Use a local variation within the target’s qualified range. A simplified display may preserve the comparison. A default answer may hide the difference a reviewer has to identify. If equivalence remains open, treat the variation as proposed and use subject evidence and A.3.1.MV to qualify it where needed.

#### IS.3:4.5 - Make performance and support jointly obtainable

For the option or options under consideration, identify who would perform each decisive operation and what it requires at that moment. Check actual capability, usable means, access, timing and permission.

Use C.32.MWA.PP to compare materially different arrangements for performance and provision. Examples include shared specialist service and provision within each branch, or expert execution and supported local performance. Consider where the work falls, simultaneous demands and dependencies. Use an applicable existing comparison or selected configuration. A proposal that depends on new relations still needs those relations to become effective. Use C.32.MWA, A.15.8.MS or OCE where the question concerns interacting structures, method-use support or organizational change. Continue with the helping operations and a receiving instruction where either is missing.

Check demand at the times people or resources are needed. Count shared preparation once per arrangement and include recurring case-specific interpretation and help. Two independent demands cannot both use the same expert hour. A single joint review may supply service and coaching. Count its time once for each person involved, include any additional preparation and instruction, and assess the service and learning results separately. A replacement provider, including a qualified AI system, is an option when it can meet the receiving conditions.

Use the commitments and permissions already in force. Obtain any additional access, staff time, voluntary participation or provider response window the selected work needs. Where continuing service competes with the change, use a sufficient coexistence arrangement or develop one with OCE.11.

If a required condition remains unavailable, compare a smaller slice, another provider, staggered work, another strategy or a revised promise. An option that violates a binding protection or authorization condition is ineligible while that condition applies.

#### IS.3:4.6 - Compare complete alternatives at the decision's scale

Use an adequate C.32.MWA.PP comparison directly. When a support choice remains, use the same current baseline, receiving use and relevant horizon. Include preparation, participant time, provider time, tools, coordination, inquiry, retained support, transition and displaced work where they can change the choice. Separate total effort from the demand on a particular scarce contributor.

Exclude options known to violate a required condition. Keep unresolved feasibility explicit. Compare the relevant differences among the remaining options, including time to usable result, future dependence, promised mastery, error exposure, response delay, effort and adaptability. Use C.11.CRC for finite comparison and C.11 for a choice by its authorized holder. Combine ordinal ratings only under an applicable rule that supports the comparison.

When results differ, make the trade-off explicit. A temporary expert service can be preferable for immediate continuity while a learning arrangement remains necessary for the promised later independence. For a combined offer, establish that the additional work and transition can be supplied. To report a completed service or learning outcome, use evidence of each promised result.

Select further inquiry only when its attainable answer could change the strategy, finding or grounds for reliance enough to justify its full burden. A bounded first attempt can be the comparison when its protection, recovery and receiving conditions are available. When a useful comparison cannot be obtained, retain the alternatives and state what remains unresolved.

#### IS.3:4.7 - Provide the bounded start and its return

For a selected strategy, explain what it is intended to make possible, how the contributions fit and who supplies them. If a start is requested, establish the effective permissions, inputs and commitments before the dependent work begins.

Select observations appropriate to the promised finding or to a decision about the attempt. They might concern actual use, fit, burden, the intended learner's contribution or a failed handover. Each has its own subject. A.3.1.MI keeps introduction work, later work, target adaptation, support adaptation and outside results separate.

Return the supported assessment, comparison or design. If the undertaking includes a start, distinguish the supplied conditions from those still missing. A usable design can be complete even when access delays an attempt. On an observed failure, follow the evidence to the affected target, contribution or arrangement.

### IS.3:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.3:5.1 - Compare complete first-week alternatives

In this constructed Harbor Design case the following quantities are stipulated for the comparison. Six analysts are available. After the local diagnosis, suppose four have adequate current capability for the selected case family and two need a supported attempt with feedback. The intended first-week contribution is eight eligible package checks, with the two additional analysts beginning to acquire the missing comparison operation. In the current continuation, the four capable analysts can provide those eight checks at 0.20 hours each, using 1.6 hours already available for checking. The two analysts' acquisition requires additional work.

All three serious alternatives require the same access repair, taking 1.5 technical hours, and a 0.5-hour managerial contribution that makes the review interval and receiving decision effective. These two hours are counted once in each alternative.

| First-week alternative | Scarce qualified provider time | Other direct time, including ordinary checking | Complete effort for the selected work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full six-person workshop, followed by brief individual follow-up | 2 hours preparation + 2 hours teaching + 6 × 0.25 hours follow-up = 5.5 hours | 6 × 2 hours attendance + 6 × 0.25 hours follow-up participation + 2 common hours + 1.6 hours checking = 17.1 hours | 22.6 hours |
| Targeted workplace rehearsal for the two analysts | 1 hour preparation + 2 × 0.75 hours coached rehearsal = 2.5 hours | 2 × 0.75 hours participation + 2 common hours + 1.6 hours checking = 5.1 hours | 7.6 hours |
| Qualified expert supplies the checking contribution for eight cases | 8 × 0.20 hours = 1.6 hours | 2 common hours; the expert replaces the analysts' checking contribution | 3.6 hours |

The totals include eight checks and the implementation work in each constructed alternative. Other unchanged package work lies outside the comparison. Relative to 1.6 hours of ordinary checking, the additional effort is 21, 6 and 2 hours respectively. The workshop uses the instructor’s time and each participant’s time. In the expert option, the expert performs the checks in place of the analysts.

Suppose only three qualified support hours are available after existing commitments. The workshop is unavailable in that window. Targeted rehearsal needs 2.5 hours. Two agreed 45-minute intervals and the available preparation hour give it a workable schedule.

The expert service can supply eight immediate checks with less first-week effort than either learning arrangement in the table. If those checks are the entire requirement, the expert service is eligible alongside the current analysts wherever each can meet the receiving conditions. If the authorized promise also includes developing the two analysts’ judgement, the extra learner work and support remain necessary.

The parties select targeted rehearsal for the acquisition attempt and retain the existing qualified review condition until later performance supports another allocation. They obtain the rehearsal and assessment from HCD and the subject provider. Use A.3.1.MI to examine what happens when the strategy is introduced.

#### IS.3:5.2 - Build the local instruction rather than paste a strategy name

The target requires identifying whether a source revision changes the support for a claim. The initial implementation instruction says:

> Use the new review method before release.

A walkthrough shows that the analyst cannot find the source pair and that the technical lead receives only an unqualified approval flag. The local instruction is changed:

> When a package cites a revised source, open the cited edition and the proposed current edition. Locate the passage supporting the claim. State whether the change affects that support, with the relevant passage. Send an unresolved or changed-support case to the technical lead before release recommendation. If either edition is unavailable, return the missing source instead of marking the claim checked.

The source owner supplies the two-edition route. The technical lead agrees the receiving response and timing. The learning provider uses representative pairs to teach the missing comparison where needed. The instruction is a local explanation of the selected operation.

Suppose instead that the source is a continuously updated database with no recoverable historical edition. The instruction's input condition fails. The helper returns the record-preservation and claim-support question to the source/subject owner. Inventing a timestamp from memory would not preserve the target operation.

#### IS.3:5.3 - Strong AI changes the obtaining way

Suppose a qualified AI service can perform the source comparison under the actual access and protection conditions, and its current evidence supports the required case family. One alternative lets it supply the comparison while the technical lead retains the receiving decision. Another uses it to prepare contrasts for the analysts' practice.

In the first arrangement the service can reduce demand for human comparison work and deliver the requested result. In the second, the analysts make the comparison and receive feedback as part of the proposed learning arrangement. Assess their capability from their own work under the conditions of the claim. Attributing a change in capability to the feedback requires evidence of that effect. Use the arrangement that meets the promised result.

If the service loses access to a protected source, the alternative becomes unavailable for that case.

### IS.3:6 - Bias-Annotation

The strategy designer can mistake a familiar delivery format for an operative Method, or count the provider's reduced cost while moving work to recipients. Trace the work required from each participant in the case, including any burden transferred to the receiver.

The arithmetic case favors a small initial slice because that is the stated receiving use. A later promise to qualify all six analysts would require its own complete work, support and evidence. Cost the larger result using those requirements.

### IS.3:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Each serious alternative explains the contribution and operation that could obtain the receiving result.
- An adequate existing service or strategy can supply the requested result.
- Target changes and changes to its introduction or support are distinguishable.
- Local instructions preserve the decisive operation and its input and receiving conditions.
- The necessary performers, means, access, time and permissions can be supplied together, or the shortfall is explicit.
- A comparison includes the additional work of each alternative and distinguishes the results each promises.
- If a start is promised, it has a defined scope, effective protection and suitable observations. Report a start as completed when the work has begun.
- Where an unavailable Method or provider leaves a required contribution missing, its effect on the result is explicit.

### IS.3:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Select “training” or “facilitation” as though the name explains the intervention. | Describe the actual operation, expected contribution and conditions. |
| Make a local adaptation by deleting the difficult target step. | Obtain a qualified variant or repair the missing means. |
| Count shared preparation once but omit recurring local support. | Follow each actual receiving use and count its demand. |
| Compare expert execution with learning as equivalent outputs. | Compare the different results against the undertaking. Use an applicable decision or obtain a choice from its authorized holder if one is needed. |
| Promise the same scarce hour to incompatible service and coaching demands, or count one joint contribution twice. | Obtain a feasible allocation. Where one review serves both uses, count the time once for each participant and separately against any scarce provider’s window. Include additional preparation and instruction. Qualify the service and learning results separately. |
| Use a workflow configuration as proof that the Method is enacted. | Inspect the actual performance and its operative conditions through A.3.1.MI. |

### IS.3:9 - Consequences

The assessment distinguishes the intended contribution, required local work and available provision. A targeted arrangement or continuing expert service can make a broader programme unnecessary when it meets the actual promise.

The construction can expose a subject or organization question that support design cannot answer. In that case, identifying the missing contribution or narrowing the promise can complete the work.

### IS.3:10 - Architectural Rationale

Implementation support combines interventions with different mechanisms and responsibilities. Specify how each operation contributes to the receiving result and who is responsible for it.

The same account distinguishes Method choice from provision. An appropriate operation may be unavailable. A service with ample capacity may supply a different operation from the one needed.

### IS.3:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The implementation-support guide informs the choice of the kind and intensity of help for the local need. Here that choice depends on the operation, receiving use and full effort.

Use C.32.MWA.PP for a comparison of performance and provision, including transferred effort and simultaneous demands. Use A.15.8.MS for method-use support, OCE.11 for coexistence with continuing service and A.3.1.MI:4.5 for interpreting delivery, mechanism, adaptation and context. Apply sufficient existing results directly.

Use a ready target-specific strategy when it fits. A changed input, provider, workload, target or promised result calls for reconsideration when it could affect that fit. Construct a new strategy where the existing one no longer suffices.

### IS.3:12 - Relations

Use the undertaking and decision boundaries from IS.1 and the diagnosis or needed contribution from IS.2 where these remain relevant. Use A.3.1.MI to conduct and interpret a bounded introduction. Use IS.5 to examine continuation or changed provision.

Use existing selections and instructions where they fit. C.32.MWA.PP supports comparison of alternative arrangements. A.3.2.DE and A.15.8.MS address reusable explanations and method-use support. Use HCD for human acquisition or assessment, C.32.MWA for interactions among Method, Work, capability and support, and OCE for required organizational changes. C.11 and C.11.CRC guide choice and comparison. Use C.11.DUA to assess the worth of further inquiry.

## IS.5 - Sustain, Transfer, Extend or End Implementation Support

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable
>
> **Primary working result:** an account of what continued use requires, a feasible continuation or a responsible end to support. When support ends, identify and settle its remaining obligations within the applicable authority.

### IS.5:0 - Use This When

Use this pattern to examine which help remains necessary after introduction, when a provider or condition changes, when another setting proposes using the practice, or when its benefits no longer justify support.

Identify the use at issue and the conditions that enable it. Check for contributions that can be easy to miss, such as an expert’s private help with difficult cases. A finding about that dependency may answer the question. Where a continuation decision is needed, consider retaining, reducing, replacing, transferring, extending or ending the contribution. Continued purchased service is one possible result.

Use an adequate continuing arrangement and its ordinary correction route directly. If continued use is no longer promised, establish whether any obligations remain. Settle them within the relevant authority.

### IS.5:1 - Problem Frame

Implementation support changes while the target practice continues. Temporary experts leave, licences expire, workloads grow, evidence changes, new recipients arrive and an organization transfers responsibility. A practice that worked under unusual attention may become unavailable under ordinary conditions.

Several different continuations are possible. The recipients may want to acquire a missing operation, continue obtaining it from someone else, retain occasional specialist help, substitute a capable AI service or stop using the practice. A community can transmit it among participants. Independent practitioners can also continue using a practice with suitable access and means.

Establish whether the wanted practice can continue, who can provide the necessary contributions and under what conditions. A wider development decision can be taken up when it has a separate purpose.

### IS.5:2 - Problem

How can a practitioner establish what will remain possible when help changes or ends? A successful assisted episode may depend on support that will soon be unavailable. Recipients may want continued expert supply, independence for named tasks or an end to the practice. Extension to another setting requires grounds that apply there.

### IS.5:3 - Forces

- **Visible performance and hidden rescue.** A recipient can appear independent while another performer resolves every difficult case, repairs inputs or absorbs delay.
- **Continuity and dependence.** A continuing supplier may be the best way to obtain the result. Reliance becomes a problem when the promised contribution cannot be supplied under the required conditions.
- **Economy and capability.** Acquiring a capability can reduce some later dependencies but consumes present learning opportunities, feedback and qualified support.
- **Local success and new settings.** An adequate local result can justify reuse without establishing the same result for another population, workflow or consequence.
- **Ending help and ending practice.** These are different changes with different affected parties and remaining obligations.
- **Cultural continuation and control.** Independent carriers can preserve a practice without a service office. Establish the authority for any proposed changes to their work.
- **Current provision and further development.** Ordinary delivery must continue when wanted, while worthwhile harder problems may demand new target Methods or capabilities.

### IS.5:4 - Solution

#### IS.5:4.1 - Recover the practice that should remain obtainable

Use the undertaking and the available evidence of work to establish which result is at issue, for which recipients, case families and conditions. For a continuation decision, distinguish the following questions.

1. Should the target practice continue for this use?
2. What contribution is needed for it to remain obtainable?
3. Who should perform or provide that contribution?

Choosing to keep a practice leaves its provision to be established. Ending a grant may leave obligations to receivers. Use sufficient evidence from A.3.1.MI or another source, including evidence of non-use or adverse results. Reopen the introduction only if its evidence leaves the present question unresolved.

Use an ordinary case and, where it could expose a consequential dependency, a relevant variation. For example, a specialist may be absent, an input ambiguous, a source changed or two demands simultaneous. Use an existing account when it already establishes the condition that matters.

Trace the actual contributions on which the work relies. They might include settling a difficult judgement, restoring access, selecting a method variant, correcting an obsolete instruction, answering a late question or authorizing an exception. Establish who supplied each relevant contribution and who could supply it under the proposed conditions.

#### IS.5:4.2 - Choose the continuation that obtains the wanted result

Use a sufficient current arrangement directly. Where provision remains open, assess an available option or compare the alternatives that could satisfy the need. The table identifies different possible continuations.

| Continuation | What must be obtained | What would make the choice inappropriate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retain the present help | A dependable contribution under ordinary demand and agreed terms. | The provider cannot continue, the contribution no longer matters, or its burden exceeds its benefit. |
| Reduce or make help conditional | Recipients can handle the ordinary cases, recognize the named exception and reach support when it occurs. | Apparent independence still relies on unobserved rescue or exceptions cannot be recognized. |
| Transfer provision | Another provider can obtain the required inputs, perform the operation and supply its result in a form the continuing work can use. | Only files or a role title have moved. |
| Develop recipient capability | Suitable practice, feedback and evidence of performing the required operation. | Personal acquisition is not wanted, or the needed opportunities and time cannot be supplied. |
| Replace the obtaining way | A different qualified source, tool, service or Method can supply the result. | Its limits or access exclude the actual cases. |
| Extend use to another setting | The target and its support fit that setting, or an appropriate adaptation can be qualified. | Local results are treated as a universal adoption instruction. |
| End support or practice | Remaining work and affected dependencies have a legitimate disposition. | A date or funding decision is being used as evidence that dependent work is complete. |

Continued specialist provision can be preferable to local acquisition. A qualified AI service can supply the work when its evidence and conditions cover the receiving task. If recipients must recognize cases outside the service’s scope, establish that they can do so.

Compare alternatives against the promised result. A service can supply a specialist answer. For a promise of independent later performance, establish what the recipient can already do under the required conditions. Arrange learning or demonstration where suitable existing evidence leaves a relevant gap. Use IS.3 for the strategy and full work comparison when continuation calls for a new arrangement.

#### IS.5:4.3 - Make transfer a performed receiving connection

For a planned transfer, identify the contribution to move, what the successor needs to do and what the receiver needs from it. Include material exceptions, unfinished cases and the grounds of the current qualification. Delivery of a folder establishes a material handover. A claim of transferred practice requires evidence about the successor’s contribution.

Establish whether the successor can provide a representative contribution under the intended conditions. Use a sufficient observed case if one exists. Where demonstration is still needed, arrange one with the required access and authority. For a completed transfer, establish that the successor supplied the promised result and that it reached the receiver in usable form. If subsequent use matters to the claim, establish what the receiver did with it. In source review, the successor needs to obtain the relevant source, compare it and return an interpretable judgement in time.

Identify any continuing dependence on the old provider. A planned overlap may be appropriate. If the outgoing provider still performs the decisive work, qualify the claim that the successor has taken it over. Use HCD if a person needs to learn an operation. Obtain missing permission, staffing, protected time or access from an appropriate source. The ability to perform does not itself supply authority or an absent historical record.

Establish who is responsible for late questions from earlier work. Use the old provider’s surviving obligation only where it can still be met. Where the obligation must continue, obtain a successor who has accepted it and can receive the open cases. Otherwise establish their authorized disposition or report the unresolved obligation. Use an adequate OCE.11 coexistence or exit arrangement when the change is organizational.

#### IS.5:4.4 - Qualify ordinary use and maintain the contributions that can change

Use existing evidence when it answers the question under the relevant conditions. Obtain further evidence when it can change the finding, its grounds or a decision enough to warrant the work. For example, establish whether the recipient recognized the escalation condition without prompting, or whether the service met demand after its introductory subsidy ended.

Use evidence of the result and the help it required. Correct performance with repeated expert intervention can establish what the combined arrangement achieved. Determine which operations the recipient performed before attributing individual ability. One case can identify a missing input without yielding an estimate of the failure rate.

Provide a correction route proportionate to the consequential change. The performer should know where to return a missing input, questionable answer or obsolete instruction; the responsible supplier must be able to receive that return. A low-risk personal practice may need only a maintained source and an available specialist. A service whose wrong result can harm others may require stronger monitoring, response and interruption arrangements from its governing professional practice.

Different contributions need different maintenance. A target-specific explanation can become obsolete when the target Method changes. A local access instruction can fail after a software change. A support strategy can become unsuitable when recipient preparation or workload changes. A source of research evidence can change the claim about why the practice works. Establish which claim or provision the change affects. Make an authorized change within your remit or take the open question to the relevant holder.

Where the organization needs a continuing capability to arrange such help, distinguish that capability from the supported subject capability. Its performers must be able to recognize implementation difficulties, obtain qualified contributions and arrange actual support in later cases. Use HCD for any human acquisition required. Establish the organization’s continuing contribution from representative work and the conditions that enable it.

#### IS.5:4.5 - Extend through changed conditions, not through a success label

A request to extend use starts with the new receiving work. Recover what is already the same and which differences can change applicability or provision. Differences can concern inputs, case distribution, authority, cost of error, available expertise, language, demand or access. Examine the differences that affect the target operation or the support contribution.

Reuse what remains sufficient. A shared explanation, common expert service or reusable exercise can serve several settings. Preserve a local contribution when it depends on the subject matter, available inputs or receiving judgement. For example, one service can maintain the comparison tool, while each technical practice retains its own criterion for whether a changed source defeats a claim.

An equivalent use with adequate support can proceed under its existing permissions. If a difference breaks a relied-on condition, qualify an adaptation or reconsider the target. Use IS.2 or IS.3 for the relevant open question, and A.3.1.MI if a bounded introduction is warranted.

Use C.36 to establish the carriers and transmission relations required for a claim of cultural continuation. Use C.36.RP when access, acquisition or provision of a shared Method must change. A joint repertoire developed with CB.14 may also serve independent users. Where the intended continuation depends on a communicating community, establish how it will be formed or maintained.

#### IS.5:4.6 - End the contribution without abandoning dependent work

State what ends, such as target use, a support service, one provider’s participation, a subsidy or a strategy. Identify any unfinished work and the receivers who depend on it. Establish the disposition already in force or obtain an authorized disposition where still needed.

For a completed low-risk trial with no continuing promise, this may be a short explanation and closure of its remaining questions. For a continuing service, it may require transfer, a fallback or a staged reduction. For a target practice found unfit, it may require stopping its use and restoring a qualified alternative. Establish a safe stopping procedure with the governing practice when the target or its risks require one.

Collect further reports when a legitimate use warrants them or an applicable obligation requires them. Preserve material needed by continuing receivers under the governing access, privacy and custody terms. A historical finding can use existing evidence without continuing personal-data collection.

End when the promised contribution and actual remaining obligations are complete, transferred or legitimately withdrawn. The continuing arrangement can then rely on the contributions it actually has. If a necessary receiver or authority is absent, return that unresolved condition plainly.

#### IS.5:4.7 - Return the next development question to its proper practice

Continued use can reveal a worthwhile harder problem: the old Method reaches its limit, recipients can now undertake more demanding work, or better ways of developing and supporting practice become possible. Where a further problem is worth pursuing, state the new desired result and current limit. Address it with the relevant development, subject, research or method-engineering practice.

If the needed target Method is unavailable, obtain or develop it where the task warrants that work. A changed future result may reopen the opportunity or undertaking. Implementation support can help introduce the resulting candidate once it is sufficiently developed.

A stable adequate service can remain ordinary work. Continue development when its attainable contribution warrants the work and can be provided.

### IS.5:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.5:5.1 - Transfer a real contribution while keeping current delivery possible

The following constructed case illustrates transfer under a four-week notice period.

Harbor Design has introduced source-revision comparison. Four analysts already perform the ordinary comparison adequately. Two are still learning it. A temporary specialist has been resolving unusual references while the technical lead receives release recommendations. The specialist's contract ends in four weeks.

The first proposal is to close support after a final webinar. Following actual use changes the decision: analysts do not know how to resolve an inaccessible superseded source, and the specialist has answered three such questions privately. The ordinary practice therefore depends on source recovery as well as comparison capability.

The source owner can maintain access. A specialist is available for unusual source interpretation on named days. The technical lead agrees to hold affected release recommendations until a qualified response arrives. Urgent work needs a separately qualified route. The plan assigns particular contributions now supplied by the outgoing specialist.

A representative transfer case includes an unavailable edition. The analyst returns the missing input, the source owner supplies the recoverable edition, the subject specialist interprets the consequential ambiguity, and the technical lead uses that result. This establishes receipt and use for that case. A failure rate or qualification across other technical subjects needs additional evidence.

The two learners continue their qualified practice under HCD. The other four continue ordinary reviews. The temporary provider transfers two unfinished queries to the agreed successors with their actual receiving uses, then ends its participation. If the source owner had no access to the archive, the transfer would remain incomplete despite the webinar and copied materials.

#### IS.5:5.2 - Retain supply by choice, and distinguish it from learning

In the review workflow from IS.1:5.2, a specialist supplies the independent translator’s rare terminology judgements. As project support ends, the translator checks that the service remains affordable, dependable, timely and compatible with confidentiality. If those conditions hold, the translator may retain ordinary provision. The usable scope and a route for disputed judgements help sustain the practice. Learning the specialty would be another undertaking.

Now suppose the translator begins accepting work under conditions where the service cannot be reached. Personal acquisition or another available supplier becomes relevant. The previous service results may help construct learning cases, but they do not prove that the translator can make the required judgement unaided. Use HCD if the translator wants to acquire the operation and IS.3 when available ways of obtaining the result need comparison.

If a qualified AI service becomes available, the same questions apply to its actual cases, inputs, protection and receiving use. Its availability changes the alternatives.

#### IS.5:5.3 - Continue among independent carriers

Several independent repair practitioners use a published diagnostic method. They have no common employer and do not communicate as a community. The publisher maintains the explanation and correction channel. Each practitioner supplies their own tools and works within the Method’s applicable conditions.

The support question is whether those contributions continue to make the practice available. A maintained correction, discoverable source and actual local access can be sufficient. If a new equipment family breaks an assumption, the publisher needs a qualified method revision or an explicit limit; forming a discussion group does not by itself supply the missing diagnostic operation.

A later proposal for peer learning can provide another way to learn if its expected contribution warrants the work.

### IS.5:6 - Bias-Annotation

Helpers can prefer continuation because it preserves their role, or prefer a clean exit because it improves programme statistics. Return to wanted practice, actual dependencies and remaining obligations.

Recipients may reasonably prefer dependable provision to mastery of a rare operation. If they seek independent local performance, establish what they did themselves and what the service supplied.

### IS.5:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The continuing result and the support contribution that may change are distinguishable.
- Hidden rescue and important exception handling are considered where they affect continued use.
- Retain, reduce, transfer, develop, replace, extend and end are possible responses. The choice or assessment fits the continuing need.
- The assessment identifies whether continuing provision is feasible under ordinary conditions. Where a continuation is selected, its dependencies and unresolved conditions are clear.
- A transfer includes a usable receiving connection and the disposition of unfinished work.
- Evidence supports the actual claim about joint performance, recipient capability or provider substitution.
- Extension reuses sufficient contributions and reopens conditions whose change matters.
- An assessment identifies the disposition of remaining obligations, including unresolved ones. For a completed exit, the disposition is authorized and any required stopping procedure has a professional basis.
- New development questions are addressed through the practice that can answer them.

### IS.5:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Declare sustainability because supported performance was good. | Establish what support ordinary use requires and whether that support remains available. Use a new observation where existing evidence is insufficient and the result warrants its cost. |
| Require every recipient to become self-sufficient. | Compare wanted capability with dependable external supply. |
| Transfer a folder and assume the role has transferred. | Establish that the successor can perform and the receiver can use the contribution. |
| Scale a successful local strategy unchanged. | Recover the new receiving work and consequential differences. |
| Close on a calendar date while late cases still depend on the old provider. | Transfer, complete or legitimately withdraw the actual obligation. |
| Treat community formation as a repair of a missing technical operation. | Identify and obtain the qualified subject contribution; use community work when it helps supply that result. |
| Turn the next harder development problem into another adoption campaign. | Return the changed target or missing Method to development and subject practice. |

### IS.5:9 - Consequences

Practice can continue through an arrangement whose real dependencies are understood. Support can become smaller, change provider, remain as a service or end without making independence the universal success criterion.

A practitioner may have grounds to stop a previously successful arrangement if ordinary provision is unavailable or the target no longer serves the intended use. If stopping is selected and authorized, carry it out within the undertaking.

### IS.5:10 - Architectural Rationale

The continuing practice, its performer capability, its supporting services and its cultural carriers can change independently. Identify which of these has changed so that the affected contribution can be transferred or ended within the relevant authority.

Continuing support needs performers, inputs and time. When provision ends, establish separately what has happened to commitments, unfinished work and planned work. A completed introduction may still leave a receiver dependent on an outgoing provider. A further development question can remain open after those obligations are settled.

### IS.5:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The implementation-support guide treats capability-building and leadership as substantive concerns. This pattern preserves the question of continuing capability while allowing qualified supply and chosen dependence where those better serve the work.

Use OCE.11 for coexistence with continuing work and exit from temporary arrangements. Use HCD.15 to reassess human development as work and support change. CB.14 can support a joint repertoire whose results reach independent users. C.36 distinguishes the claims of cultural continuation, and C.36.RP helps make a shared Method obtainable. Retain an adequate ordinary arrangement. Additional design is warranted where a consequential dependency has changed or remains open.

### IS.5:12 - Relations

Use a qualified result from A.3.1.MI for previous use. Use IS.1 when the undertaking’s purpose, authority or promised help changes, IS.2 to investigate a new difficulty and IS.3 for a strategy that needs adaptation.

Use C.39, A.3.1.MV and C.32.MWA.PP for target-method development, variant qualification and provision comparison respectively. Use OCE for organizational change and coexistence with continuing work, and HCD for wanted human acquisition. CB.14 supports joint repertoire development, C.36 distinguishes cultural continuation and C.36.RP helps make a shared Method obtainable. Take changed aims to the relevant development practice and an unresolved subject question to a qualified source or research practice.

### IS.5:End

## IS.6 - Broker a Practice Change Across Unequal Influence

**Type:** Method pattern
**Status:** Stable

### IS.6:1 - Problem frame

Use this Method when a practice change depends on knowledge, decisions or resources that cannot reach the people or organizations that need them, and unequal influence contributes to the difficulty. A referral service can count a referral that its intended user cannot complete. Staff may withhold relevant knowledge because reporting has had adverse consequences. Establish what happened to the contribution. Where a change is required, make the connection workable or identify the authority or condition that is missing.

Use suitable evidence about the receiving episode. If new participation is needed, establish a legitimate way to involve the affected parties. Use an adequate existing exchange directly. [CB.15](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/COMMUNITY-MANAGEMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#cb15---connect-practices-through-a-useful-joint-contribution) can support a joint contribution between practices. Use this Method when influence affects whether a contribution reaches implementation.

### IS.6:2 - Problem

A meeting can leave influence unchanged. The account of exclusion matters when it identifies an operation, decision or condition that affected the receiving work. Trace that connection. A claim to represent a group or decide for it depends on a mandate with the relevant scope.

### IS.6:3 - Forces

| Force | Consequence for the construction |
| --- | --- |
| Useful disagreement and unequal exposure | People need a way to make a consequential contribution under an acceptable exposure to risk. |
| Local experience and technical judgement | A recipient can expose a failed use; the warranted technical response still needs the appropriate expertise. |
| Distributed decisions | Obtain the authority and commitments needed for each organization's contribution. |
| Speed and meaningful influence | Another consultation takes time. Seek a missing contribution when it could change the finding, its grounds or the response. Assess the limits left by excluded parties. |

### IS.6:4 - Solution

#### IS.6:4.1 - Find the broken connection in a real use

Trace an observed or proposed episode from the practice to the result its recipient needs. Establish where the contribution was lost, delayed or made unusable, or where that could happen. Identify who has evidence, who can act if a change is required and who bears the consequences.

Use [PSDA.12](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/DECISION-DESIGN-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#psda12---recover-participants-concerns-and-affected-systems-for-a-decision-support-inquiry) when the inquiry lacks an account of affected parties, concerns or representation. Establish what their contributions could change. If separate practices need to exchange and use a contribution, use a sufficient connection or develop one with [CB.15](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/COMMUNITY-MANAGEMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#cb15---connect-practices-through-a-useful-joint-contribution). Establish the receiving operation and any permission it needs.

#### IS.6:4.2 - Make influence and exposure actionable

Identify who can approve a change, withhold a needed input, impose a consequence or prevent a report from being heard. Include the facilitator’s own obligations where they affect candour. Use applicable evidence about exposure, and obtain affected parties’ accounts where the matter depends on their experience. Check the terms that govern the proposed participation or use of data, including any consent they require. If a case is identifiable, describe the protection that can actually be provided.

Use [PSDA.13](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/DECISION-DESIGN-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#psda13---facilitate-decision-support-inquiry-and-preserve-material-dissent) to conduct the inquiry and preserve material dissent. Separate interviews, attributed accounts and joint discussion offer different protection. Use [OCE.10](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce10---choose-a-response-to-participation-or-working-culture-difficulties-in-the-target-organization) if sanctions, workload, access or participation conditions need to change. Establish what protection is in force and what limit remains. Private discussion can reduce exposure while leaving retaliation possible. Disclosure of personal data requires its own permission.

Where collective representation matters, use [CB.16](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/COMMUNITY-MANAGEMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#cb16---form-and-carry-a-collective-voice-into-a-decision) to establish whom a representative can speak for, on which matters and how those represented can correct the account. A willing participant can report their own experience. If a consequential group is absent or its views are unknown, qualify the conclusion that depends on them.

#### IS.6:4.3 - Translate the contribution into a changeable practice claim

Establish what the contribution shows about a benefit, action, access, population, harm or possible cause. It may confirm the earlier finding, qualify its grounds or expose a need for change. Use the original account directly when it is clear and appropriately available. If a paraphrase or disputed interpretation matters, check it with the speaker and receiver where possible. Preserve disagreement that affects the finding or a decision.

When the response is open, assess what the current practice supplies. If change is needed, develop or compare possible responses, using existing proposals where they fit. Inaccessible appointments might call for a different referral route, transport assistance, changed hours, a different provider or a narrower promise. Assess what each relevant response could achieve and whether it is feasible. Use IS.3 for an arrangement comparison and IS.7 when the intervention and its context need to change together.

#### IS.6:4.4 - Obtain the decision and the receiving connection

If a decision or new contribution is needed, give the responsible party the proposed response, the affected parties’ account, unresolved disagreement, material conditions and likely consequences. Decide within your own authority where applicable. Use [OCE.6](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce6---establish-holder-assignments-and-enabling-relations-for-organization-change) to establish needed organizational assignments or enabling relations. Identify which undertakings are already in force. A new promise from an external partner needs its acceptance.

Use sufficient existing evidence to judge whether the revised request can be received and acted on. If a remaining uncertainty matters and another test is justified, examine the relevant part at a suitable scale. In a referral, the referrer might supply the changed request, the agency show what it can do with it, or the recipient identify a remaining obstacle. Use an appropriate alternative source or report the limit where a trial is unavailable. Return the supported finding, obtainable response, warranted refusal or unresolved dependency.

#### IS.6:4.5 - Return consequences to the people and the practice

For a claim about completed brokerage, trace the contribution to the point where it had been lost. Establish whether it reached the practice, whether it affected what was done, and whether the practice became usable, as the question requires. Use A.3.1.MI to distinguish delivered change, participant response and outside result. If someone was heard but their account had no influence, report the extent of that finding.

Return the result to the affected parties or other receivers within the agreed privacy and service terms. Explain any decision, remaining question or route still needed for new reports. Maintain the connection while a need or obligation continues. If evidence challenges a relied-on claim, examine it with the appropriate holder. Use IS.8 if the participants are to maintain or build such connections themselves.

### IS.6:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.6:5.1 - A referral exists but cannot be used

In a constructed community-service example, staff report that 30 referrals were issued. Recipients report that the only appointment window overlaps with their work, and the receiving agency rejects incomplete requests. The service sponsor proposes a reminder campaign. Separate conversations under PSDA.13 show that staff had stopped reporting rejections after being blamed for low completion. Before asking staff to report further cases, the responsible manager uses OCE.10 to stop treating rejection reports as individual performance failures and provide a separate review of contested treatment. Staff decide which details from their accounts may be shared; identifiable cases retain their disclosure limits.

The broker and the two agencies use CB.15 to follow a de-identified referral together. The receiving agency distinguishes a necessary input from information that can be obtained at the first contact. The responsible owners compare a revised request, a later appointment window and the reminder-only incumbent. The latter cannot address either observed failure. One agency accepts a bounded change to its intake. The other supplies the revised request and an escalation contact under its own authority.

The arrangement establishes that the revised requests can be received under the agreed conditions. Use evidence of subsequent service to determine whether recipients actually obtained it. The 30 earlier referrals establish issuance. If a later appointment window cannot be staffed, return that provision question to IS.3.

#### IS.6:5.2 - A community without managerial authority

Independent professionals want a shared case-review practice. Less established participants will not expose client material to prominent peers. Using CB.15 and PSDA.13, the convenor obtains a protected case format and arranges a trial with consenting participants. Attendance is voluntary, and any claim to speak for other professionals needs an appropriate mandate. If the parties can already exchange useful cases on acceptable terms, that sufficient arrangement ends the brokerage question. Expansion requires its own receiving conditions.

### IS.6:6 - Bias-Annotation

Sponsor visibility can make its account appear complete. Familiar representatives can obscure absent recipients. A facilitator may prefer harmony because disagreement threatens its contract. Examine which contribution was lost, what the available evidence establishes and which judgement or action it could affect.

### IS.6:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The account identifies an observed or possible gap, its receiver, the available evidence and its consequence at the relevant scope.
- Protection and representation are supplied where needed; their actual limits remain visible.
- The recovered account establishes what the contribution could affect, what it changed or which relevant issue remains open.
- Any claimed decision or stop has an applicable authority. A missing decision or authority is identified when the receiving work depends on it.
- The result reaches its intended users under the applicable permissions. Claims about participation, changed practice and service outcomes have their respective grounds.

### IS.6:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Invite a group with little influence, then disregard a contribution that matters to the decision. | Trace how the group’s contribution reached the decision and how it was considered. If it confirms the existing decision, report that finding. If unequal influence excluded a material consideration, identify the gap. |
| Remove disagreement to obtain a shared statement. | Preserve the disputed premise and its consequence through PSDA.13; decide only what the remaining grounds permit. |
| Promise that a protected channel removes all risk. | Obtain the actual protection, disclose its limits and use a different participation arrangement when needed. |

### IS.6:9 - Consequences

The practice can use knowledge that its original decision arrangement excluded. This can require a changed promise, extra receiving work or an unpopular correction. Examine those consequences where they affect the intended change.

### IS.6:10 - Architectural Rationale

CB.15 supports joint work, PSDA.12 and PSDA.13 support inquiry, and OCE supports organizational change. Use the relevant results to establish how a previously excluded contribution could affect or has affected implementation. A consultation can establish what people said. Establish a change in practice from evidence of the practice before and after it. To attribute the change to a contribution, establish how it was used and what supports that causal connection.

### IS.6:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The [2020 ISP Practice Guide](https://implementation.fpg.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/ISP-Practice-Guide.pdf) discusses brokering across unequal influence and the use of beneficiary experience in implementation. Here the facilitator traces a contribution to the practice or decision that can use it. An adequate direct exchange can serve when the relevant knowledge already reaches its user.

[Buchanan, Filiatreau and Moore (2024)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43058-024-00572-1) argue for a community-oriented relation among implementation actors. Their argument concerns how the field should relate these participants. Estimating the effects of this Method requires a suitable empirical comparison. Reopen the construction when the proposed protection or representation excludes a consequential experience, or when practice evidence supports a better way to carry it into change.

### IS.6:12 - Relations

Use IS.1 for the undertaking, IS.2 to investigate the gap, IS.3 to compare needed help, A.3.1.MI to interpret a changed practice and IS.5 for continuation. CB.15, PSDA.12, PSDA.13, CB.16 and OCE.6/10 address the particular professional questions in §4. Use IS.7 for coupled adaptation and IS.8 where a future broker needs to develop the requisite capability.

### IS.6:End

## IS.7 - Facilitate a Practice Change with Its Participants and Context

**Type:** Method pattern
**Status:** Stable

### IS.7:1 - Problem frame

Use this Method when a practice’s usefulness depends on the interaction between the proposed operations, their performers, the people affected and conditions in or beyond the receiving organization. Establish what that interaction permits. If a change is needed, help the responsible parties adapt the practice, its support or its setting while retaining the evidence and limits of the change.

A supported explanation of one consequential interaction can answer the question. If a response is needed, identify a workable one or the condition that prevents it. Use a suitable established implementation procedure directly. If the substantive practice or its safety basis is unavailable, obtain the needed professional contribution or identify the dependent work that cannot proceed.

### IS.7:2 - Problem

A list of barriers identifies neither the response nor its owner. Training staff cannot supply absent equipment; changing equipment cannot answer a justified objection to the practice; local enthusiasm cannot alter an external funding rule. Conversely, replacing the whole intervention because one contribution was not delivered can discard a useful practice. Trace the interaction that matters to the task. Where a response is needed, choose a suitable action and use evidence from its implementation to judge what changed.

### IS.7:3 - Forces

| Force | Practical consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence and local experience | Each can support particular claims. Interpret an apparent conflict in relation to the claim before changing the practice. |
| Adaptation and retained function | A convenient local form may alter the mechanism or a protection on which the useful result depends. |
| Internal knowledge and independent challenge | A local facilitator knows the work but may share its blind spots or lack room to question it. |
| Immediate delivery and recipient capability | Supplying the result and helping recipients learn to obtain it are different, sometimes compatible undertakings. |
| Local action and external conditions | A useful change may depend on a decision beyond the implementation team. |

### IS.7:4 - Solution

#### IS.7:4.1 - Recover the intended practice through a receiving episode

Use the undertaking from IS.1 and a sufficient IS.2 diagnosis when available. Establish the target operation, intended benefit, affected participants and the receiving episode. Recover the technical grounds, required function, permitted variation and conditions needing renewed judgement from appropriate sources. Seek a practitioner’s judgement or recipient’s account when the needed knowledge or experience lies with them.

Research supports claims under the conditions studied. Professional observations can identify an operation or failure absent from those studies. Affected people can identify experienced benefit, burden or exclusion omitted from a service indicator. Connect each source to the claim it supports. Where a consequential difference remains unresolved, use subject expertise or an attainable inquiry when warranted. Technical validity and reported experience require evidence appropriate to each.

#### IS.7:4.2 - Explain the interaction that affects the result

Trace the practice, participants and setting through the episode. Find what the target operation depends on and whether that contribution was or could be obtained. Examine local, organizational and external conditions that affect the result or its grounds. A roster, an organizational incentive and an external eligibility rule can impose different conditions under different authorities.

Use [FPF C.40.JD](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c40jd---develop-a-way-its-holder-and-its-provision-together) when the way of working, its holder and its provision may need to change together. Relate the intervention’s warranted function, the recipient experience and the service conditions. An absence of use might reflect an unavailable occasion, burdensome work, a justified rejection or missing help. Distinguish plausible competing explanations where the finding or response depends on them. A.3.1.MI:4.5 can help compare delivery, mechanism and context and identify evidence that would distinguish the explanations.

#### IS.7:4.3 - Select what to change and who can change it

Use the established account of the dependency. If a response is wanted, assess existing provision or design a change in the target, facilitation, preparation, provision or several related contributions. Explain how each serious proposal could supply the required result. Use IS.3 or an adequate existing comparison where the whole arrangement needs assessment.

| Located interaction | Receiving action |
| --- | --- |
| An essential operation is not understood or cannot yet be performed. | Establish who needs to perform the operation. A qualified provider can supply the result. For a human learning need, arrange suitable explanation, practice and feedback through the target practice and HCD. Assess the promised contribution under its receiving conditions. |
| The operation is possible but ordinary work prevents it. | Establish the conflict. Use a suitable existing arrangement or OCE.9/11 to obtain needed capacity and enabling relations where change is required. |
| Participant experience exposes a wrong function, burden or access assumption. | Reopen the target or strategy claim; use IS.6 when unequal influence prevents that account from affecting the change. |
| A local variant changes a relied-on function or condition. | Obtain subject judgement and [FPF A.3.1.MV](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#a31mv---construct-and-maintain-a-method-variant)/[FPF A.3.1.FT](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#a31ft---qualify-method-fit-and-transfer); keep the variant's status and fit limits. |
| A required condition belongs to another organization or external authority. | Use an applicable result already supplied under that authority. If a required contribution remains missing, identify the dependent limit and take a usable request to the holder where needed. |

The table identifies possible responses to a dependency established from the episode. A staff objection may correctly expose an unsuitable practice. A shortage may be real or a consequence of the chosen workflow. Trace the episode before treating either as a training need.

#### IS.7:4.4 - Fit the facilitation to the work and the people

Assess the facilitating contribution and the conditions under which its performer can supply it. An internal facilitator may know the work and be available in time. An external facilitator may bring needed expertise or an independent position. A paired arrangement requires a usable division of work, permission and time. Use an existing competent service where its terms fit.

Use [FPF C.32.MWA.PP](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c32mwapp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-provision-arrangements) to compare complete arrangements when their differences matter. Include access, credibility, expertise, discretion, response time and scarce capacity. Use [FPF A.15.8.MS](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#a158ms---configure-methodological-support-for-work) for needed explanations, tools and other method-use support. If a judgement exceeds a facilitator’s competence, use available qualified expertise or state the limit. Establish what the facilitator may decide and where an unresolved question can go.

Choose how to provide the result in light of the undertaking, risks and any wanted capability. A provider can perform the entire contribution, work alongside a learner, demonstrate an action or observe the learner’s attempt and give feedback. A qualified AI system can itself carry out an assigned comparison or explanation within its access and authority. Preserve confidentiality, applicable consent and affected participants’ ability to correct a claim attributed to them. Evidence from human facilitation requires grounds for transfer to an autonomous AI service.

#### IS.7:4.5 - Prepare and perform a bounded change

For a selected change, establish that the inputs, instructions, permissions, timing, equipment and receiving responses can be supplied. Use adequate existing arrangements. Work through an unresolved handoff with the necessary participants when that would make the connection workable. Use [OCE.6](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce6---establish-holder-assignments-and-enabling-relations-for-organization-change) for changed organizational relations and [OCE.11](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce11---coordinate-organization-change-work-with-continuing-service) to reconcile the attempt with continuing service. Obtain any missing outside commitment from its holder. If a required condition cannot be supplied, assess a smaller legitimate attempt, another arrangement or a stop as the task requires.

Use A.3.1.MI for a bounded introduction or for evidence about an earlier one. Keep the target baseline, support provided and evidence of use distinct. An observed outcome has its own grounds. To attribute it to the target, the facilitation or their combination, establish the relevant causal relation. Use a suitable research method when the stronger inference requires it.

#### IS.7:4.6 - Adapt the exact contribution and return the next question

Interpret relevant observations with A.3.1.MI:4.5. A missing facilitator action may expose a provision problem. An unsuitable result after delivery can raise a question about the proposed mechanism or local fit. A recipient’s account can change the grounds for the target. Use existing observations where they answer the question. Preserve a supported response and the limit on causal attribution if the cause remains unresolved.

Where continuing provision is at issue, use IS.5 to assess whether to retain, transfer, extend or end the arrangement. Use IS.8 when participants are to conduct a subsequent change themselves. If successful use reveals that the next useful result requires a different aim or a not-yet-developed Method, return to [FPF C.40.OD](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c40od---construct-and-continue-open-ended-development), [FPF C.40.NP](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c40np---continue-from-a-result-to-new-attainable-problems) and the relevant profession. The end of one implementation is compatible with continuing inquiry and development.

### IS.7:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.7:5.1 - The handover practice and its unavailable conditions

A constructed service team is introducing a handover practice. The subject specialists have qualified its required information and protection conditions. Initial records show that the agreed conversation often does not occur. A generic barrier checklist suggests motivation training. Following actual episodes shows that the incoming team is assigned elsewhere during the overlap and cannot access one necessary record.

An internal facilitator can arrange a local rehearsal but cannot alter the cross-team schedule. The responsible manager receives an OCE.11 coexistence proposal and the data owner receives the access question. Removing the required comparison would change the target practice, so the facilitator does not quietly delete it. While a required authority has not answered, a protected rehearsal with available material can test the explanation. To report a change in ordinary service, establish what occurred under its actual permissions and schedule.

After the enabling conditions are supplied, use A.3.1.MI to establish whether the conversation takes place, whether the information is used and what happens in the receiving work. If the process now occurs but creates an unexpected burden, that result reopens the target or local arrangement. A subsequent improvement in service does not by itself isolate the facilitator's causal contribution.

#### IS.7:5.2 - Choosing a shared or a local facilitator

In a constructed comparison, two sites each need three hours of local episode work per week. One external specialist is available for four hours in total. Six hours of specialist work cannot fit in the four-hour window, even under one contract. Suppose each site's existing local facilitator can perform its three hours with one hour of specialist consultation. The joint arrangement then demands two specialist hours and six local hours, plus any actual shared preparation. It is feasible only if those local performers, permissions and protected hours exist. If one site's decisive judgement remains beyond its local performer, the paired arrangement does not meet the same promise.

### IS.7:6 - Bias-Annotation

A familiar framework can make every difficulty look like one of its dimensions. A successful past intervention can make the target seem immune to revision. An enthusiastic local champion can hide a supply constraint. Relate each proposed response to the episode, the relevant authority and its required conditions.

### IS.7:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The target, its professional basis, the affected participants and the observed or proposed receiving episode are clear.
- The account identifies which interaction matters to the finding and any action or outside contribution still required.
- Target adaptation, facilitation, preparation and contextual change retain their separate claims and owners.
- Evidence of the required competence, position, discretion, time and receiving connection supports the claimed feasibility or identifies its limit.
- Claims about delivery and use have supporting observations. An adaptation, causal conclusion or capability claim is qualified for its own purpose.

### IS.7:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Score every contextual factor but leave the next action unexplained. | Identify the interaction that affects the answer. If intervention is required, establish the response, its holder and how its result can be assessed. |
| Train staff to compensate for an unavailable permission or resource. | Establish the condition and its effect. Obtain an authorized change if required before the dependent work can proceed. |
| Call all local changes harmless adaptation. | Identify the changed operation and condition, obtain subject/variant judgement, and preserve unresolved qualification. |
| Use facilitation as a synonym for holding meetings. | Identify what the facilitator did or is expected to do and what it contributes to the receiving work. To report a resulting change, use evidence of that change and of the facilitator’s contribution to it. |

### IS.7:9 - Consequences

An assessment can identify a bounded correction, show what other contribution is needed or justify stopping the dependent attempt. A proposed change still needs the professional judgement, cooperation and authority on which it relies.

### IS.7:10 - Architectural Rationale

Use C.40.JD for related changes to the way, holder and provision, IS.3 for support design and A.3.1.MI for introduction. Here the professional grounds, experience of recipients and conditions of service matter together. A change in one can alter whether the others support the intended result. Use that dependence to explain the interaction, and to select or revise help when change is required.

### IS.7:11 - SoTA-Echoing

[Harvey and Kitson's i-PARIHS (2016)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13012-016-0398-2) relates facilitation to innovation, recipients and the local, organizational and wider context. Use that interaction to identify which practice or holder can address the difficulty. [Mi-PARIHS (2023)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43058-022-00379-y) describes how assessment can inform tailored facilitation. Its planning tool and user feedback support conclusions about that work. Effectiveness of the arrangement proposed here needs separate evidence.

[Ritchie and colleagues (2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43058-026-01056-0) make the facilitating arrangement more explicit through dimensions including position, activities, flexibility and resources. Use the dimensions that change the actual design or reporting choice. An existing procedure can answer a question about the target practice if it covers the conditions. If effort affects the choice, compare the work of using it with the work other arrangements require. Reopen when a current treatment supplies a better actionable interaction or observations defeat a relied-on fit or mechanism.

### IS.7:12 - Relations

Use IS.1–3 and IS.5 for agreement, diagnosis, strategy and continuation as required. A.3.1.MI supports introduction and interpretation of later use. Use IS.6 if unequal influence prevents a needed contribution from reaching practice. C.40.JD addresses joint development, C.32.MWA.PP and A.15.8.MS address provision, and HCD and OCE support their respective human and organizational changes. Use IS.8 when recipients need to learn to facilitate a later change.

### IS.7:End

## IS.8 - Develop Recipients' Ability to Facilitate Further Practice Changes

**Type:** Method pattern
**Status:** Stable

### IS.8:1 - Problem frame

Use this Method to develop or assess the recipient’s ability to conduct a later practice change. Identify the contribution expected of the recipient. It might involve recognizing a difficulty, involving an affected party, obtaining help, interpreting a result or adapting the response. A person can perform such work, a team can divide it, and an organization can maintain the capacity to provide it.

Begin with the facilitation work the recipient needs under the promised conditions. Establish what the intended holder can already do and which contribution still needs development. If the task requires a continuing specialist service, use IS.5 to arrange or assess it.

### IS.8:2 - Problem

A team can complete a change with an expert who recognizes the difficulty, asks the decisive question or obtains permission. Which operations can the team carry out next time? Handing over the slides or ending the contract leaves that question open. Establish which operations the recipients need to learn and which a qualified specialist can continue to supply.

### IS.8:3 - Forces

| Force | Practical consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Current service and developmental work | Protect the learning attempt and distinguish its performance conditions from ordinary provision. |
| Local ownership and specialist contribution | Recipients can own a change while retaining expert work they do not need to learn. |
| Observable performance and self-report | Confidence can identify a question for discussion. To assess the claimed ability, use evidence of the holder’s contribution under the relevant conditions. |
| Immediate success and next challenge | Repeating an already solved case can hide inability to recognize a different difficulty. |

### IS.8:4 - Solution

#### IS.8:4.1 - Specify the subsequent facilitation work and its holder

Identify the later work, its holder and the support that will remain available. A person might recognize and refer a problem. A team might divide the entire facilitation job, or an organization might keep a combination of specialists available. Choose an episode that calls for the promised contribution.

Use [HCD.1](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Human%20Capability%20Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/HUMAN-CAPABILITY-DEVELOPMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#hcd1--derive-human-capability-demand-from-representative-later-work) to identify the human capability required and [HCD.3](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Human%20Capability%20Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/HUMAN-CAPABILITY-DEVELOPMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#hcd3--diagnose-whether-a-capability-misconception-or-behaviour-limits-work) when the cause of a present difficulty is unclear. Use [OCE.9](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce9---realize-a-bounded-organization-capability-increment) for the capability of an organization. Establish how that organization can keep the qualified people and other needed contributions available together. Reuse the capability already established. Specify any specialist judgement, AI assistance or tools included in the claim.

#### IS.8:4.2 - Expose the facilitation decisions within joint work

Identify the operations the capability claim concerns. Use IS.6 for bringing excluded knowledge into practice, IS.7 for the interaction of a practice, its participants and its setting, or A.3.1.MI to interpret introduction and later use. If the recipient needs to learn an operation, establish what to teach and how the learner can obtain any professional contribution it requires.

When a joint episode will help the recipient learn a decision, make the reasoning available while it can inform the attempt. The experienced performer can explain the observation, a plausible alternative interpretation, the chosen action and the stopping condition. Give the recipient a relevant operation to perform, such as reconstructing a lost referral, identifying who can resolve an external constraint, protecting a participant’s account or explaining what an unsuccessful result establishes. Understanding the demonstration can prepare the recipient for that work. Evidence of performing it comes from the recipient’s contribution.

#### IS.8:4.3 - Arrange practice, feedback and real receiving conditions

For a person learning the operation, use [HCD.9](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Human%20Capability%20Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/HUMAN-CAPABILITY-DEVELOPMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#hcd9---perform-deliberate-practice-with-feedback) to arrange practice and feedback. Use programme or provider Methods if the work requires them. Give the person providing feedback the attempt and the criteria that matter in the receiving work. A useful correction identifies an operation the learner can change, such as including a missing participant, explaining an adaptation, revising an infeasible demand or limiting a conclusion to the observation.

Use [OCE.18](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce18---arrange-capability-development-and-receiving-work-in-an-organizational-transition) to secure practice time and real receiving work in an organizational transition and [OCE.11](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce11---coordinate-organization-change-work-with-continuing-service) to keep the service available alongside practice. Identify who can take over when an attempt exceeds its allowed risk. Obtain agreement if the fallback is still open. Preserve needed assistance and distinguish the recipient’s work from any operation a mentor takes over.

Participants can learn different parts of the same undertaking. A local practitioner might investigate the episode, a colleague obtain authorization and an external specialist assess the technical adaptation. Establish the individual contribution for a personal claim and how the results reach one another for a claim about the combined arrangement.

#### IS.8:4.4 - Qualify the promised scope before changing assistance

Use [HCD.12](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Human%20Capability%20Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/HUMAN-CAPABILITY-DEVELOPMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#hcd12--test-transfer-to-unfamiliar-work-or-learning-from-a-new-source) for a claim that the same person can transfer the operation to unfamiliar work. Use adequate existing evidence when it covers the claimed difference. If an additional attempt is needed, vary a condition that could affect the operation, such as the group affected, the authority for a rule, the target mechanism or the relation between the activity and its outcome.

For an organizational claim, use a result from OCE.9:4.4–4.7 covering the intended configuration and subsequent use. Identify the participants or qualified substitutes, support retained, ways of handling failure and exceptions, observed limits and accepted consequences for service. Use existing observations. Exercise a changed connection if a consequential question about it remains and the exercise is warranted. Use HCD findings for the personal contributions and evidence of how the organization provides and combines them for the organizational claim.

When an assessment needs a new attempt, have the recipient perform the promised operations under the intended support conditions. These may include recognizing the issue, choosing a response, obtaining a contribution and interpreting the result. If the mentor supplies the decisive diagnosis, report the joint performance and what this episode establishes about the recipient’s work. Use other relevant evidence to assess independent diagnosis. A recipient who recognizes the need for a specialist and obtains a usable answer may demonstrate the promised coordinating ability. Assess specialist judgement from evidence of that judgement.

When deciding about future assistance, use the finding to keep it, make it conditional or change the contribution supplied. For a continuing dependence, identify where the recipient can obtain help with an exception. Establish the end of a contract from its terms and events, and acquisition of an ability from the recipient’s performance.

#### IS.8:4.5 - Make further development possible in ordinary practice

When further development is wanted, examine with the intended holder what the result makes possible and what difficulty remains. Include that holder in defining the next worthwhile problem. Use [FPF C.40.NP](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c40np---continue-from-a-result-to-new-attainable-problems) when a more demanding problem has become attainable, and [HCD.15](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Human%20Capability%20Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/HUMAN-CAPABILITY-DEVELOPMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#hcd15--sustain-continuing-human-capability-development) for continuing human development. If the facilitation Method itself needs to change, use [FPF C.39](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c39---find-and-develop-a-way-to-obtain-a-result) with the subject practice to develop it.

For continuing development, keep the participants’ access to evidence, specialist help and occasions to improve practice available. Use [CB.7](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/COMMUNITY-MANAGEMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#cb7---prepare-and-sustain-distributed-organising) when preparing the next community organizer and [CB.14](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/COMMUNITY-MANAGEMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#cb14---develop-and-maintain-a-shared-repertoire-of-practice) when a community needs to maintain a shared repertoire.

Report the capability supported by the evidence, its conditions and any unresolved question. The local holder may coordinate a change with continuing specialist help or may carry out a wider range of facilitation work. If the claim exceeds the evidence, revise it while protecting any useful current service.

### IS.8:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.8:5.1 - From a jointly solved case to a locally led change

Suppose an external facilitator and a service team repair a referral process. The expert notices that the incomplete requests contain a field that only the receiving agency can fill. The team supplies examples. This case establishes a joint repair and identifies the expert’s diagnosis. It leaves open whether the team can diagnose a later problem independently.

The parties agree that the local lead should reconstruct a failed referral, identify the missing contribution and obtain a response from whoever can supply it. The mentor demonstrates the earlier reasoning. In a new case the lead first interprets a need for clarification of an eligibility rule as an employee’s knowledge gap. Following feedback under HCD.9, the lead revises the inquiry and obtains an interpretable answer from the rule owner. A later unfamiliar case tests recognition without the mentor’s prompt.

If the lead performs that coordinating operation and a specialist must still assess a proposed technical adaptation, the service can retain specialist assessment. The lead’s performance supports the bounded coordination claim. A claim about a wider capability requires evidence about its other operations.

#### IS.8:5.2 - Useful AI assistance and the holder's own contribution

Suppose a facilitator uses an authorized AI service to compare de-identified episode accounts. The service proposes three explanations. If the facilitator is learning to judge them, observe the facilitator’s judgement and its consequences. If the undertaking promises AI-assisted facilitation, assess the combined work under its access, competence and protection conditions. Reassess an affected conclusion if the model changes or the service becomes unavailable. To establish how the AI acquires a capability, examine that system’s learning.

### IS.8:6 - Bias-Annotation

An expert may infer mastery when recipients repeat a new vocabulary. Recipients can understate reliance on help to gain local responsibility or overstate it to avoid an unsafe commitment. Check the operation, conditions and evidence for the claim. Assess the mentor’s interest in the contract separately.

### IS.8:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The intended holder, subsequent work and retained assistance define the capability claim.
- A proposed learning arrangement supplies the facilitation operation to be taught and its necessary professional prerequisites.
- When acquisition is needed, the recipient can practise the required operation and receive feedback under legitimate conditions. A performance claim identifies what the recipient did and under what conditions.
- The evidence identifies the recipient’s own work, any coordination undertaken, what specialists supplied and what a mentor took over.
- A decision about future help uses the supported scope of performance. If development is to continue, the holder can help identify a worthwhile next problem.

### IS.8:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Count transferred materials or self-confidence as implementation capability. | Obtain or reuse evidence of the named holder's relevant operation under the intended conditions. |
| Teach an operation whose professional basis is missing. | Use IS.6, IS.7 or the relevant subject practice to obtain the operation or prerequisite the learner needs. Then design practice for it. |
| Equate local ownership with doing every specialty unaided. | State which operations the holder performs and which qualified contributions it obtains. |
| End external help to prove independence. | First qualify the promised performance and arrange any remaining dependency through IS.5. |

### IS.8:9 - Consequences

The recipients can acquire a specified part of the work needed for later change, with remaining dependence visible. Practice and feedback consume time and may initially reduce throughput. A sufficient supplied service can therefore remain the better choice for an undertaking that does not promise acquisition.

### IS.8:10 - Architectural Rationale

HCD addresses human development, and OCE addresses organizational capability. Here the intended work is facilitation. An experienced performer can show how a decision was made. The recipient can practise the operation. Evidence about performance under the promised conditions supports a conclusion about ability and can inform a later decision about help.

### IS.8:11 - SoTA-Echoing

[Albers, Metz and Burke (2020)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-020-05145-1) include developing recipients’ implementation capacity in the work of a helper. The [ISP Practice Guide](https://implementation.fpg.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/ISP-Practice-Guide.pdf) connects this aim with work alongside recipients. Use HCD or OCE for the human or organizational capability in question. Use IS.5 when continuing specialist provision meets the undertaking.

The [Implementation Support Competencies Assessment study (2024)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13012-024-01390-8) developed a self-report instrument that can inform reflection and identify questions for development. A claim about this holder’s performance or service outcome requires evidence of the corresponding work or outcome. [The working-alliance study (2025)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43058-025-00705-0) examines facilitator–recipient relationships in its studied setting. To attribute a learning result to this sequence, use evidence supporting that causal claim. Reconsider the arrangement when a relevant operation, support condition or finding about capability development changes.

### IS.8:12 - Relations

Use IS.5 when transferring, continuing or ending help, and IS.6 or IS.7 for the facilitation operations being learned. Sections 4.1–4.5 identify the contributions of HCD.1/3/9/12/15, OCE.9/11/18 and C.40.NP. CB.7 and CB.14 concern community organizing and shared repertoire. Use E.23.CDI to follow the intended holder and its work when assessing capability development.

### IS.8:End

# A Connected Implementation-Support Application

## IS.Application:1 - From a workshop request to ordinary provision

The Harbor Design case connects a support agreement, a diagnosis, a choice of help and later observations. It uses the assumptions given in the Methods. Start with an existing diagnosis or arrangement when it answers the question, and return to an earlier condition if later work exposes a gap.

### IS.Application:1.1 - Change the promise before changing the people

At Harbor Design, six analysts prepare technical release recommendations. A sponsor requests a workshop on comparing source editions. Using IS.1, the parties establish the work that needs help. A change in the evidence supporting a release recommendation must reach the technical lead while the lead can still act on it.

The parties agree to cover the next two eligible packages. The source owner will make the editions available and the manager will protect time for review. A consequential difference must reach the technical lead for a response. The helper can arrange and examine the work. The lead judges the technical consequence, and the manager decides about the time.

The contrasting episodes in IS.2:5.1 show an inaccessible source and an adverse response to a justified challenge. Those difficulties need work on access and on the organization’s treatment of challenges. Two analysts also need to learn the comparison operation. Restoring access lets them attempt that operation. Their need for practice has its own evidence.

### IS.Application:1.2 - Choose the help and supply its conditions

The parties extend the undertaking to eight checks in the first week and an initial learning attempt for two analysts. In the comparison in IS.3:5.1, the workshop needs 5.5 hours of a specialist whose available time is three hours. Targeted rehearsal needs 2.5 specialist hours. Expert execution needs 1.6 specialist hours for the checks and gives the analysts no personal attempt.

The parties choose a plan in which the four analysts who already compare the editions will cover the eight ordinary checks. Two learners will receive targeted rehearsal, and both groups will use the repaired route to the sources. If a wider choice arises between sharing a specialist and providing one in each branch, use an existing applicable comparison or C.32.MWA.PP to compare the arrangements.

The qualified learning provider supplies representative contrasts and feedback using HCD. The technical lead decides how the findings affect ordinary release work. Under the assumptions of the example, the two agreed rehearsal periods and preparation time make the first-week arrangement feasible. A claim about the learners’ acquired ability needs evidence about their own later performance.

### IS.Application:1.3 - Use A.3.1.MI to separate what happened from what is claimed

Suppose the team restores access and conducts the rehearsal. An analyst later detects a consequential change and reports it to the technical lead, who withholds the affected release recommendation pending clarification. In another case, the historical edition is unavailable and the comparison cannot start. One learner completes a contrast after the helper identifies the decisive passage.

Use A.3.1.MI to interpret the three observations under the stated conditions.

| What was established or attempted | Later observation | Supported continuation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source access and a route for a consequential challenge | One supported check reached the technical lead and changed that case’s continuation. | The route worked in this case. To rely on it for other packages, establish that their source and receiving conditions are covered. |
| Local instructions that require two recoverable editions | The historical edition was unavailable in one case. | Report the limit on this comparison. If the comparison is still needed, establish whether the edition or a suitable alternative can be obtained from the source or subject practice. The learner had no opportunity to perform this comparison. |
| Rehearsal intended to develop comparison | The helper supplied the decisive recognition in one contrast. | Report the learner’s performance with that help. To assess independent recognition, use evidence from an attempt in which the learner made that judgement. If development is still needed, continue or revise the HCD arrangement. |

Establish access from the repaired route, rehearsal from the work performed, later use from the analyst’s report and the lead’s response, and capability from the learner’s relevant performance. The comparison Method can remain suitable while local access or help changes. If the question is what caused an improvement, use A.3.1.MI:4.5 to distinguish explanations and assess which an attainable inquiry could resolve.

If the historical edition cannot be recovered, a Method that requires it may be unsuitable for this source family. If further comparison is needed, seek a suitable variant or an authorized change of use when either is feasible. If neither is available, state which comparison remains open and retain the findings that have independent support.

### IS.Application:1.4 - Continue the practice without preserving every temporary service

Suppose the temporary specialist is due to leave after further qualified use. The team uses IS.5 to establish that unusual cases still require specialist recovery and interpretation of sources. As in IS.5:5.1, it secures a successor’s contribution and verifies that the receiving team can obtain it. The two learners continue their own development.

The coordinator can arrange the transition and later organize ordinary programmes or service. Identify what that person actually did, the project or service role in which they did it, and whether the people and means shared between simultaneous commitments can cover both.

If a later question exceeds the comparison Method’s scope, determine what would answer it. It may require research or development of a new subject Method. When the existing result still meets the need, continue ordinary provision if that service is wanted.

## IS.Application:End

## IS.Edition:2 - Product-series rule

The **Implementation Facilitation Principles Framework** product series covers editions for those who help recipients make evidence-informed changes usable or develop the capacity to conduct later changes. Admission to the series requires an effective constitution and an admission decision. This edition belongs to the continuing IS series.

Select editions that connect agreement about the help, diagnosis of the difficulty, provision and adaptation of help, interpretation of later use through the Core introduction Method, and continuation or ending of support. They must also cover how knowledge from other parties can affect a change, how facilitation fits its participants and context, and how recipients can learn to facilitate later changes. For admission, establish that the complete edition can be used independently and that its source limits and required suppliers have been qualified.
