# Leadership Readme

## Practical entries

These are selected examples, not a catalogue of leadership situations or a boundary on what the methods can help with. Bring the actual difficulty. If none fits, use the Table of Contents and read the relevant pattern's problem frame. An assisting agent can explain the methods in ordinary language: “Explain this and give me your comments without framework jargon.”

### OWN-ACTION — A chosen purpose is not becoming action

You have an undertaking of your own but cannot connect its purpose, actual action and further development. Use [Reference:1.6](#ldrreference16---direct-ones-own-contribution-and-development) to distinguish a direction still needing choice, a missing capability, an unsuitable development arrangement and a condition that prevents work. It returns to the complete Strategy and HCD methods where those results are needed. The next useful result can be a feasible personal continuation, a changed undertaking or a specific request for help; it need not involve forming a group.

### NO-SHARED-DIRECTION — A common concern has not become something people can undertake together

People agree that something matters, but interpret the situation differently or expect someone else to decide what it means for them. Use [LDR.5](#ldr5---develop-joint-direction-and-participation) to develop the direction with the people whose experience and action it needs, obtain its subject grounds, and establish a justified first action with its participation limits. Begin directly with a sufficient agreement or prepared proposal when those results already exist.

### MISSING-HELP — The work waits for one person to intervene

- **Situation:** Participants know the work, but only the initiator explains the purpose, raises a concern or obtains needed help.
- **Question:** Which contribution is missing, and who can supply it under the actual conditions?
- **First useful result or blocker:** An agreed capable arrangement for the next needed contribution, or the specific missing competence, time, access or authority preventing it.
- **Start with:** [LDR.1](#ldr1---obtain-and-continue-the-leadership-contribution-needed-for-joint-work).
- **Stop or return:** Use adequate ordinary coordination directly. Return after a failed contribution, changed conditions or a new need for continuation without the initiator.

### WHOLE-AGREEMENT — Each offer is plausible, but they do not fit together

- **Situation:** A customer accepts a date, a provider offers help and a manager promises time, but the full arrangement cannot yet be performed.
- **Question:** What complete proposal can the affected parties actually accept and supply?
- **First useful result or blocker:** The same feasible proposal accepted by the parties whose contributions it needs, or an explicit unresolved condition or refusal.
- **Start with:** [LDR.2](#ldr2---agree-feasible-joint-contributions).
- **Stop or return:** Do not add a negotiation procedure to an already sufficient agreement. Reconsider the affected whole when a relied-on contribution changes.

### UNANSWERED-QUESTION — A productive discussion still leaves no usable answer

- **Situation:** Participants have shared views and evidence, but the receiver cannot use them to settle the technical or practical question.
- **Question:** Which relation or inference is missing, and how will capable participants construct it?
- **First useful result or blocker:** A connected explanation or proposal for the receiving use, including a justified inquiry proposal when evidence is missing, or the exact unavailable subject contribution.
- **Start with:** [FPF B.5.SD](../../FPF-Spec.md#b5sd---construct-a-useful-answer-through-substantive-discussion).
- **Stop or return:** A competent direct answer needs no workshop. Return when a consequential premise changes or the receiver cannot use the proposed result.

### JOINT-START — Get from a promising idea to a useful contribution

People understand parts of a proposal, yet advice, objections, commitments and actual work do not connect into a supported beginning. Use [LDR.4](#ldr4---prepare-a-proposal-for-decision-and-first-use) to connect the proposal to its real consequences and obtain the contribution needed next. Missing authority, means or agreement remains explicit.

Name the receiving work and keep sufficient help. Obtain trusted judgement, examine the consequences, and answer objections or retain an unresolved concern. Agree feasible contributions and obtain effective authority. Make the same start mutually visible, complete the first useful handover, and continue with the help still needed. Reopen the affected question when conditions change. These moves need not become a sequence of meetings.

Trusted advice can improve a proposal without authorizing it. An answer can be sound without being acceptable to a contributor. Acceptance can be real while its required budget remains unavailable. A shared start can be understood while the first handover still fails.

Begin where the actual gap lies. If a sound proposal is refused because it displaces another obligation, use [LDR.2](#ldr2---agree-feasible-joint-contributions); repeating the explanation adds no missing capacity. If everyone wants to proceed but the technical consequence is unknown, [FPF B.5.SD](../../FPF-Spec.md#b5sd---construct-a-useful-answer-through-substantive-discussion) obtains the missing domain reasoning. [LDR.1](#ldr1---obtain-and-continue-the-leadership-contribution-needed-for-joint-work) arranges the capable help those methods require and returns when continuation fails. Each supplying pattern provides the explanation needed to perform and adapt its Method.

A prepared, authorized routine action can start directly when capable participants understand and accept the arrangement. An unresolved condition blocks only the work that depends on it. First use does not establish independent capability, enduring culture or the next development problem.

# Leadership Principles Framework Architectural Reference

## LDR.Reference:1 - The working situation and useful result

Leadership concerns how people give direction to their own action and to work with others. It examines how they understand a situation, choose purposes and exercise initiative and responsibility. That work can begin with a settled assignment. It can also begin with a concern that nobody yet knows how to address, an opportunity without a ready group, or participants who no longer accept the old direction. The discipline investigates these relations and develops ways of acting on them. A leadership appointment is one possible condition for that work; leadership also occurs between peers and across organizations.

The useful result is a change in what a person or group can understand, choose and do: a direction they can act on, a contribution they undertake with its limits understood, a challenge that reaches a consequential decision, or a practice that others can continue and improve. These results matter through the work and lives they affect. A tightly coordinated group can pursue a harmful purpose; an enthusiastic group can pursue an impossible one. Judging the purpose and its consequences remains part of responsible use, with the relevant ethical and professional contributions.

This account connects directing one's own contribution with leading and following in joint work, change and continuing development. Its recurring question is how purposes become agency and responsible action under actual conditions. Self-direction, interpersonal influence and collective direction are related branches, not identical operations at different sizes. Strategy, organization engineering, community practice and human development supply their professional results where those results are needed. The account develops these connections for practical use without claiming that one leadership theory explains every setting.

### LDR.Reference:1.1 - People, roles and relations of leading

A participant brings purposes, experience, capabilities and a life beyond the undertaking. A role describes a contribution expected in some work. An assignment gives a particular person or agent a responsibility under an actual arrangement. These descriptions meet in performance, but they need not agree: a qualified employee can reject the proposed purpose, a willing volunteer can lack the means, and a manager can promise a contribution outside their authority.

Consider an experienced engineer asked to lead a new service. The organizational view identifies who can assign work, allocate time and decide scope. The work view identifies the explanation, exception handling and handovers the service needs. The person's view may reveal a wish to develop design mastery and a concern that permanent service coordination will remove that opportunity. Asking only whether the engineer is competent misses the participation question. Asking only what motivates the engineer misses the other people's dependencies. LDR.1 relates these views in a role conversation and follows the resulting arrangement into work.

Leading and following are relations in particular activity. One participant can propose a course, obtain attention for a neglected fact, or invite others to take initiative. The others may rely on that contribution, amend it, decline it or lead the next part themselves. A proposal to lead becomes usable through such responses and the conditions of the work. Expertise can warrant reliance on a technical judgement without giving its bearer power to allocate everyone's time. Popularity can create influence without warranting the judgement.

Formal authority, actual influence and recognition by participants therefore answer different questions. Authority concerns a valid power to act or decide. Influence concerns what changes people's interpretation or action. Recognition concerns whether they accept this person's initiative or guidance for this matter. They can reinforce one another or diverge. A new manager can possess authority before earning confidence; a trusted peer can help the group reconsider its work while lacking power to bind it. Recover the relation that the next action requires rather than searching for one person who possesses every kind of leadership.

### LDR.Reference:1.2 - Construct the situation instead of assuming a ready group and goal

The relevant group is often itself part of the question. Begin with the work, consequence or concern that connects people. Follow who experiences it, whose knowledge changes its interpretation, whose contribution could change it and whose rights or commitments would be affected. A departmental roster can omit a customer, an absent shift or an external maintainer; an open invitation can include interested people who cannot authorize the proposed action. Include people for the contribution the inquiry needs and distinguish consultation from representation and decision.

Participants can disagree about what is happening before they disagree about a solution. Compare concrete episodes and their interpretations. One participant can call a delayed release laziness while another shows that unresolved integration work repeatedly returns to the same volunteer. That changes the object from individual effort to the relationship between release promises and contribution conditions. A shared description is useful when participants can correct it and use it to consider a next move. Repeating the same phrase is weak evidence of that understanding.

Direction relates a worthwhile change to the people who could undertake it and to the consequences they will accept. It may be a bounded inquiry, a service commitment or a longer ambition. It need not erase different personal purposes. A contributor can support a common repair because it helps users while another values learning, provided the actual arrangement respects both contributions and does not rely on an unstated sacrifice. If the purposes cannot coexist, narrower cooperation, separate work or refusal can be the result.

[LDR.5 — Develop Joint Direction and Participation](#ldr5---develop-joint-direction-and-participation) supplies this construction when the joint purpose and participants' responsibility remain unsettled. Strategic and domain reasoning establish whether a proposed direction is worthwhile and attainable; the leadership work lets the people concerned shape that reasoning, relate it to their own agency and undertake a justified next action. A strategy paper can be sound before anybody accepts responsibility for it. A group can be committed while the proposed strategy remains unsound. Both questions must reach their actual answer.

### LDR.Reference:1.3 - What knowledge permits a leadership choice

Leadership uses knowledge of the work and knowledge of how people interpret and participate in it. Relevant grounds include a participant's account, an observed interaction, the consequences of a previous promise, an effective assignment, and research on comparable work. Their evidential uses differ. A person's explanation gives a reason to investigate or negotiate; it does not identify the sole cause of every missed contribution. An authorized instruction establishes what may be required, not whether it was understood or can be performed. An observed improvement after several changes does not isolate the effect of the conversation.

Construct an explanation at the scale needed for the choice. If a contributor stops raising concerns, compare possible causes that imply different actions: the issue was resolved, the reporting route failed, reporting costs too much, earlier objections were punished, or the contributor no longer supports the work. Ask for a relevant episode, seek the person's account under usable disclosure conditions and inspect the response to the concern. Restore a known broken route directly. Where several explanations support the same warranted repair, act within that basis; a causal research programme is needed only for a stronger claim that depends on it.

Leadership theories can make an overlooked relation visible. The [CCL direction, alignment and commitment account](https://www.ccl.org/articles/leading-effectively-articles/make-leadership-happen-with-dac-framework/) directs attention to collective outcomes and the interactions producing them. Here it is a diagnostic comparison: agreement on direction, compatible work and personal willingness may fail separately. It does not make every participant's sacrifice a virtue or establish that all leadership has exactly three outcomes.

Historical research also constrains easy explanations. [Edmondson's 1999 study](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Organizational_Learning_and_Change/Edmondson_1999_Psychological_safety.pdf) connected team learning with perceived interpersonal risk and with structural support. Its cross-sectional study in one company did not establish universal causality. It gives reason to examine what follows a question or error report as well as access and workload. It supplies no guarantee that an invitation to speak creates safety.

The practical return runs both ways. These distinctions select an intervention; the actual response can defeat its explanation. A supported person who still rejects the purpose needs a different continuation from a willing person who cannot obtain a tool. If capable, supported participants repeatedly cannot make the proposed leadership method work, reconsider that method through LDR.1:4.6. Protect a justified disagreement instead of making success mean compliance with the initiator's interpretation.

### LDR.Reference:1.4 - Turn understanding into agency and joint work

Information becomes useful when participants can relate it to a choice they can make. Explain what is happening, why a change matters and what the proposed contribution would involve. Invite their account of its benefits, losses and alternatives. Work with a person's stated concern, not a presumed personality type. Interest can grow when people help shape a worthwhile undertaking and experience a useful contribution, but this is a possibility to establish in the actual relationship, not a guaranteed motivational technique.

Trust concerns reliance under vulnerability. Make the relied-on undertaking specific, keep it or report its failure promptly, and respond to a valid challenge in the promised way. These actions give people grounds for their next reliance. A demand to trust, a confident speech or universal disclosure can instead increase vulnerability without improving the grounds. Some protected information cannot be shared; explain the permitted basis for reliance and obtain a suitable independent contribution when needed.

Commitment has several meanings in ordinary leadership language. A promise to perform work, willingness to support a purpose and effective assignment are distinct. An employee can perform a valid assignment while disagreeing with the policy. A volunteer can enthusiastically support an aim while offering no available time. Clarify the actual contribution and the conditions on which others may rely. LDR.2 constructs compatible terms when such contributions conflict; LDR.4 carries an examinable proposal through the decisions and first performance it still needs.

Shared responsibility adds attention to the receiving whole. A person noticing that their correct output will leave the next participant unable to work can raise the gap, help find a response within their competence, and obtain a changed assignment when necessary. They do not acquire unlimited responsibility to repair everything. Clear interfaces, accessible explanations and a response to exceptions allow this attention to extend beyond one person's task without centralizing every decision. OCE supplies organization design when those relations must change; leadership helps people use and question them.

### LDR.Reference:1.5 - Continue and revise the practice

Repeated leadership is sustained through working relations: who can raise a matter, who responds, how responsibility passes, and what others learn from the response. Meetings, models and reminders can carry these relations. Their contribution depends on what people can do through them. A dashboard that exposes a changed dependency can let peers adjust directly; a dashboard nobody can interpret or act on supplies no such continuation. A recurring meeting can preserve an otherwise lost cross-role judgement or consume time without changing work.

Independent continuation requires more than copying a successful speech or appointing a successor. Other participants need the reasons for the direction, a usable method, opportunities to exercise initiative, relevant powers and support, and ways to question what has become inadequate. LDR.1 supplies the next capable contribution and human learning where valuable. C.36.RP supplies access to and renewal of shared methods; C.36 addresses the stronger claim that a practice persists across participants. These are distinct contributions to a continuing leadership practice, not compulsory stages of every episode.

A later result can change the encompassing account. A successful pilot may show that the shared purpose is worth continuing; it may also expose that the chosen participants cannot represent an affected group, that the apparent motivation depended on a reward, or that a useful exception has become the normal work. Revisit the direction, participation boundary or method affected by that fact. Retain the sound technical result and fulfilled commitments at their actual scope. Leadership develops as a discipline when such experience is made available for criticizing and improving its explanations and methods, while stronger general claims receive the evidence they need.

### LDR.Reference:1.6 - Direct one's own contribution and development

A person can need to understand and influence their own action before asking others to follow. R10's personal development case makes this explicit: choose a worthwhile capability, arrange time and support, use what is learned, and reconsider further learning when its return changes. The historical [self-leadership proposal by Manz, 1986](https://doi.org/10.5465/AMR.1986.4306232) likewise distinguishes self-influence from merely complying with externally supplied standards, including attention to what makes the activity itself worthwhile. Use this theoretical distinction to ask whether the person is choosing the work or merely meeting an imposed standard, while qualifying the proposed method for the actual situation.

Begin with the person's actual undertaking and account of it. A difficulty in choosing a worthwhile direction needs STR.5 and, when the way is unknown, STR.6. Work that the person values but cannot perform raises a different question from work they can perform but have not chosen to undertake. HCD.3 investigates a human limitation against non-training alternatives. For an actual capability-development choice, [HCD.2](../Human%20Capability%20Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/HUMAN-CAPABILITY-DEVELOPMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#hcd2---compose-and-compare-human-capability-development-programmes-for-later-work) develops and compares complete personal programmes, including practice, feedback, access, unfinished learning and displaced work. Its comparison leaves the person's choice open. [HCD.15](../Human%20Capability%20Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/HUMAN-CAPABILITY-DEVELOPMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#hcd15--sustain-continuing-human-capability-development) supplies continued learning from experience and changed conditions, with a return to ordinary work when the learning question is answered.

For example, suppose an independent designer keeps buying courses but cannot produce the analysis needed for a chosen kind of project. The next question is whether the missing analysis, its method, a capability gap or unavailable practice conditions explains that difficulty. A qualified analysis method and task comparison reveal that the designer can perform the core operation but has never completed a whole representative case with feedback. The development comparison can then favour finishing that contribution with available qualified help over starting another course. If actual client work removes the needed practice time, change the commitment or timing; buying further instruction has not restored that time. If the intended project ceases to be worthwhile, return to the purpose instead of completing the course only because money has been spent.

For an immediate action the person already knows and can perform, inspect what prevents its occurrence before adding learning. They can inspect a missed episode, identify what interrupted the intended action, and change a condition they control: for example, make the needed material available before a protected work interval or remove a competing demand they have authority to defer. Examine the next episode before attributing the difference to the change. When the problem lies outside their control, obtain help or revise the undertaking. A persistent difficulty with a different professional explanation requires that contribution; calling it weak self-leadership gives no diagnosis.

This personal branch meets the other methods where action depends on another participant: LDR.1 for the role conversation and capable help, LDR.2 for conflicting contributions, LDR.4 for a proposal needing decision and first use, and LDR.5 for direction that must be formed together. Helping another person direct their work requires preserving their purposes and choices, as well as any actual duties. Self-direction does not make a person responsible for conditions that others must supply.

## LDR.Reference:2 - How the leadership methods and their suppliers connect

The methods address different breaks in the relationship between people, purposes and joint work. Their ordering follows what is already available, rather than a fixed leadership lifecycle.

| What the situation leaves unresolved | Useful leadership result | Where the way is explained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People have concerns or ambitions but cannot yet say what they can undertake together or who will carry the initiative. | A direction shaped with the relevant participants, the bounds of their participation and a justified first action or refusal. | LDR.5, with strategic or domain construction where needed. |
| A needed explanation, role conversation, challenge or continuing initiative lacks a capable performer or usable conditions. | The contribution is performed and its next occasion has the stated help. | LDR.1. |
| Parties' proposed contributions or terms cannot coexist. | A feasible whole accepted by those whose agreement is required, or a precise impasse. | LDR.2. |
| An examinable proposal has not reached an effective decision, supported start and useful performance. | The authorized first contribution reaches its receiver, with the remaining conditions visible. | LDR.4. |
| A person cannot connect their chosen purpose, actual action and further development. | An attainable personal continuation or a reason to change the undertaking. | Reference:1.6 connects the choice to STR.5/.6, the complete HCD.2/.15 methods and the actual work conditions. |
| Participants cannot obtain a justified subject answer. | A connected answer with its grounds and limits. | FPF B.5.SD and the relevant professional method. |

For example, a designer and service staff can begin from unlike accounts of recurring user difficulty. LDR.5 helps them form a common question and undertake its investigation; the domain method supplies the explanation. If the result supports an improvement but the necessary service hours conflict with existing obligations, LDR.2 obtains compatible contributions. If the proposal still needs a permitted first use, LDR.4 obtains the decisions and follows the use. LDR.1 can supply capable help at any of these points. An already warranted and accepted routine action enters directly at its remaining work.

The same evidence can select a different continuation. New findings can defeat the problem interpretation, returning to LDR.5 and the domain inquiry. A sound shared direction with a withdrawn support offer returns to LDR.2. An effective decision followed by an inaccessible tool needs that access restored. Repeating the direction-setting conversation in the latter cases would add no missing contribution.

These connections make a repertoire usable; they do not make all joint work one leadership Method. A proposal, agreement, plan, instruction, performed contribution and capability claim remain different things. One performer may supply several methods, while one method can require several performers and working products. FPF C.32.MWA provides the fuller account when that distinction changes the arrangement.

## LDR.Reference:3 - What remains with other practices

Leadership work helps participants obtain and use a substantive contribution; the professional practice supplies its warranted content. Engineering supplies a warranted engineering proposal. Strategy supplies a justified direction and portfolio choice. Research supplies an inquiry and its findings. Human Capability Development supplies human learning design and assessment. A helper who can conduct a conversation may lack the ability to judge its technical subject.

In a company, Organization Change Engineering supplies the design and realization of organizational arrangements. Its OCE.9 connects a change to an organization-capability increment; OCE.10 investigates difficulties in participation and recurrent ways of working; OCE.11 reconciles change with continuing service. OCE.6 establishes actual enabling relations and authority. Corporate Governance supplies applicable corporate powers and acts: CGOV.3 resolves who may act and CGOV.11 makes and records a corporate decision. These contributions are needed when the particular work calls for them, not before every conversation.

A person's career transition needs a receiving work opportunity, actual assignment and the conditions for practice alongside current duties. [OCE.18](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce18---arrange-capability-development-and-receiving-work-in-an-organizational-transition) supplies that organizational connection. This framework can help obtain the necessary conversations and contributions; it does not replace that complete method with a leadership course.

Community Management addresses useful participation, relationships and joint contribution over time. CB.13 supplies a workable mandate for shared decisions when that question is live. Participants in a loose collaboration can also perform the leadership methods without first building a community or becoming employees of one organization.

Implementation support addresses a practice in its actual setting. Development and self-development can begin before a problem or method is settled and can continue after success with a new worthwhile challenge. Leadership can help participants obtain the needed contributions. Agreement, learning an established method or improving a current result does not by itself establish open-ended development: growth of knowledge or methods that makes further worthwhile problems attainable. C.40.OD explains that developmental connection. Likewise, independent continuation of a cultural practice requires its own explanation and evidence under C.36 and C.36.RP; one successful group episode is insufficient.

FPF Core supplies the connected answer through B.5.SD, including the subject reasoning, objection and changed-premise test. Decision Support supplies professional participation and facilitation through PSDA.12, PSDA.13 when the arrangement needs representation, protected channels, consent, confidentiality or faithful return. Leadership obtains the needed contributions and agreement; it does not acquire their professional or epistemic authority.


## LDR.Reference:4 - Authority, cost and sufficient ordinary work

Start with the work needed now and the arrangements already capable of producing it. A competent lead who allocates resources, obtains help, listens, adjusts the plan and checks the result may already satisfy the need. Preserve that arrangement. Add negotiation, specialist inquiry, supported learning or proposal preparation only where its additional contribution is worthwhile.

Compare the same receiving result over the same period. Include preparation, participation, representation, inquiry, implementation, help, waiting, supervision, maintenance and work displaced elsewhere. A development arrangement that retains the expert as fallback saves none of that expert's reserved availability. Its additional value may be another participant's demonstrated contribution or a more resilient continuation; state and examine that value.

Consent, authority and feasibility each change the available action. A volunteer can refuse a new contribution. A manager may possess a valid power to assign some work, without possessing a contractor's resources or another unit's time. An institutional decision may proceed under a legitimate rule despite disagreement; report that decision as such, rather than manufacturing unanimity. Use the applicable legal and professional methods where an actual rule is disputed. No generic leadership instruction authorizes ignoring it.

The cost of further discussion is also real. C.11.DUA asks what an attainable answer could change, what it costs and which feasible continuation remains. It supports a smaller probe, an ordinary answer, a narrow authorized action, deferral or stop. An unresolved concern survives as a concern where relevant; it creates no universal obligation to investigate everything.

## LDR.Reference:5 - People, organizations and capable AI

Human learning and agent engineering require different methods. HCD applies when the subject is a human capability. An organization supplies a contribution through its arranged people, agents, resources and authority; it does not acquire a human skill simply because its members attended training. An AI system's qualification, configuration, access and operational support need their own evidence and engineering. Human accounts of motive, trust and identity provide questions about the human participants; applying those accounts to an artificial agent requires a separate basis. A qualified agent can conduct the proposed inquiry and carry delegated decisions while obtaining actual human responses where those responses are required.

A capable AI may produce the whole leadership contribution, including substantive integration or a decision delegated to it. Do not freeze the old arrangement and merely insert a drafting assistant. Compare whole alternatives: ordinary human coordination, a person with AI support, autonomous agents under effective mandates, or a changed allocation that removes a previously necessary meeting or intermediate role. Establish actual competence for the task, permitted information use, ability to make the relied-on commitments, operating capacity, exception handling and a receiver able to use the result.

Cheap computation does not make attention, transition, authority, verification or another participant's work free. Nor does it justify preserving human duplication when that work provides no needed guarantee. The constructed application below shows how the comparison can select autonomous performance under one set of conditions and restore a narrower human contribution after a consequential change.

## LDR.Reference:6 - How to use and question the result

Read the Solution and worked case of the pattern that answers the actual difficulty. Use its direct suppliers for a missing professional result. Carry out the chosen contribution and follow it to its receiver. A declined proposal, a protected current arrangement or a precise unresolved condition can be the warranted outcome.

The examples are constructed situations. Their stipulated capacities, rules, acceptances and technical results show how the methods work and where conclusions change; they are not observed trials. They establish no general causal effect or measured teaching success.

The main costs are competent preparation, participants' time and the work needed to make a proposal or agreement executable. The main risk is making a polished social process stand in for an unsupported subject claim or commitment. Retain useful working products and reasons in the ordinary place the next participant uses; create no leadership reporting system merely to prove the framework was followed.

Reconsider the smallest affected part when the task, provider, support, authority, evidence or continuation demand changes. If the methods repeatedly fail to supply the promised contribution, examine whether the missing result belongs here, needs a better domain supplier, or requires a different arrangement. Neither a broad title nor an earlier successful case closes that question.

## LDR.Reference:End

# Leadership patterns

## LDR.1 - Obtain and Continue the Leadership Contribution Needed for Joint Work

**Kind:** Method pattern. **Status:** Stable.

### LDR.1:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when joint work needs an explanation, challenge, role conversation, allocation of help or other leadership contribution that is missing or depends on one initiator. Begin with the next piece of receiving work and ask what a participant needs to be able to do differently. The first useful result is a capable, feasible arrangement for supplying that contribution; follow it into actual performance.

For example, a reviewer needs an unsupported claim returned to its author, but a novice facilitator suppresses the objection. The missing contribution is to preserve the objection and obtain a qualified answer. It is not a general need for more inspirational leadership.

A competent current participant may already be able and authorized to help. Use that arrangement directly. If a file is inaccessible, restore access; if a technical choice is unsettled, obtain the relevant professional answer. A leadership conversation is useful when it helps obtain or use such a contribution, not as a substitute for it.

### LDR.1:2 - Problem

A title, roster or training event can be mistaken for performed leadership work. Conversely, necessary help may be left to a charismatic initiator, so it disappears when that person leaves. Introducing a mentor can fail because the mentor cannot change assignments, allocate time or provide the needed instruction.

The failure can persist even in a well-run meeting. Participants may speak freely while no one constructs the answer, accepts the whole arrangement or makes the effective decision. Selecting the missing contribution first prevents one familiar conversation form from being applied to every difficulty.

### LDR.1:3 - Forces

The group needs a useful result now, while wider capability or resilience may matter later. Developing another participant can serve that later need but consumes time and may initially increase dependence on expert help.

Competence, willingness, authority and availability rarely coincide automatically. The participant best able to explain a method may not control the learner's working week. A senior decision maker may lack the needed subject knowledge. An AI system may perform the reasoning while having no mandate to make the proposed commitment.

Candour helps expose errors and consequences. Employment, status or dependence on funding can make apparent agreement unreliable. A useful arrangement permits questions and legitimate refusal where the contribution is voluntary, while retaining real obligations and decision rules.

### LDR.1:4 - Solution

#### LDR.1:4.1 - Find the contribution through the receiving work

Recover a representative next task, the result it needs and what currently prevents it. When people cannot yet say what to undertake together, use LDR.5 to form the direction and participation needed for that next task. For an established task, ask the participants what help would change their next action. Useful answers include:

- making the intended result and its grounds understandable;
- bringing an unvoiced concern into the decision;
- helping a person enter, sustain, hand over or leave a role;
- obtaining a missing specialist answer or task assistance;
- reconciling incompatible contributions;
- helping a group construct a connected explanation;
- preparing a proposal that others can examine and begin;
- allowing another capable participant to continue without the initiator.

These are recognition cues, not a required classification. State the actual contribution in the vocabulary of the work: “Help the authors distinguish the obsolete source and return the unsupported claim before review,” rather than “improve engagement.”

Locate the cause only as far as the current choice needs. If a permission or tool failure is already established, ask its responsible provider for the missing condition. If the observed non-use has several plausible causes, OCE.10 supplies the organization-specific investigation; do not infer unwillingness from absence of performance. A voluntary community may need a question about participation, not an employer's assignment.

Name the receiving occasion and a small observation that would reveal whether the help worked. If a participant should raise an exception, the relevant observation is the exception raised and received, with its consequence for work. Attendance at a session would not establish that result.

#### LDR.1:4.2 - Select a capable arrangement and retain an adequate one

Choose the conversation or practical assistance by the needed result. A short brief can connect familiar contributions before work. If people have lost or never formed a direction they can undertake, LDR.5 addresses that prior question. A huddle can adjust a shared plan after a change. A role conversation can clarify a participant's responsibility and help. A debrief can examine a completed episode. A disputed whole arrangement needs LDR.2; an unanswered substantive question needs B.5.SD; a proposal not yet connected to its first use needs LDR.4.

Identify the actual contributions required to perform the selected method. Facilitation, subject expertise, decision authority, resource allocation, coaching and peer help can come from different providers. One person may supply several when capable and authorized. Select willing, capable participants with time and access for this occasion. A job title, an available calendar slot or general fluency is insufficient evidence of a difficult professional contribution.

Compare the available arrangements against the same result and period. Begin with competent ordinary coordination, including its existing assistance and follow-through. Compare an additional helper, another provider, supported development, autonomous qualified AI or a changed scope only when the alternatives matter. Include preparation, participants' consultation, representation, inquiry, allocation, execution, continuing help, checking, waiting and displaced work. Include overlaps: do not allocate the same time twice to contributions that require the mentor's separate attention. Qualify any combined or concurrent performance under the performer's actual capacity and the required guarantees.

Suppose competent current help supplies all three discussions required in six weeks. That arrangement is sufficient if those are the only required results. Preparing a second facilitator may be justified by future volunteer rotation, but it adds provider preparation, practice, feedback and later observation. If the expert remains reserved as fallback, credit no saved expert availability. Decide whether the additional continuation evidence is worth the added burden; retain competent help if not.

C.11.DUA supplies the appraisal of an additional inquiry or optional change. Ask what a plausible answer would change before commissioning another assessment. When present evidence already supports the next contribution, perform it. When no feasible competent arrangement exists, narrow, defer or decline the unsupported work; do not turn a hopeful development plan into present capacity.

#### LDR.1:4.3 - Make help usable under actual work conditions

For each contribution that changes the arrangement, recover what the performer will do and the conditions needed to do it. A mentor might demonstrate the operation, diagnose an error, give feedback or judge a practice attempt. A manager might allocate time or alter an assignment. A specialist might supply the technical answer. Agree the contribution rather than merely naming the role.

If the help changes a person's work, examine a representative task together with the current aims, instructions, time allocation and evaluation conditions. Identify who may alter each condition. A person asked to practise careful challenge while being rewarded for immediate closure faces a work-design conflict. More persuasion does not remove it. Obtain the change from its authorized owner or choose a feasible form of help under the existing conditions.

Account for displaced work. Reserving four hours for practice in a forty-hour week requires a feasible thirty-six-hour delivery allocation, another contributor, an accepted later result or some other actual adjustment. Obtain the affected receiver's contribution to that adjustment; a mentor's advice does not revise another team's promise.

Use OCE.4 to design necessary organizational contribution relations, OCE.6 to establish actual assignments and enabling conditions, and OCE.5 only if a continuing position is needed. A one-off contribution does not require a new office. In a voluntary association, use the participant's own agreement and the association's actual decision rules. A participant needing employer time or permission to share material obtains it from the relevant provider.

Agree the purpose of the conversation, what can be disclosed, who may use observations and how concerns can be raised. Keep a learning conversation distinct from an employment, clinical or disciplinary determination. The relevant professional or institutional procedure governs those determinations. Permission to facilitate does not confer access to protected material or authority to decide its subject.

#### LDR.1:4.4 - Perform the smallest sufficient working conversation

A conversation is complete when it supplies its receiving contribution, not merely when the allocated time ends. The following forms can be combined or shortened where their results are already available.

**Before familiar joint work, brief the participants.** State the intended result and current conditions. Ask participants to describe their contributions and limits; expose missing access, time, capability or help. Confirm who receives an exception and who can make the relevant decision. Establish the next action and the condition that would stop or change it. A readable shared exchange with the necessary responses can suffice; a meeting is optional.

**When conditions change, adjust the work together.** Identify the new fact and the contributions it affects. Reconsider allocation, priorities, timing and assistance with those who can change them. Communicate the resulting arrangement to everyone relying on it, including absent providers. Retain unaffected work. If the adjustment creates incompatible commitments, use LDR.2 for the changed whole rather than silently moving the burden.

**When a role is unclear, conduct a role conversation.** Recover the expected result, actual assignment and limits. Hear the participant's understanding, aims and concerns. Compare a relevant work episode with what was expected, distinguishing a missing skill, an unclear instruction, a resource failure and disagreement over the assignment. Agree the next useful correction or assistance. Entering, sustaining, handing over or leaving the role follows the actual authority and agreement; the conversation alone does not appoint or dismiss anyone.

**After an episode, examine what happened.** Reconstruct the relevant actions and results from available evidence. Distinguish an observed event from a proposed explanation. Identify what helped, what failed, what remains uncertain and a useful change for another episode. Obtain the contribution that can make that change and name the next observation. Protect the ability to report errors; blaming a person before understanding the work can conceal the missing condition. Retain an adequate successful practice instead of inventing an improvement to justify the debrief.

A short conversation may settle an objection or a contribution conflict. Use a fuller method only for the result still missing. A facilitated session that promises an explanation is subject to B.5.SD's construction; one that promises accepted commitments is subject to LDR.2.

##### LDR.1:4.4.1 - Relate a person's aims to the role they can undertake

When accepting, sustaining or changing a role is the missing contribution, keep the result expected of the role separate from the person's own purpose. Ask what the person wants to achieve or protect through participation and what they understand the assignment to require. They may value mastery, income, belonging, a useful public contribution or a bounded amount of work. These examples do not constitute a psychological classification. Use the answer the person actually gives and keep private matters outside the work unless they choose to make them relevant.

Compare the two accounts through a real next episode. What would the role require the person to do, what discretion would they have, what would others rely on, and what would happen to their other commitments? A mismatch can justify a clearer explanation, changed scope, different allocation, practical support, a development opportunity or another performer. Generate the alternative that changes the mismatch. If the expected work is necessary and the person legitimately declines it, obtain another contribution or revise the promise. If an effective assignment governs the work, use its actual decision and challenge route rather than disguising that obligation as personal enthusiasm.

Turn the supported choice into a contribution the person can explain and attempt. Connect a personal aim to an action within their control and the result it is meant to help obtain. Distinguish a learning goal, a performance promise and an aspiration. Learning to diagnose an unfamiliar class of faults may justify exploratory attempts; a promised service window requires dependable performance. Select feedback from the receiving work and the particular operation being developed. A numerical indicator can help locate a change, but ask whether it could improve while the needed result deteriorates. That possibility changes the feedback or the proposed goal.

A goal conversation can use a familiar local practice, including suitable OKRs, without adopting a universal target regime. Make clear which result others may rely on, which attempt is exploratory, how the goal will be revisited and how observations may affect the person. The [Google re:Work account](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs) is a historical company practice: it combines organizational, team and individual goals, makes progress visible and distinguishes OKRs from employee evaluation. It does not support the proposition that every goal must be individual, unattainable or detached from joint priorities.

Obtain the decisions that make the agreed opportunity real. Review it at an interval where the next observation can still change the work; a calendar ritual without a receiving choice adds little. Use observed performance and the actual assignment to adjust the person's responsibility and support. If the person's purpose, receiving work or available conditions change, reconsider the affected arrangement. Keeping a willing person in an obsolete role is not successful continuation.

#### LDR.1:4.5 - Obtain human development when that additional result is worthwhile

When a human cannot yet supply a needed contribution, identify the target person, the particular operation, the later situation and the evidence needed to rely on performance. HCD.1 establishes capability demand. HCD.3 asks whether the limitation is changeable through human development or calls for another remedy. HCD.4 relates the target to the person's simultaneous work profile. Use their actual answers rather than translating every failed episode into a learning deficit.

Obtain a qualified learning provider. Through HCD.7, establish that the provider can diagnose the particular operation and can supply preparation, instruction, observation, feedback and assessment at the required time. A respected manager or subject expert is not automatically a capable teacher of this operation. If the provider's own capability is missing, HCD.8 addresses its development; another capable provider may be a better immediate answer.

Construct the learning opportunity around the target. Let the learner see a demonstration with reasons, attempt relevant cases, receive feedback on the missed operation and practise a consequential variation. Observe a new attempt without supplying the very step whose independent performance is being claimed. Explain how these activities address the actual difficulty. A generic course may be a suitable part of that arrangement, but course attendance does not supply the target evidence.

Arrange later work in which the contribution can be used, with access, time, tools and help. Agree the allowed use of learning observations. If failure is punished under the ordinary performance system, recover a protected opportunity or another feasible arrangement before relying on candid practice. [OCE.18](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce18---arrange-capability-development-and-receiving-work-in-an-organizational-transition) supplies the additional connection for a career transition into receiving work; a development target alone is no appointment.

Human development may be unnecessary when a qualified colleague, external service or competent AI can supply the required contribution at acceptable total cost. Compare those alternatives against the actual values, including any independent human mastery that the participants deliberately want. Do not call replacement of human performance an increase in that person's skill.

#### LDR.1:4.6 - Continue with the stated help, and change the arrangement when needed

Arrange the next contribution: when it is needed, who can supply it, what assistance remains and where an issue beyond that performer's competence or authority goes. Retain a useful example or explanation where the next participant can find it. Use the existing work product or communication channel; a new record has value only if somebody needs it.

If continuation without the initiator matters, observe a later comparable episode with another capable participant. State the retained help. A performance in which the initiator supplies the decisive question does not establish independence in asking that question. It can still be a useful assisted result. Rotate responsibilities only among willing, capable and properly supported performers; rotation alone does not develop them.

After a failure, identify the missing contribution. Revise the learning target or instruction for a human limitation, obtain workplace support for an unavailable condition, repair a tool, secure a qualified subject method or revise the promise. Repeating the same conversation is useful only if its contribution remains the actual gap.

The selected leadership Method can itself be inadequate. If capable participants with sufficient means, effective authority and a qualified subject method still cannot obtain the required contribution, examine the way the leadership work is being performed. Give A.3.1.QM the identified Method or candidate account, the receiving result, situation and allowed variation, its claimed contribution, and the available evidence about performance and local connections. Its bounded qualification can retain a usable Method, reject its use or expose an unresolved connection without demanding a new trial when current evidence suffices. Revise the failed operation or choose an adequate alternative, then qualify the affected contribution before relying on it. Where this repair needs an organization-change contribution to be constructed or specified, OCE.15 supplies that subject work. Ordinary joint work and independent-community cases need no organizational-change branch merely to question their leadership Method.

Return what was performed, what it enabled and what remains unavailable. Keep separate the agreement, performed work, bounded capability evidence and cultural-continuation claim. C.36 governs the latter; C.28 governs a causal-effect claim. One successful episode supports only the continuation warranted by that episode and its conditions.

#### LDR.1:4.7 - Reconstruct the arrangement when an AI can perform the contribution

Specify the contribution and its conditions before deciding which performer should remain human. Ask whether a qualified AI can perform the substantive reasoning, obtain a response, allocate a resource or execute a decision within an effective mandate. Establish its access, tools, operating capacity, permitted communication and exception route. The party granting the mandate needs authority to grant it; a provider's capability claim does not supply that power.

Compare complete arrangements. If an autonomous agent can conduct the inquiry and make the bounded operational allocation, requiring a human to repeat every inference may add no needed result. Remove duplication only when the required guarantee and effective rules permit it. If a human decision, independent review or participant consent remains necessary, keep that precise contribution and its workload. Do not preserve a generic “human in the loop” while leaving its job unexplained.

The change can remove a meeting, combine preparation with performance or move the limiting resource to specialist access, physical execution or recipients' response time. Recompute those dependencies and total costs. Cheap reasoning can make frequent internal reconsideration worthwhile while interrupting participants only when the new answer changes their work. Changed capability or lower coordination cost is a reason to reconsider the whole arrangement under C.11.DUA.

### LDR.1:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### LDR.1:5.1 - A mentor who does not control assignments

Suppose a member-governed standards association needs three editorial discussions over six weeks. Each discussion must return the disputed claim, a restatement confirmed by the participants and a question for a qualified source reader before the chair's editorial decision. No organization redesign is proposed.

An experienced colleague can lead all three with the necessary preparation, source expertise, access, participant time and follow-through. A willing member also wants to learn. In an initial attempt, that member replaces an objection with their preferred wording; the colleague restores the actual objection with its author. The association cannot yet rely on the member's independent restatement.

There are two adequate proposals for the current discussions. Continue with the colleague, or retain that competent fallback while adding learning. The second proposal includes varied practice, provider preparation, feedback, a new-case observation and later workplace use. The expert's reserved availability is retained, so the proposal claims no time saving. The association accepts the extra work because continuation during volunteer rotation matters to it.

HCD.7 establishes that the colleague can teach and assess these steps and has time in the weeks when facilitation and mentoring overlap. The learner and mentor agree what the observations may be used for. Participants obtain any employer time or evidence-use permission they need. The chair arranges the actual editorial opportunity under the bylaws; the mentor cannot make the chair's decision or allocate an employer's time.

On a new practice case, the learner preserves the objection and forms an appropriate inquiry question without prompts. In the later editorial discussion, the learner again supplies those steps, with the mentor available but not substituting for them. A qualified member checks the source; the elected chair makes the reserved editorial decision. The result is bounded evidence for those contributions under the observed conditions.

If the learner instead needs the mentor to form the question, the discussion continues with that help. The association compares further targeted practice with retaining sufficient assistance. If the mentor withdraws, obtain a qualified contribution or revise the affected date; earlier observations do not make the promised support present. If only the three current discussions matter, keep the competent current arrangement and omit development.

#### LDR.1:5.2 - An ordinary brief around a disputed source

Suppose an engineer and an electrical peer are preparing an evidence package. Their ordinary brief already identifies source revisions, the receiver's acceptance boundary and the person who can answer a missing-source question. They need no new leadership programme.

A rehearsal exposes confusing old and current version labels. In the debrief, the peer shows how the obsolete revision could be mistaken for the current one. The tool owner receives a concrete display-repair request. A qualified coach supplies practice in challenging an unsupported claim; the line manager provides its time without breaking the current service allocation.

In the next episode the peer raises the missing-source question without the initiating facilitator. The source owner investigates. The engineer and peer return claims that remain unsupported to their authors before completing the evidence package. This is useful continuation with the stated support. It establishes neither enduring culture nor general leadership mastery.

If the only problem had been a broken access link, restoring the link would have been sufficient. The brief helps discover that distinction; it does not oblige the group to continue with coaching.

#### LDR.1:5.3 - An experienced engineer can perform a role but does not want its present terms

Suppose a manager asks an experienced engineer to coordinate a new service. The engineer can perform the work but wants to develop design mastery and sees the coordination role as a permanent departure from it. Treating this as missing competence would select the wrong intervention.

The role conversation traces a representative week. Coordination would consume three of the four days available after existing obligations, leaving one day for design. The service needs its coordination result, but it does not require this engineer to supply every recurring handover. A capable operations colleague can cover one day if their manager approves a corresponding allocation. The engineer offers two coordination days and two design days for an eight-week introduction; the operations colleague offers the remaining day under that condition. Their managers and affected receivers accept the actual workload changes through LDR.2 and the assignment process. If that contribution is unavailable, the original three-day demand remains; the attractive two-day offer is not yet a feasible arrangement.

The personal development aim becomes a bounded learning contribution: the engineer will construct and justify a design alternative, with qualified feedback arranged through HCD and with a real receiving design question. Its exploratory result is kept separate from the service's required daily coordination. After the first weeks, inspect both receiving results and the experienced burden. If the service requires four coordination days, reopen the scope and allocation; do not treat the engineer's earlier agreement as consent to give up the design work indefinitely.

Willingness and role terms change this arrangement even when skill is adequate. The personal preference becomes feasible only through the actual allocation and accepted contributions.

### LDR.1:6 - Bias-Annotation

**Lenses:** Governance, Architecture, Ontology/Epistemology, Pragmatics and Didactics. **Scope:** Joint-work contributions under the authority and participation conditions stated here.

The method favours an observable contribution over a heroic-person account. That preference can itself overproduce procedure if every small act is catalogued. Use ordinary work directly and describe only distinctions that change performance or reliance. Preserve the participant's own aims and the difference between obligation and voluntary contribution.

### LDR.1:7 - Conformance Checklist

A conforming use of this method makes the following questions answerable at the scale the work needs:

- Which receiving task needs help, and what contribution changes its next action?
- If taking or changing a role is the difficulty, how do the person's purposes, actual assignment and needed work fit, and which goal is exploratory or relied on?
- Can an existing competent arrangement already supply that result?
- Who supplies facilitation, subject expertise, authority, time allocation and learning where each is needed?
- Do the contributions fit together in the relevant time windows, including displaced work?
- If human development is selected, what target, qualified provider, practice and later observation justify it?
- What was actually performed, with which help, and what continuation remains warranted?
- If an AI changes the arrangement, which real work or guarantee remains and which old activity can be removed?

These questions examine the same contribution as the Solution. They are not a requirement to create a separate report for a routine brief.

### LDR.1:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Invited mistake | Better action |
| --- | --- |
| Appoint a champion and count the contribution as supplied. | Name and perform the help needed by the receiving task. |
| Add mentoring while retaining every existing workload. | Obtain feasible time and the affected receivers' accommodation, or retain another sufficient arrangement. |
| Treat a manager, expert and learning provider as interchangeable. | Obtain each missing competence or power from its actual provider. |
| Count assisted success as independent mastery. | State what the performer did and what the helper supplied. |
| Add an AI assistant but retain all old coordination and review automatically. | Compare the whole arrangement against the required result, guarantee and effective rules. |

### LDR.1:9 - Consequences

The group can obtain needed help from several capable performers and see which continuation still depends on an initiator. Useful current work need not wait for broad leadership development. Where another participant's mastery is valuable, the method makes its additional cost and evidence visible.

The arrangement requires time, competent judgement and sometimes changes to work allocation. Development can be more expensive than continuing expert help. Authority or scarce specialist access can remain limiting even when communication and reasoning become cheap.

### LDR.1:10 - Rationale

The receiving contribution is the stable object across different titles and organizational forms. Selecting it first explains why a brief, debrief, mentor, technical specialist or autonomous agent can each be appropriate in one situation and unnecessary in another. Later performance connects help to use; it also reveals whether the initiator has actually ceased supplying the decisive operation.

### LDR.1:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For obtaining useful leadership work, the selected line combines task-specific contributions, competent teamwork and supported development only when its additional result matters. The serious alternative is already competent ordinary coordination, including resource allocation, assistance and feedback. It is sufficient for many cases; this method adds no value merely by renaming it. LDR.1:4.1–LDR.1:4.6 add value where a missing contribution, provider condition or continuation claim would otherwise remain implicit. The case deliberately accepts extra work for another participant's bounded performance.

**Adapt** the relational emphasis in CCL's [2026 leadership account](https://www.ccl.org/articles/leading-effectively-articles/what-is-leadership-a-definition/): contributions can be produced through several participants rather than inferred from one position. Its direction, alignment and commitment model is a conceptual source, not the ontology of every leadership practice or evidence that this method is effective.

**Adapt** AHRQ TeamSTEPPS [briefs](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/team/tools/briefs.html), [huddles](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/team/tools/huddle.html) and [debriefs](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/team/tools/debrief.html) in LDR.1:4.4: prepare familiar joint work, update its arrangement when conditions change and examine a completed episode. Healthcare permissions and evidence do not automatically transfer to editorial or engineering work. These teamwork practices remain adequate alternatives.

For human learning, **adopt** the direct HCD demand, target, profile and provider methods rather than inferring capability from participation. Qualified programmes combining instruction, coaching and workplace support are serious alternatives to assembling separate local help. Compare their fit, complete workload and attainable performance evidence. A programme title does not settle that comparison.

Reopen the arrangement when the receiving work, support, capability evidence or value of continuation changes, or when a competent lower-burden arrangement becomes available. The prospective cases demonstrate conditional reasoning; comparative effectiveness in a new setting remains an empirical question.

### LDR.1:12 - Relations

LDR.5 forms joint direction and participation when the next receiving work remains unsettled. LDR.2 supplies a feasible accepted whole when contributions conflict. B.5.SD obtains the connected answer a discussion promises. LDR.4 prepares a proposal through decision and first useful performance. None is mandatory when the ordinary contribution is already adequate.

[FPF A.3.1.QM — Qualify a Method for Its Intended Contribution](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#a31qm---qualify-a-method-for-its-intended-contribution) qualifies the leadership Method or candidate account for its bounded receiving use and examines independently decidable local connections. Return the actual failed contribution and conditions; obtaining a competent person does not settle the Method's suitability. OCE.15 constructs or specifies the organization-change contribution only when that is the receiving question.

[Human Capability Development (HCD)](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Human%20Capability%20Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/HUMAN-CAPABILITY-DEVELOPMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md), through HCD.1, HCD.3, HCD.4, HCD.7 and HCD.8, supplies the human demand, target, work profile, learning-provider conditions and development of a missing provider capability. It does not qualify an AI or constitute an organization's capability.

[Organization Change Engineering (OCE)](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md), through OCE.4–OCE.6 and OCE.9–OCE.11, supplies organizational relations, enabling assignments, capability realization, participation inquiry, and reconciliation of change with continuing service. [OCE.18](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce18---arrange-capability-development-and-receiving-work-in-an-organizational-transition) supplies the career-to-receiving-work connection.

[FPF C.11.DUA](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c11dua---make-advice-and-evidence-demands-worth-their-burden) appraises additional inquiry and changes against their whole receiving cost and value. C.32.MWA separates methods, work and arrangements; C.36 governs cultural continuation; C.28 governs causal claims. Use their explanations only when those questions are current.

### LDR.1:End

## LDR.2 - Agree Feasible Joint Contributions

**Kind:** Method pattern. **Status:** Stable.

### LDR.2:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when participants need one another's contributions but their proposed scopes, dates, costs or conditions cannot yet coexist. The object is a complete arrangement that the affected parties can actually accept and supply. Begin by recovering what each party needs, can offer and may authorize, then construct and examine whole proposals.

The useful result is acceptance of the same feasible proposal by the parties whose agreement it requires, with any outstanding condition explicit. A warranted refusal or a specific unresolved contribution is also useful: it prevents unsupported reliance.

A routine exchange with clear terms and available resources may already be agreed. Use it directly. If participants have not yet formed a direction or understood why they would undertake this work, LDR.5 supplies that construction before negotiating detailed terms. This pattern is a bounded negotiation method for joint contributions. It does not adjudicate a legal dispute or require consensus where an applicable decision rule permits another form.

### LDR.2:2 - Problem

Several individually sensible promises can form an impossible whole. A provider's help may require local time already promised elsewhere. A customer may accept a date on one assumed scope while the engineering team agrees to another. A representative may express support without authority to commit the represented party.

The apparent solution can be a numerical ranking or an enthusiastic meeting that hides those incompatibilities. A technically feasible proposal is not yet accepted; acceptance is not technical evidence; neither supplies a missing resource.

### LDR.2:3 - Forces

Exploring options benefits from freedom to reconsider. Commitments need sufficiently stable terms and real authority. Mixing those moments discourages exploration or creates accidental reliance.

The parties may value consequences differently and possess different alternatives. A common metric can assist a particular comparison, but it does not turn several independent parties into one decision maker.

Inquiry may reveal a feasible alternative, yet its cost and delay can consume the opportunity. Whole proposals need enough detail for acceptance without demanding a complete forecast of every future event.

Absent and later-affected participants can bear substantial costs. Their participation or representation is needed where the arrangement depends on their contribution or changes a protected interest.

### LDR.2:4 - Solution

#### LDR.2:4.1 - Establish who can explore and who can commit

Name the receiving result and proposed cooperation. Identify the contributions it requires, the people or bodies able to supply them, and whose existing work or rights would change. Recover a party omitted from the first conversation when its contribution or consent matters. In engineering, SYSE.17 helps discover systems that may bear consequences.

Agree a practicable way to explore: participants, time, permitted information, representation, a way to question the proposal and a legitimate way to decline. A representative needs a mandate for the contribution being discussed; attendance or seniority alone does not supply it. CB.13 can establish a workable shared-decision mandate. If an existing protocol already provides that result, retain it.

Obtain participants capable of the negotiation and its substantive judgements. A facilitator may support the process; a qualified engineer, financial practitioner or other specialist supplies the corresponding subject answer. Establish confidentiality and consultation conditions before depending on information. Do not require disclosure a participant has no authority to make; obtain a permitted narrower answer or retain the resulting uncertainty.

Distinguish the act being sought. An agreement among voluntary contributors differs from an executive allocation, a contract, a board decision or permission for a trial. Obtain the relevant authority answer where needed. A negotiation can prepare those acts without making them.

#### LDR.2:4.2 - Recover the condition behind a position

Ask what result a stated demand protects, what would fail without it and what observation could answer the concern. Retain the participant's own answer; do not reinterpret every objection as misunderstanding.

For example, “the complete interface by day 20” may protect the need for daily production data, while automatic control can wait. That distinction creates options only if the customer with the necessary authority accepts it. If complete control by day 20 is a real non-negotiable requirement, the reduced proposal does not satisfy it.

Separate a needed result from an assumed means. A required safety property may be met in several configurations; a preferred staffing arrangement may be one way of obtaining a contribution. Use the domain method to examine that claim. B.5.QD.CF helps reconsider conflicting assumptions where an assumed necessary means creates the impasse. D.4 helps when the conflict concerns value premises and acceptable consequences. Neither method grants permission to relax an effective requirement.

Recover meaningful alternatives if agreement fails: continue the present service, obtain a different provider, narrow the result, delay it, perform independent work or decline. A party with no acceptable no-agreement outcome still needs an honest account of that condition; pressure does not make an impossible proposal feasible.

#### LDR.2:4.3 - Construct whole alternatives before requesting commitment

Combine changes in scope, timing, performer, support, sequence, price and burden into complete proposals. Explain how the contributions fit. Keep more than one materially different feasible candidate where that improves the negotiation, without manufacturing alternatives merely for a count.

For each proposal, follow the needed result backwards to its inputs and enabling conditions. Then follow each promised contribution forwards to who receives it and what else it consumes. This two-way examination reveals hidden dependencies. A support offer can make the technical option feasible while taking time from another project; that displaced receiver belongs in the revised proposal.

Use explicit conditions where uncertainty remains. “Read-only first use after the permitted isolation test, with twelve local support hours each week for six weeks” is examinable. “Start small and provide support” leaves the conditions for performance unwritten.

Compare proposals using the parties' relevant criteria and credible domain grounds. State who receives benefits, bears costs, defers work or accepts risk. Shared criteria can justify terms, but a combined score does not establish agreement. Do not compensate for an unavailable permission with a high benefit score.

Apply C.11.DUA to the work of negotiation itself. Include preparation, representation, consultation, facilitation, subject inquiry, waiting, implementation, continuing support, monitoring, likely revision and displaced work. Agree an attainable effort and decision horizon. When the negotiation's burden exceeds its likely useful gain, reduce the question, use an adequate existing arrangement or stop.

#### LDR.2:4.4 - Resolve a choice-changing unknown, then rebuild the proposal

Identify the question whose answer could change a proposal's feasibility or acceptance. Ask the qualified subject practitioner for the smallest sufficient inquiry, with its result, conditions, cost and possible consequences for the choice. Compare it with using current knowledge, a narrower commitment, another source, deferral or no agreement.

Obtain permission and resources for the inquiry itself. A test authorization permits the stated test, not introduction into production. A favourable finding supports only the configuration and conditions actually examined.

Return the result to the whole proposal. A technical test may remove one uncertainty while a provider's offer adds cost, local effort or a time limit. Recalculate joint demand and reopen terms affected by that change. Do not attach new facts to the old proposal while leaving its commitments untouched.

If the inquiry defeats the option, discard or revise that option. Retain independently useful findings and alternatives. If the answer remains uncertain and the required guarantee cannot be obtained, keep the dependent commitment unavailable; uncertainty is not filled by group agreement.

#### LDR.2:4.5 - Test joint performance, not just individual promises

Before seeking acceptance, place the relied-on contributions in their relevant time windows. Check the quantities and conditions that limit performance: qualified specialist hours, a laboratory window, a delegated spending limit, particular access, a service reserve or a participant's actual availability.

Sum demands that compete for the same resource. Do not add unlike capacities as if a spare administrator hour supplied a missing engineering hour. Retain timing: a sufficient monthly total cannot supply a specialist who is unavailable before the decision. Include support and supervision retained by the arrangement; they are often the hidden constraint.

When the proposed whole does not fit, construct actual alternatives. Move a contribution only with its receiver's accepted change; narrow scope without losing the required result; share preparation where it really serves several contributions; obtain a qualified substitute or provider; change the method or sequencing; or defer the unsupported work. Compare the consequences at the level where they matter. A local saving can create a larger downstream burden.

Authority has its own compatibility test. A manager may allocate one team's time while another unit controls the required specialist. A provider may promise support only after a contract becomes effective. Obtain those contributions or keep the proposal conditional. Do not infer corporate, employment or contractual powers from the fact that participants reached an informal consensus.

#### LDR.2:4.6 - Obtain responses to the same complete version

Put the complete proposal, its grounds and outstanding conditions before the parties whose acceptance it needs. Make each party's contribution and relied-on return recognizable. Let them accept, conditionally accept, refuse or leave the matter unanswered. Those responses differ.

A conditional offer of help after funding is available is not a commitment to seek funding, nor is it present funded help. A representative who needs consultation returns to the represented party within the actual mandate. An absent participant's silence supplies no agreement.

If one party changes its contribution, identify the others whose terms or reliance change and return the amended whole to them. A sequence of separately accepted fragments is sufficient only when those fragments really compose under the same conditions; otherwise obtain responses to the combined proposal.

Retain the accepted terms in the ordinary form the participants use. The record needs enough identity and conditions to distinguish this agreement from a neighbouring version. It need not be a new negotiation template. If the applicable decision rule permits a non-consensual decision, name that rule, the actual decision and the remaining disagreement. Do not present it as mutual acceptance.

#### LDR.2:4.7 - Return the agreement to work and respond to a changed contribution

Give the receiving practitioners the accepted conditions and unresolved matters. Product and organization decisions may need coordination through OCE.7; engineering architecture and whole-result alternatives use SYSE.6 and SYSE.24; an existing promise may need an authorized response through OPS.13. In another domain, use the corresponding supplier rather than importing engineering decisions by name.

Obtain the separate acts that make the arrangement effective, then perform the contributions. LDR.4 helps when a prepared proposal still needs an effective decision, mutually visible start and first useful handover. Acceptance alone does not establish completion.

Agree a practical response to foreseeable changes: who reports the loss, who can supply a substitute, which participants reconsider dependent terms and how an existing promise is handled. When support disappears, identify the contributions depending on it. Compare feasible substitutes, changed dates or scope with the affected parties; obtain renewed acceptance where reliance changes.

Preserve unaffected results and commitments. A withdrawn support offer does not erase a valid test result. A failed proposed arrangement does not automatically cancel an existing obligation. If no feasible accepted alternative remains, return that impasse to the responsible decision makers before the unsupported work is relied on.

#### LDR.2:4.8 - Negotiate with or through capable AI

An AI may calculate alternatives, examine resource conflicts, formulate a counteroffer or accept bounded terms when an effective mandate permits it. Establish whose contribution it controls or represents, the scope of that mandate, the counterpart's ability to identify and rely on the response, and the route for matters outside it. A convincing message from an agent is not evidence of a principal's authorization.

Where qualified agents can exchange complete proposals and binding responses under agreed rules, a human negotiation meeting may be unnecessary. The substantive tests remain: feasible contributions, legitimate terms, consistent version and actual performance. Human preferences or consent need a permitted way of being represented; simulation of a person's likely preference is not that person's consent.

Compare the new arrangement's setup, integration, computation, exceptions, oversight required by the work and consequences of error. Do not retain a human approval layer unless it supplies a necessary decision or guarantee. Do not remove a required approval merely because automated bargaining is cheap. C.11.DUA supports reconsidering both the arrangement and a disputed procedural burden through the proper authority.

### LDR.2:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### LDR.2:5.1 - A limited first interface

Suppose a customer asks for a complete interface by day 20. The engineer cannot obtain evidence for automatic control before day 28. After preserving the old service, support has sixteen hours weekly available for change; eight are already allocated to an internal improvement.

The participants recover the actual day-20 need: usable daily data without interrupting production. The customer's authorized representative accepts that automatic control can be considered later. The service owner protects current service, the improvement owner controls any displacement, the engineer supplies technical judgement and the provider commits its own support. Operators contribute the conditions for actual use.

They consider full control on day 20, postponing all value, and a read-only first configuration. The first lacks evidence; the second fails the customer's accepted need. The limited configuration needs a technical answer and a support offer. The authorized parties fund a probe asking whether it returns the required records without sending control commands or interrupting the old service. The permission covers the probe alone.

In the stipulated result, the qualified team obtains the required records and verifies those isolation conditions for the examined configuration. A provider offers six weeks of support for 15,000 currency units, requiring twelve local hours each week. The whole proposal now includes that price, scope, duration, local work and separate introduction decision. It promises no automatic-control date merely because day 28 is the earliest evidence date.

Eight unallocated support hours plus four displaced improvement hours supply the twelve required. Four hours remain for improvement. Across six weeks, the improvement loses twenty-four hours, which its owner explicitly accepts. The customer accepts the reduced first scope and price; the provider accepts its support terms; the service owner accepts the allocation while preserving service; the engineer confirms the limited technical basis; operators confirm their tasks and conditions.

These responses concern the same complete version. Preparation, consultation, the probe, implementation, monitoring and possible revision remain costs beyond the quoted price and local hours. The accepted proposal supports the subsequent decisions; it is not itself permission for introduction.

#### LDR.2:5.2 - The support disappears

Now suppose the provider withdraws before introduction. Replacing its work locally would need thirty-six hours weekly against sixteen available even if all improvement stopped. Another provider can start only on day 27, which the customer refuses.

No considered proposal now supplies a feasible accepted day-20 introduction. The participants return the changed conditions to product, organization and service decision makers. OPS.13 helps obtain an authorized response to the existing customer promise. The old promise is not silently cancelled, and the earlier technical finding remains useful within its scope.

Change a different condition: the customer accepts day 27 and the second provider's terms fit the available local capacity. The affected parties can accept that revised whole and seek the corresponding introduction decision. There is no need to repeat the unaffected technical probe unless the new configuration changes its applicability.

#### LDR.2:5.3 - Joint work without a common employer

Suppose three independent researchers agree to publish a comparison. One offers a dataset, one analysis and one review. The analyst's promised date precedes the dataset's permitted release. No participant can assign another's time.

They examine an earlier anonymized subset, a later analysis and independent preparatory work. The data owner determines which sharing permission actually exists; the analyst assesses whether the subset answers the research question. If it does, each participant accepts the revised scope and date. If it does not, the group retains useful independent preparation and defers the unsupported joint result. A new office would supply neither the missing permission nor the dataset.

### LDR.2:6 - Bias-Annotation

**Lenses:** Governance, Architecture, Ontology/Epistemology, Pragmatics and Didactics. **Scope:** Negotiated joint contributions, with the relevant decision and consent rules retained.

The method favours mutually workable proposals. That can hide unequal power or the interests of absent parties. Recover meaningful refusal, representation and affected contributions; use the actual governing rule when mutual agreement is not the applicable decision method. A beneficial aggregate does not make every party's loss acceptable.

### LDR.2:7 - Conformance Checklist

A conforming use explains:

- the receiving result, necessary parties and actual representation or authority;
- the conditions protected by their demands and serious alternatives to agreement;
- whole proposals with material benefits, burdens and dependencies;
- the domain basis and joint resource feasibility of the proposed commitments;
- the work and limit of any inquiry used to resolve uncertainty;
- responses to the same complete proposal, including refusal and outstanding conditions;
- the receiving decisions and the response to a consequential change.

An already sufficient routine agreement needs no separate negotiation dossier.

### LDR.2:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Invited mistake | Better action |
| --- | --- |
| Treat technical feasibility as the parties' agreement. | Obtain the responses needed for the whole proposal. |
| Add local promises without checking their common resource. | Check joint demand, qualifications and time windows. |
| Treat majority support as every contributor's consent. | Apply the actual decision rule and retain the distinction. |
| Accept a provider's offer without its local operating burden. | Rebuild the whole proposal with the support offer and affected work. |
| Keep the old agreement after a contribution disappears. | Reconsider dependent terms and existing promises through their actual owners. |

### LDR.2:9 - Consequences

The parties obtain terms they can examine, accept and perform, or a specific reason no agreement is available. Useful partial findings survive a failed proposal. The cost is competent preparation, inquiry where justified and the coordination needed for real acceptance. Negotiation can reveal a legitimate impasse rather than remove it.

More capable or cheaper reasoning expands the alternatives worth examining, but the actual resources, rights and willingness still constrain the agreement. An automated exchange can reduce meeting work; its mandate and operating limits remain part of feasibility.

### LDR.2:10 - Rationale

Joint feasibility belongs to the combined arrangement. It cannot be inferred from the quality of separate offers. Obtaining responses to a complete version preserves the relation between what each party accepts and what the others will supply. Following changes through that relation permits selective reopening instead of restarting every discussion or silently preserving an invalid whole.

### LDR.2:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The practice question is how to obtain a workable agreement when contributions are mutually dependent. **Adapt** CBI's [Mutual Gains Approach, 2023 brief](https://www.cbi.org/assets/resource/media/cbi-mgabrief-2023.pdf): recover interests, mandates and alternatives; explore packages before commitment; use defensible criteria; plan for changing conditions. This is a method source. The brief's reported experience is not a controlled effectiveness result for these cases.

The selected synthesis adds explicit domain qualification, joint capacity and time-window checks, responses to one complete proposal, and return after a relied-on contribution changes. A serious alternative is competent direct negotiation under an adequate existing protocol. Retain it when it already supplies those results. This procedure earns its additional work where isolated promises hide a whole-arrangement conflict, as in the support case. The trade-off is added preparation and possibly a finding that the desired promise is unavailable.

CB.13's mandate and conditional team protocol are another sufficient supplier where voluntary shared decision making needs them; this method does not require a second protocol. FPF C.11 retains plural decision makers and D.4 supplies value-conflict reasoning when a single combined score would conceal disagreement.

Reopen the choice of method if participation, representation, professional competence or the inquiry budget cannot support it, or if a simpler arrangement supplies the same accepted result at lower total burden.

### LDR.2:12 - Relations

LDR.1 obtains capable facilitation, expertise and help. B.5.SD constructs missing subject answers. LDR.4 connects agreed proposals to effective decisions and first use.

[CB.13](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/COMMUNITY-MANAGEMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#cb13---establish-a-workable-mandate-for-shared-decisions) supplies representation and shared-decision mandates. [Corporate Governance (CGOV)](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/CORPORATE-GOVERNANCE-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md), through CGOV.3 and CGOV.11, resolves corporate power and the act making a decision; persuasion and informal agreement do not replace them.

[Systems Engineering (SYSE)](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/SYSTEMS-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md), through SYSE.6, SYSE.17 and SYSE.24, supplies architecture decisions, affected systems and comparison of whole ways to obtain an engineering result. OCE.7 connects product and organizational decisions. [Operations Management (OPS)](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Management%20DPF%20Suite/OPERATIONS-MANAGEMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md), OPS.13, handles commitments and authorized changes.

FPF B.5.QD.CF examines conflicting assumptions, D.4 handles ethical mediation and value premises, C.11 supplies decision reasoning, and C.11.DUA appraises the value and burden of additional inquiry. Use each supplier when its question arises; they are not mandatory preliminary stages.

### LDR.2:End



## LDR.4 - Prepare a Proposal for Decision and First Use

**Kind:** Method pattern. **Status:** Stable.

### LDR.4:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a promising proposal must become understandable, examinable and supported by real contributions before joint work can begin, or when an announced decision has not yet reached its first useful result. The proposal may concern a new practice, a one-off collaboration or an ordinary task that needs renewed preparation.

Begin with the receiving work and the result the proposal is meant to improve. If that direction or the basis for participation is unsettled, use LDR.5 and the necessary subject method rather than assume that preparation will justify the initiator's preferred proposal. Obtain the judgement people actually need, examine consequences and objections, revise the proposal, secure the required decisions and contributions, make the accepted start mutually visible, and follow the first handover into use.

This connected preparation is sometimes called “propitka” in Russian practitioner discussions: the proposal becomes sufficiently understood through repeated explanation, advice and revision. Here it is not a campaign to suppress objections. A real disadvantage, inadequate basis or unavailable resource can warrant a changed proposal, refusal or deferral.

A familiar, adequately prepared and authorized action can proceed directly. No compulsory campaign, launch ceremony or fixed waiting period is added.

### LDR.4:2 - Problem

A proposal's formal presentation may miss the advice through which participants actually judge it. A trusted colleague outside the project can expose a real burden or repeat a mistaken assumption. Ignoring that judgement can leave the decision unsupported; trying to bypass it through pressure can leave the proposal worse.

Even a well-understood proposal can stop before performance. Support may remain a promise to seek funding, the resource decision may never take effect, participants may rely on different versions, or the first receiver may still lack a usable product. An announcement does not join those missing contributions.

### LDR.4:3 - Forces

Thorough preparation can prevent a costly false start, but endless consultation can consume the opportunity. The method needs relevant judgement and attainable answers, not exhaustive discovery of every informal relationship.

The technical result, distribution of benefits and burdens, and effective authority interact. Discussing titles too early can distract from the work; postponing capability and authority too long can make an attractive proposal impossible.

Participants need enough mutual visibility to rely on one another's contributions. That does not require pretending that everyone is enthusiastic or that every uncertainty has disappeared.

A first assisted success can justify bounded continuation while still depending on substantial support. Claims of independent capability, recurrent provision and cultural continuation need further evidence and their own methods.

### LDR.4:4 - Solution

#### LDR.4:4.1 - Make the receiving work and proposed change examinable

Recover the result someone will actually receive and use. Describe how the proposal produces it: the work products, operations, producer and receiver, tools, access, help and conditions needed. Compare a representative task under the present arrangement and the proposed one.

A new form, meeting or role is useful only through a contribution. Ask what becomes possible or improves for the receiver. If an existing arrangement already supplies the same result at acceptable effort, retain it. If the proposal concerns continuing development, obtain its actual problem, worthwhile challenge and transition from the development methods; a proposal to adopt a familiar practice does not constitute that whole development process.

Locate the first useful result and the authority needed to attempt it. A permitted trial, assisted handover or narrow use may be appropriate. An urgent production episode is not automatically a suitable learning trial. The direct domain method establishes the task's acceptance and risk conditions.

Keep preparation's own resources visible: explanation, consultation, inquiry, demonstrations, revision and the decisions required before use. A future benefit does not supply those resources now. C.11.DUA helps compare further preparation with direct use, a smaller proposal, deferral or stopping.

#### LDR.4:4.2 - Obtain relevant trusted judgement

Ask the decision makers and affected participants whose advice they need, about which question and under what information conditions. A trusted former colleague, peer or specialist may sit outside the formal project. Their relevance comes from the judgement the participant relies on, not from an invented position in a stakeholder chart.

Arrange a permitted conversation, example or private consultation. Ask what consequence concerns the adviser, what experience or grounds support that concern and what could answer it. Give enough substance for a useful judgement. A slogan about the proposal's benefits cannot answer a question about duplicate work or a new failure mode.

Do not confuse the adviser with an authorized representative. Advice can change the proposal while the decision maker retains the decision and the contributor retains control of their own commitment. If the adviser is speaking on behalf of someone, establish that mandate separately.

Pursue advice that can change the present decision or an affected contribution. There is no requirement to map everyone's entire informal network, contact people covertly or circulate confidential information. If relevant consultation cannot be supported, retain the limitation and decide whether a narrower use, another source or delay is warranted. Cheap AI analysis can help formulate the question; it cannot manufacture the person's actual advice or consent.

#### LDR.4:4.3 - Trace consequences in representative work

Use a concrete task and its receiving action. Show the products and means before and after the proposal. Ask the participants where an extra operation appears, an input is missing, work is displaced, an obligation changes or an undesirable consequence becomes possible.

Different findings need different responses. A participant may have misunderstood the proposed scope; explain it. The proposal may duplicate an existing report; find the report's receiver and determine whether the duplication is needed. A claimed benefit may lack technical grounds; obtain the subject construction through B.5.SD. An actual loss may need compensation, a different option or refusal. Do not rename all these findings “resistance.”

Explain why the proposed arrangement works under its conditions and what remains uncertain. The people who will produce and receive the result are often the best source of a missing operational dependency. Include an external provider or later receiver when their work is material.

Discuss what must be done before negotiating titles as rewards. At the same time, recover competence, authority and resources as soon as they affect feasibility. A proposed first use may be impossible because the specialist belongs to another organization. That fact cannot wait until after everyone has approved the technical concept.

If the work competes with ongoing service, OCE.11 supplies the organization-specific reconciliation. If it changes a product and its producing organization together, OCE.7 coordinates those decisions. In other settings, obtain the corresponding resource and delivery contribution directly.

#### LDR.4:4.4 - Answer objections by improving the proposal or preserving a real limit

Let a participant state the claim or consequence they object to. Restate it in terms they recognize. Determine what would address it: an explanation, a qualified judgement, a bounded probe, an alternative arrangement, different terms or an effective decision under the applicable rule.

For a subject uncertainty, B.5.SD obtains the connected answer. For incompatible contributions, LDR.2 constructs the feasible accepted whole. For an unavailable resource or permission, obtain that contribution from its actual owner. For conflicting values, use the relevant ethical or institutional decision method. If the proposal cannot meet a required condition, narrow or withdraw it.

Revise the whole proposal after a consequential answer. Removing an unnecessary tracker can reduce burden; removing a tracker used by another team creates a new gap. A provider's support can make first use possible while adding cost and local effort. Check those effects before presenting the revised version as ready for commitment.

Compare further inquiry with its attainable decision value. A small test can be worthwhile if its result changes an expensive irreversible commitment. Repeating a familiar explanation to an informed person who legitimately declines supplies no new basis. A required unresolved condition remains a blocker; a non-blocking uncertainty remains a stated limit or observation for the authorized use.

The proposal need not end in consensus. Distinguish a participant's informed refusal, a decision made under a valid non-consensual rule, an unanswered concern and a matter resolved by evidence. Preserve each where it changes reliance. A proposal that is understood can still be a bad or unacceptable proposal.

#### LDR.4:4.5 - Obtain an effective decision and actual commitments

Identify the acts needed to begin: permission for preparation or a trial, allocation of time, access, funding, provider agreement, assignment and any domain decision. Determine who can perform each act and what makes it effective. Use a sufficient existing delegation; do not send every routine action to the highest authority.

In a corporation, CGOV.3 resolves the power and its limits and CGOV.11 supplies the decision procedure and usable result. Within an organizational assignment, OCE.6 supplies enabling relations. In a community, the relevant participant agreement or governing rule supplies the basis. Leadership preparation does not replace any of these acts.

Distinguish who organizes the proposed change from who operates and receives the resulting work. They can be one person, but their contributions differ. For example, a development coordinator may arrange the new review service while a reviewer remains responsible for receiving and judging each package.

Obtain commitments to the same complete proposal and retain outstanding conditions. “I support this if someone funds it” is not a funded contribution. A volunteer's required contribution remains unavailable after refusal until an alternative is actually obtained. An authorized manager's decision can allocate work within its power; that does not imply the worker agrees with every rationale or that another unit's resources are committed.

Make the decision and its limits recoverable in the ordinary form that gives it effect or communicates it. Name what can happen now, what awaits another act and what requires reconsideration. A plan for later support is not support available for the first use.

#### LDR.4:4.6 - Make the same start mutually visible

Bring the effective decision and accepted proposal to those who rely on one another's work. Confirm what each will actually contribute, when and with which help; who receives the result; what happens if a necessary input or ground is missing; and who can change the arrangement.

The practical need is that a participant can reasonably rely on the other necessary contributions. It is not merely that everyone has received the same announcement. Include absent shifts, external providers or other dependent participants through a usable exchange and their actual responses. A brief meeting may be efficient; a readable update with confirmations may be enough.

Keep a changed proposal distinguishable from the earlier one. If a late objection exposes a consequential gap, reopen the affected inquiry, agreement or decision. Communicate the revision before others rely on the old terms. Independent authorized preparation can continue while a dependent start waits.

Do not use a launch ritual to make disagreement invisible. The gathering can confirm an already prepared start and make its support apparent. It cannot supply missing technical grounds, resources or consent. Attendance, silence and a confident sponsor's announcement prove none of those conditions.

Where an effective rule permits starting despite a retained objection, state that decision, the conditions on it and the available response if the feared consequence appears. Mutual visibility is compatible with disclosed disagreement.

#### LDR.4:4.7 - Complete the first useful handover

Put the actual product, access, means and capable help in place. Perform the work through its domain method and follow the result to the receiver's action. Opening a form, completing a briefing or marking a checklist is insufficient if the receiver still cannot use the contribution.

A checklist can help attention when its questions are tied to the occasion and change what the performer does. C.41.OF supplies that fit. The performer still needs the method and competence to answer the questions. If “configuration checked” exposes an unsupported old test, obtain the needed applicability judgement or corrected contribution; a tick does not repair the ground.

Retain assistance that the first use actually needs. If a colleague supplies the decisive interpretation, report an assisted result and arrange the next use accordingly. The first result can be useful without establishing independent capability.

When the first attempt fails, distinguish the missing explanation, method, capability, tool, access, arrangement or authority. Return to its direct supplier. A revised announcement helps only if communication was the missing operation. A new calculation, support allocation or permission needs its own work and conditions.

Stop or defer the dependent result when those conditions cannot be supplied. Preserve useful preparation and findings, and give the receiver the actual limitation. In a successful continuation, establish that the receiver has used the applicable result for the intended purpose, rather than accepting the organizer's declaration that implementation occurred.

#### LDR.4:4.8 - Continue at the justified scope and reopen the right question

Return what the first episode established, what help remains and what would change the next use. LDR.1 supplies capable continuation and, where valuable, another participant's later performance without the initiator.

A first handover does not establish recurrent organizational provision; OCE.9 supplies the organization-capability connection. OCE.10 investigates difficulties in organizational participation and recurrent work. C.36.RP explains how to keep the practice's methods obtainable by later participants; C.36 qualifies a claim that the practice persists across participants or over time. Use these contributions when their questions arise. Improving a new practice's support belongs with the relevant implementation and domain methods. A causal effect requires C.28.

After success, a subject pursuing continuing development needs a method for noticing and selecting the next worthwhile challenge. Return to the development practice that owns that result. Do not require another round of proposal preparation merely because this round has ended, and do not treat smooth current performance as proof that further development is unnecessary.

Reopen the smallest affected question when support, scope, evidence, authority or receiving need changes. Earlier useful results remain at their scope. A lost twenty-minute slot invalidates the promised next occasion, not the already completed handover or the adviser’s reasoning.

#### LDR.4:4.9 - Reconsider the whole introduction when capable AI changes the cost

If qualified AI can prepare the subject reasoning, answer objections from permitted sources, obtain bounded commitments and execute the first contribution, compare that whole arrangement with the human-led introduction. It can remove a drafting handover, a meeting or a separate operational coordinator; it is not limited to accelerating the presentation.

Establish the agent's actual remit. It may have authority to reserve a resource below a limit or agree a service slot, while lacking authority to bind another organization or disclose a protected example. An inference about what a person would probably accept is no substitute for the required response. Effective delegated decisions are real contributions; they need not be repeated by a human merely because the performer is an AI.

Apply C.11.DUA to setup, qualification, access, communication, compute, exception handling, maintenance and receiver burden. If a human review supplies a required independent judgement, include it as a constrained resource. If it adds no needed guarantee under the effective arrangement, compare removing it rather than crediting an efficiency that cannot be realized.

Conditional strong-AI scenarios show how the method adapts when competence and cost change. A changed capability claim or new failure reopens the affected arrangement.

### LDR.4:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### LDR.4:5.1 - Trusted advice changes a calculation handover

Suppose an organizer proposes a checklist for handing a module calculation to its independent reviewer. The reviewer needs the identified configuration, calculation, applicability grounds and unresolved questions. The engineering lead controls the engineer's work allocation; the reviewer retains the technical judgement. The proposal initially adds a separate status tracker to the existing calculation package.

The lead wants advice from a former colleague outside the project. With permission to share a non-sensitive example, the organizer explains the proposed work and asks what consequence concerns the adviser. The adviser recalls an introduction that doubled status reporting.

The engineer and reviewer trace a representative package whose calculation is already available. Preparing and checking the handover takes twenty minutes; copying its status to the new tracker takes another five. Only twenty minutes of the engineer's time are available. Producing a missing calculation and performing the independent review have separate allocations. Understanding the purpose does not make the twenty-five-minute handover fit.

They ask who needs the second view. In this case no one uses it for a separate decision, and the responsible owner can retire it. The revised proposal keeps the existing package, places the questions at handover and makes status and unresolved issues visible to the reviewer. It needs twenty minutes. If the tracker had supplied another team's required input, removal would have needed a replacement or changed commitment.

The adviser has improved the proposal, not authorized it. The engineer and reviewer accept the revised contributions. The lead reserves the twenty-minute interval and authorizes one assisted handover. The organizer obtains source access and a capable colleague's help; that colleague's preparation and assistance have their own allocation.

In a shared brief, participants confirm the same configuration, scope, contributions and response to a missing ground. The engineer can see that the reviewer will examine the return; the reviewer can see that the engineer has the interval and help. An absent support colleague receives the terms and confirms the help before it is relied on.

#### LDR.4:5.2 - First use exposes work that agreement did not perform

On the first attempt, the checklist draws attention to a test from C6 cited in the C7 calculation package. The engineer can state the mismatch but needs help judging it. A qualified colleague identifies a changed parameter whose effect the old test did not cover. Under the stipulated review rule, applicability to C7 is unsupported.

The engineer returns the gap to the specialist; the reviewer receives the unresolved claim and withholds the dependent conclusion. The twenty-minute handover did not promise a new calculation within that interval. The first attempt has exposed the next necessary work but has not completed the intended reviewed contribution.

The specialist estimates the correction and its checking. The lead obtains a feasible allocation with the people whose work would move, and the reviewer accepts a later receiving window. If those conditions cannot be arranged, the dependent release waits. In the successful continuation stipulated here, the specialist supplies the C7 calculation and grounds. The engineer completes the assisted handover; the reviewer examines the result and makes the review decision.

This is the first completed contribution for this use. The case establishes no measured effect of the checklist or independent mastery by the engineer. It illustrates why preparation, authority, performance and evidence are separate results that must connect.

#### LDR.4:5.3 - Changed conditions select different repairs

Now remove the twenty-minute interval before the next handover. Obtain a feasible allocation or defer that start. Repeating the trusted-adviser conversation supplies no missing time.

Instead suppose the required specialist belongs to another unit. The lead's authority over the engineer cannot reserve the specialist. Obtain the contribution from its actual owner and keep the date conditional until it is supplied.

Or suppose another receiver reveals a required use of the supposedly redundant tracker. Recover that input, compare ways to supply it and obtain acceptance of the changed arrangement. The earlier no-use premise no longer holds.

If access alone fails, restore access before the dependent check. If an autonomous qualified agent can now produce and route the applicable package under its effective mandate, compare that arrangement, including the independent review still required by this case. Do not retain the engineer's copying work solely to preserve the old process.

#### LDR.4:5.4 - A routine proposal needs no preparation campaign

Suppose a volunteer reading group already agrees on the next session's subject, a capable participant has offered to explain it, the permitted materials are available and the host has confirmed the room. A short shared message identifies the same time and contributions.

The group can hold the session. It need not discover informal advisers, create a checklist or convene a launch event. If a later objection reveals that the selected material excludes an intended participant, examine that actual consequence and revise the affected arrangement. The method's longer branches become useful only then.

### LDR.4:6 - Bias-Annotation

**Lenses:** Governance, Architecture, Ontology/Epistemology, Pragmatics and Didactics. **Scope:** Preparation of a proposal through its first useful performance under real participation and authority conditions.

An initiator can assume that understanding inevitably produces acceptance, or that refusal proves irrational resistance. This method preserves real disadvantages and legitimate disagreement. It can also overburden participants if thoroughness becomes exhaustive consultation; the receiving decision and attainable value bound the work.

### LDR.4:7 - Conformance Checklist

A conforming use makes the following recoverable where they affect the proposal:

- the receiving work, first useful result and adequate existing alternative;
- the relevant trusted judgement and permission for its information use;
- representative consequences, real burdens and the response to substantive objections;
- the revised whole proposal, actual decisions, accepted contributions and outstanding conditions;
- the mutually visible start on the same effective terms;
- the performed first contribution and the receiver's use, including retained assistance;
- the next warranted continuation and the question reopened by a changed condition.

A routine prepared action does not acquire these as seven additional reports or meetings.

### LDR.4:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Invited mistake | Better action |
| --- | --- |
| Treat trusted advisers as obstacles to be bypassed. | Obtain the relevant judgement through permitted consultation and use it to examine the proposal. |
| Explain benefits until every objection sounds like assent. | Distinguish misunderstanding, missing evidence, real loss, incompatible terms and legitimate refusal. |
| Postpone resource and authority questions until after approval. | Recover them as soon as they change feasibility. |
| Use a launch meeting to manufacture common consent. | Make actual decisions and contributions visible; reopen consequential late concerns. |
| Count a completed form as first useful performance. | Follow the product into the receiver's action and supply the missing substantive work. |
| Repeat proposal preparation after every successful episode. | Continue ordinary work or return to the distinct development question when a new challenge matters. |

### LDR.4:9 - Consequences

Preparation can reveal hidden advice, real costs and missing conditions before they defeat the first use. Participants can rely on an actual supported start, and failure of the first attempt has a substantive return rather than a demand for more enthusiasm.

The work takes time and can change or stop a favoured proposal. There is no fixed duration or guarantee that everyone will agree. The method preserves useful preparation after a blocked start while refusing to count it as performance.

### LDR.4:10 - Rationale

Trusted judgement, a warranted proposal, acceptance, authority, mutual visibility and useful performance answer different questions. Their connection is the method's contribution. Removing one can leave the next apparently complete while its necessary condition is missing.

The sequence is conditional rather than a universal meeting order. New evidence can return the proposal to its construction; a changed support offer can return it to agreement; a prepared routine action can omit most of the preparation. The receiving result determines what remains to be done.

### LDR.4:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The selected line combines developed proposal preparation with direct substantive, agreement and authority methods, then follows actual use. A serious alternative is competent ordinary coordination already containing these contributions. Use it directly; the pattern adds value only where the connection is missing.

These are practitioner source contributions, developed here in LDR.4:4.1–LDR.4:4.8 and the calculation case. **Reject** universal minimum durations, blanket assurances of safety, exhaustive influence-network discovery, automatic attribution of non-use to sabotage, and suppression of newly surfaced concerns at launch. ## LDR.5 - Develop Joint Direction and Participation

**Kind:** Method pattern.

**Status:** Stable.

### LDR.5:1 - Problem frame

Use this when people are connected by a concern, opportunity or changing situation but cannot yet undertake a sufficiently understood course together. There may be no agreed group, ready proposal or recognized person to lead. Alternatively, an established group may keep performing while its members no longer understand or support the direction. The difficulty is to develop joint agency without assuming that the initiator already knows everybody's purpose or possesses their authority.

Begin with a consequential experience or possibility and the people needed to examine it. Construct the problem and a worthwhile direction with them, obtain the subject reasoning it requires, and relate that direction to what they can choose and undertake. The first useful result can be a bounded joint inquiry, a supported course of action, separate continuations or an explicit reason why cooperation cannot yet begin.

A clear, justified direction with willing, capable participants needs no new direction-setting exercise. Use LDR.1 for missing help, LDR.2 for incompatible terms and LDR.4 for a proposal already needing preparation through first use. A binding urgent assignment can require action before a broad conversation is possible; use its actual authority and qualified operating method, preserving the specific questions that can affect the work.

### LDR.5:2 - Problem

An initiator can mistake agreement with a concern for commitment to their preferred solution. Participants can use the same words while anticipating different results, or accept a task publicly while withholding knowledge that would challenge the plan. A group can also wait for a leader to supply an answer that must be constructed through its members' different experience.

Merely explaining the proposed goal more clearly does not settle these difficulties. The purpose, participants' roles in forming it and their grounds for acting may themselves need to change. Conversely, reopening an already adequate direction can delay useful action and erode reliance on valid commitments.

### LDR.5:3 - Forces

A shared direction gives work coherence, while participants retain purposes and responsibilities beyond it. They need enough common understanding to act without pretending to have identical beliefs.

Initiative can make a previously invisible opportunity actionable. Its holder can also define the problem so narrowly that only the preferred answer survives. Opening the question to others needs a real opportunity to change its terms.

Knowledge, commitment and authority have different providers. People closest to the difficulty may have important knowledge but little formal power; those who can authorize a change may have little direct experience of its consequences.

A small action can help people discover what is worth doing together. Its costs and consequences still need acceptance, and the action must be useful or informative enough to justify those costs. Endless inquiry can be as unhelpful as premature commitment.

### LDR.5:4 - Solution

#### LDR.5:4.1 - Find the connection and the people who can examine it

Recover an actual occasion, a changing condition or a credible possibility. Ask who experiences its consequences and who could contribute to understanding or changing them. Describe the connection before declaring a team or collective purpose. Several people using the same tool can have unrelated needs; an absent recipient can be essential to the apparent team's work.

Invite the contribution that is needed now: an account of the difficulty, relevant knowledge, a proposal, permission to use a resource or willingness to explore. State what this exchange can decide and what remains with another person or body. Give people a feasible way to respond, including a different time or permitted narrower example when access or disclosure limits matter. PSDA.12 and PSDA.13 supply professional participation and facilitation when the stakes or protections require that expertise.

The initiator should make their own interest and influence visible. Explain why the matter seems worth pursuing, which decisions are already binding and which are open. When a manager can require attendance or control rewards, a public expression of support is ambiguous. Obtain a way to hear concerns without advertising protection that the manager cannot provide. The existing authority or professional process governs any protection that is needed.

For a simple exchange, a short conversation with the actual participants can be enough. If the missing contribution is representation or a shared decision rule, use CB.13. Inviting a few familiar supporters cannot establish a mandate over everyone affected.

#### LDR.5:4.2 - Build an account that can change the proposed question

Let participants describe what they tried, what happened, what mattered and what they think explains it. Connect their accounts around the same event or anticipated use. Distinguish observations, interpretations, desired consequences and proposed remedies. Ask where the accounts disagree and what difference that disagreement makes to action.

For example, several delayed releases can support a request for more developer discipline. A maintainer may show that each release contains accepted changes that no one has committed to integrate. A user may reveal that frequent releases matter less than a stable version for an existing installation. These facts can change the question from accelerating everybody's work to deciding which contribution the project should undertake and how it can obtain it.

Use B.5.SD when the relationship between claims still needs substantive construction. A technical claim needs the relevant domain method. OCE.2 and OCE.10 can supply investigation of actual organizational work and participation where those are the objects. Do not substitute an attitude diagnosis for an unavailable design or resource answer.

Return the account to participants in terms they can correct. Ask them to apply it to the next consequential choice, not merely affirm that it sounds right. A difference that still changes the choice remains open. A documented account can hold the question across time and participants; writing it does not make it shared or true.

#### LDR.5:4.3 - Construct a worthwhile direction with its actual grounds

Ask what improvement or possibility would matter to the people concerned, why joint action is useful and what an initial contribution could change. Include maintaining something valuable, a smaller inquiry or independent action among the serious alternatives. If no consequential connection requires cooperation, preserve that result rather than inventing a group project.

Work between purposes and possible ways. A promising means can reveal a useful purpose; a desired outcome can expose a missing method. Obtain the relevant subject construction. STR.5 develops worthwhile contributions and direction, STR.6 develops a way to obtain them, and STR.18 relates the strategic answers when their joint coherence remains unresolved. For a community offer, CB.1 develops the participant and sponsor contribution. Leadership helps the people concerned bring their experience, aims and judgement into this work; their endorsement cannot replace its substantive grounds.

Make the proposed direction concrete enough to explain a trade-off. Whose situation should improve, through what result, under which important limits, and why this continuation rather than the serious alternative? Distinguish an ambition from the present commitment. A research collaboration may share a question and an honest stopping rule before it can promise a favourable finding. A service with a settled acceptance condition needs an executable delivery contribution instead.

Compare the full burden of pursuing the direction, including consultation, learning, support and work displaced elsewhere. C.11.DUA helps determine whether another inquiry could improve the choice enough to be worthwhile. A promising future does not supply today's available time. Retain a disagreement over value as such; use D.4 or the applicable institutional decision method when it must be resolved for action.

#### LDR.5:4.4 - Relate the direction to people's purposes and initiative

Ask each necessary contributor what participation would mean for them: the result they value, the work they can undertake, the loss or uncertainty they face, and the conditions under which they would continue. Their reasons can differ. Develop a feasible relation between the joint result and their contribution rather than asking everyone to adopt the initiator's personal motivation.

Show why a person's contribution matters to the others and what discretion it includes. Let them propose how to carry it out within the shared conditions. Where the intended role conflicts with their aims or capabilities, use LDR.1's role conversation and the relevant provider. Where terms conflict, use LDR.2. A necessary participant's refusal may require a different way, smaller scope or stop. Another person's willingness cannot commit the refuser's resources.

Make initiative and reliance explicit in ordinary work. One participant can offer to convene the inquiry; others can accept that contribution while reserving the final decision. Another can lead the technical investigation because the required competence is recognized, with peers retaining the ability to challenge the reasoning. Ask whether the proposed arrangement lets participants bring relevant concerns and actually act. Leadership can pass between people by subject or occasion without creating a new office each time.

For formal assignments, retain the actual power and its limits. A manager can decide within a valid remit despite disagreement; explain that decision and the specific discretion and duties that remain. For voluntary action, obtain the actual willingness on which the next contribution depends. A person who attends, praises the aim or accepts someone as a useful adviser has not thereby accepted every obligation.

#### LDR.5:4.5 - Establish a shared basis for the first action

Have the necessary participants explain the direction through their own intended action: what they will contribute, what they expect from others, what they can decide and what should return for reconsideration. Use a plausible difficulty to expose differences. If a user request conflicts with a proposed stability aim, can contributors explain which request should be deferred and who can make that choice? If not, repair the missing distinction or agreement before relying on it.

Choose the first action at the scope the grounds support. It may obtain a missing observation, supply an initial service or test whether the proposed collaboration is workable. Obtain its time, access, capability and authority. Incompatible offers return to LDR.2; a proposed new arrangement still needing effective decisions and supported first use enters LDR.4. Reuse what has already been obtained instead of repeating it under each pattern's name.

Explain what the action can establish and who will use its result. A trial can answer whether contributors can integrate a small change; it cannot promise long-term maintenance by a wider population. Arrange a response to an obstacle and a timely occasion for the needed judgement. Use the work's ordinary channel or product. The participants need recoverable terms and reasons, not a new leadership reporting system.

#### LDR.5:4.6 - Learn from participation and revise the affected direction

Follow what people actually do and what the receiver obtains. Notice who takes initiative, whose concern gets a response, which commitments hold and which support was decisive. Ask participants about consequential differences between the proposed and experienced work. Inspect non-participation before choosing its explanation: a missing time window, a mistaken promise and rejection of the aim call for different responses.

Reconsider the part whose premise changed. Failed integration can require a better subject method. An accepted workload that no longer fits needs a changed agreement. A useful result for the wrong population can require a revised direction and different participants. If the first experience changes what people consider worth doing, return to that purpose instead of treating their earlier acceptance as permanent consent.

Continue with adequate arrangements and support. Distribute further leadership only where people can and wish to take the contribution and the work permits it. LDR.1 develops or obtains the needed capability; HCD supplies human learning, and CB supplies continuing community participation when that becomes the actual undertaking. End an exhausted or unsupported collaboration while settling its existing commitments through their applicable arrangements.

Return both the useful work and the justified account: what people could undertake together, which conditions made it possible, and what remains unsettled. Evidence of a bounded collaboration supports its warranted continuation. C.28 is needed for a causal-effect claim, and C.36 for a cultural-continuation claim.

### LDR.5:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### LDR.5:5.1 - A direction before a software project's next release plan

Suppose an open-source project's maintainer proposes a major rewrite after several late releases. Contributors agree that the delays are frustrating, but nobody has agreed to the rewrite. The maintainer controls acceptance into the maintained repository under its existing rules; contributors control their voluntary time, and users decide whether they can adopt a release.

A contributor offers to convene a short inquiry. A documentation author, an integration specialist and two users accept the exchange. One user cannot disclose an installation's data but can describe the required compatibility. The group needs that boundary condition, not the protected data. Their accounts reveal different concerns: the maintainer wants less support work, a contributor wants to try a new design, and users need a stable version. The specialist shows a repeated gap between accepting contributions and having someone available to integrate them.

The proposed explanation changes. The domain comparison finds that an isolated adapter can remove the immediate integration difficulty without a rewrite; the new design remains a possible later undertaking. For this stipulated case, the participants consider maintaining the current release with that adapter, undertaking the rewrite and retiring active support. They choose a bounded maintenance direction because it meets the current users' need at a contribution the volunteers can actually supply. The design enthusiast declines maintenance and continues independent exploration without committing the project's name or release promise.

The integration specialist offers to lead one adapter attempt. The maintainer accepts that technical initiative while retaining release acceptance. The author offers an installation explanation; the users offer permitted compatibility observations. Each can explain why an incompatible new feature will wait. LDR.2 resolves their date and support terms, and LDR.4 supplies any remaining first-use preparation. A capable ordinary exchange can already provide those results.

In the constructed first use, the adapter supports the intended installation. The group has a useful maintenance result and evidence about this collaboration. It has neither a consensus on the rewrite nor an obligation from the participant who declined. The working account now connects an actual user purpose, technical grounds, accepted initiative and feasible contributions. That is more than the original agreement that delays were frustrating.

#### LDR.5:5.2 - Change the consequence, not merely the project name

Now suppose the compatibility inquiry shows that the adapter cannot support the intended installation. The maintenance direction has lost a technical premise. The maintainer returns the finding and its limits; the group reconsiders retirement, a narrower supported installation or a differently resourced rewrite. The previously declined contribution remains declined. A stronger appeal to loyalty supplies no missing technical way.

Change a different condition instead: the adapter remains adequate, but the integration specialist loses the offered time. Keep the technical result and reconsider the contribution through LDR.2 and LDR.1. There is no reason to reopen everybody's purposes unless the changed burden alters the direction they can support.

#### LDR.5:5.3 - A manager's decision with limited room for participation

Suppose a service manager must reduce evening coverage under an already authorized budget decision. The decision about expenditure is outside the team's present remit; the allocation of remaining coverage and the treatment of unanswered requests are open. Pretending that the whole decision awaits consensus would mislead staff.

The manager states those boundaries and obtains accounts from the affected shifts and service recipients. Their concerns show that a proposed uniform reduction leaves one promised receiving window uncovered. The domain and operations work supplies feasible alternatives; the manager obtains the required decisions on the affected promise. Staff can influence the workable arrangement, raise unresolved concerns and understand their actual assignments without being asked to endorse the budget rationale. A staff member's personal development aim enters a role conversation where it changes the allocation.

If the purpose, allocation, authority and receiving conditions were already adequate, perform the ordinary brief and the work. This method would add no useful direction-setting result. Conversely, if the budget decision's premise is defeated by new evidence, return that evidence to the body able to reconsider it; neither the manager nor the team acquires a new power merely by agreeing.

### LDR.5:6 - Bias-Annotation

The method favours participants having a real part in forming what they will do. An initiator can simulate this while keeping every consequential choice closed. An articulate participant can also dominate the account. Use actual decision boundaries, the experience needed for the question and feasible ways for less visible contributors to be heard. Protected work and legitimate urgent authority can limit participation; disclose the resulting limit instead of claiming a fully shared decision.

### LDR.5:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The connection among participants is established from the situation and its consequences; the roster and common purpose are not merely presumed.
- Observations, interpretations, desired results and remedies can be distinguished and corrected.
- The direction has a substantive basis and a meaningful alternative; agreement does not supply its professional warrant.
- Participants can relate the direction to their own purposes, contributions and limits, with formal duties distinguished from voluntary commitments.
- The first action has the contributions and authority it needs, and participants can explain a consequential choice under its terms.
- Later responses revise the affected purpose, arrangement or method while preserving valid results and commitments.

### LDR.5:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Invited mistake | Better action |
| --- | --- |
| Treat a shared complaint as agreement to the founder's solution. | Reconstruct the experience and compare the direction with the people whose action it requires. |
| Announce consultation after every consequential decision has been made. | State the actual open questions and the binding decisions; let relevant input change the former. |
| Explain non-participation through a personality or motivation label. | Inspect the actual contribution, participant's account and conditions before choosing a response. |
| Make an accepted direction permanent consent to later sacrifices. | Return a consequential change in burden or purpose to the affected participants and decision holders. |
| Require consensus before every valid ordinary action. | Retain adequate agreement and authority, and use the sufficient working conversation. |

### LDR.5:9 - Consequences

Participants can develop a direction and take initiative without first receiving a finished plan from a designated leader. The group can discover that its initial proposal addressed the wrong problem or that only a narrower collaboration is warranted. Obtaining and responding to people's experience costs time and can expose conflicts that the initiator cannot resolve. A sound result may be separate action or refusal.

### LDR.5:10 - Rationale

Joint direction is produced through the relation between an interpreted situation, worthwhile possibilities and people's actual ability and willingness to act. Changing any one can change the others. Constructing these relations before demanding commitment explains why the method can begin without a ready proposal, yet still needs the separate subject, agreement and authority results before action. A later experience returns to the premise it changes, rather than restarting the whole relationship or demanding renewed enthusiasm.

### LDR.5:11 - SoTA-Echoing

**Adapt** [Ganz's 2010 treatment of leadership in social movements](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-c5ad-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content) as a developed historical comparison: relationships, purposes, strategic reasoning and actual commitments must connect, especially where participation is voluntary. The present method retains those questions while supplying its own bounded construction. It does not transfer movement-specific narrative forms or presume that a founder represents an affected population.

**Use narrowly** [DeRue and Ashford's 2010 theoretical proposal](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.35.4.zok627) that leadership identity is constructed through claims and grants in interaction. The claim-and-grant distinction directs attention to the other participants' responses; it does not establish that a title or a successful appeal obtains their recognition.

R10's leadership discussion supplies the person–role–work tension and mutual support for joint responsibility. The CCL comparison in LDR.Reference:1.3 supplies a collective-outcome view. These perspectives answer different questions. The construction also retains formal authority, legitimate refusal and independent professional judgement, which the actual setting may require together.

The serious alternatives are a competent direct conversation, an already sufficient strategic process with actual participation, CB.1 for a community contribution, and LDR.2 or LDR.4 for a sufficiently formed proposal. Retain them where they supply the needed result. This method earns its additional work when participants must still construct what they can undertake together and how initiative will be exercised. Its use depends on the stated participation, knowledge and authority conditions. Reopen the method when a simpler available approach supplies that result, an important group or consequence was omitted, or experience defeats its explanation.

### LDR.5:12 - Relations

LDR.1 supplies capable help and the role conversation. LDR.2 constructs compatible accepted contributions; LDR.4 carries an examinable proposal through effective decision and first use. Enter either directly when the prior result is already available.

[Strategy (STR)](STRATEGY-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md), STR.5/.6/.18, supplies the worthwhile contribution, obtaining way and coherent strategic judgement. [Community Management (CB)](COMMUNITY-MANAGEMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md), CB.1 and CB.13, supplies a useful community contribution and a workable shared-decision mandate. These results can participate in leadership work without becoming substitutes for actual participation.

FPF B.5.SD supplies subject reasoning through discussion; D.4 supplies value-conflict reasoning; C.11.DUA appraises further inquiry. OCE.2/.10 supplies investigation where the subject is organizational work or participation. PSDA.12/.13 supplies professional participation and facilitation. Their wider methods retain their own conditions and results.

# Connected use: a federation begins a joint evidence service

## LDR.Use:1 - A worthwhile proposal with incompatible contributions

The following is a constructed use of LDR.1, LDR.2, LDR.4 and the supplied answer construction. Its joint purpose and initial participation are already established; LDR.5 addresses the different starting condition in which they still need construction. It does not describe an observed organization or claim that these numbers apply elsewhere.

Suppose three independent engineering communities want a monthly service in which practitioners bring a difficult evidence package, obtain a qualified diagnosis and leave with an actionable correction. The wider purpose is to help members develop their practice. The present leadership question is narrower: can the communities begin and continue a supported first service?

A competent domain team has already designed the proposed diagnostic method and its acceptance conditions. Each case needs two hours of substantive integration and half an hour of a separate qualified acceptance decision. The case owner also needs to supply permitted material, attend the receiving conversation and perform any corrective engineering work. Those activities are part of the service arrangement; the two-and-a-half-hour figure does not include them.

Four cases are proposed for the first month. The available joint specialist capacity is eight hours, of which two must remain for previously accepted work. Six hours remain for the new service. In this case, the pool's members are qualified for both integration and acceptance, and compatible windows permit a different specialist to accept each case from the one who integrated it. The available hours can therefore be allocated between those activities without losing the required independence. Four cases would require ten hours of the constrained specialist work, so the proposal is infeasible before considering the clients' correction work. Enthusiasm does not change that result.

The organizer uses LDR.1 to identify the missing contributions: a domain integration provider, qualified acceptance, client preparation, participant communication and a decision about scarce time. These are functions in the work, not five new jobs. Existing community coordinators can communicate the agreed terms; no common office is needed for that contribution.

## LDR.Use:2 - Advice changes the promise

Using LDR.4, the organizer asks community representatives whose judgement they need before accepting the offer. One wants advice from a practitioner who helped with an earlier clinic. A permitted anonymized example lets that adviser expose a misleading promise: the service can diagnose the missing ground but cannot promise to complete an unknown amount of corrective engineering within the session.

The domain team and intended clients trace one representative case. They distinguish the service's diagnosis and bounded acceptance decision from the client's later correction work. Through B.5.SD they construct a clearer account: the receiver will get the warranted finding, its grounds, a feasible next question or correction, and the limitation on further reliance. A case with no attainable correction may properly end in deferral.

The revised promise changes both expectations and resources. The client must accept responsibility for obtaining corrective work, or the provider must quote and supply it separately. The communities do not solve this by improving the advertising language alone; they revise the offered work and the terms clients can accept.

## LDR.Use:3 - Agree a feasible first month

Using LDR.2, the participants compare several whole arrangements. Two cases need five of the six available specialist hours, leaving one hour for a stated exception reserve. Four cases with unchanged scope need ten hours and do not fit. Another qualified provider can supply the missing integration, but its fee exceeds the first month's available budget. Postponing every case loses the value of beginning, while splitting the same case among several partial reviewers adds handovers without removing its needed integration.

The communities choose two cases for the first month. The acceptance provider reserves one hour; the integration provider reserves four. The remaining hour is not promised twice. Clients supply permitted materials before the allocated window and reserve their receiving time. If an exception consumes more than the reserve, the affected correction or next case is reconsidered; the reserve is not a promise to finish arbitrary extra work.

Each representative can commit only their community's contribution under its actual rules. Individual clients accept their own terms. A host reserves the permitted communication service and maintains the agreed access; it does not thereby acquire authority over all communities. The same complete proposal, including the two-case limit and client correction boundary, receives the needed responses.

The organizer's preparation, consultation and scheduling time is separately supplied by the communities. It is not hidden inside the six specialist hours. If that contribution is unavailable, an already capable coordinator can be retained, a smaller preparation arranged, or the start deferred.

## LDR.Use:4 - Begin and complete a useful contribution

The organizer uses LDR.4 to obtain the effective permissions and make the start mutually visible. The providers and clients confirm the same scope, dates, input conditions and route for an unsupported claim. A shared message with actual confirmations suffices; the federation does not create a launch ceremony.

The first case reveals that a test concerns an earlier configuration. The integrator explains the changed condition and the unsupported conclusion. The acceptance provider judges the diagnostic result within its remit. The client now knows which release claim cannot be made and which engineering question must be answered. That is the promised bounded service result, not a corrected engineering package or evidence that the client has acquired independent mastery.

If the client expected a completed correction, the receiving result would fail the promise. The provider would need a revised service, resources and agreement rather than a new definition of success after the event.

The participants use LDR.1 to arrange continuation. The second case has the same qualified support; another coordinator can conduct the preparatory exchange without the initiator. Where human skill development is separately wanted, HCD supplies its design and assessment. Successful diagnosis alone does not establish that development.

## LDR.Use:5 - Qualified autonomous AI changes the feasible whole

Now change the performer condition. Suppose, for this scenario, a qualified autonomous AI service can perform the substantive integration under the same required acceptance conditions. Its qualification, permitted data handling, operating controls and availability are established for these cases. It can reserve routine service slots and return the diagnosis within an effective delegated mandate. It cannot make a decision reserved to the independent acceptance provider.

The setup requires four hours of one-time qualified configuration and onboarding before the month begins. That work has a separately available window and an accepted cost. Recurring human exception support requires one hour per month plus fifteen minutes per case. The independent acceptance still needs half an hour per case. Four cases therefore require one plus one plus two, or four hours of the constrained human capacity. They fit the six hours available; the agent's compute and service fee also require an accepted budget. For the same four-case result, the human arrangement needs ten hours of integration and acceptance plus the same one-hour exception reserve: eleven hours. The new arrangement needs four recurring hours, with its one-time setup stated separately. Client preparation, correction work and ordinary coordination remain necessary in both arrangements and are supplied outside this specialist allocation.

This can justify four cases without an additional office. The AI performs the integration and permitted scheduling; people do not repeat those operations merely to preserve former roles. The communities retain their real acceptance of the service terms, input permissions, client work and the independent decision the scenario still requires.

Change the authority and assurance condition again. Suppose a competent independent automated acceptance service is available, is permitted by the applicable rule, and supplies the required independent judgement without the earlier human half-hour per case. That contribution can also move to AI. The recurring human demand becomes the one-hour support provision plus the four fifteen-minute exception allocations: two hours. Independence is a property of the actual judging arrangement and grounds, not merely a second agent label. The responsible parties can now reconsider the larger service scope or retain the spare capacity; no automatic expansion follows.

In the opposite variation, the agent is qualified only to draft a summary and every case still requires the original two hours of expert integration. The promised reduction is unsupported. Four cases would still exceed the six hours, with the agent's cost added. Retain the two-case arrangement or obtain another feasible source of competence.

These are conditional comparisons, not claims about a named AI product. C.11.DUA keeps setup, recurring work, required guarantees, delay, fees and displaced work in view. Stronger competence changes architecture only where the real arrangement supports relying on it.

## LDR.Use:6 - Continuation after success is another substantive question

Suppose the first month's cases succeed under their stated conditions. The communities have evidence of a bounded service, not proof of a culture of continuing development. They also notice a weak signal: practitioners can correct known evidence gaps but rarely question whether their chosen problem is still worthwhile.

A participant raises that concern through LDR.1; B.5.SD can help formulate and examine it with the relevant development practitioners. The next development question is whether and how to build problem-discovery and problem-formulation capability, not simply how to schedule more clinics. The development method supplies the problem, attainable challenge and transition; strategy and portfolio methods reconcile its value and resources with other work.

Leadership then helps obtain the contributions for whichever next move is warranted. The useful first service continues if worth its cost. It can also be narrowed or discontinued. Keeping an offer alive and continuing the subject's development are related decisions with different results.
