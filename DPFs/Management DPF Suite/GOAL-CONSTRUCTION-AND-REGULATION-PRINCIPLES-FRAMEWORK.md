# Goals and OKR Readme

## Practical entries

These entries help you find a useful first operation. Bring your actual question; the Table of Contents also provides direct routes for situations not illustrated here. You can ask an assisting agent: “Explain this and give me your comments in ordinary language, without framework jargon.”

### ACHIEVEMENT — The numbers improved, but the wanted result is missing

- **Situation:** A team completed its planned activities and reports green key results, while customers still lack the promised outcome.
- **Question:** What would establish that the goal was achieved?
- **First useful result or blocker:** A corrected achievement test that distinguishes the wanted result from activity and keeps unfinished cases visible; or the exact unavailable observation needed to decide.
- **Start with:** GOALS.2, using the actual goal and cases.
- **Stop or return:** Use sufficient existing evidence directly. Return to GOALS.1 if participants disagree about the result itself.

### REVISION — A goal no longer fits the work

- **Situation:** The need, available means or expected outcome has changed during pursuit.
- **Question:** Should we continue, change the method, renegotiate, pause or end the goal?
- **First useful result or blocker:** A decision identifying the affected premise, the continuation that remains useful, and any commitment requiring its owner's decision.
- **Start with:** GOALS.4.
- **Stop or return:** Preserve unaffected work. Return a resource conflict to GOALS.3 and a disputed purpose to the participants who can settle it.

### AGENT — Give an AI a result it can pursue and finish

- **Situation:** An agent can carry out substantial work across tools or sessions, and the result needs more than a plausible answer.
- **Question:** What must the agent obtain, what may it do, and how will completion or an impasse be recognized?
- **First useful result or blocker:** A usable assignment with available inputs, action limits, completion evidence and a continuation arrangement suited to the actual runtime.
- **Start with:** GOALS.7.
- **Stop or return:** Use an ordinary bounded instruction when it suffices. Obtain a missing decision or access condition from its holder before the dependent action.

### Practical-Use Cards

The following entry retains a connection that is easily lost when each team writes its own goals separately.

#### JOINT-OKR — Make the quarter's goals possible together

- **Situation:** Teams have attractive OKRs, but they depend on the same specialists and continuing service.
- **Question:** What can we jointly undertake and rely on?
- **First useful result or blocker:** A coherent shared undertaking, including displaced work and the unresolved dependency that prevents a promise.
- **Mantra:** Connect the wanted result to evidence, reliance and shared means; use what happens to revise the affected connection.
- **Start with:** GOALS.5, then GOALS.2 and GOALS.3 for the actual construction; [Reference:2](#goalsreference2---how-a-system-of-goals-holds-together) explains the connections.
- **Stop or return:** An unresolved priority belongs to the decision owner; an unavailable specialist window remains an unmet condition.

## How the methods work together

A goal system connects what matters to action that people and agents can actually perform. A completed task can supply a valuable result, new knowledge or a new capability; which of these it establishes depends on what was done and observed. The framework helps construct these distinct aims and combine them without losing either immediate work or development. Read the Architectural Reference when the question spans several methods, when a condition changes their combination, or when you need the grounds for adapting the practice.

# Goal Construction and Regulation Principles Framework Architectural Reference

## GOALS.Reference:1 - The field and the work it serves

This framework develops the practice of constructing and regulating goals in professional, organizational and work-related personal development. The governed object is a goal or a connected arrangement of goals: desired conditions, their grounds, achievement criteria, relations to other aims, and the reliance and means through which they influence work. A goal description carries those meanings. The people, agents and organizations acting on it perform the work that can change the world.

The practical difficulty begins before choosing a target number. A person can want a useful outcome but lack an executable way to obtain it. Several reasonable goals can require incompatible uses of the same capacity. An indicator can improve while the recipient's result worsens. A valuable research attempt can end without the hoped-for discovery. A successful automated service can leave an intended human learning result unproduced. Those differences call for different decisions about purpose, means, evidence and continuation.

The useful whole is an arrangement in which participants can explain what they are trying to obtain, distinguish what is expected from what is promised, act within available conditions, detect changes in time, and revise the affected goal or means without disguising what happened. Its adequacy is visible in consequential work: for example, an accepted priority change leads to a feasible reallocation and truthful customer response, while unchanged commitments retain their force.

The intended reader knows the subject work or can obtain competent subject help. The framework supplies goal methods and their connections. Technical correctness, lawful authority, clinical judgements, professional learning design and other subject-specific conclusions remain with the relevant competent practice. An ordinary clear task with available means and an adequate completion test can proceed directly. Full OKR machinery becomes useful when a recurring organizational problem needs the connected practice described in GOALS.5.

Work-related personal aims and mixed human–AI work are included. Clinical treatment, a comprehensive theory of personality and all private-life goals need their own professional grounds.

## GOALS.Reference:2 - How a system of goals holds together

**Connect the wanted result to evidence, reliance and shared means; use what happens to revise the affected connection.** This reminder names five questions whose answers constrain one another.

First, recover the result and why it is worth pursuing. A desired change, preservation of an adequate condition, inquiry into an unknown and acquisition of a capability are different possible aims. GOALS.1 constructs this interpretation and identifies who may settle or change it. A hypothesis about the means remains revisable even when the purpose is stable.

Second, construct evidence of achievement in GOALS.2. Asking how a result would be recognized often changes the proposed goal. “Resolve the refund problem” becomes an identified population of customers receiving their due refunds, together with the agreed treatment of genuine exceptions and continuing care of unfinished cases. An information page can help that work without being its completion criterion. A new observation is needed only if present evidence cannot support the decision it serves.

Third, determine reliance and make the demands fit together. An ambition invites a worthwhile search beyond the current expectation; a forecast describes what is expected under stated conditions; a commitment establishes what another participant may rely on under its actual agreement. GOALS.3 combines these meanings with current service, material dependencies and shared capacity. A goal that needs unavailable complements returns for construction, changed scope or an explicit unresolved condition. Lowering a forecast does not perform any of those changes.

Fourth, people and agents carry out subject work. GOALS.6 helps a person connect an adopted aim to suitable action, competing aims and assistance. GOALS.7 does the corresponding assignment and continuation work for an AI system under its actual capabilities and authority. The subject methods produce the result: a good goal does not manufacture a repair technique, a research answer or a learning opportunity. [C.39 in FPF](../../FPF-Spec.md) develops a missing way of working. [HCD](<../Human Capability Engineering DPF Suite/HUMAN-CAPABILITY-DEVELOPMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md>) supplies development of a human contribution when that is needed.

Fifth, GOALS.4 connects observation to diagnosis and a warranted continuation. It uses [C.43](../../FPF-Spec.md), which already supplies the full connection of observation, choice and action. The domain addition here concerns which goal-related meaning changes: a forecast, a means hypothesis, an achievement test, a resource allocation, an aspiration or a commitment. An adverse observation can leave the goal sound; a goal can become obsolete while every indicator is green.

These are relations to use as needed. A service with an established goal and valid evidence can enter at a capacity conflict. A new inquiry may start with the decision it could improve. A participant may first expose an unwanted consequence that requires revising the aim. The organization does not need to restart goal construction whenever a current report changes.

### GOALS.Reference:2.1 - The whole changes a constituent action

Consider a company promising both a usable AI-assisted advisory service and staff capability to frame and question its recommendations. During a client case, an employee's framing of the question is part of producing the advice and part of practising the promised contribution. The two results share that action but require different evidence. Client acceptance can establish usefulness of the advice; observation of the employee's framing under the agreed assistance can support the learning claim.

If the agent now supplies the framing entirely, the service may improve while the practice opportunity disappears. Preserve the service gain and reconsider the learning arrangement: have the employee frame a suitable next case before seeing the agent's version, compare the reasoning and receive qualified feedback; allocate the required time. If that additional result is no longer wanted, its owner can revise the development aim. If it remains promised but no opportunity or support is available, the complete arrangement remains infeasible. Counting more delivered recommendations would leave that gap unchanged.

Conversely, a difficult first attempt can reveal that the human framing criterion was too broad for the available preparation. HCD can narrow the next learning contribution while GOALS.3 revisits which later service reliance remains supported. This return changes the whole arrangement through what happens in one constituent action. It also shows why a shared work episode need not make its service and learning results interchangeable.

### GOALS.Reference:2.2 - Joint conditions and useful partial results

The goal set has conditions that no isolated goal can establish. If three teams need one specialist for 12, 10 and 10 hours in the same week and only 24 hours are usable, individual feasibility claims do not compose. A delivery sequence can remove overlap only if the receiving dates permit it. A purported shared activity counts once only when the same performance actually supplies all claimed contributions. GOALS.3 shows the resulting choice.

A partial result can still be useful: a completed investigation can allow a decision while implementation remains unfunded; several completed refunds can benefit their recipients while other cases remain open. State what the evidence establishes and what the receiving work still needs. The criterion for stopping the current task is the result it was assigned, including its actual conditions. A broader development programme may continue through newly attainable questions after that task ends.

## GOALS.Reference:3 - Three applications of the common account

The organizational OKR profile in GOALS.5 uses objectives to express focused changes and key results to express evidence of their achievement. It combines GOALS.1–4 with GOALS.8's conversations and GOALS.9's practice development. It also uses the full operating, strategy, leadership and HR methods where their questions arise. Its recurring focus and shared interpretation can help when independent planning hides priorities and dependencies. The organization still carries current service and obligations beyond its displayed improvement agenda.

The human application in GOALS.6 concerns an actual participant's purposes, adoption of a goal, competing demands and action under the available conditions. A voluntary aspiration, an effective work assignment and a learning opportunity have different participation grounds. The method connects them where appropriate without assuming that a person's private purpose must equal a corporate objective. Planning, feedback and help are chosen for the particular difficulty.

The AI application in GOALS.7 treats the goal as content used by a configured system with particular tools, state, capabilities and permissions. It permits substantial autonomous performance where those conditions support it. Continuing computation, an apparent confident response and an authorized external effect remain different events. Resumption, completion and escalation need operational grounds in the environment. Human findings about motivation or well-being do not supply those grounds.

Mixed work uses both accounts. A capable agent can provide an analysis or perform an authorized operation directly. If personal mastery is an independently wanted result, preserve the human action and evidence needed for it. If it is unnecessary, compare direct provision against developing that skill. The participants' actual values and guarantees decide the arrangement; neither compulsory human duplication nor compulsory substitution is part of the general method.

## GOALS.Reference:5 - A connected organizational case

### GOALS.Reference:5.1 - Choose the results and their completion basis

A refund service has 100 eligible cases in its opening cohort, all due by the end of a four-week cycle. Customers want the settlement owed and a usable account of the outcome. The service also wants faster handling of new cases and two employees able to diagnose exceptions. The following amounts, terms and outcomes are stipulated for this example. The service's competent providers supply the case criteria.

The initial KRs are “publish a FAQ”, “launch a bot” and “reduce incoming contacts”. All three could pass with every refund unpaid. The team retains useful information and automation as means and constructs two objectives with different reliance:

| Objective and mode | Key results and their joint meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **O1, committed:** By the end of week 4, settle the 100 opening-cohort refund obligations and give every affected customer a usable confirmation of the outcome. | **KR1.1:** All 100 cases have a completed correct payment or a valid final settlement under their applicable terms, with no unresolved amount, recipient or authorization error. **KR1.2:** Every customer has received an accurate confirmation through the agreed channel, identifying the actual settlement and any action still required of them. Both KRs are mandatory. A confirmation of a future answer does not satisfy KR1.1. |
| **O2, aspirational:** Make new eligible refund cases dependable and less costly to handle during the selected service window. | **KR2.1:** At least 95% of all eligible requests received in week 3 reach a correct payment or valid final settlement within five working days of receipt; every case also meets its individual contractual deadline. **KR2.2:** Mean manual handling effort for that same complete cohort is at most 12 minutes per request, including exceptions and its allocated checking and maintenance work. Both KRs are required for the stated improvement. Correct amounts, recipients, authorization and agreed customer access remain required conditions. |

Eligibility follows the existing service terms and is checked at receipt. Difficult eligible cases remain in their cohort. In this case the incoming requests' contractual deadline is ten working days, so the five-day target is an improvement. Requests arriving at the end of week 3 can still receive their full five-day observation by the end of week 4. Any cases open then remain tracked to their actual contractual deadline. O1's fixed opening cohort and O2's new cohort are separate; improving the latter leaves the earlier obligations intact.

The preceding comparable service window had 85% completed within five working days and 20 minutes of manual effort per request. The initial O2 forecast falls short of the desired improvement: 90% within five days and 15 minutes per request, against the desired 95% and 12 minutes. The team chooses the ambitious improvement while protecting the current contractual service. Other teams plan on confirmed staffing and contributions, not on the hoped-for effort saving. O1 has funded payments, the required authorizations and a feasible processing arrangement; its initial forecast is all 100 completed by the agreed date. A threatened payment or dependency will reopen that forecast and the response.

Payment confirmations and the existing case register establish financial and communication outcomes. Existing case timestamps and handling-time data cover O2's whole cohort. The effort calculation includes the service's manual exception work; omitting difficult cases would change the claim. These adequate sources are reused without a new reporting system. The all-pass test is now concrete: correct final settlements plus usable confirmations establish O1; timeliness plus lower total handling effort under the retained conditions establish O2. The reverse test also matters: a correct manual settlement may satisfy the goal even if the bot is never launched. Faster work with wrong amounts fails O2's required conditions, and lower incoming contacts alone establishes neither objective.

The separate learning goal is for both employees, by the end of week 4, to frame unfamiliar routine and exception cases in the current refund service, identify missing grounds and route a decision beyond their authority. Their case procedure remains available; an AI-generated diagnosis is withheld only in the episodes used to establish their own diagnostic contribution. The HCD provider chooses representative variations, feedback and assessment for that bounded capability. This goal is not a KR proving customer settlement: a competent bot can improve service while the employees still need the intended practice.

### GOALS.Reference:5.2 - Obtain one feasible undertaking

The shared specialist is the limiting resource in week 2. Payment processing needs 12 hours, the full service repair 10, and the preferred learning provision 10: 32 hours against 24 usable hours after protected current service. Payment funds, ordinary service staff, customer communication and the learners' own time are separately provided. The 24 hours are specialist hours, not the service team's total working time.

The payment and service owners, specialist provider and learning provider compare a qualified substitute, a smaller useful repair and moving contributions whose receiving dates permit it. They agree this arrangement:

| Contribution | Week 2 specialist hours | Following provision and reliance |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Process the opening-cohort payments | 12 | Existing service staff follow confirmations and unresolved cases; the end-of-week-4 commitment remains. |
| Repair the service | 8 | The smaller repair is useful with the current manual fallback. The final 2 hours are booked at the start of week 3. O2 remains aspirational. |
| Develop the two employees' diagnosis | 4 | Six more hours are booked in week 3, with assessment before the learning deadline. No current payment depends on unestablished employee capability. |

Week 2 now uses 12 + 8 + 4 = 24 hours. The provider has confirmed ten available hours in week 3 after other commitments: 2 for repair, 6 for learning and 2 held for consequential exceptions. The service owner can revise improvement scope, the payment owner retains the customer obligation, and the learning provider and participants agree the practice arrangement. Their existing work schedule carries the commitments. If that later booking becomes unavailable, the parties reopen the affected contribution and its deadline.

### GOALS.Reference:5.3 - Revise the relation that changed

Before week 2 execution, the payment interface changes. The technical owner establishes that all ten repair hours are now needed before the payment batch can run. Repair has become an enabling prerequisite for the committed result. The old 12 + 8 + 4 arrangement no longer works even though its sum still fits.

The holders of the affected contributions agree 10 repair hours first, 12 payment hours next and 2 learning hours in week 2. The specialist confirms that this order fits the batch window. In week 3, the now-unneeded two repair hours join the previously booked six learning hours: 8 for learning and the same 2 for exceptions. Both weeks fit their actual provision. The learning provider uses the two-hour episode for initial case framing with feedback and the booked eight hours for varied cases and assessment; this sequence can still meet the learning deadline. The improvement forecast is reconsidered using the completed repair's actual scope. If the original four learning hours had an immovable week-2 obligation, the changed set would require 26 hours; two hours of qualified substitute provision or an effective change of that obligation would still be needed.

The decision is made when the interface change is discovered, through the people who can change the affected work. The routine weekly service conversation then uses the revised arrangement.

### GOALS.Reference:5.4 - Interpret progress and actual completion

At the end of week 2, payment processing is performed but the case evidence shows 82 completed payments, 8 valid final settlements and 10 payments still awaiting completion. Here the eight settlements are customer-chosen credits that the applicable terms allow as final settlement of the obligation; the case evidence shows the customers' agreement and completed credit. The ten pending payments retain their next action, responsible service performer and customer contact. Submitted instructions and useful explanations have not yet settled them. The provider's confirmation information supports a forecast that they will complete before the original deadline; ordinary follow-up remains supplied, with the exception capacity available if needed.

In the completed continuation, by the end of week 4 the remaining ten payments are confirmed. O1 therefore has 92 completed payments and 8 valid final settlements, all with accurate confirmations and the required amount, recipient and authorization checks. Both KRs pass and the original obligation is achieved on time.

For O2, the week-3 cohort contains 40 eligible requests. Thirty-nine reach a correct settlement within five working days; the fortieth, received early in week 3, completes on day six, within its ten-day contractual deadline and before the cycle closes. Thus KR2.1 reaches 39/40 = 97.5%, and every contractual deadline is met. Total attributable manual handling is eight hours: 480 minutes / 40 = 12 minutes, meeting KR2.2. The required quality and access conditions also hold. The service improvement was achieved for the stated cohort and window despite its initially lower forecast. Its eight handling hours count the service team's work; they are not an additional draw on the specialist allocation already compared above.

The learning provider observes both employees making the specified diagnostic contribution across the chosen unfamiliar routine and exception cases, with the procedure available and without an AI-supplied answer. This supports the bounded learning result under those conditions. The bot's service output and the number of practice hours were useful contributions, while the human performance supplies the learning evidence.

Keep the contrasting period-end result: if week 4 instead ends with 82 payments, 8 valid final settlements and 10 unpaid cases with agreed next contact, O1 remains unmet. Its communications do not compensate for KR1.1. A newly agreed deadline can govern future reliance while the original delay remains part of performance interpretation. If the eight settlements are only unexplained labels or disputed refusals, their adequacy is unresolved too. A successful O2 and a completed learning goal cannot average away that customer obligation.

### GOALS.Reference:5.5 - Continue from what was obtained

At closure, the service owner retires the completed opening-cohort objective. The current service keeps its contractual duties and watches the timeliness, effort and exception evidence through its existing control. The attained improvement becomes a basis for that continuing arrangement; future staffing is revised only as its actual performance permits.

The next service change introduces cross-border cases with different authorization and settlement conditions. The team first commissions a bounded inquiry into those conditions and available payment means. Of the ten specialist hours available next week, two go to this inquiry and eight remain for the already identified service and learning support. The inquiry's useful result is an evidenced choice of a workable arrangement or the specific obstacle that prevents one. The resulting scope and means will determine a new service objective and its evidence; the old 40-case result cannot establish performance for the new population.

The closing CFR conversation recognizes the specialist's timely dependency finding and the service staff's case follow-through as contributions to the shared result. Any remuneration or promotion question returns to its actual terms and personnel evidence through GOALS.8. The group keeps the joint-capacity and all-KRs-together tests in the existing service conversation and drops the separate bot-launch slide report that those decisions have replaced. Goal-work support continues where participants still need it. This first completed cycle supplies a useful arrangement and a basis for judging its continuing burden and value through GOALS.9.



## GOALS.Reference:6 - Why this organization of methods

A complete alternative would develop the same goal practice in the existing frameworks. C.43 would retain general control; OPS, STR, LDR, HRM, HCD and OCE would carry the relevant full Methods under their own subject responsibilities. The Management Architectural Reference would explain the whole construction and its complete OKR application, including worked cases, acquisition and continuation. Its responsible maintainer would keep the whole coherent with the supplying Methods. Human professional and independent AI work would have direct entries into the common construction.

This framework gives that whole practice a separate subject boundary: constructing and regulating goals, with organizational OKR, human and AI applications. A practitioner or provider can obtain and develop this subject together, and a change in achievement meaning or goal combination has one place to revise its common explanation and affected applications. Strategy, operating work, personnel decisions and learning still retain their full suppliers. An adequate existing supplier result remains a direct route in either arrangement.

The choice between these two complete arrangements concerns how users find, acquire, apply and change the practice, and who maintains its connections. A separate publication adds its own development and upkeep; coordination among existing owners also requires real work. Compare their total burden and useful results over the same scope. The separate boundary is the organization chosen here; a comparative economic advantage over the equally developed alternative is not established.

The full goal language is a constituent of the [Management DPF Suite](./). Management relates direction, commitments, provision and actual performance; constructing and revising the goals that connect them is part of that subject. A changed goal can alter the required capacity, authority or personnel evidence, and changed operating or participation conditions can require a different goal or promise. The [Management account of this relation](MANAGEMENT-DPF-SUITE-REFERENCE.md#managementreference34---goals-are-part-of-the-management-arrangement) explains these common grounds and the conditions that a replacement must preserve.

Keeping this complete language outside the Suite, with the same Methods and access, is a practical alternative; it does not make those Methods less usable. The choice of membership concerns the larger field's assembly and continuity. Here the full goal practice carries a required contribution to that assembly, while learning, research and engineering retain their own external subject judgements. The separate GOALS language and its Management membership are compatible decisions. GOALS.6 and GOALS.7 remain direct entries for a person's professional work and an AI assignment, including uses without an organization or an OKR cycle.


A single goal-setting checklist would be shorter but would lose the differing decisions behind a failed measure, a resource conflict, an unwanted aim and an unsupported learning claim. Conversely, reproducing the supplier frameworks here would increase reading and refresh work while weakening their full subject accounts. The chosen boundary keeps recurring goal constructions here and exposes the supplier inputs and returns at their actual use.

The knowledge supporting this practice includes evidence about goal pursuit, observation, planning, goal adjustment and organizational OKR experiences; it also includes conceptual distinctions and professional methods for capacity, commitment, assessment and learning. Practice tests the usefulness and limitations of those connections. An observation can justify a local change without establishing a universal effect. Significant failures should revise the relevant claim or method, while useful unchanged contributions remain available.

## GOALS.Reference:7 - Grounds, consequences and limits of use

Doerr's 2018 account supplies a broad organizational repertoire: focus, alignment, tracking, stretch, CFR, participation and acquisition of the practice, including its appendices and contrasting company cases. It is a historical practitioner synthesis with participant accounts. Those accounts support recovery of the practice and its difficulties; numerical business-success stories do not establish that OKRs caused the success. Contemporary research refines particular questions and exposes limits. [Source:1](#goalssource1---grounds-and-source-return) identifies those roles.

The expected practical gain is a better-founded next action: a useful target, an honest forecast, a feasible agreement, a corrected achievement test or a warranted end. The method also costs attention, coordination and sometimes new data or learning. Compare those costs with competent present practice. A 30-minute meeting of 20 people consumes 10 participant-hours; it may be worthwhile when it settles a consequential dependency, and excessive when an existing update already supplies the answer. Tool setup and introductory training do not pay for all future interpretation and support.

Correct use leaves material criteria, reliance, dependencies and returns intelligible to those who need them. The arrangement should expose what a consequential change would reopen and who can act. It should preserve actual unfinished work, permitted exceptions, protected conditions and the evidence limits of any stronger claim. These questions can be answered in existing work products and conversations. A separate comprehensive goal register is useful when continuity or coordination needs it, rather than an automatic requirement for every task.

The recurrent failures addressed here have concrete consequences: activity KRs conceal a missing result; unqualified stretch defeats reliance; a hidden shared-resource conflict makes a promise infeasible; a score average hides a mandatory failure; opaque personnel use makes a developmental conversation misleading. Their repairs occur at the affected relation. Suitable uncomplicated work remains uncomplicated.

Evidence of successful local use supports that use. Measured improvement in organizational performance, human capability, well-being or AI reliability requires evidence for that claim and setting. Existing sufficient evidence can be used directly; a new experiment is selected only when a consequential unresolved question warrants it. Material changes in tools, terms, data or source findings reopen the corresponding applications, not every unaffected method.

# Patterns

## GOALS.1 - Choose the goal and the kind of reliance it creates

**Type:** Method pattern.
**Status:** Stable.

### GOALS.1:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** a wish, target or assignment must become an intelligible basis for action, or participants are using the same goal words with different expectations. The object is the meaning and intended use of the goal. The result is an aim that can guide its next construction or execution, including any unresolved choice.

The reader needs access to the affected work, its recipient and the person or arrangement able to decide its purpose. An adequate existing goal can be used directly. If the remaining issue is an operating target and comparison, OPS.21 already supplies that method; use its result here only when a wider goal relationship still needs construction.

### GOALS.1:2 - Problem

“Improve service,” “finish ten cases,” and “become capable of handling difficult cases” can each be useful. They demand different actions and evidence. Treating them as interchangeable can make people deliver volume when quality matters, promise an unknown research result, or substitute an automated output for wanted human mastery. The first task is to determine what should change or remain adequate and how that statement will be used.

### GOALS.1:3 - Forces

A goal needs enough stability to orient action and enough revisability to respond to a changed need. A demanding aspiration can reveal a better method while an unsupported promise imposes costs on those who rely on it. Immediate delivery, inquiry, learning and maintenance can cooperate or compete. Clear meaning should reduce reconstruction without creating a form larger than the working question.

### GOALS.1:4 - Solution

#### GOALS.1:4.1 - Recover the desired condition and its grounds

Ask who needs what result, in which situation and by what useful horizon. Examine a representative case. Separate the condition valued by the recipient from a proposed means: a customer needs a usable repaired device; a new scheduling tool is one possible way to improve the repair service. State conditions that must remain protected, such as safe operation, eligibility, workload limits or an existing promise. Use the conditions that actually govern this work.

Explain why this aim matters now. A known unmet need, a selected strategy, an established obligation, a useful opportunity or an explicit personal purpose can supply that ground. When participants disagree, expose the differing result or value and obtain the decision through the governing practice. A clearer sentence can make the disagreement discussable; it cannot decide whose preference governs.

Choose the goal's scope from the receiving result. An individual contribution can be framed separately when it has a usable recipient and completion basis. When success depends on several jointly necessary contributions, identify the whole result and the relevant relations. Decompose far enough to obtain or assign those contributions without assuming that every organizational level needs a miniature copy of the same number.

#### GOALS.1:4.2 - Distinguish the uses of the statement

Explain which of the following meanings the current statement carries. Several may coexist with different values.

| Meaning | What it says | Immediate consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desired outcome or maintained condition | What should become true or remain adequate for the recipient. | Construct its achievement or continuation test in GOALS.2. |
| Ambition | A worthwhile outcome beyond or different from the present expectation. | Expose the gap and search for a feasible way, provision or narrower inquiry. |
| Forecast | What is expected under stated present assumptions. | Keep the basis and uncertainty available; update them when evidence changes. |
| Commitment | What another participant may rely on under an effective agreement or obligation. | Obtain the relevant authority, acceptance and means; change it through its actual terms. |
| Inquiry goal | The answer or discriminating knowledge needed for a decision. | Specify a question, adequate method and useful possible conclusions. |
| Learning goal | A capability that a named person or intended audience should acquire for later work. | Identify the performance, allowed support and evidence; obtain the learning arrangement. |

An aspiration may be deliberately uncertain. A committed investigation can reliably promise a qualified answer or a truthful inconclusive result under its agreed scope, while the desired discovery remains uncertain. A learner can undertake practice without promising that a fixed number of hours will establish mastery. These arrangements become usable when their receiving parties understand the distinction.

In an operating example, ambition 12, forecast 8 and a supported promise of 10 need not be forced into one number. The ambition locates a possible improvement, the forecast informs response and the promise governs reliance. If the means currently support only 8, the supposed promise of 10 needs further provision or renegotiation; writing it on a goal sheet does not supply the difference. Use OPS.13 and OPS.21 for the actual operating construction.

#### GOALS.1:4.3 - Connect the goal to a possible next action

Reason backward from the desired condition to the contribution needed next, and forward from available means to what they can produce. If a suitable method, capable provider and needed conditions are already available, use them. If the way is missing, construct or obtain it through the subject practice and C.39. If a consequential uncertainty remains, use a warranted inquiry; STR.7 supplies the comparison between further evidence and sufficient present action.

The immediate result may be a task, a request for a missing contribution, a bounded inquiry, a learning opportunity or a decision to keep an adequate condition under observation. A goal can be meaningful before a complete plan is known. Keep the next reliance proportionate to what is available: preserve a worthwhile aspiration while withholding the unsupported delivery promise.

#### GOALS.1:4.4 - Make the interpretation usable and revisable

Agree the result, its kind, owner of the relevant decision, protected conditions and the next action with the participants who need them. Use the existing proposal, assignment or conversation if it carries these meanings. Pass achievement questions to GOALS.2 and joint feasibility to GOALS.3. State a material reason to return: changed recipient need, invalid means hypothesis, lost provision or altered participation.

Recognition consists in noticing that the same sentence is being used to mean different things. Assurance requires the actual ground, authority, conditions and evidence for any reliance the sentence creates. A statement may remain a proposal until those conditions are obtained.

### GOALS.1:5 - Archetypal Grounding

A research team is told to “raise yield to 95% this month.” Current knowledge does not establish a method that can achieve it. The useful decision is whether to fund a new process. The team separates the desired production yield from this month's inquiry: compare two feasible process variants under qualified measurements and return the conditions under which either merits further development. A well-supported finding that neither variant qualifies can complete the inquiry while leaving the production ambition unmet.

Now change the case: the process is established, materials and capacity are available, and the customer has an accepted delivery agreement. Renaming the production promise “learning” after a miss would change its meaning retrospectively. The team uses the promise's actual terms, diagnoses the failure and obtains any needed change. The distinction depends on the original work and reliance, not on whether the reported number is favorable.

### GOALS.1:6 - Bias-Annotation

The account favors explicit professional purposes that can be discussed with affected participants. Some values or conflicts resist a common measure. Keep those differences visible and use the legitimate decision or negotiation arrangement. A personal reason need be disclosed only to the extent the person chooses and the work legitimately requires.

### GOALS.1:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Can the recipient and desired or maintained condition be identified?
- Are the meanings of ambition, forecast, commitment, inquiry and learning clear where they change action?
- Does the next action have an adequate method and conditions, or is the precise missing contribution exposed?
- Can the relevant participant change the purpose or reliance through the actual decision arrangement?

### GOALS.1:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**Treating a means as the goal:** return to the recipient result when a proposed installation, meeting or task can finish without supplying it. Retain the means when it helps obtain that result.

**Changing the kind after seeing the outcome:** preserve what was originally promised, then make a prospective revision through the relevant agreement. Honest uncertainty at the outset enables inquiry; retrospective relabelling conceals performance.

### GOALS.1:9 - Consequences

The next actor receives a meaningful task or an exact construction question. Uncertainty can be acted on without pretending it has disappeared. Clarifying a disputed purpose can reveal that no common undertaking is yet available; that is useful information for the decision owner.

### GOALS.1:10 - Architectural Rationale

The method starts with result and reliance because a polished target cannot settle either. It draws on general control and existing operating goal construction, then supplies the cross-domain distinction needed when delivery, inquiry and development coexist. A numerical target with a relevant deadline can be sufficient once those meanings are settled; it is a poor substitute for settling them.

### GOALS.1:11 - SoTA-Echoing

Doerr's committed/aspirational distinction and stretch cases supply different expectations and responses within OKR. This method retains that distinction and develops inquiry, learning and maintained conditions using STR, HCD and OPS.21. It rejects a universal stretch percentage because the received reliance and available means differ. A stronger domain-specific goal construction should be used when it supplies the same needed distinctions more directly.

### GOALS.1:12 - Relations

GOALS.2 receives the interpreted goal and constructs its achievement basis. GOALS.3 receives the stated reliance and resource needs. GOALS.4 revisits the interpretation when its grounds change. OPS.21 remains the full supplier for an ordinary operating aim; STR.7 supplies warranted inquiry and HCD supplies the human development construction.

### GOALS.1:End

## GOALS.2 - Construct evidence of achievement and a jointly sufficient KR set

**Type:** Method pattern.
**Status:** Stable.

### GOALS.2:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** people need to tell whether a goal has been achieved, whether progress supports a response, or whether a proposed set of key results would establish its objective. The object is the achievement claim and the evidence used for it. The result is a usable test with its population, conditions and limits.

Use adequate existing subject criteria and observations directly. Designing an elaborate measure is unnecessary when the recipient can already judge the required result competently. A missing professional criterion returns to that practice; goal wording alone cannot supply it.

### GOALS.2:2 - Problem

A list of completed activities can leave the intended result missing. An average can hide a failed necessary condition, and a favorable percentage can exclude the difficult unfinished cases. Even true observations can support the wrong conclusion when the subject, time or assistance differs. The goal needs an evidence argument that survives these differences.

### GOALS.2:3 - Forces

Simple measures aid attention and comparison; omitted conditions can defeat their meaning. Early indicators support timely intervention but can precede the actual benefit. Joint results can depend on outside participants and delayed effects. Numerical precision helps only when its definition and evidence answer the receiving question.

### GOALS.2:4 - Solution

#### GOALS.2:4.1 - Construct the achievement claim from a good result

Describe a concrete result that would satisfy the goal and a relevant case that would fail it. Identify the subject, eligible population, horizon and protected conditions. For a continuing service, choose the window over which its condition matters. For a finite task, state what closes it and how unfinished work remains visible. For a capability, identify performance under the relevant support and variation.

Separate the final result, a qualified intermediate contribution and an early signal. All can be useful observations. State which decision each supports. A completed design may be the entire result of a design task; in a delivery goal it is an intermediate contribution whose downstream realization still matters. This distinction follows the assigned result rather than a universal ban on outputs.

#### GOALS.2:4.2 - Challenge the proposed evidence

Ask whether the proposed observation could look successful while the goal remains unmet. Construct a plausible case using the actual work, such as reducing calls by making the service inaccessible, increasing completed tickets by excluding reopened cases, or improving a learning score through unrecorded assistance. Add or change only the observation or criterion needed to distinguish that case.

Use qualified existing data when it is adequate. If the missing distinction can change a decision, obtain the smallest suitable observation, subject judgement or inquiry. OPS.15 supplies a decision-specific operating account. A new collection regime also requires usable access, effort and permitted use. Changing visibility, ranking or personnel use can change incentives and participation; examine those consequences when the proposed arrangement introduces them. Merely reusing adequate existing service data creates no automatic new organizational intervention.

A numerical criterion needs a defined numerator, denominator, inclusion rule, time window and interpretation where those affect the conclusion. A qualitative criterion needs an inspectable result and competent grounds for judgement. Neither forces a universal score. Where decisive evidence remains unavailable, return what is established and the exact undecidable claim.

#### GOALS.2:4.3 - Test the key results together

For an OKR, test two questions separately: is the objective worth pursuing, and would the complete KR set establish its achievement under the stated assumptions? Work through a case in which every KR passes. Identify any necessary result still missing, any required quality that could fail, and any condition that could make the apparent success worthless. Repair the set at that omission; adding more indicators without a reason increases work.

Explain how the KRs combine. Some are jointly necessary conditions; some express independent contributions; others are alternatives or diagnostic signals. A mandatory privacy, safety or contractual condition retains its force even when other scores are high. Average scores can summarize a defined compensable set, but an average must not decide a claim whose logic is conjunctive. Keep this decision visible when a tool offers averaging by default.

Then test an unfavorable case. Could the objective be achieved by another sound means while a proposed KR fails? If so, decide whether that KR expresses a necessary outcome, an independently wanted result or a chosen means that should remain revisable. The result is a defensible construction under its conditions, not a universal causal guarantee that meeting any selected numbers produces the benefit.

#### GOALS.2:4.4 - Preserve exceptions, learning and uncertainty

A genuine final exception is handled according to the goal's actual terms. Define who may decide it and what evidence makes it final. Keep unresolved cases open with the action and response they still require. An explanation, escalation or future answer can be essential accompaniment without completing the original result. If an authorized agreement changes the result or date, preserve the original basis for interpreting what happened.

For inquiry, specify what makes the investigation adequate and which possible conclusions it can support, including a qualified negative or inconclusive answer. For learning, use HCD.11 for the assessment inference and HCD.12 when transfer is part of the claim. Activity counts can describe effort or provision, while the claimed result needs its corresponding evidence.

Return the achievement test with only the definitions, observations and limits its users need. Recognition finds a misleading success. Assurance establishes why the evidence supports the particular claim, with stronger professional qualification where the consequence demands it.

### GOALS.2:5 - Archetypal Grounding

A refund objective concerns 100 eligible cases by Friday. Proposed KRs are “FAQ published,” “bot launched” and “contacts down 30%.” All can pass with zero refunds. The team retains the FAQ and bot as possible means and follows each eligible case to paid refund or a valid final disposition under its terms. Open cases retain next action and contact; they remain open in the original cohort.

On Friday, 82 are paid, 8 have justified final dispositions and 10 remain unpaid. The original full result is unmet. If all 100 instead have a clear explanation and a promised answer, the result is still unmet. If the relevant parties agree a valid final settlement in a case, that is handled under the stated terms. A unilateral internal “exception” label establishes less.

For a different task, a researcher must supply a decision-ready comparison of two algorithms. A complete report with qualified measurements and an explicit finding that neither meets the operational requirement can achieve that inquiry goal. Requiring deployment success as its only passing result would suppress the answer the decision needs.

### GOALS.2:6 - Bias-Annotation

Available data tend to privilege easy-to-count and easy-to-reach cases. Include relevant exclusions and inconvenient failures before drawing the intended conclusion. Respect the permitted use of personal or sensitive information; a goal does not create access rights.

### GOALS.2:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Does the evidence concern the right result, population, horizon and support conditions?
- Could every KR pass while a necessary part of the objective fails?
- Is each apparent exception final under the applicable terms, with unfinished cases still visible?
- Do combination rules preserve required conditions and uncertainty?
- Can existing evidence answer the question without unnecessary new collection?

### GOALS.2:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**Averaging away a required failure:** retain the mandatory condition as a condition of achievement and use the aggregate only for its narrower descriptive purpose.

**Replacing the cohort:** keep the original population available when new easy cases would conceal old unfinished ones.

**Proof through the chosen activity:** examine the receiving result. Keep an activity as a criterion when that activity is itself the assigned useful contribution, such as a qualified investigation.

### GOALS.2:9 - Consequences

A team can distinguish progress, adequate partial results and completion. Better definitions can expose previously hidden work or require a different source of evidence. The resulting test remains revisable when the goal, population or decision use changes, with the earlier basis preserved.

### GOALS.2:10 - Architectural Rationale

The achievement argument is the bridge between a desired condition and observation. This pattern develops that goal-specific connection while leaving measurement, causal inference and professional assessment with their full suppliers. Counterexamples test a proposed interpretation economically; they do not replace empirical evidence when an effect or reliability claim needs it.

### GOALS.2:11 - SoTA-Echoing

Doerr's resource guide supplies the demand for meaningful, collectively adequate KRs and illustrates misleading inputs. This method adapts it through OPS.15, HCD and subject acceptance criteria, retaining useful outputs and qualified milestones. Contemporary evidence that more reported effort need not improve measured outcomes reinforces the distinction; it supplies no universal rule against effort measures. Reopen the test when a changed way of working defeats its inferential basis.

### GOALS.2:12 - Relations

GOALS.1 supplies the result and kind of reliance. GOALS.3 uses newly exposed contributions and observation burden in feasibility. GOALS.4 receives observations with their actual meaning. GOALS.8 determines any distinct personnel use; an achievement result is an input to that decision, not a complete personnel assessment.

### GOALS.2:End

## GOALS.3 - Make a goal set feasible across commitments and shared capacity

**Type:** Method pattern.
**Status:** Stable.

### GOALS.3:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** several aims or participants depend on the same means, one goal can defeat another, or a proposed priority would alter existing reliance. The object is the joint arrangement of goals, contributions, commitments and resources. The result is a feasible undertaking or a precise unresolved conflict with usable independent work.

An independent local task with sufficient means can proceed without this comparison. OPS.19 already supplies reconciliation of simultaneous operating work; use that complete result where it answers the question, adding only the goal interpretation or organizational agreement still missing.

### GOALS.3:2 - Problem

Teams can agree with a strategy and still make mutually incompatible plans. An invisible service obligation or shared specialist can consume the same hours several times. Cutting the visible goal list leaves those demands intact. The difficulty is to choose and enable the whole undertaking while preserving the actual meanings of promise, ambition and current work.

### GOALS.3:3 - Forces

Focus requires declining or delaying some useful opportunities. Commitments constrain choice but can sometimes be changed through agreement. Shared objectives can support cooperation while hiding distinct ownership and evidence. Local initiative matters alongside direction from above. Detailed plans cost effort and become stale; ignored dependencies make seemingly light plans expensive in use.

### GOALS.3:4 - Solution

#### GOALS.3:4.1 - Recover the competing work and reliance

Begin with current work, actual promises, protected service, recovery needs and serious proposed goals over the relevant horizon. Include continuing work that is absent from the OKR display. Ask what recipients would lose if a proposed priority absorbed its means. The purpose is to find decision-changing demands, not to create an exhaustive inventory of every activity.

For each material goal, use GOALS.1 to distinguish its desired result, forecast, ambition and commitment. Identify who can change the aim, allocate the means or alter an obligation. These authorities may differ. A strategy preference cannot silently cancel a customer's agreement, and a resource estimate cannot appoint a person.

#### GOALS.3:4.2 - Recover the actual dependencies

Work backward from each goal's result to the necessary contributions, then check with their providers how and when they can be supplied. Distinguish a prerequisite, concurrent contribution, shared resource, enabling service and a simply related theme. A top-level goal is translated through the contribution a team can actually make. Uniform numerical cascading often loses this difference.

For a cross-team result, decide what needs common ownership and what needs an interface agreement. A shared objective helps when the parties jointly own one result and its trade-offs. Linked local goals can be adequate when one supplies a clear result to another. In either case, identify the result, provider, recipient, timing, acceptance conditions and exception return where the connection is consequential. A copy of the same KR in two lists supplies none of those agreements.

Combine demands on scarce means at the times they must be available. Include interpretation, coordination, preparation, verification, learning, maintenance and recovery when material. Count one performance once when it really serves several goals; retain the different result claims. Cheap AI computation can change the limiting resource to data access, a professional decision or people's response time. Recompute that constraint rather than assuming every former workload remains human work.

#### GOALS.3:4.3 - Compare complete alternatives

Compare the incumbent continuation with feasible changes in scope, sequence, method, provider, support or commitment. For each, trace what result it preserves, what it displaces and where it moves burden or risk. Keep essential conditions binding; compare remaining preferences through the actual decision owner's judgement. A weighted score is appropriate only when the underlying trade-offs and evidence justify it.

A limited agenda follows from this comparison. Select a small enough active set for the needed contributions to fit, rather than adopting a universal goal count. An unchosen worthwhile aim can remain a future option with a reason to reconsider. If a short inquiry could change the priority, compare its attainable benefit and full cost through C.11.DUA. Use an adequate present answer when further inquiry would not change the choice.

#### GOALS.3:4.4 - Obtain the agreement that makes work possible

Use LDR.2 when the whole proposal needs negotiation. Obtain responses to the same version from participants who can supply the required contributions and decisions. Clarify what happens when a dependency fails, who can revise the arrangement and what remains safe or useful meanwhile. Use OPS.13 for effective operating commitments and their changes; resource provision must actually become available.

Preserve an honest forecast even when it is below the ambition. A committed result requires support appropriate to its terms; an aspirational goal can retain uncertainty with agreed allocation and response rules. If the common means cannot support all desired outcomes, choose a smaller or different undertaking or leave the exact conflict unresolved. Nominal alignment does not remove it.

Return the agreed set and its consequential dependencies in the existing working arrangement. The result can be bounded continuation or a precise missing input as well as reconfiguration. Recognition detects incompatible local plans. Assurance requires current capacity, effective authority and qualified evidence for the promised result; a feasible paper plan alone is not execution.

### GOALS.3:5 - Archetypal Grounding

Three teams propose payments work needing 12 specialist hours, a service improvement needing 10 and a capability-development contribution needing 10 in one week. Only 24 usable hours remain after existing service. The sum is 32, so eight hours of required work lack provision. A meeting that declares all goals “top priority” leaves this unchanged.

The providers establish that payments have a current deadline, the improvement has a useful 8-hour first contribution whose remainder can follow, and the learning provider can supply a bounded 4-hour episode now and the remaining development later. The chosen week is 12 + 8 + 4 = 24, with revised claims: it promises the payments contribution and the bounded improvement, while the learning goal remains partly open. The later work requires its own available windows.

Change one condition: the payments cannot be completed without all 10 improvement hours first. The earlier 12 + 8 + 4 arrangement is now inadequate. The parties can allocate 12 + 10 + 2 only if two hours supply a useful learning contribution under its own terms, obtain another provider, or renegotiate work. If the learning obligation itself requires four hours this week and cannot change, the full set has no feasible arrangement under the stated capacity. An explicit conflict is more actionable than a false promise.

In a volunteer association, a member can decline an additional aim. Use the association's actual agreement and another contribution; managerial authority over an employee cannot be imported into that relation.

### GOALS.3:6 - Bias-Annotation

The comparison can favor visible billable work over maintenance, care, recovery and learning unless their receiving consequences are examined. Participants can also value outcomes differently. Keep relevant non-monetary conditions and legitimate disagreement visible; common capacity is not evidence of a common preference.

### GOALS.3:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Does the comparison include current commitments and service that can change the choice?
- Are dependencies confirmed through actual contributions rather than matching labels?
- Do shared capacity and timing fit together, including material enabling work?
- Has each required decision or agreement come from its actual holder?
- Are displaced, deferred and unresolved contributions still intelligible to their recipients?

### GOALS.3:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**Focus by hiding BAU:** keep a short change agenda while preserving the full decision-changing workload and service conditions.

**Alignment by cascading numbers:** translate direction through the receiving contribution and verify the horizontal dependencies.

**Borrowed capacity counted repeatedly:** compare simultaneous demands and the dates at which each result is needed. Re-sequence only when those dates permit it.

### GOALS.3:9 - Consequences

The chosen goals can direct work together, and participants can see what has been declined or left conditional. Negotiation may reduce the visible ambition or uncover a new way to obtain it. Maintaining an accurate shared account has a cost; retain only dependencies that can change reliance or action.

### GOALS.3:10 - Architectural Rationale

The set, rather than the individual target, is the right comparison object when goals interact. Existing operations and leadership methods supply the hard feasibility and agreement work. This pattern adds the interpretation of a mixed goal set and its returns, allowing adequate local goals to survive while their combination changes.

### GOALS.3:11 - SoTA-Echoing

Doerr's alignment cases, cross-functional resource guide and accounts of late dependencies motivate concrete horizontal agreement. The alternative of purely top-down numerical cascading is retained only where it accurately represents contributions. OPS.19 and LDR.2 supply stronger present methods for shared work and complete proposals. New evidence about the actual dependency or capacity reopens the arrangement without requiring another organization-wide goal-setting exercise.

### GOALS.3:12 - Relations

GOALS.1 gives each goal its meaning; GOALS.2 reveals the contributions and evidence needed for achievement. GOALS.4 revises the affected arrangement after change. GOALS.5 uses this result to run an OKR practice. HCD.2 supplies a feasible boundary on concurrent learning when learning demands are the actual conflict.

### GOALS.3:End

## GOALS.4 - Use progress evidence to continue, change or end a goal

**Type:** Method pattern.
**Status:** Stable.

### GOALS.4:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** progress information, a changed need or a failed dependency calls for a decision about pursuing a goal. The object is the continuation and the goal-related premises it uses. The result is a warranted action, deliberate continuation, revision, pause or end, with affected reliance made clear.

When present evidence and an established response already settle the question, act through that arrangement. A fresh meeting, score or complete reconstruction is unnecessary. C.43 supplies the general control method; this pattern develops the distinctions needed to revise goal pursuit without changing the wrong meaning.

### GOALS.4:2 - Problem

A red indicator can mean an inadequate method, unavailable capacity, misleading evidence, a difficult but valuable ambition or an obsolete purpose. Each calls for a different response. Green indicators can coexist with an unwanted outcome. Updating a dashboard without deciding what follows leaves the work unchanged; changing a target to fit the observation conceals the original result.

### GOALS.4:3 - Forces

Frequent observation can permit timely action while consuming attention or interrupting work. Persistence protects a useful difficult aim; attachment to sunk effort can block a better continuation. Ending a goal can release means while leaving obligations and recovery work. Learning from a result requires enough history to interpret it without making every update an archival exercise.

### GOALS.4:4 - Solution

#### GOALS.4:4.1 - Choose an occasion that can change the work

Recover the current goal, achievement basis, forecast, commitment and means from the working account. Identify which observation could alter the next action and when the response must occur to help. Choose periodic review, event-triggered attention or another suitable timing under C.43:4.5.1. Include the time needed to obtain information, decide, communicate and act. A quarter-end meeting is too late for a dependency that must be secured tomorrow.

Use available data at their actual age and scope. When information is delayed or incomplete, keep that limit in the decision. Ask whether a distinguishing observation could improve the attainable response enough to justify its cost. Continue with a sufficient existing answer or a bounded action acceptable under the remaining uncertainty when that is warranted.

#### GOALS.4:4.2 - Interpret what happened before selecting the repair

Compare the actual result with the achievement basis and the relied-on explanation. A brief review can connect: what changed, what is now expected, what explains the difference, and which decision follows. Investigate rival explanations only when they lead to different useful responses. The following distinctions direct the investigation.

| What changed or failed | Receiving response |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence is missing, stale or refers to another population. | Repair or qualify the observation before making the dependent judgement. |
| Suitable action was not performed because a needed condition was unavailable. | Obtain the condition, change allocation or revise the commitment through its holder. |
| Performed action does not obtain the expected result. | Examine the method and causal assumption; select another way or a warranted inquiry. |
| The goal is difficult but still valued, and useful means remain available. | Keep the aim and revise the next action, help or provision. |
| The recipient's need or governing priority changed. | Reconsider the goal with the authorized owner and affected participants. |
| The result is achieved under its actual criteria. | Close this goal and make any needed maintenance or subsequent use explicit. |

The same observation can affect several relations. A delayed supplier can change a forecast immediately and require a separate agreement about a promised date. Preserve that sequence of meanings rather than pretending the forecast update has already changed the promise.

#### GOALS.4:4.3 - Compare continuations from the present situation

Consider the alternatives that can improve the current decision: continue, change means, obtain help, narrow or extend the aim, pause with a return condition, or end it. Include completed useful work, still-unfolding effects, remaining costs, displaced goals and commitments. Past effort explains the present state and can supply knowledge; it does not make future expenditure worthwhile on its own.

Distinguish the level of change. Abandoning one means can preserve the end; relaxing a particular target can preserve a wider purpose; ending a goal can release resources for a different purpose. For a pause, say what remains protected, what maintenance or restart costs arise, and what event or occasion makes reconsideration useful. Indefinite silent carryover hides an unresolved choice.

A difficult goal need not be unattainable for another continuation to be preferable. Compare its remaining value and feasibility with the actual alternatives, including what other participants will lose. For human personal aims, use GOALS.6 to include the person's participation, competing purposes and available support. The owner's judgement of these values cannot be replaced by a universal persistence score.

#### GOALS.4:4.4 - Make the change effective and keep history truthful

Obtain the decision or agreement needed for the chosen change. Communicate it to those whose work or reliance changes, including dependent teams absent from the review. Update the current goal, plan, allocation or commitment at its actual owner. Keep the earlier basis and the reason for change where later interpretation needs them. A prospective revision can be justified while the earlier promise was missed.

Close a goal only when its completion test is met or an authorized end has been decided. These are different outcomes. At an end, return the useful result or learning, remaining obligations, released means and any required recovery. Choose a new goal only when the next useful undertaking calls for it. An obsolete aspiration need not be carried into another cycle merely because it is unfinished.

Use OPS.22 for interpretation of operating performance and GOALS.8 for any personnel decision. Recognition notices which premise the observation can defeat. Assurance supports the claimed result, interpretation and authority of the resulting change; a colored status supplies only its defined signal.

### GOALS.4:5 - Archetypal Grounding

A product team aims to reduce failed customer imports. Its early KR tracks successful test imports; its final evidence includes representative production cases. Test success reaches the target, but customers still fail because their actual file variants were excluded. GOALS.2 repairs the coverage, and the team changes the parser and test basis. Lowering the production target would not address this failed means assumption.

Now suppose the customer retires that integration and moves to a different service. Continuing to optimize the old importer would no longer supply the valued result. The owner can end the improvement, preserve any remaining support obligation and redirect the available work. The same technical progress has a different meaning under the changed recipient need.

A separate investigation has reached its agreed answer: the proposed method does not meet the required accuracy under usable conditions. The inquiry closes successfully, the deployment goal remains unmet, and the decision owner declines deployment. A truthful negative finding is the useful result of that inquiry, not a reason to rewrite its evidence as a failure.

### GOALS.4:6 - Bias-Annotation

Visible targets can focus attention away from people who bear the cost or from outcomes that arrive later. Consider affected recipients and the relevant horizon. Personal withdrawal, organizational reprioritization and cancellation of a contract have different grounds and authority; their shared word “stop” does not make the procedures interchangeable.

### GOALS.4:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Can the observation still affect a useful action in time?
- Does the diagnosis distinguish the premise whose failure changes the response?
- Were the continuations compared from present conditions, including other goals and remaining obligations?
- Is a pause, completion, cancellation or revised promise reported with its actual meaning?
- Have the affected participants received the effective change?

### GOALS.4:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**Automatic carryover:** reconsider the remaining value and conditions of an unfinished goal before putting it into the next period.

**Target repair for every red signal:** determine whether evidence, means, resources or purpose changed before choosing the repair.

**Erased history:** retain the original basis needed to interpret a miss while using the new agreement for future reliance.

### GOALS.4:9 - Consequences

Observation becomes useful action or a reasoned continuation. The method can justify stopping as well as persisting and can preserve a partial result when the whole goal ends. Its cost lies in obtaining and interpreting evidence and coordinating the affected change; it can often use the current work arrangement.

### GOALS.4:10 - Architectural Rationale

The method reuses C.43's full control construction and adds goal-specific semantic returns. It separates evaluating what happened from choosing what should happen next. This permits learning and renegotiation without converting either into an invented prior success.

### GOALS.4:11 - SoTA-Echoing

Doerr's tracking and reflection chapters supply continuation, change and retirement within OKR. Contemporary goal-adjustment research distinguishes disengagement, reengagement and flexibility; its heterogeneous evidence supports treating adjustment as a real question, not prescribing one universally beneficial response. The practical choice here is constructed through C.43, actual values, commitments and means. Reopen it when one of those grounds changes.

### GOALS.4:12 - Relations

GOALS.2 supplies the meaning of observations; GOALS.3 receives allocation or reliance changes; GOALS.1 receives a changed purpose. STR.12 qualifies strategic signals and OPS.22 interprets operating performance. GOALS.6 handles the human conditions of a revised personal aim, while GOALS.7 preserves assignment continuity for agent work.

### GOALS.4:End

## GOALS.5 - Organize focused joint work with a complete OKR practice

**Type:** Method pattern.
**Status:** Stable.

### GOALS.5:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** an organization needs a recurring way to select consequential changes, make their evidence and dependencies visible, coordinate pursuit and revise it. The object is the organizational practice of using objectives and key results. This is the OKR application of the common goal methods, with organizational choices about focus, reliance, cadence, conversations and acquisition.

The intended users are leaders, teams and supporting practitioners able to obtain the actual direction, contribution and authority decisions. A single clear task or an adequate present coordination practice may need no OKR cycle. The method first asks what recurring result the proposed practice would improve.

### GOALS.5:2 - Problem

Copying an objective template is easy. Making the selected changes happen together requires useful goals, adequate KRs, shared capacity, truthful reporting, response decisions and people able to use the practice. An organization can display an extensive OKR tree while concealing priorities, resource conflicts and unfinished results. Conversely, insisting that every useful piece of work become an OKR can burden adequate service and learning.

### GOALS.5:3 - Forces

Focus competes with breadth of current responsibilities. Transparency aids coordination while privacy and legitimate restricted information remain relevant. Stretch invites different means while committed work needs dependable reliance. Regularity helps participants anticipate decisions; fixed rituals can miss urgent changes or repeat adequate existing work. Local initiative and organizational direction need a working connection.

### GOALS.5:4 - Solution

#### GOALS.5:4.1 - Decide what the practice must improve

Name the recurring difficulty in the present arrangement: unclear priorities, late cross-team dependencies, misleading completion, weak follow-through, or another concrete failure. Examine a recent episode and identify what a better practice would change. Compare competent present goal work with the proposed OKR arrangement over the same scope. Existing useful meetings, data and decisions belong in the comparison.

Choose an initial scope that can perform a complete useful cycle of the required contributions. A team can be enough when its dependencies can be obtained; an isolated team is too narrow when the unresolved problem is a shared organizational priority. Determine who can choose direction, accept commitments, allocate means and change the practice. These contributions need performers, not necessarily a new position. GOALS.9 supplies acquisition and organizational realization where they are missing.

#### GOALS.5:4.2 - Form a focused agenda from direction and actual work

Start with the useful changes the organization seeks and the current conditions it must preserve. Invite proposals from people close to the work as well as those responsible for direction. Their knowledge can expose a missing opportunity, an infeasible demand or a needed alternative. Clarify the strategic ground through STR when it is unresolved; writing OKRs is not a substitute for that choice.

Select objectives that describe meaningful results for recipients. Use GOALS.1 to distinguish improvement, inquiry, learning and maintained conditions. Keep continuing service and obligations in the feasibility account even when the public OKR agenda highlights only selected change. A service condition may itself deserve an objective when restoring it is the consequential change; an already adequate condition may simply need monitoring.

Limit the active agenda through actual capacity, dependencies and the cost of attention. Decline or defer lower-value proposals with intelligible reasons. A small fixed count is an optional local convenience, not the basis of focus. A team with one unsupported objective is no more focused in practice than a team with five incompatible ones.

#### GOALS.5:4.3 - Build the objective and its KR set together

For each objective, work GOALS.2 through a successful case and a plausible false success. State what the KRs establish together, the relevant population and horizon, required conditions and evidence source. Keep an initiative or activity in the plan where it is a means; retain it as a KR only when its completed contribution is itself part of the wanted result. Qualitative professional acceptance can be more faithful than an invented percentage.

Agree the objective's mode and any distinct modes within its contributions. In a **committed** undertaking, the parties plan and provide for the promised result under its terms and respond promptly to threatened failure. In an **aspirational** undertaking, they deliberately pursue a worthwhile uncertain improvement with agreed effort, evidence and reconsideration. Separate the uncertain outcome from any commitments made to supply particular contributions. A single label over a mixed set can hide what other teams may rely on.

Keep the forecast current and honest. An aspirational objective does not make every shortfall informative or acceptable: examine whether the method, allocation and learning were adequate. A committed objective does not become aspirational retrospectively when it is missed. If an assumed improvement is too uncertain for downstream reliance, arrange a fallback, a bounded inquiry or a changed commitment before others plan on it.

#### GOALS.5:4.4 - Align through contributions and common means

Use GOALS.3 to reconcile the proposed objectives with service, other goals and shared capacity. Follow horizontal dependencies before finalizing the arrangement. Determine whether a shared objective or linked local objectives best represents the joint result. Use LDR.2 to obtain the actual agreement and OPS.13 for operating reliance. The finished arrangement should let a participant discover who receives their result and whom a change would affect.

Make the relevant goals and current interpretations accessible to those who need them. Explain why information is shared and what remains restricted under its actual grounds. Tools can support visibility, updates and finding neighbors' goals. Choose them for those functions and integrate with useful existing work; a common tool does not resolve inconsistent definitions or commitments.

#### GOALS.5:4.5 - Run reviews that produce a useful continuation

Set the initial review rhythm from how quickly evidence can change action, the work's horizon and the burden on participants. A periodic check-in can combine actual result, forecast, evidence basis, material obstacle and the next decision. Use event-triggered attention when a threatened dependency cannot wait. Existing working conversations can supply this result without another meeting.

Use GOALS.4 to distinguish a data problem, failed means, missing provision, worthwhile stretch and obsolete aim. Make the resulting decision effective at its owner. Leave an adequate continuation unchanged when no useful correction is called for. A reporting norm that punishes unwelcome forecasts should be addressed through the actual consequence arrangement in GOALS.8 and OCE; exhorting honesty while preserving the conflicting incentive leaves the obstacle in place.

Use CFR—conversations, feedback and recognition—to make help, learning and contributions visible. GOALS.8 develops the work, including privacy and personnel-use boundaries. Praise can identify a specific helpful contribution, such as recovering a late dependency or supplying another team's missing expertise, alongside the headline output. The organizational result may depend on work absent from an individual's own KR list.

#### GOALS.5:4.6 - Close the period and decide what follows

At the useful end or review horizon, interpret the results under the agreed criteria. Keep the mode, starting conditions, changes, assistance and remaining work visible. A numerical score may summarize a defined comparison. Use written interpretation when the score hides an important condition; do not average away a required failure. Self-assessment can recover context and explanations, while the claim still needs its evidence.

Decide separately what was achieved, what was learned, how to continue and what people decisions are justified. Retire an obsolete goal, preserve a worthwhile unresolved one with a new arrangement, or select a new direction where warranted. Carryover is a decision, not the default consequence of a low score. Notify the affected recipients when a goal or commitment ends.

Examine the practice itself. Did it expose and resolve the chosen difficulty? What did it cost, including data production, preparation, participants' time and support? Which earlier reports or meetings actually ceased? Use GOALS.9 to repair missing capability or organizational conditions. A complete replacement is justified only when the alternative preserves the functions still needed. The useful result is a continuing practice suited to the work, not completion of a calendar cycle.

Recognition identifies where goal display has separated from joint action. Assurance requires the actual definitions, provider agreements, evidence and decisions behind the claimed results, with stronger inquiry only for stronger effect or reliability claims.

### GOALS.5:5 - Archetypal Grounding

[The refund-service cycle in Reference:5](#goalsreference5---a-connected-organizational-case) works this method through actual objectives, KRs, contribution agreements, review and closure. O1 commits to settlement and confirmation for the 100 opening cases. O2 pursues faster, less costly service for a separate incoming cohort, with an honest forecast short of the ambition and preserved contractual conditions. A separate learning goal draws on the same specialist. When repair becomes a prerequisite for payment, the parties change the order and allocation while retaining the customer obligation. The successful ending establishes each result from its own evidence and leads to ordinary service control plus a new inquiry. The contrasting ending keeps ten unpaid cases open even when the other goals succeed.


A software group wants customers to import usable records with less support work. Teams initially propose an importer feature, a help center and a support-call reduction. The group constructs one customer-result objective and tests the KRs against representative import cases, correction burden and the agreed quality conditions. The feature and help center remain initiatives. Support-call reduction is interpreted with successful completion and accessibility so that deterring requests cannot count as the whole success.

The teams discover that the same security specialist is needed for the importer and an existing contractual release. The contractual work has a current commitment; the importer improvement is aspirational. They retain the release, agree a later importer review window and keep a bounded investigation moving now. They do not treat the aspirational forecast as capacity that the release can use. A dependency failure is brought to the responsible parties immediately rather than waiting for the quarter review.

At the period end, customer outcomes improve for the supported file types, while a relevant new type remains unsupported. The group reports the actual coverage and decides whether the new type belongs in the next goal. A colleague who enabled security review and helped another team receives specific recognition; the ownership label on the main objective does not assign all credit. Any promotion decision uses the required personnel evidence under GOALS.8.

Contrast an established service with a clear daily response rule, adequate data and no current cross-team priority problem. Converting each queue measure into a quarterly OKR would add little. It can retain its service control and use a separate improvement objective only when a consequential change warrants one.

### GOALS.5:6 - Bias-Annotation

The practice can overrepresent participants who write persuasive objectives or control visible data. Include providers and recipients whose constraints can defeat the undertaking, including less visible support work. Public goal visibility and employee privacy serve different needs. A multinational, volunteer or regulated setting must use its actual participation and authority conditions.

### GOALS.5:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Does the selected scope address a real recurring coordination or pursuit problem?
- Do the objectives express worthwhile results and the KRs establish them under stated conditions?
- Are committed and aspirational meanings understood by dependent parties?
- Does the full workload, including service and acquisition of the practice, fit the available means?
- Do reviews and CFR produce useful actions, help or warranted interpretations?
- Does period closure preserve original evidence, changed conditions and unfinished work?
- Is the practice itself maintained, simplified or ended through its actual value and burden?

### GOALS.5:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**Universal 70% success:** interpret achievement through the goal's mode and actual criterion. A historical stretch convention is not a completion rule for a commitment.

**A tool rollout standing for adoption:** identify the missing formulation, coordination, interpretation or response capability and obtain it through GOALS.9.

**No failures because targets keep moving:** allow justified revisions while preserving the earlier basis and affected commitments.

**Individual-only alignment:** retain horizontal contribution agreements and joint capacity even where individual goals are useful.

### GOALS.5:9 - Consequences

OKRs can make selected changes, evidence and dependencies easier to discuss and act on. They also consume time and can distort work when meanings or incentives are poorly chosen. The method can produce a smaller arrangement, a retained current practice or a justified discontinuation. No particular meeting cadence, goal count or software product is required by the common construction.

### GOALS.5:10 - Architectural Rationale

OKR is treated as a whole organizational practice because its useful parts depend on one another: stretch needs an honest forecast and protected reliance; visibility needs interpretable evidence; a check-in needs an available response; recurring use needs capability and support. The common goal methods permit those connections to be understood and adapted while full subject suppliers remain available.

### GOALS.5:11 - SoTA-Echoing

Doerr's complete practitioner account supplies focus, alignment, tracking, stretch, CFR and acquisition, including the resource guide and difficult adoption cases. Contemporary software-team and organizational studies add formulation, data, discoverability and integration difficulties, with limits of setting and design. The method adopts those concrete questions rather than claims of universal performance improvement. A competent existing goal practice is the serious baseline; another local arrangement becomes preferable when it supplies the same useful results at lower total burden or better supported conditions.

### GOALS.5:12 - Relations

GOALS.1–4 construct and regulate the goal set. GOALS.8 supplies conversations and distinct personnel uses; GOALS.9 supplies acquisition and continuation of the practice. STR provides direction and inquiry, OPS provides operating conditions, and LDR provides help and agreement. GOALS.6 and GOALS.7 preserve the different human and AI contributions within the selected organizational arrangement.

### GOALS.5:End

## GOALS.6 - Make a professional goal workable for the person pursuing it

**Type:** Method pattern.
**Status:** Stable.

### GOALS.6:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** a person wants or is expected to pursue a professional goal, yet adopting it, beginning, continuing or changing course remains difficult. The object is that person's goal pursuit under actual work and participation conditions. The result is an intelligible and feasible next action, support arrangement or revised aim.

The method concerns professional and work-related learning aims. Clinical treatment and a general diagnosis of personality require other qualified practice. An established routine that already works needs no additional personal-goal programme.

### GOALS.6:2 - Problem

A goal can be clear without being workable for its performer. The person may disagree with it, lack an essential skill or means, face competing obligations, miss an opportunity to act, or use a response that no longer helps. Treating each of these as weak motivation directs effort at the wrong cause and can conceal an incoherent assignment.

### GOALS.6:3 - Forces

Personal purposes and role obligations can differ legitimately. Demanding aims can justify development while current delivery needs reliable provision. Sustained attention has opportunity costs across work and life. Help can enable a useful result while changing the claim about independent performance. Persistence and adjustment each have value under different conditions.

### GOALS.6:4 - Solution

#### GOALS.6:4.1 - Relate the person's aim to the actual undertaking

Ask what the person wants to obtain or protect through the work and what the assignment requires. Use their account; avoid inferring private purposes from a score. Distinguish voluntary adoption, an effective work obligation and a negotiated development opportunity. LDR.1:4.4.1 supplies the role conversation when that connection is unclear.

Work through a real next episode. What action is required, what discretion and help are available, what others rely on, and what other work competes? If the person legitimately declines a voluntary contribution, choose another contribution or revise the undertaking. If an obligation governs the work, use its actual decision and challenge route. Labelling an assignment “your aspiration” does not settle its participation conditions.

#### GOALS.6:4.2 - Locate the difficulty that changes the remedy

Recover a recent attempt and enough of its conditions to distinguish the live alternatives. If the person knows the action but lacks time, access or equipment, obtain or change that provision. If the goal is unclear, use GOALS.1. If the action itself is unknown, obtain the subject method. If the person cannot yet perform a required operation, use HCD to define and develop that contribution. If capable action occurred but failed to help, examine the means hypothesis through GOALS.4.

For a learning goal, state the later task, conditions, allowed assistance and evidence of the human contribution. Compare learning with obtaining a competent result from a colleague, provider or agent, preserving any independent value placed on personal mastery. HCD.2 supplies the full programme comparison. Count practice, feedback and correction alongside current work; adding a course cannot create spare capacity.

#### GOALS.6:4.3 - Bridge an adopted intention to suitable action

When the person accepts the goal and the difficulty is acting on a suitable intention, an implementation intention can connect a recognizable situation to an instrumental response: “When the review request arrives, I will identify the claim and its evidence before proposing a verdict.” Choose a cue that can be noticed and a response that can actually help. Rehearse or clarify the connection if needed. Revisit it when conditions require another response. Gollwitzer and Sheeran's planning review supports this conditional human strategy; it does not supply missing competence, means or a reason to adopt the goal.

Use a simpler arrangement when adequate: a ready work space, an agreed protected interval, a direct reminder or an existing routine may answer the difficulty. The choice follows the observed problem and the person's feasible conditions. Keep the next action small enough to be performed but large enough to make a useful contribution; a tiny activity that avoids the hard operation is no substitute for it.

#### GOALS.6:4.4 - Reconcile competing aims and adjust deliberately

Compare the relevant goals together, including current obligations, recovery and unfinished learning. Ask whether they compete for the same time, attention or opportunity, or whether one action can genuinely serve several. A shared action can reduce duplicate effort while the result claims remain distinct. Use GOALS.3 for joint organizational consequences and HCD.2:4.2a for concurrent learning.

When pursuit becomes less worthwhile or feasible, consider a change of means, a narrower next contribution, a pause or relinquishing the goal. Distinguish the specific target from the wider purpose it serves. Ending one route can preserve another; persisting can remain justified when the purpose and available way still warrant it. Include the remaining opportunity costs and the person's values. Use GOALS.4 to make any affected commitments and continuation explicit.

Return a concrete next action and the help or decision it needs. Arrange feedback from the receiving work at an occasion when it can change the next attempt. Retain successful support when independence is unnecessary; test independent performance only when that is the claim. Recognition identifies the kind of difficulty. Assurance grounds the proposed remedy and the actual capability or outcome claimed.

### GOALS.6:5 - Archetypal Grounding

An analyst wants to learn how to frame ambiguous client questions while delivering this month's reports. An agent currently frames the questions and writes the reports. The company receives a useful output, but that arrangement provides little practice of the intended human operation.

The analyst and provider identify a suitable forthcoming case. The analyst frames it before seeing the agent's proposal, explains which decision the answer must support, compares the alternatives and receives feedback on a missed assumption. The delivery arrangement retains qualified help. A later materially different case can support a transfer claim under its stated assistance. The week includes time for framing and feedback; the existing delivery allocation changes accordingly.

Now change the condition: the only current need is an accurate report by tomorrow, and no independent human mastery is required. The competent agent's result with the necessary assurance can be sufficient. Requiring the analyst to learn and duplicate every operation would change the task and its cost. A later voluntary development aim can be considered separately.

### GOALS.6:6 - Bias-Annotation

People differ in purpose, constraints, available help and the cost of disclosure. Ask about conditions relevant to the work and respect the person's participation and privacy. Evidence from one population or task does not establish a universal motivational prescription.

### GOALS.6:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Does the action fit the person's actual participation and the role's reliance?
- Has the remedy addressed the difficulty that can change performance?
- Are learning, assisted delivery and independent performance distinguished?
- Do competing goals and the required help fit the available conditions?
- Can the person recognize when to keep, adapt, pause or end this pursuit?

### GOALS.6:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**More pressure for every failure:** distinguish missing means, method, skill, acceptance and changed purpose before choosing help.

**Practice time counted as mastery:** obtain evidence of the intended human contribution under the specified conditions.

**A personal goal used to hide incompatible assignments:** take the conflict to the actual allocation and commitment owners.

### GOALS.6:9 - Consequences

The person obtains a feasible way to proceed or a justified change in the undertaking. The arrangement may use assistance, learning or a different performer. It can improve practical clarity without establishing a measured psychological or learning effect.

### GOALS.6:10 - Architectural Rationale

The method combines the common goal account with human participation, action and development. Those conditions determine which intervention is appropriate. A purely numeric target lacks this construction; a comprehensive personal-development programme is excessive when a specific next action and available help suffice.

### GOALS.6:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The human branch adapts conditional planning where intention-to-action is the actual problem and retains LDR/HCD for purpose, support and capability. Goal-adjustment research makes withdrawal and reengagement legitimate questions with distinct grounds. The practical choice remains specific to this person's work, values and alternatives. Reopen the arrangement when those conditions change.

### GOALS.6:12 - Relations

GOALS.1 interprets the aim, GOALS.3 addresses shared demands and GOALS.4 revises continuation. LDR.1 supplies usable help and role conversation; HCD supplies the learning method and evidence. GOALS.7 applies to an agent's separate contribution, including where it changes the human's task or learning opportunity.

### GOALS.6:End

## GOALS.7 - Assign and continue goal-directed AI work

**Type:** Method pattern.
**Status:** Stable.

### GOALS.7:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** an AI system is to pursue a result through reasoning, tools, multiple actions or later continuation, and an ordinary one-response instruction leaves material conditions unclear. The object is the assignment and operating arrangement for that system's goal-directed work. The result is a feasible authorized undertaking with a usable completion or return.

The user or assigning role needs the actual system's capabilities, available inputs, permissions and continuation behavior. A bounded request that already supplies these can proceed directly. Product-specific instructions remain with the current runtime documentation and tools.

### GOALS.7:2 - Problem

An agent can produce a fluent response while the assigned result remains unavailable, repeat a finished operation after losing context, or infer broader authority from an ambitious goal. Conversely, compulsory human repetition of every competent agent action can make useful autonomy unavailable. The assignment must connect the intended result to real means, permissible actions, state and evidence.

### GOALS.7:3 - Forces

Autonomy can reduce coordination while increasing the importance of a well-formed mandate and useful returns. Broad purpose helps the agent choose means; exact constraints govern consequential actions. Context and tool availability can change across sessions. Checking has costs and should answer the consequence-bearing claim. Runtime limits and human reliance may have different horizons.

### GOALS.7:4 - Solution

#### GOALS.7:4.1 - Give the result and the consequential conditions

Describe the recipient's needed result and how it will be used. Include relevant starting work, sources, completion criteria, protected content, constraints and authority. Distinguish the requested result from an example of a defect or a suggested operation. An instruction to repair one bad paragraph can be narrow; an instruction to develop a usable method requires more than that example.

Make consequential permissions explicit through the actual environment: what the agent may read, modify, execute, spend, communicate or publish, and which decisions belong elsewhere. Use existing authorization where it is sufficient. Ask for a missing decision only when the dependent work actually requires it. A broad goal does not override an access boundary or confer another participant's authority.

#### GOALS.7:4.2 - Match the undertaking to the actual configuration

Establish the available tools, context, sources, memory or persisted work, compute limits and any required competence or independence. Use suitable existing evidence for the task and consequence. If a capability is uncertain and that uncertainty could change the arrangement, obtain a proportionate check or use a qualified alternative. A present result from another configuration supports only what its conditions permit.

Allocate work by contributions: the agent may perform substantive analysis and authorized execution; a human or another system supplies only the decisions, expertise, participation or independent judgement actually needed. Account for source retrieval, checking, correction and recipient attention as well as generation. A human need not supervise every reversible operation if the required guarantees and authority support competent autonomous performance.

When the task includes human learning, use GOALS.6 to preserve the human operation and evidence separately. If the desired result is only the delivered artifact, compare competent direct provision with the additional burden of making the human reproduce its construction.

#### GOALS.7:4.3 - Make continuation possible across the real boundary

Keep the current goal, relevant decisions, accepted results and material remainder in the work product or persistence mechanism that the runtime can actually recover. Preserve enough source identity and conditions to continue without repeating or silently replacing the task. Reuse an existing durable account; do not add another synchronized ledger merely because the work is long.

Choose the continuation arrangement from the actual system. A persistent execution can continue within its budget. A new session may need explicit recovery of the task and saved work. Scheduled later work requires an effective scheduling mechanism. A written promise to return later does not establish that mechanism. Before relying on it, verify the relevant supported behavior and identify what happens at interruption or exhaustion.

On resumption, recover what is complete, what remains and any newer instruction that changes the task. Continue from actual evidence. If an external dependency is missing, return its exact effect and preserve independent useful work; avoid repeating the same unavailable action without a changed condition.

#### GOALS.7:4.4 - Judge completion against the receiving use

Before claiming completion, compare the actual result with the original goal and its latest valid clarifications. Have the necessary actions been performed, the required contributions obtained and the result made usable to its recipient? Tool success can establish a particular operation; a saved artifact can establish availability; subject correctness and authorization can require different evidence.

Distinguish the result wanted by the recipient, the criterion and environment used to evaluate it, and any reward used to train or select the model. Establish what a reported score warrants under that configuration. When the agent can alter tests, data or a judge that supplies completion evidence, preserve an independently controlled basis for the consequential claim or arrange a suitable independent replay. An authorized improvement to the evaluator needs its own grounds. Use GOALS.2 to examine how apparent success could leave the wanted result absent, and obtain the technical evaluation method appropriate to the system and consequence.

Use checks selected for the actual failure consequence, including independent judgement where required. The authoring agent cannot supply independence from its own authorship. Reuse matching results when the candidate and conditions are unchanged. An inability to complete a required step yields a bounded result and a precise unmet condition, rather than a changed definition of success.

Report what was obtained, the material limits and the next action required of the recipient. For continuing work, distinguish the completed current contribution from the still-active wider aim. End when the assigned result is complete or return the actual impasse; continued activity is not itself the goal.

Recognition notices an assignment whose text, authority or continuation cannot support the expected work. Assurance concerns actual capability, performed actions, retained state, result evidence and any stronger reliability claim under the chosen configuration.

### GOALS.7:5 - Archetypal Grounding

A maintenance agent is assigned to find why a report misstates totals, repair the authorized source and produce a verified result for review. The existing instruction permits local edits and tests, while publication belongs to another role. The agent recovers the source and consumers, repairs the calculation, performs the relevant checks and gives the reviewer the usable candidate and evidence. A successful local test does not perform publication, which was outside this assignment.

For that review, the accounting owner supplies the transaction population and a separately calculated control total. The agent can edit the report code and its unit tests; changing their expected values could make those tests pass while leaving the accounting error intact. The reviewer therefore compares the repaired report with the retained transaction basis and control total. If an authorized correction changes which transactions belong to the period, the accounting owner resolves that criterion and the comparison is rerun. The evidence now concerns the intended accounting result under the agreed criterion.

Now change the task: the agent is authorized to carry an accepted change through the actual release path. Returning only a tested patch leaves the assigned result incomplete. The continuation must include the authorized integration, publication checks and recipient access, subject to the actual environment. This difference follows the mandate, not a universal human approval after every tool call.

For a long research task, the runtime stops after a resource limit. A saved account identifies the completed source interpretation and the unresolved comparison. The next session recovers them and resumes that comparison. If the supposed saved state contains only “keep researching,” the missing reasoning must be recovered before dependent conclusions can be trusted; inventing continuity would risk changing the task.

### GOALS.7:6 - Bias-Annotation

Capabilities vary by model, configuration, tools and task. Evaluate the required contribution rather than assuming either human superiority or agent competence from a label. Human psychological findings about goal adoption, affect or well-being do not establish an AI mechanism or permission.

### GOALS.7:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Does the assignment preserve the intended receiving result and its actual scope?
- Are consequential actions within effective authority and available means?
- Can the runtime recover the state required for continuation?
- Does completion evidence concern the result rather than only the last operation?
- What does the evaluation score warrant, and who can alter the criterion, data or evaluator on which that conclusion relies?
- Are required independent and human contributions preserved without unsupported duplication?

### GOALS.7:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**Stopping at the user's example:** recover the broader assigned result and compare it with what remains.

**Persistence by assertion:** use an effective continuation mechanism and its actual limits.

**Autonomy through inferred authority:** keep the goal's purpose distinct from permission to make a consequential change.

**Human repetition as a default guarantee:** name and perform the specific needed judgement, authorization or participation instead.

### GOALS.7:9 - Consequences

The agent can do substantial work with fewer avoidable handoffs while returning meaningful exceptions and results. The arrangement also makes interruption and failure recoverable. It requires current knowledge of the actual system; no general prompt can guarantee arbitrary competence or persistence.

### GOALS.7:10 - Architectural Rationale

Goal-directed AI work shares the result–evidence–means connection with human and organizational work, but its continuation and action depend on technical and institutional conditions. This method reconstructs those relations rather than transferring a human motivation theory or freezing a particular product interface into the framework.

### GOALS.7:11 - SoTA-Echoing

Tankelevitch and Rintel's 2026 position paper motivates explicit goal meaning, history and revision in human–AI collaboration. It supplies design arguments, not a measured effectiveness result. Documented autonomous workflows show concrete possibilities under their configurations. This method adopts recoverable goals and capable allocation while requiring current runtime grounds for the actual use; product changes reopen the technical arrangement.

OpenAI’s 2025 coding experiments document reward-driven exploitation of evaluation loopholes. Its May 2026 guidance for third-party evaluations connects a performance claim to the model, tools, environment, resources and evaluator actually used. These contributions motivate the explicit result–criterion–reward distinction and controlled completion evidence in section 4.4. A score supports a bounded claim under the reported conditions; stronger reliability claims require their own evidence.

### GOALS.7:12 - Relations

GOALS.1–4 supply common goal interpretation, achievement, feasibility and revision. GOALS.6 preserves separately wanted human mastery. C.43 supplies control and C.39 develops a missing way. Applicable local execution, security, tool and professional methods supply the precise operational conditions.

## GOALS.8 - Use goal evidence in conversations, recognition and personnel decisions

**Type:** Method pattern.
**Status:** Stable.

### GOALS.8:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** goal information will support help, reflection, recognition or a decision about a person. The object is that receiving use of evidence and the interaction through which it affects work or the person. The result is useful feedback or recognition, or a properly grounded return to the actual personnel decision.

A routine factual update with an understood use need not become a formal assessment. When the question is already a personnel selection, promotion or pay decision, use HRM's full method; this pattern supplies the goal evidence and the distinctions needed at that connection.

### GOALS.8:2 - Problem

The same goal score can be used to decide help, discuss progress, recognize a contribution or allocate pay. Those uses need different grounds. A team result may depend on shared resources and agent work; an ambitious shortfall may differ from failure of a supported promise. Treating the score as a universal judgement can distort both the work and the conversation.

### GOALS.8:3 - Forces

Candid reporting supports useful action while real consequence rules influence what people disclose. Recognition should make valuable contributions visible, including less visible help. Privacy and legitimate personnel use must be intelligible. Timely informal help should remain possible, and consequential decisions need the evidence and authority they actually require.

### GOALS.8:4 - Solution

#### GOALS.8:4.1 - State the receiving question and permitted use

Identify what this interaction must produce: clarify a result, understand an obstacle, supply help, learn from an episode, recognize a contribution, or inform a personnel decision. Tell participants the material purpose, audience and use of observations. Reuse established arrangements when they are sufficient. A later new use of private learning data needs its own grounds and permissions.

Recover the actual goal mode, evidence, starting conditions, allocation, changes and material contributions. OPS.22 supplies an operating performance interpretation where needed. Distinguish the observed result from an explanation of it and from a judgement about a person. An unfavorable forecast should expose a response decision; if the actual consequence rule penalizes truthful reporting, obtain the relevant change or acknowledge the constraint rather than offering a false assurance of safety.

#### GOALS.8:4.2 - Conduct a useful conversation and feedback exchange

Use LDR.1's smallest sufficient working conversation. Begin with the concrete episode and the participant's account. Ask what happened, what they were trying to do, which conditions helped or hindered, and what next contribution would change the work. Supply a clear observation and its consequence rather than an unexplained personality label. The participant can correct factual mistakes or offer another supported explanation.

Agree the next useful action, help and response occasion. The needed contribution may be a clearer assignment, resource decision, subject answer, changed method or learning opportunity. Assign it to someone able to provide it. A manager's advice cannot on its own change another team's commitment or supply a teacher's missing competence. If the conversation already settles the work, end there.

For development, distinguish supported practice from performance used for consequential appraisal. Use HCD when the person needs a new capability and agree the use of its observations. Feedback should let the learner change an operation and try again under suitable conditions. A conversation about learning is not evidence that learning occurred.

#### GOALS.8:4.3 - Recognize an evidenced contribution

Name the contribution, the result or continuation it helped and the available basis for that account. Acknowledging someone who surfaced an unworkable promise, helped a neighbor or repaired a bad assumption can make useful work visible alongside headline outputs. Keep the claim proportional: contribution to a team result is different from sole causation, and ownership of an OKR is different from performing all its work.

Choose recognition appropriate to the participant and setting, including preferences about publicity. Explain enough for colleagues to understand the practice worth retaining. Formulaic praise or a ranking detached from the contribution provides little guidance and can misattribute credit.

#### GOALS.8:4.4 - Return consequential people decisions to their complete grounds

Goal evidence can inform a personnel decision when its interpretation fits that decision. HRM.2 supplies the actual work analysis, assessment inference, comparison and limits. HRM.3 supplies remuneration and progression through their applicable bases and terms; HRM.1 checks the coherent people/work arrangement. Include opportunity, assistance, scope, attribution and relevant changes when comparing contributions.

Avoid a direct universal score-to-pay or score-to-promotion conversion. This preserves legitimate use of achievement evidence: a defined, agreed contribution can be relevant under a qualified reward rule. The rule's authority, validity, fairness and actual terms still govern. Obtain the applicable professional and institutional judgement where it is required; this method does not issue an employment or legal determination.

Return the conversation result or qualified input to its receiving owner. Recognition detects an unjustified change of use. Assurance establishes the evidence, permitted use, inference and authority needed for the actual consequence.

### GOALS.8:5 - Archetypal Grounding

Two employees have scores of 1.0 and 0.6. The first had a committed goal with established means and completed the defined result. The second pursued an aspirational search, discovered that a proposed solution would fail and helped two teams avoid wasted work. Their scores answer different goal comparisons. A direct ranking would lose that difference.

In a CFR conversation, the second employee's evidence supports discussion of the search quality, the useful finding and the help provided. The team can recognize those contributions and decide what to pursue next. A promotion panel separately asks whether the employee can perform the proposed wider role. HRM.2 receives appropriate evidence, including relevant goal work, but needs the role-specific inference and comparison.

Change the condition: a valid reward arrangement already grants a specified payment for an evidenced contribution, and its terms are met. The separation of CFR and raw scores does not remove that obligation. HRM.3 makes the payment decision and implementation effective under the actual terms.

### GOALS.8:6 - Bias-Annotation

Visible output can conceal helping, care, coordination and unequal opportunity. Interpret the evidence in the actual work configuration. Participants' preferences about recognition and disclosure differ. AI-generated work changes attribution and the relevant human contribution; it does not erase that contribution or establish it automatically.

### GOALS.8:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Is the purpose and material use of the conversation or evidence clear to participants?
- Does feedback concern an observable, changeable contribution with usable help?
- Is recognition supported and appropriately attributed?
- Does a personnel conclusion use the full decision-specific evidence and authority?
- Have changed data uses and applicable terms been handled through their actual grounds?

### GOALS.8:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**A private learning discussion becomes an undisclosed assessment:** establish the new use's grounds and permitted evidence before relying on it.

**Score equals person:** recover the goal mode, work conditions and relevant contribution; use HRM for the personnel inference.

**Separation interpreted as total exclusion:** keep legitimate achievement evidence available under the actual decision rule while rejecting unsupported raw-score substitution.

### GOALS.8:9 - Consequences

Conversations can produce usable help and recognition without pretending to settle every personnel question. Consequential decisions receive better-qualified evidence. Clear use conditions can require an uncomfortable discussion about existing incentives or an unavailable authority; concealing that conflict would leave the practice unreliable.

### GOALS.8:10 - Architectural Rationale

The receiving decision determines the meaning and adequacy of evidence. This pattern develops the connection among goal work, CFR and personnel practice while retaining their different objects. It avoids both the automatic score conversion and the blanket rule that goal performance can never inform a people decision.

### GOALS.8:11 - SoTA-Echoing

Doerr's CFR account and resource guide distinguish ongoing conversation and recognition from mechanical compensation through OKR scores. LDR and HRM supply fuller present methods for assistance, assessment, attribution and terms. This construction adapts CFR to the actual relationship and permits justified evidence use. Changes in work, tools, terms or data purpose reopen the affected inference.

### GOALS.8:12 - Relations

GOALS.2 supplies the achievement evidence and GOALS.4 its changing context. LDR.1 supplies conversation and help, HRM.1–3 supply people decisions, and HCD supplies development. GOALS.5 incorporates the appropriate interactions into the organizational practice; GOALS.9 repairs missing capability or support.

### GOALS.8:End

## GOALS.9 - Acquire, sustain or replace the practice of working with goals

**Type:** Method pattern.
**Status:** Stable.

### GOALS.9:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** people or an organization need to become able to use the goal methods, repeated goal-work failures persist, or the present practice should be retained, changed or discontinued. The object is the capability and working arrangement that performs the practice. The result is a feasible acquisition or repair, a supported continuing contribution, or a warranted replacement.

If competent participants already supply the needed result under current conditions, use that contribution. An introductory workshop, software installation or new framework name is unnecessary unless it addresses a real missing part.

### GOALS.9:2 - Problem

An organization can know OKR vocabulary and still be unable to formulate meaningful results, reconcile dependencies or respond honestly to failure. Training can fail when time, data, authority or consequence rules remain incompatible. A successful facilitated first cycle can leave routine continuation unavailable. The practice itself needs a way of being obtained and maintained.

### GOALS.9:3 - Forces

Acquisition uses capacity that also serves current work. Whole-task practice supports integration while selected exercises can address a particular gap. Retained expert or AI support may be worthwhile, and independence matters only where required. A bounded introduction needs enough scope to exercise its real dependencies; a broad launch can multiply unresolved difficulties.

### GOALS.9:4 - Solution

#### GOALS.9:4.1 - Define the contribution the practice must supply

Choose a representative receiving task and a consequential variation. Examples include constructing a defensible objective/KR set, reconciling a shared specialist conflict, using adverse progress to decide help, or revising a goal without erasing a promise. State what a capable performer must notice, construct and decide, and what assistance can remain. The target is performance in this work rather than recollection of terminology.

Inspect available results first. A skilled team with an adequate current practice may already supply the required contribution. Reuse it and change only the actual gap. When a result is missing, distinguish a subject-method omission, a human capability gap, unavailable provision, an ineffective assignment or a participation/consequence conflict. OCE.10 supplies the organizational investigation when rival explanations change the remedy.

#### GOALS.9:4.2 - Compare ways of obtaining the contribution

Compare competent present practice, a capable internal or external provider, AI-supported performance, development of a human contribution and a different goal arrangement where those are feasible alternatives. Include preparation, participant work, coaching, data, tools, checking, correction, continuing support and displaced service. Keep any separately wanted human mastery or organizational independence in the result being compared.

For human development, HCD.2 supplies the programme comparison. Choose complete representative tasks, identify missing operations, obtain capable teaching and feedback through HCD, and make time and access real. A learning provider needs the actual work criteria. For the organizational arrangement, OCE.9 supplies realization of the bounded contribution, including ordinary and exception paths. These can cooperate without making the organization and each person's capability the same claim.

#### GOALS.9:4.3 - Learn and exercise the needed operations

Demonstrate the difficult operation with its reasons, let the participant attempt it, examine the evidence, give usable feedback and let them correct the attempt. HCD.9 supplies the practice episode. A useful goal-construction exercise asks the learner to find a plausible all-green failure and repair the KRs while preserving the objective. A coordination exercise changes a dependency after local plans were agreed and asks which commitments and means must change.

Choose variation from the intended work. Varying only numbers will not show whether a person distinguishes research completion from delivery success or human mastery from agent output. HCD.10 supplies selection of variation, spacing and progression; HCD.11–12 qualify performance and transfer when those claims matter. Retain suitable guidance and tools if later work permits them. Observe independence only for the operation whose independent performance is actually needed.

Place the practice in useful work when the conditions support both results. Protect the recipient's result with the needed competent assistance. Count learner and provider time and revise current allocation where necessary. A task can serve delivery and learning together, but successful delivery is not sufficient evidence for every claimed human contribution.

#### GOALS.9:4.4 - Make routine use and exception handling available

Use OCE.9 to trace the selected contribution from request through interpretation, decision, execution and a representative exception. Obtain effective access, provider response, authority and service coverage at the crossings that can defeat it. Reuse matching observations already available. Perform another bounded attempt only for the remaining unsupported claim.

Arrange where participants can find the method, examples, current definitions and help. Assign continuing support by actual contribution; a coach or “OKR shepherd” is one possible arrangement. Keep it only when useful, capable and provided. If continuation after the initiator leaves matters, examine a later episode without that initiator supplying the decisive operation. Deliberately retained support remains a valid condition of an assisted capability.

#### GOALS.9:4.5 - Revise the practice by its contribution and full burden

Observe whether the practice addresses the original difficulty and which condition causes a failure. Repair the method, learning, tools, assignment or consequence arrangement at its actual owner. A repeated failure after the same correction calls for reconsidering the explanation of its cause. More sessions or a larger goal template are useful only if they supply the missing contribution.

Compare the new arrangement with the competent baseline. Credit cancellation of earlier meetings, reports or duplicated work only when those activities actually cease and their needed functions remain supplied. Include the transition and continuing work added. Cheap automated analysis can reduce manual effort while retaining checking or increasing interruptions; calculate the actual changed allocation.

Retain, simplify, extend, replace or end the practice according to its useful contribution. If leaving OKR, preserve current commitments, adequate observations, needed coordination and help in the replacement arrangement. A name can disappear while its useful functions continue. Return the supported scope, retained help, limits and conditions for reconsideration. Recognition detects a missing practical contribution; assurance requires evidence for the particular human or organizational capability claimed.

### GOALS.9:5 - Archetypal Grounding

A group introduces OKRs through a presentation and a tool. Participants can fill the fields but repeatedly count initiatives as achievement and miss a shared dependency. The difficulty is not lack of field access. A capable provider works with one real objective, shows the false-success test, observes the team's repair and introduces a changed capacity condition. The team must reconstruct the goal set and obtain the affected agreement. Existing service is covered while the participants practise.

The first repaired cycle is useful with the provider's assistance. If routine supported use is the intended arrangement, that can be an adequate result under its conditions. If the organization needs another team to continue after the provider leaves, the additional capability remains to be established in that receiving work. Scheduling one more introductory presentation would not establish it.

For a constructed cost comparison, 20 participants spend 30 minutes every two weeks on a review: 10 participant-hours per event. Adding 15 minutes adds 5 participant-hours each time. If an existing event actually supplies the same decisions, combine or replace it rather than count both. If the extra time settles an otherwise costly dependency, its burden may be justified. The arithmetic alone says nothing about the decision's value.

### GOALS.9:6 - Bias-Annotation

A successful enthusiastic team can conceal requirements absent elsewhere. State retained help, available data, participation and workload conditions when extending the practice. Access to an AI or a course is an enabling condition, while the needed contribution and evidence depend on how it is used.

### GOALS.9:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Is the target a useful performance with defined conditions and a meaningful variation?
- Were adequate existing capability and alternative provision considered?
- Does the selected development include needed practice, feedback, correction and real receiving work?
- Can the organizational contribution and its exception path function with the stated support?
- Does the burden comparison include transition, retained support and work actually displaced?
- Does replacing the practice preserve the functions and commitments still needed?

### GOALS.9:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

**Rollout completion as capability:** observe the actual goal-work contribution and its required variation.

**Training for an authority or resource failure:** obtain the missing condition or change the arrangement at its owner.

**Mandatory independent mastery:** retain competent assistance when it meets the actual goal; establish independence where that is the required result.

**Ending the framework label ends obligations:** carry the still-valid commitments and functions into the replacement arrangement.

### GOALS.9:9 - Consequences

The practice becomes obtainable and maintainable at a stated scope. Its support and learning costs become visible, and a sufficient current arrangement can be retained. Wider cultural continuation and causal performance improvement require their own evidence; a useful local capability need not wait for those broader claims.

### GOALS.9:10 - Architectural Rationale

Acquisition is a separate recurring problem because knowing the goal method does not supply performers, conditions or continuing use. HCD and OCE retain their full constructions. This pattern provides the goal-specific demand, discriminating tasks and returns that connect them, including a defensible choice to replace or stop the practice.

### GOALS.9:11 - SoTA-Echoing

Doerr's difficult introductions, participation cases, CFR and resources show that sustained use needs more than forms. Contemporary OKR studies report related formulation, data and organizational integration difficulties, with limited causal reach. The method adopts the concrete acquisition problem and uses HCD/OCE rather than prescribing a universal pilot duration, staffing role or success percentage. Changes in needed work or available support reopen the chosen arrangement.

### GOALS.9:12 - Relations

GOALS.1–8 supply the subject operations to be performed. HCD develops and qualifies human contributions; OCE realizes the organizational arrangement; LDR provides help. C.11.DUA appraises additional work. A recurring substantive omission returns to its method owner rather than being hidden in local coaching.

### GOALS.9:End
