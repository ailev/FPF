# IS.Readme - Find the first useful contribution

Practice Implementation Support helps people and systems make a practice workable in their actual setting and keep it usable. Enter at the missing contribution; a sufficient local answer can be enough. The examples below show ordinary entries, not a catalogue or the field boundary. Use the [pattern index](#table-of-contents) for a different question.

**A tool is available but cannot be used.** A practitioner tries to compare two source editions and discovers that the older one cannot be opened. Start with IS.2. Recover the failed input and obtain the source owner's access result. If that makes the already understood operation possible, the useful answer can stop there. If the source does not exist, return the target operation to its subject owner; another invitation to use the tool will not supply it.

**A sponsor asks for training while ordinary work makes the practice costly.** Start with IS.1 to distinguish the recipient result from the requested activity. IS.2 then follows a real episode: is an operation missing, or are people capable but unable to obtain time, permission or a workable response to their findings? Obtain the appropriate contribution. A learning programme belongs with HCD when personal acquisition is needed; organizational decisions belong with their actual authority and OCE. A workshop is not the default answer to either question.

**Temporary help is ending.** Start with IS.5 using what the work already shows. Identify which contribution must continue, who can supply it and what unfinished work remains. Retain adequate current provision, arrange an actual transfer or end the contribution responsibly. Reopen strategy construction through IS.3 only where the continuation needs it.

The [Preface](#ispreface1---make-useful-practice-obtainable-in-real-work) explains the connected practice and its distinctions. The pattern bodies develop the Methods and worked cases. [Framework relations and sources](#isreference1---obtain-the-external-contribution) locate professional suppliers and explain when their conditions must be reconsidered.

# IS.Preface - Make useful practice obtainable in real work

## IS.Preface:1 - Make useful practice obtainable in real work

Use this framework when people or systems are expected to use a practice, but a description, invitation, tool or training event has not made the intended work obtainable. A practice may be new to its recipients, a familiar way that has become difficult to continue, or a proposed way whose local fitness remains under examination. The support practitioner helps obtain the contributions that make its use possible and worthwhile, examines what actually happens, and changes or ends the help accordingly.

Start with one receiving use. Ask the performer to show what they are trying to obtain, the occasion in which they need it and the first contribution they cannot obtain. A missing permission may need an authorized decision; a difficult judgement may need an expert; a missing personal ability may need practice and feedback; an unsuitable method may need revision or replacement. The first useful result can be one repaired connection. It need not be an implementation programme.

This framework concerns providing and revising help that changes a condition for establishing, restoring, adapting or continuing a recipient's practice. It does not cover every professional service merely because that service supplies a useful result. If an adequate service already makes the wanted result obtainable, use it directly. A helper can arrange qualified performance as part of a support strategy without requiring the recipient to acquire that specialty; arranging the support and performing the target operation remain different contributions. The field also differs from designing every part of the organization or choosing the recipient's whole development strategy.

The practitioner needs to understand the proposed support operations, recover the receiving work with its participants, and obtain qualified subject judgement where the decision depends on it. They need not personally master every supported profession. A source, expert or capable AI can supply a contribution, but its availability, fitness and permission to act remain conditions of the proposed arrangement.

## IS.Preface:2 - Keep the target, the help and their results distinct

The **target practice** is what the receiving performers are trying to do. The **support strategy** is the proposed way of helping them establish, use or revise it. The **support arrangement** supplies the people, systems, time, tools, access and decisions needed for that strategy. A **support undertaking** is the bounded work and commitments through which that help is provided.

These distinctions change action. A different explanation of a target method may repair its description without changing the method. Replacing a classroom session with rehearsal changes the support strategy. Repairing an unavailable reference changes the arrangement. Changing the target's decision criterion can change the method itself. A successful result does not settle which change made it possible.

The receiving promise also matters:

| Wanted result | What must actually be obtained |
| --- | --- |
| A useful result from the target practice | The result and conditions the receiver needs, including qualified external performance when that is the chosen way. |
| A person's independent or appropriately supported ability | That person's contribution in representative work under the promised support conditions, using the applicable human-development and assessment methods. |
| A continuing organizational contribution | The combined work, effective relations, means and ordinary provision conditions that allow the organization to deliver it. |
| Ability to arrange later implementation | The intended holder's performance of noticing a difficulty, obtaining suitable help, making permitted decisions and using the result, at the promised scope. |
| Continuation among other carriers | Obtainable material and support, actual receiving uses and the particular cultural relations claimed; publication or a first successful handover is insufficient for a population-wide claim. |

A target need not be an immutable, fully validated manual. Selection, construction and adaptation can remain open. For a particular attempt, recover the intended contribution, the operations actually to be performed, their conditions and the result that can change the next decision. Preserve the status of a proposed method account. IS.4 explains the stronger Method, Work and evidence distinctions when the use relies on them.

If there is no useful target question yet, open inquiry rather than invent an implementation plan. B.5.PI and C.40.CD help form and revise questions; DOCA helps construct and compare development opportunities; ME and the relevant subject practice develop the obtaining way. Return to implementation support when its help can make a consequential difference. Continued development is wider than successful implementation and can open another worthwhile problem after this undertaking ends.

## IS.Preface:3 - Use the connected Methods from the contribution that is missing

IS.1 makes the support undertaking intelligible and workable. IS.2 locates the consequential difficulty and obtains the next useful contribution. IS.3 constructs the actual support strategy and its provision. IS.4 performs and examines a bounded introduction, keeping target and support changes separate. IS.5 obtains continuing use, a qualified transfer, expansion or a responsible end.

Their relations do not prescribe a lifecycle. A sufficient current arrangement can enter at IS.4. An inherited service can require only an IS.5 handover. A failed attempt can return directly to IS.2 or to the subject method's owner. Existing evidence can settle a question without another trial. Follow an ordinary path only while its next contribution is needed.

**Long working mantra**

> Recover the receiving result and who must obtain it; agree what help is wanted and permitted; follow a real use to the consequential difficulty; distinguish the explanations that change action; obtain or construct the needed contribution; compare and provide appropriate help with its recipients; perform the bounded introduction and examine later work; separate changes to the target, the help and the conditions; revise the exact unsupported claim; preserve, transfer, extend or end support on the basis of what continued use really requires; return a changed developmental aim or an unavailable subject method to the practice that can settle it.

The return connections matter as much as the forward ones. Diagnosis may reveal that the original request promised the wrong result. A good target can require different support. A useful local adaptation can become a method-development question. A successful episode can reveal an unsustainable dependence. The practitioner follows the affected contribution rather than restarting every activity.

## IS.Preface:4 - One performer, several practices; several performers, one result

A local leader, internal specialist, external provider, independent practitioner or qualified AI can perform implementation-support work. There is no requirement for a dedicated office, common employment, communicating community or permanent consultant. Several parties can contribute without becoming one organization or one decision-maker.

One residency coordinator, for example, can develop a method during a development project and later organize programmes and cohorts as ordinary provision. Project membership, method of work, capability and organizational assignment are different relations. The framework division does not demand another employee whenever the practice changes.

The target operation still needs its actual subject expertise. The same facilitator can help two professions investigate a difficulty without being qualified to decide both professions' technical questions. Shared scheduling, access or explanation tools do not confer that qualification. Conversely, a sufficiently capable AI can supply a previously scarce contribution; preserving a human job is not an invariant of the Method. Permissions, physical access, reliable evidence and the intended holder's own performance remain separate questions.

Use ME.6 when materially different configurations of performance, Method co-use and provision must be compared. Its selected configuration and conditions feed the support strategy; proposed relations still need realization. ME.10 supplies method-use support, and C.32.MWA distinguishes the interacting structures where that changes the comparison. Obtain OCE and Operations results for organization, allocation and continuing service. Use their sufficient results directly, without a second comparison or introduction.

## IS.Preface:5 - Recognize a useful local result before claiming a larger one

A plan, delivered workshop, configured tool, attempted use and successful receiving result are different findings. A record of activity can be complete while a required condition is missing. Begin with the strongest useful conclusion the actual grounds support.

For ordinary help, a short account can suffice: the difficulty, the contribution supplied, what the recipient could do with it, what remains conditional and the next justified action. Use more precise models or stronger evidence when the decision depends on them. A capability claim needs the intended holder and work conditions. A cultural claim needs the actual carriers and relations. A causal claim needs its own C.28 qualification. These are different reasons to examine the work, not a demand to complete every assurance branch.

Avoid making every unknown a research assignment. C.11.DUA compares what an attainable answer could change with the work, delay and interruption required to obtain it. Preserve a robust bounded response when live explanations do not change it. A mandatory protection condition remains binding until changed by its actual authority.

## IS.Preface:6 - Sources, alternatives and limits

The Methods connect implementation help to actual work and to the professional contributions on which it depends. A generic catalogue of barriers or strategies can suggest a question, but cannot determine the operation, provider, timing or appropriate result by itself. A target-specific rollout recipe can be enough in a stable setting; use it when it supplies the whole needed contribution. Reopen the support construction when conditions, recipients or the target change.

Implementation-support literature develops a helping practice. The [2020 conceptual account](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-020-05145-1) and [Practice Guide to Supporting Implementation](https://implementation.fpg.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/ISP-Practice-Guide.pdf) emphasize tailored help and building recipients' implementation capacity in service systems. They distinguish that help from taking over delivery. Here, recipient participation, contextual judgement, actual use and feedback are retained. Continued qualified expert or AI supply is also an option when the receiving practice needs that contribution but personal acquisition is not the promise. This extension depends on the actual provider, capability, permission and receiving-use conditions; it is not an effectiveness result inherited from human-service research. Human learning mechanisms are not models of AI change.

IS.4 supplies a general introduction Method: an implementer can use it directly without a separate helping relationship. Its wider independent use supports this language without making every implementation activity an instance of support. It distinguishes implementation observations, target adaptation, support-strategy adaptation and causal explanation. HCD, OCE, CB and ME remain suppliers of their complete Methods. The constructed cases illustrate reasoning and operations, not empirical effectiveness.

This framework does not supply each target profession's technical Method, a universal learning theory, every organization's design or a general development strategy. It tells the support practitioner how to obtain and use the needed contribution, keep its conditions intact, and return the work when it cannot be supplied. A provider should reduce a promise that depends on unavailable expertise rather than fill the gap with a procedure name.

The short entry routes foreground the receiving work, helper contribution and next decision. They omit the detailed technical, learning and organizational constructions supplied by the pattern bodies and the external practices in IS.Reference:1. Return to those bodies when the shorter account leaves an operation, condition or evidence claim unresolved. The examples illustrate selected ways of combining the Methods; they do not define the field's coverage.

## IS.Preface:End

# Patterns

## IS.1 - Agree a Useful Implementation-Support Undertaking

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Proposed
>
> **Primary working result:** a bounded undertaking in which the intended help, recipient result, contributions, decisions and conditions for changing or ending the work are sufficiently clear to act.

### IS.1:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** someone requests implementation help, offers a service or sponsors a change, but the participants do not yet share a workable account of what that help must make possible. Also use it when a previously sensible agreement no longer fits the target, recipient, provider or available means.

The first move is to ask for one receiving use: what should someone be able to do, on what occasion, and what contribution is missing now? Compare that use with the help being offered. A request for a course may concern learning, unavailable time, inaccessible material or a target that does not fit the work. A workshop is one possible contribution, not the purpose of every request.

The governed subject is the support undertaking: the bounded work and commitments through which help is provided. A note, conversation or service specification can describe it. The description is not itself the agreed commitment, permission or performed work.

Use an adequate existing agreement directly. A routine request covered by a current service can end with its usable response. A legal contract, employment decision or professional authorization still needs its own qualified process; this Method does not create those powers.

### IS.1:2 - Problem

A sponsor can ask for adoption, a learner expect instruction, a manager expect productivity and a provider promise attendance. Each can complete what they understood while the receiving practice remains impossible.

Unclear help also creates dependence and mistrust. A person may reveal mistakes to obtain assistance while their helper uses the same information for an undisclosed performance decision. An external provider may promise a result that depends on time the customer has not allocated. A recipient can assume that the provider will make technical decisions the provider is neither qualified nor authorized to make.

The practical problem is to construct a small undertaking that makes useful help obtainable, while allowing the parties to revise an unsuitable request before consuming the larger commitment.

### IS.1:3 - Forces

| Force | Consequential tension |
| --- | --- |
| Useful ambition and current means | The requested benefit can be valuable while a required contribution is unavailable. |
| Several recipients | The sponsor, performer and beneficiary may need different results and hold different rights. |
| Trust and accountability | Candid discussion can improve help; undisclosed reporting can make it unsafe or misleading. |
| Expert help and retained responsibility | A provider may supply a difficult contribution without acquiring the recipient's decision authority. |
| Clarity and discovery | Enough must be agreed to begin; some target and support questions can only be settled through use. |
| Simple entry and consequential commitments | A short conversation can suffice for ordinary help, while some undertakings require formal protection or contract work. |

### IS.1:4 - Solution

Recover a real receiving use, distinguish the help from the result, and make each necessary contribution and decision obtainable. Begin with a bounded useful undertaking; retain the condition that could change its promise.

#### IS.1:4.1 - Recover the request through the receiving work

Ask the person requesting help to show a recent case or describe the next intended use. Recover the input, action, receiver and consequence. “Improve evidence practice” becomes more useful when it means “the reviewer can find which source edition supports this claim before recommending release”.

Ask the intended performer what they currently do and where the proposed contribution would enter. Ask the receiver what makes the returned result useful or unusable. In a small self-directed use these can be the same person; do not invent separate roles merely to fill positions.

Compare the accounts. A sponsor's requested training can be retained when the missing contribution is learning. If the performer already knows what to do but cannot access the source, the immediate undertaking can instead repair access. If the supposed benefit is disputed, obtain the legitimate target decision or open a bounded inquiry through IS.2. Do not conceal that disagreement in a broad service label.

Keep only the detail that can change the first work or its promised result. A complete map of every stakeholder is unnecessary when the receiving use and applicable rights are already clear.

#### IS.1:4.2 - Distinguish the promised result from the help

Ask what would count as a useful completion. Separate the target result, the recipient's acquired ability, the continuing provision condition and any cultural continuation claim. A provider can correctly perform a task while the recipient remains unable to perform it; that can be a successful service and a failed learning promise at the same time.

Then name the contribution the helper will supply. For example:

| Requested help | Contribution to make explicit |
| --- | --- |
| “Coach us.” | Which action or judgement the coach helps the performer construct, when help is available and what remains the performer's work. |
| “Train the team.” | Which later work each intended learner must become able to perform, with what practice, feedback and assessment. |
| “Implement the tool.” | Which configuration, integration, access and receiving-use conditions the provider establishes, and which technical decisions need another owner. |
| “Help us introduce the practice.” | Which local conditions, explanation, participation, trial, observation or revision work the helper supplies. |
| “Make us independent.” | Which contributions the recipient must perform or arrange without this particular helper, and which continuing services remain legitimate dependencies. |

These are examples, not a fixed service taxonomy. One undertaking can combine several contributions. Explain their connection rather than promise the combined result merely by listing them.

Check the helper's actual capability and access for the decisive operation. Subject competence can be obtained from another provider. The undertaking remains conditional when that provider has not agreed or the needed evidence cannot be obtained.

#### IS.1:4.3 - Agree decisions and information use with the relevant parties

Identify the consequential decisions: selecting or changing the target, committing time or money, permitting access, accepting a result, changing ordinary work, reporting observations and stopping an attempt. Recover who can make each decision and what has actually been delegated.

A practical way to expose ambiguity is to ask: “If this attempt reveals that the proposed criterion is wrong, who can change it? If the participant cannot attend without missing current work, who can change the allocation? If the helper sees a harmful condition, where can that concern go?” Resolve the actual branch the undertaking may encounter.

Agree what information the helper receives, how it will be used and who will receive the result. Where support and evaluation coincide, explain that relationship before inviting candour. A useful arrangement may separate confidential problem-solving from a formal assessment, share only agreed task evidence, or use another qualified performer. Do not promise confidentiality that the actual rules or obligations cannot support.

For a voluntary participant, obtain real agreement rather than infer it from membership or a sponsor's enthusiasm. For an employee or contracted provider, distinguish the accepted obligation from optional participation. Ask the governing organizational or contractual practice to settle an unresolved right; persuasive conversation does not establish it.

#### IS.1:4.4 - Construct the smallest useful undertaking

Choose an initial result that reaches a receiver. “Hold a meeting” is an activity. “Recover the failed lookup, agree its permitted repair and let the reviewer use the repaired route on the next case” supplies a bounded contribution.

Name the minimum inputs and contributions needed for that result. Check that they can be supplied together at the relevant time. If they cannot, compare changing the first result, rescheduling, obtaining another supplier, using a qualified incumbent or stopping.

Agree the limit of the first attempt and the conditions that change it. A short engagement can end with a repaired condition. A discovery engagement can end with a qualified question and an available next decision. A larger implementation promise needs IS.3's actual strategy and provision. Keep the smaller undertaking honest when the larger benefit remains conditional.

Put the result where the parties can use it: an existing work note, service request or ordinary agreement can be sufficient. The required content is the receiving result, helper contribution, recipient contribution, effective decisions, protection and next change condition. A new form is not required.

#### IS.1:4.5 - Reopen the agreement when its working basis changes

When the target, provider, workload or recipient changes, ask which promise is affected. Preserve unaffected work. A new provider can change access and confidentiality without changing the target Method; a revised target can leave the same support relationship useful but require different expertise.

Return a workable undertaking, adequate direct help, a revised request or a precise missing decision. If the required contribution cannot be supplied on acceptable terms, say which result remains unavailable and what would permit reconsideration. Do not leave the recipient with an unchanged promise and an implicit obligation to invent the missing means.

### IS.1:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.1:5.1 - A workshop request becomes a bounded implementation undertaking

This constructed case concerns Harbor Design, where six analysts review technical evidence packages. A manager requests a two-hour workshop on checking source editions. The expressed purpose is fewer late corrections.

The support practitioner asks for one recently corrected package. The analyst shows that the correct source was known but unavailable through the shared lookup path. A second analyst describes avoiding an early challenge because it had previously been treated as a failure to meet the release date. These accounts do not yet establish how common either problem is, but they change the proposed first work.

The parties rewrite the request:

> For the next two eligible packages, make the current source available, make the challenge recipient and response clear, and observe whether the analysts can complete the check under those conditions. The support practitioner arranges and examines this attempt. The source owner repairs access. The manager authorizes the protected review interval. The technical lead retains the source-validity judgement and release authority.

That is different from cancelling all learning. If the attempt exposes a missing recognition or comparison operation, the support practitioner returns that actual later-work demand to a qualified learning provider. The agreement no longer treats attendance as its first success condition.

During the conversation, the manager asks for individual performance rankings from the same observations. The helper explains that the bounded attempt was designed to diagnose support conditions, not compare people. The manager either obtains a separately suitable assessment or retains the original purpose. The helper does not silently repurpose the records.

#### IS.1:5.2 - Make a review practice workable without acquiring the specialty

A self-employed translator is introducing a workflow that checks consequential technical terms before delivery. They can compare the source and translation but cannot decide several specialized meanings. The missing contribution is an obtainable qualified judgement at the review point, not a general lack of willingness to learn.

The translator and a specialist agree which questions need review, the source material, confidentiality conditions, response time and how an unresolved term returns before delivery. The specialist supplies the subject judgements; the translator uses them in the workflow and retains the responsibility for the translation. This makes the intended review practice possible without pretending that the translator has acquired the specialty.

The bounded support undertaking can end once those contributions can be obtained and used on the required project. If an adequate existing review service already supplies all of them, the translator can simply commission that result; no separate implementation undertaking or learning programme is needed. Repeated later work can justify reconsidering continued provision or personal acquisition, but does not decide that choice in advance.

#### IS.1:5.3 - A capable AI lacks authority for one required contribution

An AI assistant can compare descriptions, propose a support strategy and prepare permitted practice material. Its user asks it to arrange a colleague's participation. The assistant has no current authority to commit that colleague.

The useful return separates available preparation from the missing agreement. The assistant can prepare the request or identify a surviving alternative; it cannot count the colleague as supplied because the plan names them.

### IS.1:6 - Bias-Annotation

A provider can interpret every request as demand for its standard service. A sponsor can speak as though their own benefit represents every participant's interest. Counter both by recovering the receiving episode with those who perform and use the work, and by comparing the proposed help with the contribution the episode actually needs.

The examples emphasize professional services. In a personal use, the same questions can be settled by one person without a contract, meeting or role map.

### IS.1:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The first receiving use and useful result are recognizable without knowing the provider's service catalogue.
- The helper's contribution and the recipient's necessary contribution are explicit enough to act.
- Consequential decisions and information uses have an actual basis rather than a role-label assumption.
- The required contributions can be obtained together, or the promise retains the exact missing condition.
- A changed aim, provider, burden or protection condition can revise or end the undertaking.
- A delivered service is not reported as acquired mastery unless that distinct result was obtained.

### IS.1:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Sell the standard workshop before recovering the receiving work. | Inspect one intended use and compare what the workshop would actually supply. |
| Promise that the client will become independent without naming independent work. | State which contribution will transfer and which support remains. |
| Treat a sponsor's request as every participant's consent. | Recover the actual right or agreement for the contribution. |
| Gather candid failures while leaving their assessment use undisclosed. | Agree information use and resolve incompatible support/evaluation roles before collection. |
| Keep the broad promise after a necessary provider becomes unavailable. | Narrow the undertaking, obtain a substitute or stop the dependent result. |

### IS.1:9 - Consequences

The parties can begin useful help without pretending that every uncertainty is settled. A smaller undertaking can reveal whether the larger request is appropriate, and a sufficient ordinary service can close the work without a programme.

The cost is some early inquiry and explicit negotiation. Keep it proportionate: an already covered routine request should not acquire a new contracting exercise.

### IS.1:10 - Architectural Rationale

A support undertaking is the working connection between a recipient's need and the help actually obtainable. Starting from it prevents a service label from selecting the intervention in advance.

The Method preserves different relations rather than forcing them into one helper–client hierarchy. The same performer can hold several responsibilities; several providers can jointly supply the result. What matters is whether the contribution and decision are effective under the actual conditions.

### IS.1:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The practice question is how to start useful help when expectations and responsibilities differ. The implementation-support guide's co-design and role-tension material informs involving recipients and making support conditions explicit. Its human-service setting does not establish a universal contract model.

[LDR.4](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/Development%20DPF%20Suite/LEADERSHIP-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#ldr4---prepare-a-proposal-for-decision-and-first-use) supplies the developed route from discussion and objection through an effective decision and first useful work. Use that contribution when preparation for joint work is the actual problem. IS.1 connects help to the recipient's undertaking across settings, including a recipient who wants expert execution rather than personal acquisition.

A stable standard service with already agreed rights and inputs is a serious simpler alternative. Use it directly. Reopen the undertaking only when its actual promise or conditions no longer fit.

### IS.1:12 - Relations

IS.2 supplies a response when the receiving difficulty is not yet understood. IS.3 supplies the selected strategy and its provision. IS.4 returns what happened and what its observations change. IS.5 handles continuing dependence, transfer and closure.

MKT.11 is a supplier when a commercial service promise must become feasible; [OCE.6](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce64---solution) supplies effective organizational assignments and enabling relations; [LDR.4](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/Development%20DPF%20Suite/LEADERSHIP-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#ldr44---solution) supplies preparation for decision and first use. HCD supplies a human-development result when acquisition is promised. ME supplies the target or support Method when its operations or qualification remain open. These are direct professional returns, not automatic extra engagements.

### IS.1:End

## IS.2 - Find the Implementation Difficulty and Obtain the Next Useful Contribution

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Proposed
>
> **Primary working result:** a supported response to an implementation difficulty, with the contribution to obtain, its receiving action, consequential uncertainty and a workable return or stop.

### IS.2:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** intended practice cannot begin, later use fails or current help is ineffective, and different explanations would lead to different action. The target can be established or still being examined. What matters is that a concrete intended use and its difficulty can be investigated.

Begin with one episode: what result was needed, what was understood and attempted, what was available, and where the receiver could no longer continue. Ask the performer to show the difficult point. A missing operation, inaccessible input, unavailable time and an unsuitable target call for different contributions.

The governed subject is the implementation difficulty and the proposed response. An interview, observation or small comparison helps understand it; none is compulsory when a current qualified result already settles the action. The Method returns useful help or a request that can actually be fulfilled, not a taxonomy entry.

Use an adequate known response directly. When an unexpected result after an intervention needs explanation before adaptation, IS.4:4.5 supplies that developed comparison. When the question is already a human capability diagnosis or an organizational participation difficulty, use HCD.3 or OCE.10 rather than recreate their work.

### IS.2:2 - Problem

Labels such as resistance, low readiness and missing capability can conceal different failures. A person may know the Method but lack permission, begin correctly but lose access, or complete the prescribed work without receiving any useful result. A capable provider may answer too late to help.

An indiscriminate diagnostic exercise can make matters worse. Long inventories consume scarce participant time, produce categories without obtainable repairs and delay an already supported response. Conversely, an attractive quick fix can repeatedly move the difficulty elsewhere because it repairs a symptom without following the receiving work.

The problem is to distinguish only what changes the next useful action, and then obtain the contribution that action needs.

### IS.2:3 - Forces

| Force | Consequential tension |
| --- | --- |
| Several plausible explanations | The same missed action can have different causes and several can operate together. |
| Local knowledge and authority | Participants know important conditions, but their explanations do not alone settle empirical claims or commitments. |
| Timely help and uncertainty | A useful response may be justified before one cause is established. |
| Diagnostic depth and interruption | More detail can improve a decision or consume the window in which help matters. |
| Target integrity and local adaptation | A local repair can enable the intended practice or silently replace a decisive operation. |
| Adequate expertise and scarce supply | The needed answer may exist while no qualified provider is available at the required moment. |

### IS.2:4 - Solution

Reconstruct a receiving episode, compare explanations through their different practical consequences, and obtain the smallest contribution that changes the next use. Retain uncertainty at the place where it limits the decision.

#### IS.2:4.1 - Follow the episode through the intended result

Recover the actual occasion, input, interpretation, action, output and receiver. Ask what the participant believed they were doing and what was expected to happen next. Compare that account with permitted task material: a case, input, tool response, work product or receiving decision.

An effective question is “Show me where you could no longer continue”. Follow the answer both ways. Backward, identify the missing condition or earlier contribution. Forward, ask what result the next person or system needs and why the current result is insufficient. B.1.5.EW helps when the action's contribution to the encompassing work is unclear.

For example, finding a source edition can be part of judging support for a claim, which is part of preparing a release recommendation. Correct retrieval is not sufficient if the retrieved source cannot support the claim. Equally, debating the recommendation cannot repair an unavailable source.

Include a successful contrast when it can distinguish the failure. The same performer using a different source route may reveal an access problem; success with an expert present may instead reveal the expert's missing contribution. Preserve that assistance in the account rather than treating both episodes as independent performance.

If the intended event has not occurred, use an explicit prospective case and keep its status clear. A walkthrough can identify a contradiction or unavailable input without proving that later work will succeed.

#### IS.2:4.2 - Construct the action-changing alternatives

State explanations as working claims about the episode. “The lookup route cannot retrieve this edition” is more useful than “technology barrier”. Explain what would differ if the claim were true and which response it would warrant.

| Possible explanation | Useful contrast when it can change action | Different response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The needed source or tool cannot be used. | Can the qualified performer proceed when effective access is supplied? | Repair access or select another qualified obtaining way. |
| An operation or judgement is missing. | What contribution does an expert make that is absent from the ordinary attempt? | Obtain that contribution, explain the Method or develop the intended performer's capability. |
| The target does not fit the receiving case. | Which relied-on condition differs, and does the known alternative address it? | Reconsider the target or qualify a variant through the subject practice and ME. |
| Correct work conflicts with current commitments. | What occupies the necessary interval, and who can change the allocation? | Obtain a feasible coexistence or workload decision. |
| Correct participation brings adverse consequences. | What actually follows the action, compared with the stated rule? | Obtain an effective change to those consequences; instruction alone is insufficient. |
| A completed action returns no wanted value. | What did the receiver need, and what remained unresolved after completion? | Repair the contribution or reconsider the desired action. |

The table is illustrative. Form another explanation when the case requires it. Several can survive: an unclear instruction and unavailable time can both matter. A determinant inventory can prompt a question, but its label supplies neither an operation nor a causal conclusion.

Do not diagnose a person from silence or non-use. Ask for their account where it can be obtained appropriately. Refusal can reveal an unacceptable burden, a valid objection or a legitimate choice. When a clinical or specialist judgement is actually needed, return it to a qualified professional instead of extending implementation advice into that domain.

#### IS.2:4.3 - Decide whether another observation is worth obtaining

First ask whether existing grounds already support a response. If all live explanations support repairing the same unavailable input, the current decision may not need a unique cause. If one explanation requires training and another requires changing sanctions, a discriminating observation can prevent substantial wasted work.

Describe the possible answer before commissioning the inquiry. Name how it would change the action, who can obtain it, the conditions required and when it can arrive. Compare its whole burden with an adequate current response, a small reversible start, narrowing the promise or stopping. C.11.DUA supplies that comparison.

A low-burden contrast can be useful: try the same lookup through an authorized working route; inspect one task with and without the existing aid; ask the receiver to show what made the result unusable. Preserve the relevant conditions and the limitation of the contrast. A successful assisted attempt can show what the combined performance obtained; it leaves independent capability and the assistance's isolated effect unsettled.

When the proposed explanation and causal reliance require more than the local contrast can establish, use IS.4:4.5, C.28 and the appropriate RMP design. Do not promise an effect estimate from a convenient before/after count. Conversely, unresolved causality does not prevent every useful local repair.

#### IS.2:4.4 - Obtain the response through its actual supplier

Convert the chosen response into a contribution someone can supply. State the case and receiving result, the condition to be changed or question to be answered, the needed timing and the qualification on which use depends.

For a missing judgement, ask the expert to demonstrate the relevant comparison on the actual kind of case, including the condition that changes the answer. For a learning need, give the learning provider the later-work action and the observed difficulty rather than request generic training. For an allocation conflict, show the simultaneous commitments and the decision required from their owners.

Choose the professional route from the question:

- HCD.3 and the appropriate HCD development Method distinguish and address a human learning target.
- OCE.10 supplies organizational participation diagnosis and intervention; OCE.11 supplies coexistence with continuing service.
- ME.10 supplies a method-use support configuration. ME's fit, variant and construction Methods handle a target that needs qualification or change.
- CB.12 supplies a wanted repeated participation action; CB.14 supplies a shared repertoire contribution when those are the actual problems.
- RMP receives a research question when existing knowledge does not supply an adequate answer and its research conditions hold.

These routes can supply the whole bounded response. Use the returned result, not another version of the same inquiry. A source or provider name alone is insufficient: recover what it returns, the conditions of use and the next recipient.

#### IS.2:4.5 - Make the returned contribution usable in the next episode

Inspect the returned result against the question that required it. A repaired access claim needs an actually usable route. An explanation needs to settle the disputed interpretation or preserve the exact unresolved question. An agreed allocation needs to be effective when the work occurs.

Arrange the receiving action. Place the corrected reference at the point of use, obtain the permitted next case, connect the expert response to the decision or make the protected interval available. IS.3 constructs a larger strategy only when several contributions or alternatives still require it.

Observe the next use only as far as it can change the response or the claimed result. Keep failed entry, missing observation and justified non-use distinct. If the response did not supply its promised contribution, repair that connection or return it to the provider. If it did supply the contribution but the larger result still failed, continue from the next consequential difficulty.

#### IS.2:4.6 - Reconsider the target when support is the wrong repair

A support investigation can reveal that the target is no longer appropriate, that a new possible result is more valuable, or that a needed Method does not exist. Preserve that discovery. Do not make the support service's original scope a reason to retain an unsuitable target.

Return the changed target question to its decision owner with the evidence, useful existing results and receiving consequence. ME and the subject practice can construct or qualify a way. DOCA can help compare development directions; B.5.PI/C.40.CD can help form a question when the initial interpretation fails. Implementation support can continue independent useful work while the dependent branch remains open or stopped.

The completed result states the supported response, what it made possible, what remains uncertain and which next action is available. A precise stop is a useful return when no permissible contribution can meet the current promise.

### IS.2:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.2:5.1 - Distinguish access, capability and consequences

Continue the constructed Harbor Design case. The helper reconstructs three episodes:

| Episode | What happened | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A source check with a working direct link | The analyst identified the relevant revision and explained its effect. | This episode supports use of the known operation with that access; it does not prove every analyst's general capability. |
| A check through the usual shared portal | The needed revision was unavailable, and the analyst used an older one. | The portal condition is a concrete repair target. |
| An early challenge with the correct revision | The manager deferred the challenge to preserve the release date; the analyst received an adverse response for delay. | Correct detection is insufficient while the receiving consequence defeats its use. |

The helper does not average these into “low readiness”. The source owner repairs the portal; the responsible manager and technical lead settle how a legitimate concern reaches the release decision and how its time is provided. OCE.10 supplies that organizational response.

Suppose the next supported episode succeeds with one analyst, while a second still cannot compare the two revisions. That different finding opens a bounded HCD question about the second analyst's operation. It does not retrospectively turn the portal failure into a learning defect.

If the manager refuses the needed change, the helper reports that the promised early-challenge practice remains unsupported. The source repair can still be useful. Another workshop cannot be represented as closure of the unresolved organizational condition.

#### IS.2:5.2 - An independent carrier needs a different input, not a community

A freelance repairer obtains a diagnostic procedure from a publisher. The new instrument exports a different identifier format, and the procedure's lookup fails. The repairer is not part of a practice community.

The support practitioner asks for an allowed sample and follows it through the lookup. A subject expert can map the new identifier unambiguously; the existing instruction assumed the older format. The immediate response is a qualified input adapter and an explanation of its boundary. No peer group, habit programme or personal learning diagnosis is necessary.

If two new identifiers map to the same older code, the mapping is no longer sufficient. That changed condition returns to the target Method and subject expert. The helper must not choose arbitrarily merely to keep the implementation moving.

#### IS.2:5.3 - Preserve uncertainty when the response does not depend on it

A service's help link has moved, and users cannot reach the support page. Records do not distinguish whether each user forgot the old route or followed a broken bookmark. Restoring the correct route and communicating it is supported under both explanations.

The local response can proceed without interviewing every user. If a later decision concerns reducing reminders, the memory and cue question becomes material then. The earlier limited diagnosis was not defective merely because it left that unrelated question unresolved.

### IS.2:6 - Bias-Annotation

The helper may favor explanations that match their own service: trainers see learning gaps, tool providers see configuration gaps and managers see motivation. Use the episode, recipient account and actual contribution to challenge that preference.

A successful contrast can also mislead when it changes several conditions. Keep expert assistance, case difficulty and effective access visible; their combined result does not isolate one cause.

### IS.2:7 - Conformance Checklist

- A concrete intended or actual receiving use grounds the difficulty.
- Live explanations differ in the action or warranted conclusion they imply.
- An adequate current response can end diagnosis without another observation.
- A selected inquiry has a possible answer that changes the decision and warrants its burden.
- The response names an obtainable professional contribution and receiving action.
- Capability, organizational conditions, target fitness and support are not treated as interchangeable.
- The target can be reconsidered when help would preserve the wrong practice.

### IS.2:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Score a barrier inventory and call the highest score the cause. | Recover the case and compare explanations through their different expected observations and actions. |
| Diagnose a missing skill whenever a person does not perform. | Inspect access, task conditions, consequences and the actual operation. |
| Ask for more evidence without saying what it changes. | State the possible answer and next action before selecting the inquiry. |
| Forward a problem to “an expert” without a usable request. | Supply the case, needed result, timing and receiving condition. |
| Repeat an adequate OCE or HCD diagnosis under a new label. | Consume its current result and act on the remaining question. |

### IS.2:9 - Consequences

The next intervention can address the contribution that actually limits use. Some requests become smaller repairs; others correctly return to a target decision or a professional supplier.

The Method requires access to enough task evidence and participant understanding. Where those are unavailable, its conclusion remains limited. A narrow useful response can still be possible without a complete causal account.

### IS.2:10 - Architectural Rationale

The unit of diagnosis is a receiving episode because implementation difficulties arise at connections among an occasion, action, means and result. A label about an individual or organization can hide those connections.

The Method separates explanation from response selection. More than one explanation can support the same response, while one generic symptom can require very different actions. That distinction makes the work useful under uncertainty and limits unnecessary inquiry.

### IS.2:11 - SoTA-Echoing

OCE.10 supplies a developed comparator: reconstruct a consequential gap, distinguish explanations, obtain the actual intervention and examine receiving use and burden. Its sanctions and workload distinctions are retained. HCD.3 supplies the human diagnosis when that is the question. IS.4:4.5 supplies the more demanding explanatory comparison after an implementation result.

The pattern uses those whole Methods where their subject fits. Its distinct connection is from an implementation-help request across different receiving settings to an obtainable next contribution. A generic determinant catalogue remains a prompt for inquiry, not the diagnosis or prescription.

The simpler alternative is a known adequate response. Retaining it avoids spending participant time on uncertainty that cannot change the current action. Reopen when another episode defeats its premise or a stronger claim makes the unresolved distinction material.

### IS.2:12 - Relations

IS.1 supplies the intended benefit and help boundary. IS.3 consumes the response when a support strategy remains to be constructed. IS.4 returns failed entry, adaptations and later-work observations. IS.5 reopens diagnosis when transfer or continuation loses a necessary contribution.

B.1.5.EW explains the constituent contribution in its encompassing work. C.11.DUA governs the worth of further inquiry. C.28 and RMP supply stronger causal or research work. The supplying HCD, OCE, CB and ME results remain professional contributions; this diagnosis does not certify them by naming them.

### IS.2:End

## IS.3 - Construct and Adapt a Support Strategy with Its Recipients

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Proposed
>
> **Primary working result:** an explained support strategy with obtainable contributions, an intelligible local use, decision-relevant observations and a bounded start or precise missing condition.

### IS.3:1 - Problem frame

**Use this when** the useful contribution is sufficiently understood but several forms, combinations or intensities of help remain plausible. Also use it when a current strategy no longer fits the recipient, target, available means or support demand.

Start from the contribution the next receiving use needs. Compare the existing adequate way with one materially different way of supplying it. Explain what a performer would actually do, what conditions make that useful, what the next receiver obtains and who can provide the whole contribution at the required time.

The governed subject is the support strategy: the way of helping establish, use or revise the target practice. Its description, schedule and provided arrangement are different things. A strategy can include teaching, expert performance, facilitation, condition repair or a change of target, but naming these activities does not supply their Methods.

Use an already sufficient strategy and arrangement directly. A routine access repair needs no portfolio of alternatives. When the primary unresolved question is technical validity, an organization's architecture or the recipient's development direction, obtain that professional result before treating its answer as a support-design choice.

### IS.3:2 - Problem

A selected strategy can be plausible in the abstract and unworkable at the moment of use. Training can occur without the inputs needed for later practice. An expert can be available after the receiving decision. A common service can save preparation while creating an excessive queue. A local adaptation can improve convenience by deleting the very operation the target needs.

A second problem is confusing the target with its introduction. A failed briefing need not invalidate the target Method. A different technical criterion is not merely better coaching. Without that distinction, teams repeat all support after a local failure or silently change the target while reporting fidelity.

The practical problem is to construct complete support alternatives, choose one at acceptable whole burden, make its local actions correspond to the intended contribution and preserve a route for revising the correct object.

### IS.3:3 - Forces

| Force | Consequential tension |
| --- | --- |
| Subject integrity and local fit | The decisive operation must remain, while inputs, timing or permissible forms can vary. |
| Personal acquisition and provided results | Learning can improve future autonomy; expert provision can meet an immediate need without that acquisition. |
| Common support and local knowledge | Reuse can save effort, while context reconstruction and queues can consume the saving. |
| Support intensity and burden | Too little help can defeat use; too much can consume capacity or obscure the intended performer's contribution. |
| Current service and change | The same qualified time may be needed for ordinary obligations and for development or implementation. |
| Comparability and different promises | Cheap alternatives may supply different results or move work to a less visible participant. |
| Action and inquiry | The strategy must support useful work while obtaining only the observations that can change its next use. |

### IS.3:4 - Solution

Construct alternatives from the contribution to be obtained, not from a catalogue of intervention names. Work through the local operation with its recipients, make its support obtainable and compare whole consequences before selecting the bounded attempt.

#### IS.3:4.1 - Recover the contribution and preserve a sufficient incumbent

Use the current IS.1 undertaking and IS.2 response when available. Recover the target practice, receiving result, intended performer, supported starting conditions, protection and remaining uncertainty. A provider may already have supplied an adequate strategy or arrangement; inspect its actual result before reconstructing it.

Ask what must change for the next use and what can remain. If only a reference has moved, repair the route. If the undertaking requires a person's acquisition of a missing operation, obtain the appropriate demonstration, practice, feedback and development arrangement. If it requires the operation's result without personal acquisition, compare qualified provision. A useful demonstration or explanation can support either choice without creating a learning obligation. If the target is unsuitable, another delivery of the same support is not an answer.

Choose a bounded receiving result for comparison. The alternatives need not have identical long-term effects, but their differences must remain visible. If one supplies a task result and another promises human mastery, do not rank them as interchangeable services without the recipient's actual preference.

#### IS.3:4.2 - Generate alternatives by changing the obtaining way

Start with the competent current continuation, including its real support and burden. Then vary the operative relation that could change the result. Useful changes include:

- make an existing ability usable by repairing access, time, equipment or authority;
- obtain the decisive contribution from a qualified expert, service or AI;
- develop the intended performer's ability through an appropriate HCD arrangement;
- make a tacit operation recoverable through an explanation, demonstration or usable tool;
- conduct joint problem solving while knowledge is being developed;
- choose a different target or reduce the promised use when the needed contribution is unavailable.

For each serious alternative, follow one case from input to useful result. Describe the critical action rather than its service label. “The coach asks the reviewer to compare the two source editions, identify the changed claim and explain its effect before offering feedback” specifies a contribution. “Provide coaching” does not.

Keep the actual subject Method intact. A learning designer chooses practice and feedback but does not invent an absent engineering criterion. An implementation facilitator can organize discussion while the participants' qualified judgement supplies the subject answer. When the Method itself is missing, ME and the relevant profession supply its construction; the support strategy records that dependency honestly.

Do not manufacture alternatives whose outcomes cannot change the choice. One adequate way can suffice. More alternatives are useful when they reveal different result, dependence, resource or protection conditions.

#### IS.3:4.3 - Explain why each contribution could help

Connect the intended benefit to the action that could obtain it and the conditions on which it depends. A rehearsal may help a person recognize a stop condition; an expert inspecting the final result may instead supply recognition directly. Both can improve the immediate result, but only one is intended to develop the person's own operation.

Ask what should be observable if the contribution is delivered and useful. Ask what would differ if it is unavailable, not performed, misunderstood or inappropriate to this context. Keep that explanation modest when its causal basis is uncertain. Existing professional knowledge can justify a bounded attempt without supplying a causal effect estimate.

Use IS.4:4.5 when the proposed adaptation requires the fuller delivery–mechanism–context comparison. That Method connects rival explanations, attainable inquiry and return to the affected result. Do not create a separate obligatory evaluation programme here.

Distinguish the support strategy from the target. Changing the time of a briefing or the availability of help can leave the target unchanged. Replacing its decision criterion, accepting a different input interpretation or removing a decisive comparison can change it. Preserve the proposed function and the evidence for its actual fulfillment separately; unchanged intended function does not prove unchanged Method identity.

#### IS.3:4.4 - Construct the local way with the people who will use it

Bring a representative input to the intended performer and receiver. Work through the critical operation together. Ask the performer to show where the explanation, input, tool or assistance enters. Ask the receiver to show what they must obtain to continue.

Use the conversation to change the construction. If an instruction requires information the performer cannot access, obtain that input, select a compatible operation or return the target condition to its owner. If the result arrives too late, change the timing or obtaining way. If the participant's objection identifies a harmful consequence, compare a different arrangement rather than relabel the objection as resistance.

Make the first-use instruction concrete enough to act: how the relevant situation is recognized, what input is needed, what the performer does with it, how they inspect the result and when they seek help, return or stop. Put that instruction where the action occurs. ME.8 supplies a fuller description-construction Method when the reusable explanation is the missing contribution.

Agree only the local variation that the target's qualification permits. A simplified input display may preserve the needed comparison; a default answer that hides the relevant difference may not. When the equivalence is unresolved, retain a proposed variant and obtain subject/ME judgement. A convenient tool configuration cannot settle that question by itself.

#### IS.3:4.5 - Make performance and support jointly obtainable

For the selected serious alternatives, identify who performs each decisive operation and what they need at that moment. Check actual capability, usable means, access, timing and permission. A role title or available calendar does not establish these conditions.

Use ME.6 when the alternatives differ in their configuration of performance and support: for example, shared specialist provision versus provision within each branch, or expert execution versus supported local performance. It supplies the comparison of the relevant structures, moved burden, simultaneous demand and actual dependencies, together with a selected configuration and its conditions. Use that result directly; a selected proposal still needs its effective relations. C.32.MWA clarifies the structures when needed, while ME.10 supplies particular method-use support. Obtain OCE's contributions when assignments, authority, workload or the organization itself must change. Continue here with the helping operations and recipient-facing explanation that the selected arrangement still needs.

Check simultaneous demand on scarce means. Count shared preparation once when its result serves several recipients; case-specific interpretation and help remain recurring work. Two independent demands cannot each consume the same expert hour. A joint review may instead supply current service and coaching in one contribution: count that shared time once, retain setup, recipient effort and additional instructional work, and qualify the service and learning results separately. A qualified AI or provider can replace a contribution when the receiving conditions permit it, not merely because it is cheaper.

Obtain the real commitments. A client must provide promised access; an employer must allocate the employee's time; a volunteer must agree the contribution; a provider must accept the required response window. Where continuing work competes with change, OCE.11 supplies a bounded coexistence and recovery arrangement. Use it once, including its existing results.

If a required condition remains unavailable, compare a smaller slice, another provider, staggered work, another strategy or a revised promise. Preserve a hard protection or authorization condition as a condition, not a disadvantage that a larger benefit can buy away.

#### IS.3:4.6 - Compare complete alternatives at the decision's scale

Use an adequate ME.6 comparison directly. When a support choice remains, use the same current baseline, receiving use and relevant horizon. Include preparation, participant time, provider time, tools, coordination, inquiry, retained support, transition and displaced work where they can change the choice. Separate total effort from the demand on a particular scarce contributor.

First remove alternatives that cannot meet the required result or conditions. Then compare the remaining consequential differences: time to usable result, future dependence, human mastery where promised, error exposure, response delay, effort and adaptability. C.11.CRC supplies finite comparison; C.11 supplies the legitimate choice. Do not add unlike ordinal ratings into an unexplained total.

When results differ, make the trade-off explicit. A temporary expert service can be preferable for immediate continuity while a learning arrangement remains necessary for the promised later independence. Combine them only if the additional work and transition are actually supplied. The combination is not automatically the best of both.

Select further inquiry only when its attainable answer could reverse the strategy or permitted conclusion enough to justify its full burden. A bounded first attempt can be the comparison when its protection, recovery and receiving conditions are available. Retain alternatives with an honest limit when no useful comparison is obtainable.

#### IS.3:4.7 - Provide the bounded start and its return

Explain the selected strategy through the receiving case: what becomes possible, how each contribution helps, who supplies it and what changes the next action. Obtain the effective permissions, inputs and commitments before the work that depends on them begins.

Select the observations that can change the attempt. They might concern actual use, fit, burden, the intended learner's contribution or a failed handover. Each has its own subject. IS.4 keeps introduction work, later work, target adaptation, support adaptation and outside results separate.

Return the explained strategy and provided conditions, or the exact unsupported condition and surviving useful work. A strategy description can be complete while the attempt remains blocked by unavailable access. On an observed failure, return to the affected target, contribution or arrangement rather than repeat the entire strategy by default.

### IS.3:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.3:5.1 - Compare complete first-week alternatives

This constructed extension of Harbor Design uses invented quantities to show the comparison. Six analysts are available. After the local diagnosis, suppose four have adequate current capability for the selected case family and two need a supported attempt with feedback. The intended first-week contribution is eight eligible package checks, with the two additional analysts beginning to acquire the missing comparison operation. In the current continuation, the four capable analysts can provide those eight checks at 0.20 hours each, using 1.6 hours already available for checking. The two analysts' acquisition requires additional work.

All three serious alternatives require the same access repair, taking 1.5 technical hours, and a 0.5-hour managerial contribution that makes the review interval and receiving decision effective. These two hours are counted once in each alternative.

| First-week alternative | Scarce qualified provider time | Other direct time, including ordinary checking | Complete effort for the selected work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full six-person workshop, followed by brief individual follow-up | 2 hours preparation + 2 hours teaching + 6 × 0.25 hours follow-up = 5.5 hours | 6 × 2 hours attendance + 6 × 0.25 hours follow-up participation + 2 common hours + 1.6 hours checking = 17.1 hours | 22.6 hours |
| Targeted workplace rehearsal for the two analysts | 1 hour preparation + 2 × 0.75 hours coached rehearsal = 2.5 hours | 2 × 0.75 hours participation + 2 common hours + 1.6 hours checking = 5.1 hours | 7.6 hours |
| Qualified expert supplies the checking contribution for eight cases | 8 × 0.20 hours = 1.6 hours | 2 common hours; the expert replaces the analysts' checking contribution | 3.6 hours |

These totals include the eight checks and the implementation work in the constructed alternatives. Other unchanged package work remains outside this comparison. Relative to the 1.6-hour checking continuation, the additional effort is 21, 6 and 2 hours respectively. Shared workshop time is counted separately for the instructor and each participant because they are different people occupying that interval; replaced checking work is not counted twice. No effect size or learning success is assumed from the arithmetic.

Suppose only three qualified support hours are available after existing commitments. The workshop is unavailable in that window. Targeted rehearsal fits the time limit; that inequality alone does not prove a workable schedule, but the two agreed 45-minute intervals and the preparation time provide one.

Expert execution supplies the immediate result at lower first-week effort. It does not supply the intended analysts' acquired comparison operation. If the recipient requires only the eight immediate checks, that service can be selected. If developing the two analysts is part of the authorized promise, the service is not an equivalent cheaper answer.

The parties select targeted rehearsal for the bounded acquisition attempt and retain the existing qualified review condition until later performance supports another allocation. The actual rehearsal Method and assessment come from HCD and the subject provider. IS.4 examines what happened; the table itself proves neither learning nor the target Method's effectiveness.

#### IS.3:5.2 - Build the local instruction rather than paste a strategy name

The target requires identifying whether a source revision changes the support for a claim. The initial implementation instruction says:

> Use the new review method before release.

A walkthrough shows that the analyst cannot find the source pair and that the technical lead receives only an unqualified approval flag. The local instruction is changed:

> When a package cites a revised source, open the cited edition and the proposed current edition. Locate the passage supporting the claim. State whether the change affects that support, with the relevant passage. Send an unresolved or changed-support case to the technical lead before release recommendation. If either edition is unavailable, return the missing source instead of marking the claim checked.

The source owner supplies the two-edition route. The technical lead agrees the receiving response and timing. The learning provider uses representative pairs to teach the missing comparison where needed. The instruction is a local explanation of the selected operation, not evidence that anyone has performed it.

Now change the condition: a source is a continuously updated database with no recoverable historical edition. The instruction's input condition fails. The helper returns the record-preservation and claim-support question to the source/subject owner. Inventing a timestamp from memory would not preserve the target operation.

#### IS.3:5.3 - Strong AI changes the obtaining way

Suppose a qualified AI service can perform the source comparison under the actual access and protection conditions, and its current evidence supports the required case family. One alternative lets it supply the comparison while the technical lead retains the receiving decision. Another uses it to prepare contrasts for the analysts' practice.

The first can reduce demand for human comparison work without developing analyst mastery. The second can support learning only if the analysts perform the intended operation and obtain suitable feedback. The strategy choice follows the wanted result; it does not preserve a human coach merely because the earlier arrangement had one.

If the service loses access to a protected source, the alternative becomes unavailable for that case. A general capability claim about the AI does not restore the missing input.

### IS.3:6 - Bias-Annotation

The strategy designer can mistake a familiar delivery format for an operative Method, or count the provider's reduced cost while moving work to recipients. Recover the actual case and all consequential participants' effort.

The arithmetic case favors a small initial slice because that is the stated receiving use. A later promise to qualify all six analysts would require its own complete work, support and evidence. Do not reuse the small-case total for that larger result.

### IS.3:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Each serious alternative explains the contribution and operation that could obtain the receiving result.
- An adequate current strategy can be retained without forced novelty.
- Target changes and changes to its introduction or support are distinguishable.
- Local instructions preserve the decisive operation and its input and receiving conditions.
- Qualified people, tools, access, time and permissions can be supplied together.
- Comparison includes the complete additional work and keeps different promised results visible.
- The selected attempt has an actual bounded start, protected conditions and decision-bearing observations.
- An unavailable subject method or provider remains an explicit gap rather than a named box in a plan.

### IS.3:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Select “training” or “facilitation” as though the name explains the intervention. | Describe the actual operation, expected contribution and conditions. |
| Make a local adaptation by deleting the difficult target step. | Obtain a qualified variant or repair the missing means. |
| Count shared preparation once but omit recurring local support. | Follow each actual receiving use and count its demand. |
| Compare expert execution with learning as equivalent outputs. | Preserve the different result and ask the legitimate recipient to choose. |
| Promise the same scarce hour to incompatible service and coaching demands, or count one joint contribution twice. | Obtain a feasible allocation. Where one joint review serves both uses, count its time once and qualify each result separately; retain the additional preparation, recipient and instructional effort. |
| Use a workflow configuration as proof that the Method is enacted. | Inspect the actual performance and its operative conditions through IS.4. |

### IS.3:9 - Consequences

The strategy becomes performable and revisable: its intended contribution, local work and actual provision can be examined separately. A smaller targeted arrangement or continuing expert service can displace an unnecessary broad programme.

The construction can expose a subject or organization question that support design cannot answer. Its useful result may therefore be an exact required contribution or a narrowed promise rather than a start.

### IS.3:10 - Architectural Rationale

Implementation support combines interventions with different mechanisms and responsibilities. Describing the constituent operations and their receiving relations prevents a bundle of activities from standing in for a Method.

The same account distinguishes Method choice from provision. A good operation can be unavailable; a well-provided service can perform the wrong operation. Comparing both together explains why neither a strategy list nor a staffing chart is sufficient.

### IS.3:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The implementation-support guide's tailoring contribution motivates choosing the kind and intensity of help from the local need rather than a fixed schedule. This pattern develops that choice through actual operations, whole effort and receiving use.

ME.6 supplies the developed comparison of performance and provision configurations, including moved burden and simultaneous demands. ME.10 supplies particular method-use support. OCE.11 supplies coexistence with continuing service. IS.4:4.5 supplies interpretation of delivery, mechanism, adaptation and context. Their sufficient results are used directly; another round of equivalent design or inquiry adds no implementation benefit.

A ready target-specific strategy is the serious simpler alternative. Retain it when its actual conditions fit. The new construction is warranted when a changed input, provider, workload, target or promised result makes that fit consequentially uncertain. The examples show conditional arithmetic and operations, not an empirical superiority claim.

## IS.4 - Introduce a Method into Practice and Decide What to Keep or Change

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Proposed
>
> **Primary working result:** a bounded decision about what to keep or change after introducing a Method or examining a proposed way in real practice. Explain what was attempted, what was performed, what later use showed, and how that evidence changes the target, its introduction or the conditions of use. Preserve uncertainty where no decision is yet supported, with the next useful observation or stop.

### IS.4:0 - Use This When

Use this pattern when a Method or a sufficiently interpretable proposal can be tried in real practice and the next decision depends on what its introduction and later use actually reveal. The target can remain a proposal: preserve that status and distinguish it from the independently admitted Methods actually enacted in the attempt. Start with the receiving result and the decision the observations could change.

For an ordinary first use, explain what the team tried to establish, what it did, what was later observed or could not begin, and what to keep, revise, branch, replace or stop. Separate a change to the target way from a change to how people obtain, learn or use it. The [Harbor application](#isapplication13---use-is4-to-separate-what-happened-from-what-is-claimed) shows this small account leading directly to a receiving action.

Add the precise subjects, relations and evidence needed by the claims on which that decision relies. Sections 4.1–4.2 keep those conditions available: a capability claim needs its holder and grounds; a change needs its actual authority; a causal explanation needs C.28. A description, plan, completed introduction and successful later use remain different results. Introduction can finish while access, fitness or later performance still fails.

Use an adequate current capability without prescribing development. Bring in E.23.CDI or the relevant domain capability-development Method when the attempt requires that change; an absent result returns to that supplier or prevents the dependent attempt. IS.4 does not itself produce or certify capability development. Use ME.17 when the primary question concerns transmission, recognition, selection, retention, loss or another cultural result across a population.

#### IS.4:0.1 - Working Distinctions

| Question | Distinction that changes the next action |
| --- | --- |
| What should become possible? | The target practice and its intended receiving result differ from the way used to introduce it. Changing a lesson, access condition or support arrangement need not change the target Method. |
| What is already established? | An admitted Method differs from a candidate account of a proposed way. Keep the proposal's status; describing or trying it does not admit or enact a candidate whole. |
| What happened? | A description or plan, performed introduction and later performed work are separate. An attempt can finish without establishing the intended condition. |
| Whose capability is needed? | Qualified supply can make a result available without the recipient acquiring the supplier's operation. A capability or capability-change claim needs its own grounds. |
| What does an observation show? | Acceptance, fit, feasibility, enactment, burden, reach, continuation and an outside result answer different questions. A favourable observation in one does not settle the others or establish its cause. |
| What changes next? | Decide which target, description, strategy or condition to keep or revise, and return it to the responsible practice. A revision decision supplies no new authority to act. |

Use the detailed positions in §4.2 when the decision needs their precise kind, identity or evidence; they are not a requirement to populate every position for every introduction.

### IS.4:1 - Problem Frame

A new procedure arrives with instruction, expert assistance, a tool, changed duties, management attention and a different review occasion. Later results are credited to “the Method”, while failure is blamed on resistance or ability. Either attribution can hide the contribution that actually needs changing.

The practitioner needs to decide what to do next: retain a useful way, repair its explanation or provision, develop a needed capability, adapt the introduction, replace the target or stop. Follow the intended change through the attempt and later receiving work. Keep observations and explanations separate until the required grounds are available; otherwise an access failure can provoke redundant training, or a completed workshop can be mistaken for usable practice.

### IS.4:2 - Problem

A rollout plan can be reported as adoption. Training can be reported as capability. Tool availability can be reported as use. A manager's sponsorship can be reported as permission or authority. Later Work can be merged with the introduction Work, and a favourable domain result can be assigned to the Method without an alternative or contribution basis.

When several positions are compressed into one intervention label, the team cannot revise locally. It either repeats the whole programme or rejects it, even when the defect lies in one description, support relation, capability condition, assignment, permission, authority boundary, or outside-result assumption.

### IS.4:3 - Forces

| Force | Tension |
| --- | --- |

### IS.4:4 - Solution

Prepare and perform a bounded introduction, observe its later use, and decide what to keep or change. Distinguish the target from how it is introduced and supplied. Begin with the small working account in §0; make each relied-on claim precise through the applicable steps below. Use C.28 only for causal reliance, and return a correction or alternative to the particular maintained result or receiving work it changes.

#### IS.4:4.3 - What Changes in Practice

Teams stop reporting that a Method was “rolled out” and start distinguishing the Method-related target, the introduction strategy and its status, its description, any separate WorkPlan, performed introduction Work, later Work, named relied-on relations, and separate observations. An introduction attempt can be completed without establishing the intended conditions. A favourable outside result can remain useful without filling every observation position or becoming causal proof.

Revision becomes local. A stale target description, unsuitable target variant, failed support relation, missing capability result, weak introduction strategy, stale strategy description, unworkable WorkPlan, or local-use adaptation can be reconsidered through its own maintained claim or edition without repeating or rejecting the entire programme.

#### IS.4:4.5 - Explain a changed result before selecting an adaptation

Use this continuation when an unexpected result, changed setting or proposed adaptation leaves materially different explanations that would change the next application. Existing observations and a supported local correction may already settle the decision; a new study is not required merely because some causal uncertainty remains.

1. **Recover how the proposed contribution is expected to work.** Connect the needed result to the function each relevant contribution serves, the concrete actions or form that supply it, and the conditions under which the proposed mechanism could operate. Read the target and the introduction strategy separately. For example, a rehearsal may be intended to develop recognition of a stop condition, whereas an expert checking the completed result may supply that recognition directly. Use C.28.CM and the relevant subject practice to construct or challenge this explanation. Keep assumptions and unknown functions visible. Preserving a function does not by itself preserve Method identity.

2. **Derive observations that could distinguish the live explanations.** Compare what should be seen if the relevant contribution was not delivered, was delivered but did not activate the proposed mechanism, operated differently in this context, or was supplemented by another contribution. Several explanations may hold together. Link observations of actual actions, the proposed intermediate change and the outside result for the same cases and relevant times. Include failed entry and cases where the result differs. A procedure-completion count alone cannot decide among these explanations. Assess fidelity only against an interpretable baseline and only when that comparison can change the decision; unknown essential functions or an intentionally flexible strategy may support a narrower account of what was done instead.

3. **Select an attainable comparison.** First ask whether existing evidence distinguishes enough for the receiving action. If a further inquiry is worthwhile, pass the competing explanations, expected observations, case differences, timing, available access and whole burden to RMP.2. Its selected design and limits, operationalized through RMP.3, become inputs to the ME.11 trial or other inquiry. Check separately whether the proposed application can be performed and whether the evaluation can obtain an interpretable answer. Specify which changes the design permits: holding a configuration stable and studying an adapting configuration answer different questions. If no useful comparison is attainable, return the supported course or unresolved alternatives with the limitation that matters now.

4. **Combine observations through the explanation.** Use the selected methods to recover what was delivered, how participants used or responded to it, the pertinent context and the resulting benefit, harm or burden. Where broad records show a pattern, selected observations or interviews can investigate the difference; where a particular account suggests a mechanism, look for its expected consequences in the relevant records. Repeated observation can reveal a changed contribution or context. Keep the same cases, periods and measurement meanings recoverable when combining results. Separate an observation, a participant's explanation and the investigator's inference; use C.28 for any resulting causal reliance.

5. **Make each adaptation interpretable.** Compare the actual change with its prior target or introduction-strategy baseline. Recover the affected action or component, proposed function, reason, decision-maker and authorization, conditions, decision time and actual implementation time when their difference matters. Keep coupled changes together when their contributions cannot be separated. Determine what supports a claim that the function was retained, changed or remains unknown. An adaptation description supplies neither that functional conclusion nor an effect estimate. Return changed reusable operations or conditions to ME.15 even if the intended function stays the same.

6. **Return what the evidence changes.** ME.13 receives the conditions and configuration actually examined; ME.14 receives comparable consequences, support demand and displaced work; ME.15 receives the semantic change with separately qualified function and evidence; IS.4 receives the basis to retain, refine, branch, replace or stop the target, its introduction, or their support. A favourable result despite poor delivery may support investigating another contribution; it neither confirms the proposed mechanism nor makes all useful work a failure. A new supported explanation or useful alternative may warrant development without contradicting the earlier bounded account. Revisit uses whose relied-on premises change through their governing patterns. Use ME.24 when the change affects a source-coverage or reconstruction claim.

The result is an explanation sufficient for the named next action, with its evidence and unresolved alternatives. It can be a bounded continuation, a changed introduction strategy, a proposed target variant, a useful refinement of the explanation, or a stop. Additional detail is selected for the decision it can change.

#### IS.4:5.2 - Correct outputs with an undelivered learning mechanism

In this constructed example, a team introduces a report-review Method requiring the reviewer to trace each measurement to the equipment identifier and check its unit before recommending release. The proposed introduction strategy combines a lesson with individual rehearsal. Its working explanation is that rehearsal will enable newcomers to recognize identifier and unit mismatches independently. Release still requires a separately qualified reviewer; the introduction attempt has no authority to remove that safeguard.

Records show that the lesson occurred, but individual rehearsal was repeatedly cancelled. Reports released during the attempt have the required corrections. Observation of review meetings shows that experienced colleagues identified the mismatches and newcomers then corrected them. The attendance records and corrected reports therefore concern different contributions: neither establishes independent newcomer capability. The proposed learning mechanism remains unsupported, while the experienced reviewers' actual corrections are useful observations.

The method engineer connects delivery records, the sequence of detection and correction, current capability evidence and report outcomes. This supports continuing the existing safeguarded review under its established conditions. It also identifies a rival explanation worth considering: supported joint review may supply the immediate checking function while creating a different opportunity for learning. Whether newcomers learn from that participation remains an open question.

Suppose a receiving decision now concerns assigning newcomers independent reviews next month. A feasible comparison uses archived reports with known mismatches, including an unfamiliar unit convention, and records each newcomer's answer before help, the help actually given and the final correction. The inquiry designer must justify case coverage, comparison, timing, assessment and attainable burden; merely scheduling the exercise is not a result. If the protected inquiry window is unavailable, independent assignment stays unsupported and the existing review can continue.

After such an authorized comparison, suppose the observed records show that newcomers detect familiar identifier mismatches unaided but require expert help for the unfamiliar unit convention. Return that differentiated result to the capability owner and to ME.13; it supports neither a blanket failure nor unrestricted transfer. ME.14 includes the expert's time. IS.4 can develop a proposed introduction variant using joint review and later independent rehearsal. ME.15 determines whether its changed reusable operations constitute a variant; retaining the intended checking and learning functions does not settle that identity question.

The adaptation account records when the joint-review change was chosen and when it actually operated. If rehearsal and coaching change together, the observed benefit belongs to that examined arrangement until a useful comparison separates their contributions. The original lesson remains a delivered event. Its proposed mechanism is reconsidered on the new evidence; the history is not rewritten as if the later strategy had been used from the start.

### IS.4:9 - Consequences

Method introductions become observable interventions. Teams can learn from use, non-use, adaptation, and outside consequences while keeping the target, introduction strategy and its status, description, any separate WorkPlan, support, capability, authority, identified organizational Systems or selected structures, Work, and domain changes distinct.

The cost is a more explicit baseline and observation design. Some programmes end with bounded association and residual causal uncertainty, and a capability gap can stop introduction even when training was delivered.

### IS.4:10 - Rationale

An intervention can change several subjects, relations, and descriptions at once. Learning requires each claimed change to retain its own subject, governor, baseline, evidence, and receiving use. Otherwise the intervention label becomes a substitute for both Work and explanation.

Separating the Method-related target from the introduction strategy, its status and description, any separate WorkPlan, capability development, and cultural continuation prevents IS.4 from becoming an adoption lifecycle. It remains the practice for introducing one bounded Method-related change, observing later Work and consequences, and using the resulting evidence to correct, refine or develop the particular maintained account or next application.

### IS.4:End

## IS.5 - Sustain, Transfer, Extend or End Implementation Support

> **Type:** Method pattern
> **Status:** Proposed
>
> **Primary working result:** Continued practice has a feasible support arrangement, or support ends with its remaining work, consequences and receiving responsibilities settled.

### IS.5:0 - Use This When

Use this pattern when an introduction has produced enough experience to decide what help should remain; when a helper, sponsor, tool or source is changing; when another setting wants to use the approach; or when the supported practice is no longer wanted or sufficiently useful.

Begin with one ordinary use that must remain possible and one actual dependency that may change. Find what currently enables that use, including help that is easy to overlook because an expert quietly supplies it. Then decide whether to retain, reduce, replace, transfer, extend or end that contribution. The useful result can be a continuing purchased service. Independence is not an obligatory maturity stage.

Do not run a sustainability project merely because a bounded attempt has finished. If continuing use and its support are already adequately provided, use that arrangement and its ordinary correction route. If no continued use is promised, settle the attempt's actual remaining obligations and stop.

### IS.5:1 - Problem Frame

Implementation support changes while the target practice continues. Temporary experts leave, licences expire, workloads grow, evidence changes, new recipients arrive and an organization transfers responsibility. A practice that worked under unusual attention may become unavailable under ordinary conditions.

Several different continuations are possible. The recipients may want to acquire a missing operation, continue obtaining it from someone else, retain occasional specialist help, substitute a capable AI service or stop using the practice. A community can transmit it among its participants; independent practitioners can also continue using it without forming a communicating community.

The receiving question is therefore how wanted practice remains obtainable, by whom and under which conditions. It is broader than keeping the original support team occupied, but narrower than determining all future development.

### IS.5:2 - Problem

How can the practitioner change or end implementation support without mistaking a successful supported episode for sustainable ordinary use, while avoiding compulsory independence, indefinite assistance and expansion into settings where the result has not been qualified?

### IS.5:3 - Forces

- **Visible performance and hidden rescue.** A recipient can appear independent while another performer resolves every difficult case, repairs inputs or absorbs delay.
- **Continuity and dependence.** A continuing supplier may be the best obtaining way; reliance becomes a problem when the promised contribution cannot actually be supplied.
- **Economy and capability.** Acquiring a capability can reduce some later dependencies but consumes present learning opportunities, feedback and qualified support.
- **Local success and new settings.** An adequate local result can justify reuse without establishing the same result for another population, workflow or consequence.
- **Ending help and ending practice.** These are different changes with different affected parties and remaining obligations.
- **Cultural continuation and control.** Independent carriers can preserve a practice without a service office; participation does not confer authority over their work.
- **Current provision and further development.** Ordinary delivery must continue when wanted, while worthwhile harder problems may demand new target Methods or capabilities.

### IS.5:4 - Solution

#### IS.5:4.1 - Recover the practice that should remain obtainable

Start from the current undertaking and what actual use has shown. State the continuing result, its recipients, important case families and conditions. Separate three questions:

1. Should the target practice continue for this use?
2. What contribution is needed for it to remain obtainable?
3. Who should perform or provide that contribution?

A decision to keep the practice does not answer who should provide it. A decision to end a grant does not establish that its dependent work can safely stop. Use the already sufficient result of IS.4, including its non-use and adverse results; do not reopen the entire introduction merely because the decision now concerns continuation.

Follow an ordinary case and one consequential variation. Ask what happens when the usual expert is absent, an input is ambiguous, a source changes or demand overlaps with another obligation. Select variations because they can expose a particular dependency, not to demonstrate every conceivable failure.

Recover actual support from the work: who supplies the difficult judgement, restores access, selects the current method variant, detects an obsolete instruction, answers a late question and authorizes an exception? A support contribution can be distributed among a performer, an AI service, a source maintainer and a manager. There need not be a support office or one person who owns them all.

#### IS.5:4.2 - Choose the continuation that obtains the wanted result

For each dependency that matters to continued use, compare the actual alternatives. Use a sufficient current arrangement directly. Otherwise consider:

| Continuation | What must be obtained | What would make the choice inappropriate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retain the present help | A dependable contribution under ordinary demand and agreed terms. | The provider cannot continue, the contribution no longer matters, or its burden exceeds its benefit. |
| Reduce or make help conditional | Recipients can handle the ordinary cases, recognize the named exception and reach support when it occurs. | Apparent independence still relies on unobserved rescue or exceptions cannot be recognized. |
| Transfer provision | Another provider can obtain the required inputs, perform the contribution and be received by the continuing work. | Only files or a role title have moved. |
| Develop recipient capability | Suitable practice, feedback and evidence of performing the required operation. | Personal acquisition is not wanted, or the needed opportunities and time cannot be supplied. |
| Replace the obtaining way | A different qualified source, tool, service or Method can supply the result. | Its limits or access exclude the actual cases. |
| Extend use to another setting | The target and its support fit that setting, or an appropriate adaptation can be qualified. | Local results are treated as a universal adoption instruction. |
| End support or practice | Remaining work and affected dependencies have a legitimate disposition. | A date or funding decision is being used as evidence that dependent work is complete. |

The table distinguishes choices; it does not prescribe a progression. Continuing specialist provision can remain preferable to local acquisition. A highly capable AI service can replace substantial human help where its actual performance and conditions justify the replacement. Conversely, a service that supplies correct answers can leave the recipients unable to notice when it is outside its qualified use.

Compare alternatives against the actual promise. Do not reject a service because it fails to teach when teaching was never needed. Do not call it equivalent to capability development when the recipients must later act without it. IS.3 supplies the connected strategy construction and full work comparison when the continuation requires a new arrangement.

#### IS.5:4.3 - Make transfer a performed receiving connection

Transfer only the contribution whose provider or conditions must change. Recover what the successor needs to recognize, obtain, do, judge and return. Include important exceptions, unfinished cases and the basis of the current qualification. A folder of materials is useful when it actually supplies those needs; its delivery alone is not a transfer of practice.

Have the proposed successor perform or supply a representative contribution under the intended conditions, using an already adequate demonstration where available. Let the real receiver use that result. For a source-review service, the question is not whether the successor can repeat the old expert's explanation, but whether it can obtain the relevant source, make the required comparison and return an interpretable judgement in time.

Identify any continuing dependence on the old provider. A planned overlap can be appropriate; indefinite hidden rescue cannot count as completed transfer. If the successor needs learning, use HCD for the specific acquisition. If it needs permission, staffing, workload protection or access, obtain those conditions through the responsible organization or provider. Training cannot confer authority or create an absent historical record.

Agree what happens to late questions from earlier work. Keep the previous provider's obligation only where it still exists and can be performed; otherwise obtain an actual successor or a legitimate disposition. OCE.11 supplies coexistence and exit from temporary arrangements when this is part of organizational change. Use its full result rather than reconstructing an informal second transfer process.

#### IS.5:4.4 - Qualify ordinary use and maintain the contributions that can change

Use existing evidence when it answers the continuation question under the intended conditions. Obtain another observation only when it can change a decision: for example, whether the recipient recognizes the escalation condition without prompting, or whether a service meets the actual demand when its introductory subsidy ends.

Observe the result and the support consumed. Repeated correct performance with constant expert repair supports the joint arrangement, not independent recipient capability. A single case under ordinary conditions may expose a missing input without estimating a failure rate. Keep the claim at the strength of the evidence.

Provide a correction route proportionate to the consequential change. The performer should know where to return a missing input, questionable answer or obsolete instruction; the responsible supplier must be able to receive that return. A low-risk personal practice may need only a maintained source and an available specialist. A service whose wrong result can harm others may require stronger monitoring, response and interruption arrangements from its governing professional practice.

Different contributions need different maintenance. A target-specific explanation can become obsolete when the target Method changes. A local access instruction can fail after a software change. A support strategy can become unsuitable when recipient preparation or workload changes. A source of research evidence can change the claim about why the practice works. Return each change to the owner of that contribution instead of treating every problem as a need for another learning event.

Where the organization needs a continuing capability to arrange such help, distinguish that capability from the supported subject capability. Its performers must be able to recognize implementation difficulties, obtain qualified contributions and arrange actual support in later cases. Use HCD when acquisition is wanted. Participation in the present introduction does not by itself establish that broader capability.

#### IS.5:4.5 - Extend through changed conditions, not through a success label

A request to extend use starts with the new receiving work. Recover what is already the same and which differences can change applicability or provision. Differences can concern inputs, case distribution, authority, cost of error, available expertise, language, demand or access. Examine the differences that bear on the target operation or the support contribution; an arbitrary catalogue of site characteristics adds no assurance.

Reuse what remains sufficient. A shared explanation, common expert service or reusable exercise can serve several settings. Preserve a local contribution when it depends on the subject matter, available inputs or receiving judgement. For example, one service can maintain the comparison tool, while each technical practice retains its own criterion for whether a changed source defeats a claim.

A new location does not automatically need a full development programme. An equivalent use with adequate existing support may proceed directly. When a difference breaks a relied-on condition, obtain the needed adaptation or return to target selection. IS.2 and IS.3 supply those contributions; IS.4 supplies a bounded introduction when that uncertainty warrants one.

Cultural continuation can require transmission beyond a service contract. Use C.36.RP to distinguish the needed carriers and transmission relations. CB.14 supplies a joint repertoire undertaking when such work is wanted; its maintained result can also serve independent users. If a communicating community is itself needed, community practice supplies its relationships and maintenance. Do not invent a community requirement for people who can obtain and use the practice independently.

#### IS.5:4.6 - End the contribution without abandoning dependent work

State exactly what ends: the target use, a temporary support service, a provider's participation, a subsidized arrangement or a particular strategy. Identify actual unfinished work and recipients who relied on the contribution. Obtain their disposition through the relevant authority and professional practice.

For a completed low-risk trial with no continuing promise, this may be a short explanation and closure of its remaining questions. For a continuing service, it may require transfer, a fallback or a staged reduction. For a target practice found unfit, it may require stopping its use and restoring a qualified alternative. The implementation-support practitioner does not infer the safe stopping procedure from the fact that support is ending.

Do not keep collecting reports after they can no longer change a legitimate decision. Preserve materials that a continuing receiver needs; do not retain unnecessary personal information merely to document a past programme. Resolve access and custody under the applicable agreement.

End when the promised contribution and actual remaining obligations are complete, transferred or legitimately withdrawn. The old provider's availability can then stop without being silently assumed by the new arrangement. If a necessary receiver or authority is absent, return that unresolved condition plainly; a closing meeting cannot supply it.

#### IS.5:4.7 - Return the next development question to its proper practice

Continued use can reveal a worthwhile harder problem: the old Method reaches its limit, recipients can now undertake more demanding work, or better ways of developing and supporting practice become possible. State the new desired result and the current limit. Return that question to development, subject practice, research or method engineering as appropriate.

Do not automatically label every next challenge an implementation barrier. If a sufficiently useful target Method is unavailable, the next work is to obtain or develop it. If the wanted future result changes, reopen the opportunity and undertaking. Implementation support can help a resulting candidate enter practice without becoming the whole practice of development.

Likewise, successful use need not create an obligation to keep changing. A stable adequate service can remain ordinary work. The question is whether further development is worthwhile and feasible, not whether the implementation programme needs a new cohort to justify its existence.

### IS.5:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### IS.5:5.1 - Transfer a real contribution while keeping current delivery possible

This case is constructed to demonstrate the operations; it is not a reported organizational outcome.

Harbor Design has introduced source-revision comparison. Four analysts already perform the ordinary comparison adequately; two are still acquiring it. A temporary specialist has been resolving unusual references while the technical lead receives release recommendations. The specialist's contract ends in four weeks.

The first proposal is to close support after a final webinar. Following actual use changes the decision: analysts do not know how to resolve an inaccessible superseded source, and the specialist has answered three such questions privately. The ordinary practice therefore depends on source recovery as well as comparison capability.

The source owner can maintain the access route. A subject specialist is available for unusual source interpretation, but only on named days. The technical lead accepts holding the affected release recommendation until a qualified response arrives; urgent work must use a separately qualified route. The successor is not assigned the previous provider's entire role merely because it is available.

A representative transfer case includes an unavailable edition. The analyst returns the missing input, the source owner supplies the recoverable edition, the subject specialist interprets the consequential ambiguity, and the technical lead uses that result. This demonstrates the receiving connection for that case. It does not establish a failure rate or qualification for all technical subjects.

The two learners continue their qualified practice under HCD. The other four do not attend a redundant general course. The temporary provider transfers two unfinished queries to the agreed successors with their actual receiving uses, then ends its participation. If the source owner had no access to the archive, the transfer would remain incomplete despite the webinar and copied materials.

#### IS.5:5.2 - Retain supply by choice, and distinguish it from learning

An independent translator has established the terminology-qualified review workflow in IS.1:5.2. A specialist supplies the rare subject judgements at its review point. When the project-support engagement ends, the translator checks that this contribution remains affordable, dependable, available before delivery and compatible with confidentiality. If it does, retain ordinary provision without creating another support programme. Continued access, intelligible limits and a response to disputed judgements sustain the practice; becoming that specialist is not part of the promise.

Now suppose the translator begins accepting work under conditions where the service cannot be reached. Personal acquisition or another available supplier becomes relevant. The previous service results may help construct learning cases, but they do not prove that the translator can make the required judgement unaided. HCD supplies acquisition and its evidence; IS.3 compares the feasible obtaining ways.

If a qualified AI service becomes available, the same questions apply to its actual cases, inputs, protection and receiving use. Its availability changes the alternatives; the phrase “AI support” does not decide the continuation.

#### IS.5:5.3 - Continue among independent carriers

Several independent repair practitioners use a published diagnostic method. They have no common employer and do not communicate as a community. The source publisher maintains the explanation and a correction channel; each practitioner supplies its own tools and follows the method's applicability limits.

The support question is whether those contributions continue to make the practice available. A maintained correction, discoverable source and actual local access can be sufficient. If a new equipment family breaks an assumption, the publisher needs a qualified method revision or an explicit limit; a new discussion group would not repair the missing diagnostic operation.

A later proposal for peer learning can be useful if its intended contribution warrants the work. It is an additional obtaining way, not evidence that the earlier independent practice was impossible.

### IS.5:6 - Bias-Annotation

Helpers can prefer continuation because it preserves their role, or prefer a clean exit because it improves programme statistics. Return to wanted practice, actual dependencies and remaining obligations.

Recipients can reasonably prefer dependable provision to mastery of a rare operation. Treating independence as the only successful outcome undervalues that choice. Conversely, do not call a service independent merely because the visible performer is local.

### IS.5:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The continuing result and the support contribution that may change are distinguishable.
- Hidden rescue and important exception handling are considered where they affect continued use.
- Retain, reduce, transfer, develop, replace, extend and end are alternatives rather than mandatory stages.
- The selected continuation can obtain its required contribution under ordinary conditions.
- A transfer includes a usable receiving connection and the disposition of unfinished work.
- Evidence supports the actual claim about joint performance, recipient capability or provider substitution.
- Extension reuses sufficient contributions and reopens conditions whose change matters.
- Ending support does not silently abandon continuing work or dictate an unqualified stopping procedure.
- New development questions return to their proper practice rather than becoming endless implementation work.

### IS.5:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Declare sustainability because supported performance was good. | Identify ordinary provision and observe the dependency that is changing. |
| Require every recipient to become self-sufficient. | Compare wanted capability with dependable external supply. |
| Transfer a folder and assume the role has transferred. | Establish that the successor can perform and the receiver can use the contribution. |
| Scale a successful local strategy unchanged. | Recover the new receiving work and consequential differences. |
| Close on a calendar date while late cases still depend on the old provider. | Transfer, complete or legitimately withdraw the actual obligation. |
| Create a community to fix an unavailable technical operation. | Obtain the missing subject contribution first. |
| Turn the next harder development problem into another adoption campaign. | Return the changed target or missing Method to development and subject practice. |

### IS.5:9 - Consequences

Practice can continue through an arrangement whose real dependencies are understood. Support can become smaller, change provider, remain as a service or end without making independence the universal success criterion.

This Method can also justify stopping an arrangement that had looked successful. That is useful when its ordinary conditions cannot be supplied or the target no longer serves the receiving work. It does not guarantee that another arrangement exists.

### IS.5:10 - Architectural Rationale

The continuing practice, its performer capability, its supporting services and its cultural carriers can change independently. Keeping these objects distinct permits a precise transfer or ending instead of forcing every change through one institution.

Continuing support requires work, inputs and performers. When provision ends, settle the commitments to receivers and any unfinished or planned work separately. Ending must therefore respond to the receiving practice and remaining obligations, rather than follow automatically from a completed implementation plan. New development remains a different question even when the same participants supply both contributions.

### IS.5:11 - SoTA-Echoing

The implementation-support guide treats capability-building and leadership as substantive concerns. This pattern preserves the question of continuing capability while allowing qualified supply and chosen dependence where those better serve the work.

OCE.11 supplies temporary coexistence and exit; HCD.15 supplies reconsideration of human development as work and support change; CB.14 supplies a joint repertoire undertaking whose results can reach independent users; C.36.RP distinguishes cultural continuation beyond one organization. The simpler alternative is to retain an already adequate ordinary arrangement. Additional continuity construction is useful only when a consequential dependency changes or remains unresolved.

### IS.5:12 - Relations

IS.4 supplies the bounded use result and the claims it can support. IS.1 reopens the undertaking when purpose, authority or promised help changes. IS.2 locates a new difficulty; IS.3 constructs a changed support strategy.

ME supplies target-method revision and support configuration. OCE supplies organizational changes and the coexistence of ordinary work with temporary change work. HCD supplies desired human acquisition. CB.14 supplies joint repertoire development, and C.36.RP distinguishes cultural continuation; development and research practices receive changed aims and unavailable subject knowledge. None is replaced by a sustainability label.

### IS.5:End

# A Connected Implementation-Support Application

## IS.Application:1 - From a workshop request to ordinary provision

This is a constructed application, using the Harbor Design assumptions from the patterns. It demonstrates how contributions connect; it is not a reported intervention or a mandatory sequence. A reader with an adequate existing diagnosis or support arrangement can enter later, and an unresolved earlier condition can require a return.

### IS.Application:1.1 - Change the promise before changing the people

Six analysts prepare technical release recommendations. A sponsor asks for a workshop on comparing source editions. IS.1 recovers the wanted receiving result: relevant changes in source support should reach the technical lead before a release recommendation becomes unusable.

The first undertaking concerns the next two eligible packages. The source owner must make the relevant editions available; the manager must provide a protected review interval; the technical lead must receive a consequential difference. The helper can arrange and examine this work, but cannot replace the lead's technical judgement or the manager's allocation decision.

IS.2 follows the contrasting episodes described in IS.2:5.1. An access failure and an adverse response to a correct challenge need organizational and source contributions. The remaining difficulty of two analysts concerns the comparison operation. Their learning need survives the access repair; the access failure does not become evidence that all six require instruction.

### IS.Application:1.2 - Choose the help and supply its conditions

The parties extend the bounded undertaking to a first week containing eight eligible checks and an initial acquisition attempt for the two analysts. IS.3 compares the three constructed alternatives in IS.3:5.1. The full workshop requires 5.5 scarce provider hours against three available; targeted rehearsal requires 2.5. Expert execution requires 1.6 but supplies a different result.

The selected continuation uses the four already capable analysts for the eight ordinary checks, targeted rehearsal for the two learners and the repaired source route. A wider choice between shared specialist provision and branch-local provision would use ME.6; its sufficient comparison would be consumed here. No extra office follows from selecting rehearsal.

The qualified learning provider supplies representative contrasts and feedback through HCD. The technical lead retains the ordinary receiving decision. The two agreed rehearsal intervals and preparation time make the selected first-week work feasible under these constructed conditions. The calculation is a provision comparison, not evidence that rehearsal develops capability.

### IS.Application:1.3 - Use IS.4 to separate what happened from what is claimed

Suppose the next observations are also constructed as follows. The source route is repaired and the rehearsal occurs. An analyst later detects a consequential change and returns it to the technical lead, who withholds the affected release recommendation pending clarification. Another case cannot begin because the historical edition is absent. One learner completes a contrast only after the helper identifies the decisive passage.

IS.4's first plain account can now distinguish the observations:

| What was established or attempted | Later observation | Supported continuation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source access and a route for a consequential challenge | One supported check reached the technical lead and changed that case's continuation. | Retain the usable route for that case family, with its source conditions. |
| Local instruction requiring two recoverable editions | One case lacked the historical edition. | Return the missing input and the applicability question to the source and subject owners. Do not report a failed personal capability test. |
| A rehearsal intended to develop comparison | The helper supplied the decisive recognition in one contrast. | Preserve the helped-performance observation. Continue or revise the HCD arrangement; do not infer independent mastery. |

The repaired route, performed rehearsal, actual later check and intended learner capability are different subjects. The target comparison can remain suitable while the local access or support arrangement changes. If the group wants to explain why the receiving result improved, IS.4:4.5 develops the rival explanations and attainable inquiry; the sequence above does not isolate the cause.

An unavailable historical record can make the target unsuitable for that source family. The professional return is to obtain an appropriate variant or change the target use. The helper does not preserve implementation progress by accepting an invented record.

### IS.Application:1.4 - Continue the practice without preserving every temporary service

After further qualified use, suppose the temporary specialist is due to leave. IS.5 discovers that unusual source recovery and interpretation still depend on that specialist. It obtains the successor contribution and an actual receiving connection as described in IS.5:5.1, while the two learners continue their own development.

The same coordinator can arrange this temporary change and later organize ordinary programmes or service. Development-project membership, performed work and organizational assignment remain distinct; shared people and means must remain available for their simultaneous commitments.

If later work exposes a more demanding problem that the current comparison Method cannot answer, the development question reopens. It can require research or a new subject Method rather than more implementation support. If the present result remains adequate, its ordinary provision can continue. Success does not decide whether further development is worthwhile.

## IS.Application:End

## IS.Reference:2 - Keep source contribution and evidence reach visible

The [Implementation Support Practitioner Competencies](https://implementationpractice.org/resources/implementation-support-practitioner-competencies/) resource page provides access to the [Practice Guide to Supporting Implementation](https://cippro.wpengine.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/ISP-Practice-Guide-v1-10.27.22.pdf), dated December 2020. Its helping perspective informs IS.1–3 and IS.5. Transfer beyond human services remains a question for the receiving use.

The detailed implementation, adaptation, process-evaluation and causal comparator sources are named in IS.4:11. Their adopted questions and transfer limits stay beside the Method that uses them. A comparator supplies a useful question or developed treatment; it does not establish the causal effectiveness, universal transfer or organizational feasibility of the local support strategy.

Harbor Design, the translator, the independent repairers and the other explicitly constructed cases teach operations and conditional comparisons. They are not observations of completed interventions. Evidence of actual use must identify the target, support, conditions, performed work, observed result and claim for which it is used. A locally useful result can be retained without upgrading it into a general effectiveness claim.
