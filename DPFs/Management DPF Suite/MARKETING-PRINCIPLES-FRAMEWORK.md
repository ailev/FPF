# Marketing Principles Framework Readme

## Practical entries

Bring the question you are actually trying to answer. These entries are selected examples, not a catalogue or a coverage boundary. If none fits, search the Table of Contents or enter a pattern directly. Each body explains its situation, construction, limits, worked cases and source returns. You can ask an assisting agent: “Explain this and give me your comments in ordinary language, without framework jargon.”

### MKT-ENTRY-QUESTION - Find out enough to make the next decision

- **Situation:** A team disagrees about why customers decline an offer, and another round of interviews has been proposed.
- **Question:** What answer could change our next action, and how can we obtain it?
- **First useful result or blocker:** A bounded inquiry tied to that action, or an existing qualified answer sufficient to stop investigating.
- **Start with:** [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision); use MKT.1 when relevant customer differences remain unclear.
- **Stop or return:** Stop when the decision has a sufficient answer. Return a missing permission, unavailable service or disputed objective to its owner.

### MKT-ENTRY-CHOICE - Make one customer's next choice workable

- **Situation:** A customer understands a suitable offer but cannot tell what accepting it requires.
- **Question:** Can this person make an informed choice and carry out the agreed next action?
- **First useful result or blocker:** An understandable, feasible choice with its terms, preparation, help and next step, or the exact condition preventing it.
- **Start with:** [MKT.4](#mkt4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible); use MKT.9 if suitability of the variant is still unsettled. For work spanning several people and encounters, follow [the account application](#mktapplication4---app-mkt-account---carry-a-customers-changing-question-from-first-contact-into-useful-work).
- **Stop or return:** A justified refusal or deferral can finish the choice. Return an unavailable contribution to MKT.11 before inviting reliance on it.

### MKT-ENTRY-MEDIA - Keep the promise intact when an intermediary changes it

- **Situation:** A placement combines or generates material, offers incomplete reporting, or cannot be stopped at the level the offer needs.
- **Question:** Can the permitted expressions and actual controls preserve this offer and its receiving work?
- **First useful result or blocker:** A supported placement and response arrangement, or the control, information or provision that requires another route.
- **Start with:** [The intermediary application](#mktapplication2---app-mkt-media---keep-a-supportable-offer-through-a-changing-intermediary), using MKT.3 and MKT.11.
- **Stop or return:** Revise the affected family, route or promise when its conditions fail; preserve existing customer obligations.

### MKT-ENTRY-PROGRAMME - Share preparation while keeping promises feasible

- **Situation:** Several organisations could use related services, but shared preparation competes with local exceptions and existing work.
- **Question:** Which whole programme can the team support for the intended result and period?
- **First useful result or blocker:** A supported combination of common work and separate continuations, or the capacity, permission or capability that defeats it.
- **Start with:** [MKT.14](#mkt14---develop-and-change-a-programme-for-selected-customer-organisations), using MKT.1/12, MKT.2/6, MKT.11 and MKT.13 where their results are missing; follow [the B2B application](#mktapplication3---app-mkt-organisations---turn-a-programme-comparison-into-actual-customer-work).
- **Stop or return:** Remove or change the affected opportunity when a condition fails. Recompare the whole programme when the changed opportunity alters common preparation or scarce support.

### MKT-ENTRY-LEARNING - Offer learning while preserving the learner's purpose

- **Situation:** A learning provider, an employer and a voluntary community can help the same person, but seek different results.
- **Question:** What can each party responsibly offer and expect from this arrangement?
- **First useful result or blocker:** Distinct learning, participation and commercial arrangements with usable connections, or a conflict requiring changed terms or separate activity.
- **Start with:** [The learning and community application](#mktapplication5---app-mkt-learning---connect-a-learning-offer-with-personal-development-and-voluntary-exchange), MKT.6/2/4, HCD.28/2 and the selected Community Management methods.
- **Stop or return:** A purchase does not establish learning. Revisit the affected promise when help, personal participation, community terms or whole provider capacity changes.

## How to use the language

Use a body when its practical problem is present. MKT.12 can end an inquiry without a new campaign; MKT.4 can support a single choice without a customer programme. Read the Architectural Reference when deciding how several contributions fit. The applications develop five connected uses and return to the separate suppliers of engineering, organisation, operations, learning and community work.

The patterns describe ways of working. A description, a proposed arrangement and work actually performed are different things. Obtain the people, tools, access, time and receiving decisions that the selected way requires. An AI assistant may help recover evidence, compare alternatives and prepare wording; the relevant person or organisation still has to supply any permission, commitment or human contribution on which the result depends.

# Marketing Principles Framework Architectural Reference

## MKT.Reference:1 - Problem frame - Useful offers in actual customer situations

Marketing here is a language of methods for understanding customers and markets, constructing and communicating offers, helping people choose and obtain useful results, and changing the commercial arrangements that connect these activities. It is for practitioners who can investigate their domain, work with customers and obtain engineering, operating or economic contributions when needed. A founder, service team, learning provider or business-to-business programme owner can enter with one bounded problem.

The broader field also contains brand strategy, media buying, price research, distribution systems, public policy and many specialised practices. This edition develops the branch concerned with customer situations, useful offers and their feasible commercial continuation. It does not promise a complete method for every marketing occupation. Its governed objects include a customer question, offer, choice, experience concept, relationship, commercial model and selected-customer programme. The regulated moves are investigating, constructing, agreeing, supplying, evaluating and revising those objects.

Use the language when misunderstanding a customer's work, overextending evidence, disconnecting a promise from provision or confusing one kind of result with another could change the next decision. A known suitable offer with a workable ordinary purchase may need only its existing operating method. A personal learning problem, a community mandate, an engineering design or a liquidity decision keeps its own professional owner even when commercial work depends on it.

## MKT.Reference:2 - Problem and forces - Connect usefulness, choice and provision

A company can attract attention without helping a customer act. It can deliver a requested feature without improving the result that matters to that customer. It can also construct a useful offer that its own organisation cannot sustain. Each local success leaves a different unresolved question. Treating them all as a conversion problem invites more persuasion where changed provision or an agreed refusal is needed.

Several forces shape the answer. Customers differ in their work, circumstances, authority and available help, while preparation is costly and some of it can be shared. A clear promise aids choice but can create reliance before provision is established. A timely decision needs a bounded inquiry, while a causal or population claim may require more evidence than that decision does. Current service, new exploration and learning often compete for the same people. A common programme must respect those simultaneous demands as well as its total cost.

The language keeps these differences usable. It supports an adequate small answer, construction when no suitable option exists, and a larger programme when several interdependent opportunities warrant it. Customer usefulness, learning, voluntary participation, provider solvency and commercial return require distinct questions and evidence.

## MKT.Reference:3 - Solution - Enter through the result that is missing

Start by naming the decision and what its recipient needs to act. Recover relevant facts and existing results before commissioning more work. The following connections explain the repertoire; the Table of Contents remains the pattern index.

| Missing contribution | Construction and usable return | Receiving use and consequential return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A qualified customer or market answer | MKT.12 selects inquiry by a decision; MKT.1 distinguishes the problem, roles and circumstances. | MKT.2, MKT.4 or MKT.14 receives an answer with its scope. Uncertainty about a different population remains open. |
| An offer worth choosing | MKT.6 helps shape the useful result; MKT.2 constructs and tests an offer and its positioning. | MKT.3 can invite a meaningful response and MKT.4 can support choice. Missing provision returns to MKT.9/11 and its engineering owner. |
| A connection between intended experience and actual activity | MKT.5 develops an experience concept; MKT.8 constructs a map for a specific decision. | MKT.11 obtains a change in real work. If the explanation of an observed connection could change the action, MKT.12 obtains the needed evidence. |
| A fitting variant and supported continuation | MKT.9 compares options for a customer; MKT.11 aligns promises and contributions. | MKT.4 receives feasible terms and transition; MKT.6 supports useful use; MKT.7 handles continuation, restoration or ending. |
| Grounds to change or expand commercial work | MKT.10 evaluates a specified intervention; MKT.13 examines ordinary repeatability and deployment limits. | The responsible programme or deployment owner uses bounded conclusions together with supplied capacity and dated financial conditions. |
| Work across selected customer organisations | MKT.14 constructs common preparation and distinct continuations for qualified opportunities. | Customer teams receive their own next actions; providers receive actual obligations; the programme owner receives a whole comparison and revision conditions. |

These connections are conditional. MKT.6 need not invent a new goal when the customer's useful result is already clear. MKT.12 can obtain an existing answer instead of launching a study. A customer journey map is useful when relationships across activities matter to a decision; it is unnecessary paperwork for an already understood local action. A commercial programme is worthwhile when its common preparation and coordination change what the whole team can achieve.

When a contribution is absent, ask for its actual result. “Can an appropriately prepared worker and accessible room support this service at that time?” can return a feasible arrangement or a precise blocker. Keep a conditional offer conditional until its consequential contributions are supplied.

## MKT.Reference:4 - Composition and performance - Preserve the conditions of the whole

Some connections use an earlier result: qualification informs an offer, and evidence from a performed trial informs a later choice. Others are simultaneous. During a customer meeting, a facilitator may be eliciting a useful result, helping the person understand an offer and performing part of a larger programme. The same worker may also be responsible for an existing service. Those descriptions identify different contributions and constraints of the work occurring then. Account for the shared worker’s time against the whole programme.

For a proposed combination, name one intended result, the included opportunities, the period and the permitted conditions. Obtain preparation, capability, assignment, access, performance, support, coordination, updating and exception handling that the result requires. Count a shared contribution once where it is actually shared; count local work for each case that needs it. Preserve different resource kinds and time windows. Compare complete ways of producing a comparable result, including a smaller or later programme where the original promise cannot be supported.

Apply this whole-combination condition to the applications below. An apparently cheaper message can increase the number of unsupported responses. A common demonstration can save preparation while increasing coordination. An AI-generated explanation can reduce drafting time while leaving a subject judgement, an individual learning action or a timely human check unsupplied. Inspect those differences before replacing one constituent in a larger arrangement. [FPF C.32.MWA.PP](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c32mwapp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-provision-arrangements) develops the method and performance comparisons; engineering, organisation and operations supply the actual arrangements.

The four-trial calculation in [MKT.13](#mkt13---test-commercial-model-repeatability-and-deployment-limits) and the 44/40-hour comparison in [MKT.14](#mkt14---develop-and-change-a-programme-for-selected-customer-organisations) are deliberately separate constructed situations. In the latter, three organisations contain four independently changing opportunities. Keep the unit of work and the recipient of each result explicit.

## MKT.Reference:5 - Recognition and assurance - Know what the evidence establishes

Recognising a situation selects a potentially useful method. Establishing its result requires the appropriate work and evidence. A stated difficulty may justify a clarifying conversation without establishing its prevalence. A feasible prototype can justify a bounded trial without establishing willingness to buy. A paid trial can demonstrate an acquisition without establishing sustained benefit or ordinary repeatability.

Give each consequential conclusion its object, source, conditions and limit. A customer's account, an observed action, a provider commitment, a calculation and a causal estimate answer different questions. MKT.12 determines sufficient inquiry for a decision. MKT.10 uses causal modelling and identification when an effect claim is required, then carries uncertainty into the economic comparison. MKT.13 asks whether the ordinary arrangement can repeat and sustain the work at a named scale. None of these results supplies a missing permission or allocation.

For a learning offer, human performance under stated help conditions belongs to HCD; satisfaction and purchase are different observations. For community work, attendance can describe reach while leaving useful exchange and the burden of organising unresolved. The constructed applications show how to reason about these distinctions. They are neither observed success cases nor evidence of the effectiveness of the framework as a whole.

## MKT.Reference:6 - Bias-Annotation - Recover whose result and whose burden

The paying organisation, buyer, user, learner, referrer, organiser and provider can have different purposes and authority. Ask whose result is being described and whose account is absent. A manager can fund a programme without determining what a worker personally wants to learn; a community host can invite discussion without promising its participants as sales prospects.

People who declined, could not gain access, needed unusual help or left early may reveal conditions that successful participants conceal. Include their relevant circumstances when the receiving question requires them. Avoid extrapolating from the customers easiest to contact or serve. An average can hide both an unserved group and a scarce contribution that makes the average possible.

Evidence access and disclosure themselves have conditions. Reuse a result only for the permitted purpose and adequate population. Obtain an appropriate anonymised example or customer-local continuation when sharing identifiable material is unavailable. These are working conditions to resolve with the relevant holders, not assumptions granted by a common programme.

## MKT.Reference:7 - Conformance and common failures - Follow the intended use

Before relying on a connected result, follow one real or representative use from the opening question to the receiving action. Can the recipient understand the input, perform the needed operation and act on the returned result? Identify where a changed condition requires another decision. Use the following questions where they can change that reliance:

- Is the useful result distinguished from the requested product, communication response and provider revenue?
- Do the customer roles and differences change a real choice, rather than merely rename market segments?
- Does the offer retain the conditions of its engineering, learning or operating supply?
- Are the particular promise, recipient, permissions and next performer available?
- Does the evidence support the actual claim, population, conditions and time horizon being used?
- Can the whole programme fit its consequential resources and timing while keeping existing obligations?
- Can an affected person decline, defer, obtain help, correct a mistake or end the arrangement on understandable terms?

Several errors are invited by familiar marketing shorthand. A funnel can turn every pause into a sales failure even when the person has obtained useful help. A persona can substitute a stereotype for differences that actually change the offer. An experience concept can become a theme unrelated to work the provider supplies. Using one account score can make the team treat every opportunity as advanced after one contact. Correct the specific inference: recover the person's work, the actual difference, the supplied contribution or the independently changing opportunity. The direct bodies develop each correction and its ordinary stopping point.

## MKT.Reference:8 - Consequences - What changes in practice?

The team can make a smaller justified promise, obtain a missing contribution, stop an unnecessary inquiry, or decline an unsuitable programme before creating unsupported reliance. Customer knowledge can change actual provision. Shared preparation can be used where it remains adequate, with exceptions and continuing service included in the comparison. A negative or uncertain result remains useful when it changes the next decision.

This work also costs time. Jointly shaping a result, qualifying evidence, obtaining commitments and supporting exceptions can be more expensive than producing campaign materials. Use existing qualified results and direct local methods where sufficient. Deeper construction is warranted when it changes the available offer, its assurance or the whole burden.

The descriptions themselves have an obtaining cost: learning the language, locating a method, adapting it, checking an AI contribution and maintaining the explanation. Compare that cost over a named range of uses if it matters to adoption. Count common development once and repeated human work where it recurs.

## MKT.Reference:9 - Architectural Rationale - A repertoire with connected results

A single chronological sales sequence would be easy to display but would conceal returning, parallel and noncommercial work. A list of independent tactics would preserve choice while leaving practitioners to invent the connections. This edition instead provides separately usable methods with explicit receiving results and shared applications. It allows a local answer without requiring every body, while developing the connections that make a larger answer possible.

Research and qualification remain distinct from offer construction because new evidence can change the problem without producing a solution. Offer positioning, experience design and execution remain distinct because a meaningful promise, a desired experience and available provision can fail separately. Customer success and continuation remain distinct from purchase because useful change and remaining obligations outlive the transaction. Intervention evaluation remains distinct from repeatability because a supported effect in one setting does not establish a sustainable commercial arrangement elsewhere.

MKT.14 owns construction of a programme for selected customer organisations. Its contribution is to combine shared preparation, individual opportunities and whole provision under change. A conventional single-customer enquiry can use MKT.1/2/4 without it. An advertising platform or customer database may assist the programme but does not establish the programme's useful result, permissible commonality or receiving work.

Specialised contributions stay with their owners. Marketing can identify a missing service and consume a constructed variant without supplying systems engineering. It can frame the commercial question of an experiment without owning the organisation of search. It can offer education without defining how human capability develops, and it can take part in community activity without making that activity a stage of acquisition. These boundaries preserve usable differences when several methods address the same event.

## MKT.Reference:10 - SoTA-Echoing - Preserve distinct answers to distinct questions

The source discussions in each body's §11 state the adopted or adapted contribution and its limits. Together they support the following choices.

Customer research does not have one interchangeable Jobs-to-be-Done method. Inquiry into progress and switching and analysis of a job's desired outcomes can ask different questions, use different units and support different decisions. MKT.12 selects the line from the receiving question; MKT.1 qualifies its customer differences. Positioning then uses relevant alternatives, distinctive capabilities and value under the customer's circumstances. It is not a substitute for investigating whether the proposed contribution is useful.

Experience work contributes both the construction of an intended experience and attention to what actually occurs in use. MKT.5 retains that distinction; MKT.8 uses a journey map to expose relevant connections without manufacturing one compulsory route. Historical experience-economy and modular-service treatments are used for their specified constructions in MKT.5/9, with later experience and customer-success research informing the limits on what a provider can claim. Experience during use, engagement and useful change remain distinguishable.

Customer-success work makes the useful outcome and enabling participation consequential before and after purchase. MKT.6 adapts this contribution to joint goal formation and help; MKT.7 applies it to continuation and ending. A provider's success metric cannot settle the customer's own goal.

Commercial search and account-based work contribute different ways to investigate, organise and repeat customer work. MKT.13 preserves the difference between search and ordinary repeatable delivery. MKT.14 retains both individual programmes and justified common work, including their preparation and exception costs. Automation changes some work and introduces other work; it does not erase the difference between a contact signal and a qualified opportunity.

For causal questions, MKT.10 adopts the distinction between describing observations, identifying an effect under justified assumptions, estimating it and using it in a prospective choice. A fitted model, a before/after difference and a complete economic comparison are separate contributions. Reopen the affected inference when the population, intervention, supply conditions or relied-on source changes.

## MKT.Reference:12 - Edition and use

This is the Marketing Principles Framework's first edition, comprising MKT.1–14 and the common account and applications in this publication. MKT is its public reference code. Numeric locators remain stable when titles or reading placement change. Cite the framework, pattern or section and the edition used; the order of the Table of Contents is a reading aid, not a compulsory work sequence.

If a required contribution is inaccessible, obtain another adequate source or defer the dependent action.

# Marketing pattern language


## MKT.1 - Qualify a Market Problem and Differences between Customers

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.1:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a team must decide whose difficulty to address, which customers can reasonably receive the same offer, or whether an apparent market opportunity warrants further work. Start with a concrete activity and the people whose conditions can change the offer. A list of organisations or a demographic description is useful only insofar as it helps locate those conditions.

The first useful result is a bounded market hypothesis: a worthwhile customer difficulty, the relevant roles and circumstances, consequential differences between cases, and a justified next commercial move or inquiry. It can support a small offer test without claiming the size or permanence of a market.

The intended reader knows the subject service or product and can obtain qualified accounts of customer work and feasible supply. No prior command of segmentation terminology is required. If the difficulty is already understood for a known customer and a suitable offer exists, proceed directly to adapting the offer or arranging acquisition. Reconstructing an entire market would add no useful answer.

### MKT.1:2 - Problem

“The customer” can name a legal organisation, the person doing the work, a purchaser, a payer or an access authority. Treating them as one actor can produce an attractive offer that nobody can use or obtain. Grouping customers by an easy-to-find attribute can conceal the difference that determines usefulness; dividing every person into a unique group can make a reusable offer impossible.

Qualify the difficulty and group cases only around differences that change a useful decision. Keep the grounds and limits of that grouping available when selecting customers, constructing an offer or revising the market hypothesis.

### MKT.1:3 - Forces

| Tension | What the practitioner must preserve |
| --- | --- |
| A common offer can reduce preparation; customers differ. | The shared contribution and the local differences that it can tolerate. |
| Commercial focus requires selection; evidence may still be sparse. | A target hypothesis with an affordable next test, rather than an invented population estimate. |
| A user can want a change that a payer or permitting party cannot support. | Distinct results, burdens and decision powers. |
| Existing categories help reach people; they may explain little about usefulness. | Separate a way to find customers from a reason to treat their situations alike. |
| A supplier needs a viable contribution; a worthwhile problem may exceed its capabilities. | Retain the problem while qualifying, changing or declining this supplier's proposed part. |

### MKT.1:4 - Solution

#### MKT.1:4.1 - Recover the difficulty without selecting the answer in advance

Name the activity, the inadequate or unavailable result, the circumstances in which it matters and what presently happens instead. Use an actual account or an explicitly proposed case. “Need our platform” is a proposed answer. “A reporting operator must re-enter the same permitted data before the deadline” is a difficulty whose remedy remains open.

Ask what would make addressing it worthwhile and for whom. Consider the result, transition effort, continuing burden and effects on people who do not make the purchasing decision. Use [FPF C.40.PC](../../FPF-Spec.md#c40pc---construct-a-worthwhile-problem-and-proposed-contribution) when the worthwhile problem or proposed contribution itself remains unresolved. It can return a supported problem, an attributed value claim, a hypothesis or rejection. Marketing must preserve that strength instead of treating every lead as established demand.

The scope must be large enough to retain the result and small enough to investigate. Separate performing the underlying work from finding and acquiring a means to perform it. A purchasing obstacle can coexist with a well-served operational activity; an urgent operational need can coexist with no attainable purchase.

#### MKT.1:4.2 - Recover the roles and the alternatives available to each

Identify the people or groups that perform, choose, pay, permit, maintain and are materially affected. Record only distinctions that change the present question. One person may perform several roles. One organisation may contain several independent opportunities; its name is not a single goal or purchasing state.

For each consequential role, ask what useful result or protection matters, what effort or exposure it would accept, what it can decide, and what it would do without this proposal. Verify an attributed authority when the next action relies on it. A friendly contact may supply insight while having no ability to commit a budget or permit access.

Compare feasible alternatives, including continuing the present way, using internal assistance, another supplier, a smaller change or deferring the task. This exposes the relevant competition for the proposed contribution. An internally impressive capability is commercially irrelevant if the people can obtain the needed result more suitably elsewhere.

Use [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) for the missing answer. Its result-investigation route can establish differences in desired execution outcomes; its choice-history route can explain a particular transition. Keep each result's activity coverage, uncertainty and population limits. When an unresolved omitted outcome could change the proposed grouping or contribution, obtain that answer or narrow the target hypothesis before relying on the comparison.

#### MKT.1:4.3 - Construct and challenge useful groupings

Place concrete cases side by side. Ask whether the same contribution would address their difficulty under compatible conditions. Compare the activity, desired result, available alternative, access, preparation, buying arrangement and supply constraints. Keep a distinction only when it changes the offer, evidence needed, way to reach the people, acquisition route or capacity to supply.

Propose a group in terms that make inclusion and exclusion intelligible. For example, “teams using compatible files for a periodic report, with permission for a bounded export and an operator available to inspect it” gives a testable use boundary. “Mid-sized innovative organisations” may help locate candidates but does not supply those conditions.

Look for a nearby case that would break the shared offer. If a similar organisation needs continuous integration rather than a periodic file exchange, decide whether it needs a different variant, a separate target group or no current offer. Do not widen the original group's meaning to retain every attractive lead. Conversely, a different industry need not require another group when the action-changing conditions really match.

Keep the observed cases, the hypothesis about a wider group and the estimate of its extent separate. A handful of cases can justify the next limited test while leaving the latter two uncertain. Larger population, pricing or investment decisions may require a different inquiry. A statistical cluster also needs an interpretable difference that a real offer or action can address.

#### MKT.1:4.4 - Select a target and a bounded next move

Compare plausible groups under the same intended investment and horizon. Ask how important and poorly served the result appears, how the relevant people can be reached, which alternative must be improved upon, whether the contribution can be supplied, and what learning or preparation it needs. Include the cost of discovering and serving the group as well as attracting it.

A material unknown is a reason for a bounded inquiry when its answer could change the choice. It is not automatically a zero value, a high score or grounds for rejecting a genuine customer problem. Where criteria conflict, state the trade-off: a small reachable group may be preferable for a trial, while a larger group with unresolved delivery conditions remains a later hypothesis. Do not hide a necessary condition inside an average score.

Select one of the live results:

- construct or test an offer for a supported group and stated use;
- obtain a specified missing answer before selecting the group;
- change the proposed contribution or obtain a missing supplier;
- retain the lead as a hypothesis with a return condition; or
- stop because the relevant problem, attainable improvement or commercial contribution is not supported.

There is no obligation to maximise the number of groups or pursue every worthwhile difficulty. Respect an existing adequate solution when further differentiation would not change the action.

#### MKT.1:4.5 - Carry the hypothesis into an offer and keep its conditions live

Give [MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) the relevant roles, activity, contribution, actual alternatives, inclusion conditions and uncertainties. That method constructs the specific offer and positioning. Give [MKT.4](#mkt4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible) the separate buying or transition difficulty rather than assuming that operational usefulness makes acquisition easy.

For example, the working result can say: “For teams with compatible periodic exports and an available reviewer, a bounded file-mapping trial could reduce duplicate entry. Access approval and the customer's purchasing window remain separate conditions. Continuous integration cases need another supply arrangement. The next move is to verify those conditions with two candidate teams before quoting the trial.”

While identifying a missing access condition, the marketer is already doing part of the larger offer-construction work: the condition limits what may be proposed. If the encompassing programme reduces its support capacity, revisit the groups that require that support. If a customer changes its purchasing procedure, revise that acquisition route without automatically discarding the evidence about its underlying activity.

Reopen the affected hypothesis when the activity, relevant roles, feasible alternative, supply arrangement or evidence of an important difference changes. Preserve unrelated qualified cases. A group is a maintained decision aid whose utility depends on its conditions, not an identity imposed on its members.

### MKT.1:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.1:5.1 - Three organisations, four opportunities

In a stipulated case, a file-transfer supplier considers Sever, Vector and Luch. Sever's reporting department and Vector use compatible periodic files; Sever's production department and Luch need continuous integration. The supplier has an existing file-transfer operation but no suitable continuous integration service.

The initial “three similar businesses” list cannot select an offer. The marketer recovers four activities and their roles. Reporting operators care about repeated entry and preserving an inspection step; purchasing staff care about expenditure and timing; access staff need an acceptable data arrangement. Sever's two departments cannot be assigned one condition merely because they share an organisation name.

The first target hypothesis groups the two compatible reporting opportunities for a bounded trial. Each retains separate access and purchasing conditions. The two continuous integration opportunities remain worthwhile problem hypotheses with missing supply. They are not counted as file-trial demand. MKT.2 receives enough to construct the reporting offer; MKT.4 receives Vector's later purchasing window as a distinct issue.

#### MKT.1:5.2 - A change that splits one use without erasing the rest

Suppose Vector can no longer export the required data. Its original inclusion condition fails. Ask whether a permitted local variant or another supplier can still supply the result. Retain Sever's supported reporting case; a changed rule at Vector does not establish that all periodic reporting customers are unsuitable.

In a consumer repair service, one person may choose, pay and use the result. The role account then becomes smaller. Two otherwise similar households can still need different offers because one can wait and the other must restore an essential activity today. The discriminating condition is useful even when demographic descriptions are identical.

### MKT.1:6 - Bias-Annotation

A successful reference customer can become an unjustified model for everyone. Inspect both the conditions that enabled the success and a nearby case that lacks them. A sales database also contains the people the organisation happened to reach; it need not represent those who stayed with another solution or could not get access.

Descriptive categories can become stereotypes when inferred characteristics replace actual evidence. Use a category to locate a question, and confirm the condition before relying on it for a consequential individual offer.

### MKT.1:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The difficulty and desired result are distinct from the proposed product or technology.
- Action-changing roles, available alternatives and authority conditions remain separate.
- Grouping has an intelligible use condition and an exclusion or revision case.
- Observed cases, a wider group hypothesis and a population estimate retain different evidence strength.
- Target choice includes reachability, supply and full relevant burden under a declared horizon.
- The result gives an offer, inquiry, supplier request, qualified deferral or stop a usable starting point.

### MKT.1:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Treat every employee of a named organisation as sharing one need and buying state. | Recover the opportunities and consequential roles within it. |
| Use an easy database attribute as proof of a shared problem. | Check whether it predicts a difference that changes the offer or action in the actual cases. |
| Fragment every customer into a separate persona. | Retain common work and supply conditions where a reusable offer remains valid. |
| Call willingness to discuss a product established demand. | Preserve it as the particular response observed and test the missing use or acquisition condition. |

### MKT.1:9 - Consequences

The team can pursue a smaller justified opportunity while retaining other problems and unknowns honestly. It can also discover that the proposed supplier is a poor fit even when a customer difficulty is real. Maintaining useful group boundaries costs inquiry and revision; creating distinctions that never change action adds expense without a marketing gain.

### MKT.1:10 - Architectural Rationale

Problem, roles, alternatives and grouping are joined because each constrains the target that an offer can reasonably address. A broad strategy can identify a promising direction, and an exchange method can construct one deal. Neither alone explains how repeated differences among customers should guide a market hypothesis and several offers.

Grouping is conditional reuse: it permits a common starting contribution while preserving consequential differences. Selecting a group does not yet organise a multi-organisation programme; MKT.14 supplies that larger choice of preparation, coordination and investment.

### MKT.1:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For finding distinctions that change an offer, **adapt** [Strategyn's public ODI account](https://strategyn.com/outcome-driven-innovation/): executor, activity and differing poorly served results can reveal groups that a product list or demographic label conceals. Its useful contribution is the question-relative grouping in §4.3. The serious lighter alternative is an existing descriptive segment that already locates the decisive conditions. Retain that simpler grouping when it works; the additional inquiry must change selection or supply. Do not adopt permanent segment stability, exhaustive need coverage or a supplier's success rates. Reopen when the proposed common offer fails in a consequential included case.

For qualifying the underlying opportunity, **adopt** C.40.PC's distinction between a supported problem, value claim and unconfirmed hypothesis, and **adapt** the comparison in [ECO.3](../ECONOMIC-REASONING-AND-COORDINATION-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#eco3---discover-an-exchange-between-different-plans) with each party's alternative. These developed internal treatments improve §§4.1–4.4 over assuming that one favourable contact establishes an organisation's demand. They preserve modest ordinary accounts while making an unsupported commercial leap visible; they do not supply market prevalence.

### MKT.1:12 - Relations

[MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) supplies question-specific evidence and receives unresolved differences. [MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) constructs the offer for the qualified situation. [MKT.4](#mkt4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible) develops feasible choice and acquisition. A failed offer or transition can reveal a missing group distinction and return here.

C.40.PC and Strategy supply worthwhile problems and possible contributions; their results retain their own conditions. MKT.13 asks whether the commercial arrangement can repeat at another volume. MKT.14 uses this market hypothesis when selecting named organisations and arranging common or individual work; membership alone does not supply that programme.

### MKT.1:End


## MKT.2 - Construct and Test an Offer and Its Positioning

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.2:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when people cannot tell what a proposed product or service will do for them, why it is worth considering against their actual alternatives, or what obtaining its result requires. It also applies before an offer exists: a useful capability must be turned into an understandable, supportable proposal for a particular customer situation.

The result is a proposed offer with a clear recipient, contribution, conditions, consideration and grounds for comparison, together with what its test established and left open. Positioning is the context that helps the intended customer interpret that offer. A headline, pricing page or sales conversation can express it; none is the offer's complete content by itself.

The intended reader understands the product or service and can obtain customer evidence and a competent supply answer. Start from a qualified situation through [MKT.1](#mkt1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-customers) or an equally sufficient existing account. When a suitable understood offer already exists, use it. When the customer problem is still unknown, investigate that question before polishing an assumed answer.

### MKT.2:2 - Problem

A list of capabilities does not tell a customer which useful change it enables under their conditions. A compelling claim can also imply capabilities, support or terms that the provider cannot supply. Teams then try to repair the resulting misunderstanding with more messages while the actual proposal remains inconsistent.

Construct the contribution, its context of comparison and its supply conditions together. Test what the intended people understand and can use. Let failed understanding, failed usefulness and failed feasibility lead to different repairs.

### MKT.2:3 - Forces

| Tension | Required response |
| --- | --- |
| A simple explanation helps recognition; qualifications can change the promise. | Make consequential limits visible with the claim they limit. |
| Different roles value different results. | Preserve their separate contributions within one compatible proposal. |
| Differentiation attracts attention; the alternative may already be adequate. | Compare actual options and identify a worthwhile difference, including convenience or access when those matter. |
| A customer can want a result outside the supplier's control. | Promise the supported contribution and identify the other work and conditions it needs. |
| Testing costs attention and preparation. | Test the uncertainty that changes the proposal, and reuse an already sufficient answer. |

### MKT.2:4 - Solution

#### MKT.2:4.1 - Establish the receiving result and what could contribute

Recover the customer's activity, important difficulty, existing alternative and the roles that can change the proposal. Ask what useful difference is sought, who receives it and how it could be recognised. If the purchaser wants a lower fee while users need less repeated work, retain both questions. Do not let one convenient indicator become the goal of every participant.

When the useful result is contested or not yet known, use [FPF C.40.PC](../../FPF-Spec.md#c40pc---construct-a-worthwhile-problem-and-proposed-contribution) to separate the problem, proposed contribution and acceptance expectation. The people involved can then choose a shared limited result, retain distinct goals, or decline the wider proposal. A narrower agreed contribution may be possible without pretending that all goals coincide.

Connect a candidate capability to that result through a concrete use: who would use what, under which conditions, to change which part of the work? A new or changed product-service arrangement may need the linked use and system concept in [SYSE.2](../Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/SYSTEMS-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#syse2---develop-linked-use-and-system-concepts), followed by the relevant engineering and supply work. Ask its owner for the needed result and constraint; do not replace a missing function with stronger promotional wording.

#### MKT.2:4.2 - Select a truthful context for comparison

Recover what the intended customer could actually do instead. Compare the contribution with those alternatives, identify the useful difference that its capabilities can support, and identify the circumstances in which that difference matters. Then choose a familiar description or category that helps the intended people interpret it without implying an unavailable result.

For example, “automated reporting platform” may invite an expectation of continuous integration and unattended reports. “Assisted file-mapping trial for periodic reports” gives a different context. Choose between them from the actual proposal and its receivers, not from which phrase sounds larger. A category can help recognition while a qualifier makes the material boundary clear.

If the customer already has an adequate alternative, determine whether the proposal offers a worthwhile difference in result, effort, access, risk or terms. It need not be unique in the world. If no worthwhile difference is supported for this situation, change the contribution, select a different appropriate situation or stop. Competitor claims and the provider's own claims both need grounds where comparison relies on them.

Ask each relevant participant two questions: what supported difference favours this option among feasible alternatives, and what missing condition would exclude it? Use the first answer to explain preference and the second to retain the conditions for choice or supply. Classify the capability for this situation: dependable support can be a prerequisite in one acquisition and a reason to switch or renew in another.

Keep the comparison open when evidence changes. A new feasible alternative can defeat the proposed advantage without altering the product. A misunderstood category can require another explanation while the underlying contribution remains suitable.

#### MKT.2:4.3 - Construct the complete proposal

Make the customer able to recover the following content from the offer's actual presentation:

| Content | Construction question |
| --- | --- |
| Recipient and situation | Who can use this contribution, for what activity and under which entry conditions? |
| Supplied result and scope | What will be provided, what useful result is sought, and where does the provider's contribution end? |
| Customer and partner contributions | What access, preparation, decisions, effort or other service is required? |
| Supply and support | Who can deliver and support it, when and at what attainable volume? |
| Price and other consideration | What is charged or exchanged, when, for what unit, with what consequential conditions? |
| Evidence and acceptance | What grounds support the claims, and what can the recipient inspect to establish that the proposed contribution was supplied? |
| Continuation and limits | What happens on a failed prerequisite, unsuitable result, changed condition, cancellation or end of the limited offer? |

Use the amount of detail the decision needs. An ordinary standard service may already carry this content in established terms. A new compound service may need explicit coordination among providers. The purpose is a recoverable proposal, not a mandatory form.

Distinguish the provider's deliverable, the customer's later result and the evidence for each. A prepared report is different from a timely management decision; a course place is different from an acquired ability. State the contribution and the conditions on which a further benefit depends. A guarantee is a specific stronger commitment whose means and terms require their own support.

Construct price and terms alongside the contribution. Identify the unit—such as a trial, period, case or available capacity—and the costs or obligations outside the headline figure. Use [ECO.3](../ECONOMIC-REASONING-AND-COORDINATION-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#eco3---discover-an-exchange-between-different-plans) to compare the parties' contributions and alternatives. Use [ECO.5](../ECONOMIC-REASONING-AND-COORDINATION-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#eco5---choose-exchange-terms-for-private-information-and-effort) when private information, later effort or risk makes a choice of terms consequential. Their economic construction does not establish a legal permission or enforceability claim.

#### MKT.2:4.4 - Obtain a supply answer before relying on the promise

Ask the responsible supplier for the result that can actually be provided under the named conditions: configuration, available capability, people, time, access, dependencies, support and unresolved constraints. Separate a planned improvement from an available contribution. Include preparation and continuing support when comparing feasible versions.

For each important promise, follow the required contribution to a responsible provider or to an explicit gap. Resolve incompatible timings, shared scarce resources and conflicting claims made to different roles. A technically possible service can still be unavailable in the proposed window. Return an unsupported part for engineering, operational or organisational work, or reduce the proposal to a supported part.

The supply answer constrains both the offer and its positioning. If only batch processing is supported, “continuous” must disappear from the current promise. If an operator must inspect every output, the offer must leave that work visible.

#### MKT.2:4.5 - Test the uncertain connection

Choose a test from the uncertainty that could change the proposal. For understanding, present the actual description to an appropriate person and ask what they expect to receive, what they must do, what it costs and where it would not fit. Let them explain before supplying the intended answer. Compare their account with the proposed content; a repeated sentence can reflect recall without usable understanding.

For usefulness, place the proposal beside the person's actual alternative and ask them to work through a concrete situation. What would change? Which required contribution is missing? A suitable sample or limited trial can expose a mismatch that an opinion about attractiveness cannot. If willingness to acquire is the unresolved question, use an attainable next choice with honest terms through [MKT.4](#mkt4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible). Do not report hypothetical enthusiasm as a completed purchase.

For supply, obtain the provider's relevant demonstration, observation or qualified answer. Customer understanding cannot establish supplier capacity. For a claimed benefit caused by the intervention, use the distinct causal question and evidence design; a successful presentation is not that evidence.

After the test, identify the failed connection. Misunderstanding may change wording or context; an unwanted result may change the target or contribution; an unavailable prerequisite may change supply or scope. Return to [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) only for a question whose answer is still needed. Keep adequate parts instead of repeating the whole inquiry.

#### MKT.2:4.6 - Use and revise one coherent offer

Return the proposal, grounds, test result and unresolved conditions at the strength supported. Keep its expressions consistent across a quotation, demonstration, acquisition interface and delivery instruction. Different roles can need different explanations, but they must not receive incompatible commitments.

Pass MKT.4 enough content to make the next choice feasible. Pass the delivery owner the result, scope and conditions actually agreed if an acquisition occurs. A material change in target, alternative, result, price, supplier capacity or conditions of data use reopens the dependent part. Editing the words without changing the affected operating arrangement leaves the inconsistency in place.

### MKT.2:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.2:5.1 - Turn a broad automation claim into an inspectable trial

In this stipulated case, Sever's reporting department wants less repeated entry while keeping a human inspection step. Its feasible alternative is the existing spreadsheet procedure. Engineering confirms a bounded file-mapping operation for one compatible file family; continuous integration is unavailable. The purchaser can consider a fixed-fee trial, and the access owner must approve the particular sample.

“Eliminate reporting work with our automation platform” implies more than this contribution. The constructed proposal is an assisted trial over two working weeks: configure one permitted file mapping, provide the mapped output and exception explanation, and help the operator compare the resulting work with the current procedure. The offer names the sample, operator inspection, limited support, quoted fee, access prerequisite and end of the trial. It does not promise production integration or a specified time saving before evidence exists.

The proposed reason to prefer this trial is reduced repeated entry; permitted data and operator inspection keep it feasible. If recurring support failures prompted the search, dependable assistance could itself become a supported reason to choose.

Positioning it as a file-mapping trial makes the relevant comparison intelligible. In a stipulated understanding test, the purchaser correctly recovers the limited result but expects the fee to include future production use. The team clarifies the trial's end and the separate continuation decision. That repairs the offer's interpretation; it does not establish willingness to buy or achieved benefit.

#### MKT.2:5.2 - Change a whole condition

Now the available operator cannot inspect outputs during the trial window. The same technical mapping no longer supports the proposed arrangement. Possible responses include another qualified operator, another window, an actually supported additional service or no trial now. Removing inspection from the description would conceal the missing contribution. MKT.4 must receive the revised feasible choice.

For a learning product, the analogous distinction is between supplying worked material and helping someone acquire a contribution to later work. A generated explanation can serve the former. An offer of the latter must include the needed practice, feedback and capability observation from HCD, with the learner's required work visible.

### MKT.2:6 - Bias-Annotation

Teams often prefer the capability they built most recently or the category with the largest apparent market. Challenge that preference with the customer's actual alternative and a use condition that could defeat the promise. A friendly participant who knows the team can also fill an explanation gap; distinguish their prior knowledge from what the presented offer supplies.

### MKT.2:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The intended recipients, useful contribution and consequential conditions can be recovered from the offer.
- The comparison concerns feasible alternatives and a supported difference for the stated situation.
- Deliverable, later benefit, proposed promise, supplier ability and actual fulfilment remain distinct.
- Supply, preparation, support, consideration and customer effort are included at the scope of the promise.
- The selected test answers the live uncertainty and its result retains the proper strength.
- Different expressions and role-specific explanations carry compatible commitments and a usable next choice.

### MKT.2:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Fill in a positioning sentence before investigating its terms. | Construct the contribution and comparison; use the sentence only to express the supported result. |
| Repair missing supply with a more persuasive explanation. | Obtain the missing contribution or change the promised scope and its dependent messages. |
| Treat a participant's positive reaction as understanding, demand and successful use. | Ask the separate questions and retain what each observation actually establishes. |
| Compare a cheap introductory fee with a complete alternative. | Include the required continuation, customer work and other costs in the comparison. |

### MKT.2:9 - Consequences

The customer can assess a proposal whose meaning and conditions are inspectable, and the provider has a more precise request for the work needed to fulfil it. Construction can reveal that the offer should become smaller, be directed elsewhere or not be made. Coordination and testing cost effort; an existing suitable offer remains the cheaper choice when no material uncertainty changes its use.

### MKT.2:10 - Architectural Rationale

An offer joins the customer's situation, a useful contribution, a context for comparison and feasible terms of supply. Positioning belongs in that construction because it can change which capabilities and limits the customer infers. It cannot settle the truth of those capabilities or the customer's choice.

A messaging-only remedy is sufficient for a local expression problem. Product engineering is needed when the contribution does not exist. This method identifies which connection failed and joins the appropriate results without turning every offer into a new product project or taking over the supplier's professional work.

### MKT.2:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For making a contribution intelligible, **adapt** [April Dunford, A Quickstart Guide to Positioning, 2021](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning). The selected line relates actual alternatives, differentiated capabilities, customer value, fitting customers and an intelligible market context. It improves §4.2 over a category-first fill-in statement whose assumptions remain untested. The extra investigation is worthwhile when context changes understanding; a sufficient established category remains cheaper. The guide's B2B cases are explanatory examples, not evidence of universal growth effects. The [2025 distinction between preference and exclusion](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/value-vs-objection-handling) sharpens §4.2 without assigning a capability or profession a fixed role in every choice. Reopen the comparison when another alternative, audience or category changes the inferred promise.

For constructing the complete offer, **adopt** the developed C.40.PC, SYSE.2 and ECO.3/5 contributions used in §§4.1, 4.3 and 4.4. They supply the useful problem, compatible use and supply concept, and feasible exchange terms. A persuasive value statement alone leaves these dependencies unanswered. The trade-off is more construction effort for an offer that the customer and supplier can both interpret.

### MKT.2:12 - Relations

[MKT.1](#mkt1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-customers) supplies a qualified situation and relevant differences; [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) supplies evidence and receives unresolved questions. [MKT.4](#mkt4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible) turns the understood proposal into a feasible choice and acquisition.

MKT.5 expands the design of client experience, MKT.6 develops shared useful outcomes and help in obtaining them, and MKT.11 coordinates promises and delivery. Their changed results reopen dependent promises here. Engineering, Operations, HCD and economic coordination retain their respective contributions; a marketing offer does not establish their results.

### MKT.2:End


## MKT.3 - Reach a Suitable Audience and Obtain a Meaningful Response

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.3:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a useful offer or invitation is not reaching the people for whom it could matter, when recipients infer a different proposal, or when attention produces no usable continuation. It covers selecting where to meet those people, constructing an appropriate message and invitation, making the response possible, and learning from a bounded attempt.

The result is a feasible way to reach a specified audience with a truthful proposal, together with the observed response and a justified continuation. A meaningful response supplies something relevant to that continuation: a request, an answer about a condition, an informed choice or a refusal. A click can be useful evidence of an action without establishing any of these meanings.

The intended reader knows the proposed contribution and can arrange its receiving work. A qualified situation from [MKT.1](#mkt1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-customers) and an understood offer from [MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) are sufficient starting points. A known customer who has requested a quotation usually needs that quotation through the agreed means; a new audience study or communication campaign would add unnecessary work.

### MKT.3:2 - Problem

A large audience may contain few people able to use the offer. A suitable person may encounter the message at an unsuitable moment, misunderstand its category, lack a feasible response, or reply to a team that cannot continue. Treating all these failures as insufficient exposure leads to more messages without resolving the broken connection.

Construct the connection from the recipient's situation through the message and possible response to the work that receives it. Test the uncertainty that could change that construction. Keep exposure, understanding, response and later use distinct.

### MKT.3:3 - Forces

| Tension | What the construction must preserve |
| --- | --- |
| Reach can grow faster than relevance. | The circumstances that make a person an appropriate recipient. |
| A short message must compete for attention. | The contribution and any condition that would change its meaning. |
| Different places support different forms of participation. | The recipient's expectations and the permitted form of contact. |
| A response can arrive before delivery capacity is available. | An attainable next step and an honest account of its timing. |
| A small attempt gives useful clues but incomplete evidence. | What was observed, what remains unknown and the decision that can use the result. |

### MKT.3:4 - Solution

#### MKT.3:4.1 - Choose whose response could change the next action

Start with the activity, difficulty and conditions that make the contribution useful. Identify the people who encounter that difficulty, the people who can authorize a relevant next step, and the people who might introduce them. These can be different audiences with different invitations. An introduction from a trusted colleague does not establish that the recipient needs the offer.

State what response is useful now and why. A new service might need to learn whether a proposed input can be made available; an established offer might need an appointment with a qualified purchaser. Asking for a purchase before the parties understand a necessary condition can conceal the very information needed to construct the offer. If the useful result itself is still contested, use MKT.6 before making a definite promise.

Define the boundary of the attempt: which recipients, situation, period and volume can be served, what preparation is available, and what would warrant continuing or stopping. Base the boundary on the consequential uncertainty and capacity, not a standard number of messages. An existing answer can justify direct execution without a new probe.

#### MKT.3:4.2 - Find a fitting place of attention and an attainable route

Locate where the intended people already seek help, exchange relevant information or make the decision. Ask how the subject is encountered there: a requested email, professional meeting, search result, public explanation, referral or another appropriate contact. Recover evidence from actual customer accounts, existing communication or a bounded inquiry; the team's preferred platform is only a candidate.

Compare candidate routes on the same receiving situation. Consider whether the relevant people can be reached, whether the context makes the message intelligible and credible, what explanation the format permits, what contact is allowed, and what effort a response requires. Include preparation, distribution and the work of receiving replies. A route with fewer contacts can be preferable when it reaches the responsible people at a usable moment.

Select one adequate route or a small set whose roles are clear. A public explanation may enable later recognition while a requested conversation resolves a specific condition. Do not force every recipient through both. If no available route reaches suitable people, return a concrete access question or reconsider the audience; broader distribution does not repair missing access by itself.

#### MKT.3:4.3 - Construct a message whose inferred promise is supportable

Build the message around a recognizable situation and a supported contribution. Give the recipient enough context to understand why it may matter, why the claim deserves consideration and what to do next. Keep a consequential limitation with the claim it qualifies. A short invitation can link to details, but it must not depend on those details to reverse its main promise.

Check the actual expression in its intended format. A heading, illustration, category or omitted condition can imply more than the literal sentence. Compare what the recipient is likely to infer with the offer and supplier's actual ability. Replace an overstated implication or change the contribution through its responsible practice. Presenting an assisted trial as unattended automation changes the inferred offer even when the small print mentions assistance.

When an intermediary combines, omits or generates material, use [the intermediary application](#mktapplication2---app-mkt-media---keep-a-supportable-offer-through-a-changing-intermediary) to construct a supportable family of expressions with actual controls, observations and receiving work. Approval of separate elements or one preview does not qualify every permitted combination.

Adapt the explanation to the role without inventing different commitments. A purchaser may need scope and consideration, a user may need the required work, and an access authority may need the conditions of a trial. Each should be able to recover a compatible proposal. When an important distinction is unfamiliar, use a concrete comparison rather than more promotional adjectives.

#### MKT.3:4.4 - Make the invitation and its receiving work usable

Specify one proportionate next action and what follows it. Explain the information actually needed, an attainable way to provide it, the expected effort and timing, and who will respond. Provide an appropriate way to decline or defer. Do not request sensitive working material in a public reply when an agreed protected means is needed for the next stage.

Arrange the receiving contribution before distributing the invitation: the responsible person, access to the necessary information, capacity, and a fallback when the proposed next step cannot occur. A message promising a consultation needs available consultations. If only a later place is available, state that timing and let the recipient decide whether it is still useful.

Test the response path in the form the recipient will encounter. An inaccessible form, missing appointment or unexplained account requirement can defeat an otherwise suitable message. [MKT.4](#mkt4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible) develops the subsequent choice and acquisition; this method must at least make the offered transition possible.

#### MKT.3:4.5 - Observe the attempt without merging different answers

Before the attempt, decide which observations can answer the live question. Keep the following distinctions even when a single conversation supplies several observations:

| Observation | What it can support | What it does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery or display | The material reached an address or surface under the recorded conditions. | That the intended person noticed it. |
| Notice or attention | A specified person attended to some material. | That they understood the contribution and its limits. |
| Account of meaning | The person can describe the relevant proposal or distinguish its consequences. | That they prefer it, can acquire it or will benefit. |
| Reply, request or refusal | A particular response occurred, with its stated grounds when available. | The unstated reason for everyone else's silence. |
| Later action | The named appointment, acquisition or use occurred. | That the message caused it or the promised benefit followed. |

Observe meaning by asking a fitting recipient to explain what would be supplied and what they would need to do, or by using another suitable direct task. Do not teach the missing explanation during the probe and then credit the original message with the repaired understanding. A friendly colleague familiar with the product can help with drafting but may not expose the intended audience's inference.

Retain the unit, denominator, period and selection conditions of any count. Distinguish ten replies from ten suitable recipients, and unique people from repeated exposures. If two routes reach different populations at different times, their response rates do not isolate the effect of the route. Use [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) for a missing evidence question and MKT.10 when a causal contribution or cost comparison is needed. A bounded communication attempt does not automatically require either inquiry.

#### MKT.3:4.6 - Repair the connection indicated by the evidence

Locate the consequential mismatch. Evidence of delivery without meaningful attention can change the route or moment; an incorrect account of the promise can change its expression or positioning; a well-understood but unsuitable contribution can reopen the offer or audience; an attempted response blocked by access can change the response operation. Several failures can coexist. Investigate a consequential unknown rather than assigning every silence to the most convenient cause.

Continue, revise or stop at the scale the evidence supports. Retain working parts and change the failed connection. An informed refusal can settle the present invitation; repeated contact is not a remedy for an understood decision to decline. Return the selected route and expression, the feasible next action, the observed result and the limit on its interpretation to the people who will continue the work.

### MKT.3:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.3:5.1 - An invitation to an assisted reporting trial

A provider can help an operations team map one approved file into a periodic report. A staff member at Sever has asked about reducing repeated entry. The provider wants to establish whether an approved sample and an operator's time can be available before offering a trial date. Its first draft says, “Automate your reporting; book a demo.” A purchaser reads that as a promise of unattended recurring reports.

The team changes the invitation to an assisted file-mapping trial for a periodic report. It states that the trial uses one approved sample, needs an operator for one agreed session and does not establish continuous integration. The requested reply is whether those conditions can be met or which one needs discussion. A named person can receive the answer and offer one of the sessions actually available. A public upload is unnecessary; sample transfer will use the agreed means if the trial proceeds.

In a constructed probe, the provider uses requested email so contacts can clarify the conditions with colleagues before scheduling a session. It sends individually relevant invitations to eight contacts who have agreed to receive this information. Delivery is confirmed for six addresses. Four people send substantive replies: two can provide the required contributions, one needs access approval, and one declines because continuous integration is essential. Two intended recipients who discuss the message can explain the assisted scope. The remaining contacts' understanding and reasons for silence are unknown. Four substantive replies out of eight invitations describe this attempt; they do not establish market demand or a superior channel.

The provider can arrange the two feasible trials, help clarify the access condition and close the unsuitable invitation. It retains the qualification in later messages. If the operator at Vector is unavailable until the next reporting period, changing the calendar is a possible next step; calling the same trial “effortless automation” would instead conceal a required contribution.

#### MKT.3:5.2 - Learning to recognize an altered promise

A team preparing messages repeatedly adds unsupported implications to a factual service description. An instructor uses [HCD.6.1](../Human%20Capability%20Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/HUMAN-CAPABILITY-DEVELOPMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#hcd61---help-a-learner-discern-a-needed-distinction-in-practice): the learner compares the original with a promotional rewrite, identifies the changed expectation and decides what must be corrected before use. The examples make the consequential difference visible, such as assistance becoming a guarantee of independent success.

The instructor then changes a superficial cue and presents another pair, so success cannot depend solely on the word “guaranteed.” The learner must explain the consequence for this offer. Producing the exercise is different from demonstrating that the learner can make the distinction; success here does not establish general immunity to persuasion, retention or transfer to every kind of message. When no learning problem exists, a competent author can simply correct the expression.

### MKT.3:6 - Bias-Annotation

Teams can overvalue the channel they know, treat every reply as interest, or attribute silence to insufficient pressure. Start with a response that would change a real decision and include informed refusal among useful outcomes. Existing familiarity, prior contact and self-selection can all help an invitation work; preserve those conditions when interpreting the attempt.

### MKT.3:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The receiving situation and the useful response are specific enough to select an appropriate audience and route.
- The message's material implications agree with the supported offer and its conditions.
- A recipient can take, decline or defer the proposed step, and someone can receive the result.
- Delivery, attention, understanding, response and later action remain distinct.
- Observations retain their unit, period, selection and interpretive limits.
- Continuation addresses the supported mismatch or an explicit unknown.

### MKT.3:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Select a popular platform before identifying a receiving situation. | Compare where the relevant people can encounter and use this invitation. |
| Hide a material qualification behind an attractive main claim. | Repair the inferred promise where the expectation is formed. |
| Count any click as demand or meaningful understanding. | Obtain the observation needed for the actual decision. |
| Increase distribution while replies remain unanswered. | Restore the receiving work or reduce the invitation to available capacity. |
| Treat nonresponse as an informed refusal or permission to keep contacting. | Preserve the unknown and the applicable conditions of further contact. |

### MKT.3:9 - Consequences

Attention can lead to a useful, serviceable continuation instead of an unexamined count. The work can reveal an unsuitable audience, offer or route early. Preparation and observing meaning take effort, and a small attempt leaves population and causal questions open. A direct requested communication remains appropriate when those uncertainties do not matter.

### MKT.3:10 - Architectural Rationale

Communication connects people and their situations with a proposed contribution and a possible action. The connection fails in different places, so one exposure measure cannot govern all repairs. Keeping the receiving work inside the construction prevents an invitation from creating a promise that no one can honour.

The method can be used for one message or for repeated communication. Neither use requires a universal sequence of stages: a recipient can ask for details, introduce another responsible person, return later or decline. The continuation follows that response and the supported offer.

### MKT.3:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For intelligibility, **adapt** the alternatives-and-value construction in [Dunford's positioning guide, 2021](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning), with the [2025 distinction between preference and exclusion](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/value-vs-objection-handling). It improves message construction when a familiar category implies an unavailable capability. It does not select a universally best channel or establish that an intelligible message causes acquisition; an existing suitable explanation is cheaper when the comparison is settled.

For the teachable distinction in §5.2, **adapt** the original-versus-promotional-rewrite contrast in [the source discussion](https://ailev.livejournal.com/1738906.html), using HCD.6.1's developed construction and action-changing second example. This gives a bounded learning task instead of promising a fixed number of repetitions or general resistance to influence. Reconsider the exercise when the actual mistake or receiving use changes.

For the whole communication attempt, **synthesize** these contributions with MKT.12's decision-specific evidence and the actual response operation. A distribution-only account leaves meaning and continuation unanswered. The additional work is warranted when either can change the result.

### MKT.3:12 - Relations

MKT.1 supplies the receiving situation; MKT.2 supplies the supported offer; MKT.4 receives a possible choice or acquisition. MKT.6 can establish a useful result before a definite invitation. MKT.12 supplies missing evidence, while MKT.10 addresses causal contribution and cost. MKT.11 can change what the receiving team can promise, and MKT.14 can combine common preparation with different invitations to named organisations.

HCD.6.1 supplies actual instruction when making a distinction is a learning problem. Communication does not establish the learner's capability, the operational result or a customer's later benefit.

### MKT.3:End


## MKT.4 - Make Choice and Acquisition Meaningful and Feasible

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.4:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a person or organisation is considering an offer but cannot yet compare it meaningfully, make an authorised choice, arrange the transition or complete acquisition under the stated conditions. Interest may be present while an operator, payer or access authority still needs a different answer.

The useful result is an understood and attainable next choice: acquire on known terms, try a bounded option, revise the proposal, defer with a meaningful return condition or decline. The result is useful when the relevant people can act on it; a completed purchase is one possible outcome.

The intended reader can explain the offer and coordinate with the people responsible for supply and exchange. Start with a sufficiently qualified offer through [MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) or an existing equivalent. If a routine understood purchase can proceed, use its ordinary route. An already completed acquisition whose use is failing belongs with the service, capability or operating practice responsible for the missing result.

### MKT.4:2 - Problem

A sales process can label someone as interested, qualified or ready while the actual decision remains blocked. More persuasion does not provide a missing budget, authority, access approval or feasible transition. Conversely, an unsuitable offer can be treated as though it merely needs less friction.

Recover the live choices and what each requires. Help the relevant people understand and obtain a suitable option while keeping a meaningful refusal available. Change the proposal when the problem lies in what is offered.

### MKT.4:3 - Forces

| Tension | Practical consequence |
| --- | --- |
| A provider seeks acquisition; the customer has its own alternatives. | Judge whether the next choice is understood and suitable, not only whether it increases conversion. |
| Different people can control use, payment and permission. | Recover the required contributions and actual authority without assuming one contact speaks for all. |
| Reducing unnecessary effort helps action; some checks protect a real interest. | Remove a needless step only after identifying what it was meant to establish. |
| A limited trial can reduce uncertainty; it creates obligations and work. | Bound its purpose, cost, data, support and end before it begins. |
| An immediate decision can be valuable; readiness may depend on another event. | Supply a useful deferral or refusal instead of manufacturing urgency. |

### MKT.4:4 - Solution

#### MKT.4:4.1 - Recover the actual decision and its participants

Ask what is being decided now: whether to investigate, compare alternatives, obtain permission, try, contract, pay, start use or continue an existing arrangement. Recover the relevant participants, what each can decide and what information or contribution they need. A sequence recorded in a CRM is a proposed or reported account; check the actual case before acting on it.

Use a concrete recent event to establish where the decision stands. “We are ready” can mean that a user likes the demonstration while purchasing has not seen the terms. Preserve the first fact and obtain the second. Do not invent a stage for an unknown condition.

Define the attainable alternatives, including continuing the current way, another supplier, a narrower contribution and no action now. Investigate the reason for uncertainty when it can change the next move. [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) supplies a choice-history inquiry when the reported obstacle is not yet understood.

#### MKT.4:4.2 - Locate the condition that changes the next choice

Distinguish the following difficulties before proposing a remedy:

| Observed difficulty | What to establish | Useful response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The person cannot interpret the proposal. | What result, cost, required work or limit they currently infer. | Explain or repair the offer; return to MKT.2 if its content is inconsistent. |
| The understood offer is unattractive or unsuitable. | Which result, alternative or burden makes it so. | Change the contribution or terms, offer another appropriate option, or accept refusal. |
| Someone cannot make the required commitment. | The missing budget, authority, access or other contribution. | Obtain the competent participant's answer or retain a conditional hold. |
| Transition disrupts valued current work. | What must be preserved, moved, learned or recovered on failure. | Construct a feasible transition with its responsible suppliers. |
| The chosen acquisition cannot be completed. | The actual failure in quotation, contracting, payment, scheduling or access. | Repair that operation without repeating an already sufficient choice. |

These conditions can coexist. Resolve one without claiming that the others have disappeared. A missing permission may prevent a trial even after its value and price are understood.

#### MKT.4:4.3 - Make comparison and terms usable

Put the live options on a comparable basis: the useful contribution, prerequisites, total relevant consideration, customer effort, timing, support and important exposure. Explain what changes if an option is declined or deferred. Do not count an unsupported future function as part of today's available alternative.

Ask the relevant people to explain the option in their own situation: what they expect, what they must provide, what could fail and what choice they can actually make. Correct a consequential misunderstanding before relying on their next decision. A person may understand the offer and still prefer an alternative.

Use [ECO.3](../ECONOMIC-REASONING-AND-COORDINATION-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#eco3---discover-an-exchange-between-different-plans) to construct who gives what to whom, when and under which conditions. Use [ECO.5](../ECONOMIC-REASONING-AND-COORDINATION-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#eco5---choose-exchange-terms-for-private-information-and-effort) for a consequential choice among terms such as a limited trial, staged consideration or supported warranty when information and later effort differ. Obtain a competent legal or other specialist result when the relied-on condition requires it. Marketing coordination does not create that authority.

Where people disagree about the intended result, return the disagreement to the offer rather than concealing it in acquisition language. A purchaser's lower price and an operator's required support can be reconciled by a smaller scope, supplied by another arrangement or remain incompatible. The customer need not accept the provider's preferred compromise.

##### MKT.4:4.3.1 - Choose how to work on the criteria

Recover the result each criterion protects, who judges it, its importance and the grounds for comparing the options. Keep three judgements separate: importance to the customer, this offering's fit and another option's fit. Being better than a competitor does not establish adequacy. Include keeping the current arrangement and another way of doing the work among the alternatives.

Enter through the customer's actual question. An experienced buyer may already have criteria and reasonably want to discuss them before describing needs. Examine how those criteria serve the work; do not insist on restarting a discovery script. A screening condition may exclude an offer from one small purchase or from all future consideration. If the exclusion is justified and no worthwhile attainable change is available, accept it. When a consequential exclusion rests on a mistaken comparison or a revisable preference, ask the responsible buyer whether its basis can be examined. A seller's interest supplies no permission to bypass a binding condition.

Choose the work from what is missing:

| Situation | Useful move and its result |
| --- | --- |
| A useful need has been established but is absent from the comparison. | Derive a criterion from that need: what would distinguish an adequate result, under which conditions, and how could the customer judge it? Confirm it with the affected people. |
| An important criterion is understood and an option meets it. | Make its warranted importance and that option's fit inspectable. Carry the same meaning and evidence into the later decision; repetition of a product claim is insufficient. |
| A potentially consequential difference receives little attention. | Find out whether it was never considered or deliberately set aside. Examine the relevant consequence and evidence. Restore its importance only if that changes a justified comparison; retain the customer's reason for setting it aside when that reason still holds. |
| An important criterion is not met by the present offering. | Establish whether a different comparison or provision can meet the receiving need. Use the alternatives below; retain another provider, deferral and refusal as possible answers. |

These are alternatives that can combine. They have no compulsory order. Start with a consequential question that is both answerable and worth answering. Promoting a seller's strongest attribute has no value when it does not matter in the customer's work.

For an important criterion the present offer does not meet, examine four different ways forward:

| Way forward | Construction | Limit that changes the answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Change relative importance** | Establish another actual consequence and let the customer compare its importance with the original one. For example, an available adequate service may matter more than an extra feature whose benefit would arrive too late. | The original consequence remains in the comparison. A preferred advantage cannot cancel a protected minimum. |
| **Clarify or revise the criterion's meaning** | Recover the use behind the label. “Easy to use” might concern first setup, ordinary operation, correction or available support. Define the relevant distinction, make the evidence judgeable and return to its importance. | The new meaning needs the customer's grounds and agreement where it changes the comparison. Relabelling an unmet condition as a strength does not meet it. |
| **Compare an acceptable trade-off** | Show which combinations are actually attainable and what each gives up. Compare the same consequences for the affected people; ask who can accept the sacrifice. | Do not invent a trade-off where another attainable option meets both needs. Incompatible protected conditions require changed provision or a stop. |
| **Change how the result is supplied** | Construct another configuration, supplier, sequence or support arrangement that could meet the need. For example, local stock with replenishment can change the relation between manufacturing lead time and availability. | Obtain the supplying practices' answer about the whole arrangement, including resources, responsibilities and operating conditions, before presenting it as available. |

Often two moves are needed: clarifying “availability” can expose a provision problem; comparing a different configuration can reveal a permissible trade-off. Ask which move changes the next decision and carry its result into that decision. Stop when the available comparison is sufficient. Unresolved supply remains an explicit condition.

[PSDA.5](../DECISION-DESIGN-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#psda5---generate-decision-alternatives) supplies alternative construction where needed; [PSDA.6](../DECISION-DESIGN-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#psda6---represent-values-and-trade-offs) and PSDA.8 develop a disputed criterion or consequential trade-off. Use their substantive result in this choice. The repertoire above selects the needed work; it does not qualify technical performance, create a measurement scale or confer authority to revise a requirement.

##### MKT.4:4.3.2 - Prepare and revise a negotiable proposal

Use negotiation when the parties have some common basis for exchange, different interests over limited contributions, and discretion to change relevant terms. A ready acceptable purchase can proceed. Without a shared worthwhile possibility, more concessions need not produce one. Without discretion, obtain the appropriate decision or state the fixed terms.

First prepare what can be offered. Recover the customer's priorities from the comparison, including the need behind a stated position. A request for earlier delivery may concern the date work must start, a funding condition or an assumed transition time; these lead to different proposals. Ask rather than choosing the convenient explanation. Obtain economic, operating and other competent answers for terms whose effects are uncertain.

Keep distinct the outcome sought, the changes the representative may authorize, and the boundary beyond which an exchange is not worth accepting compared with an attainable alternative. Recover that alternative with the responsible party; it must be something they can actually do. Authorization to discount is not proof that every authorized price is economically acceptable. The customer has its own comparison and authority, which a seller's estimate cannot settle.

Use known needs, available competing offers and the consequences of rejection to select a realistic opening and a working range of proposals. A hoped-for maximum may be implausible; the largest allowed concession need not be offered. Too ambitious an opening can make a useful proposal unworthy of further attention; an unnecessarily low one can consume room to meet a later consequential need. These are reasons to prepare, not a formula for discovering the other party's limit. Keep an uncertain expectation uncertain.

Construct complete packages across issues when their values differ. The customer may prefer specialist installation or supported learning to a price reduction, while the provider may be able to supply that contribution for less than the revenue forgone through the discount. A smaller initial scope can offer another acceptable combination. Establish each party's comparison separately. Compare participation and actual provision through ECO.3/5. For each package, include the reciprocal contribution, timing, conditions and full support demand. Check concessions together: two individually affordable promises can consume the same scarce hours. Tie a conditional service to the condition it really needs, such as operation by prepared people; confirm that the customer can supply its part.

During the exchange, explain the proposal and inquire into the response. Show that a concern is understood without claiming agreement. Revise the part whose grounds have changed, then compare the whole revised package with the protected conditions and feasible alternatives. A new request outside discretion returns to the responsible owner; it is not a promise awaiting later approval. Pause, narrow, change counterparties or decline when no supported package is mutually acceptable.

Use the size and timing of revisions deliberately, but do not treat smaller successive concessions as proof of anyone's true boundary. Avoid trading away a condition merely to keep the conversation moving. Record and resolve consequential ambiguities while they can still change the decision: per unit versus per order, help available versus help included, a target date versus a committed date.

Return the understood proposed package to the people who can accept and supply it. Their commitments, the resulting contract or order and later performance are distinct results. Pass the agreed terms and remaining conditions to §§4.4–4.5. A material impossibility should be raised when discovered; waiting for a supposedly correct negotiating stage only increases wasted work.

#### MKT.4:4.4 - Construct the transition or bounded trial

Identify what must change from the current situation to usable supply: data or material preparation, access, handover, scheduling, installation, human learning, customer work and support. Ask which of these must precede use, which can occur independently and which share a scarce resource. Assign an attainable next action and receiving result; do not merely list departments.

Preserve valued current work until the selected change supports replacing it. The needed arrangement can be a parallel limited use, a staged switch, a smaller scope or continuation of the existing service. Its suitability depends on the actual service and constraints; a generic “free trial” does not decide them.

For a trial, state the unresolved question, permitted work and data, responsible participants, resource limit, observation, end and next choice. The trial should be able to return an unfavourable or inconclusive answer. Agree any continuing obligations explicitly instead of treating participation as an indefinite commitment. If the required preparation or safe return cannot be supplied, revise the proposed trial or stop it.

Use the actual supplying practices: engineering for a changed technical arrangement, Operations for executable work and capacity, OCE for organisational changes, HCD for needed human capability. Send each a bounded request such as “establish whether this permitted sample can be processed and inspected in the trial window”. Consume the returned condition in the choice. A promised later answer does not satisfy the current prerequisite.

#### MKT.4:4.5 - Complete the selected action and pass on what was agreed

When a suitable option is understood and its necessary conditions hold, make the selected action possible through the ordinary acquisition route. Supply the correct quotation, authorisation contact, payment or scheduling step and confirmation of the actual result. Remove a duplicate request when the needed fact is already available and usable.

Pass the delivery recipient the contribution and terms actually selected: scope, conditions, customer and provider work, timing, support and unresolved matters. Confirm that the recipient can use that information. A booked call is not a contract; an order is not delivery; payment is not achieved usefulness. Preserve these distinctions where they change what follows.

If the customer declines, record only the useful reason and permitted future contact condition, if any. If a decision is deferred, state the event or missing result that could reopen it. A purchasing window may warrant a later return; an unsuitable contribution requires a changed offer. Repeated reminders do not supply either change.

#### MKT.4:4.6 - Reopen the affected choice when conditions change

Check a newly changed prerequisite before acting on a previously valid choice. A different price, unavailable support, revised access rule or altered customer goal can require renewed comparison or agreement. Preserve valid decisions and fulfilled obligations; a new promotional programme does not cancel an existing supply commitment.

When learning about one transition exposes a recurring market difference, return it to [MKT.1](#mkt1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-customers). When misunderstanding recurs across cases, repair the offer through MKT.2. A local payment-system failure goes to its operating owner. The result determines the return, not a compulsory sales-stage order.

### MKT.4:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.4:5.1 - A user wants the trial; the organisation is not yet ready

In a stipulated Vector case, a reporting operator understands the bounded file-mapping offer from MKT.2. Purchasing can decide only at the next spending window, and the access owner has not approved a sample. Calling the organisation ready would hide two independent conditions.

The marketer explains the fixed trial scope and its end to purchasing, asks the access owner for the permitted-sample decision, and obtains the service owner's available window. The sample decision and purchasing preparation can proceed independently, but actual use requires both the agreed acquisition conditions and permitted data. The operator keeps the existing reporting procedure until the trial supplies an inspected alternative.

The first useful result is a conditional, scheduled choice with known recipients and return conditions. When the necessary answers arrive favourably, the ordinary acquisition completes and the service owner receives the agreed scope. If they do not, the marketer can offer a feasible later window or accept no trial now. A higher response rate to reminders would not settle the missing conditions.

#### MKT.4:5.2 - Change access after apparent agreement

Suppose the access owner allows only a different, insufficient data sample. Return to the trial's question: can it still be answered? A demonstration on generic data may explain the service but cannot establish compatibility with Vector's actual work. Offer that smaller result honestly or wait for another permitted basis. Retain the operator's understanding without calling the original trial possible.

In a routine consumer repair, the same person may choose, pay and bring the item. A clear quotation, attainable appointment and understood limits may be enough. Requiring separate stakeholder records, a new research study or a trial would make an already meaningful choice harder.

#### MKT.4:5.3 - A criterion changes the choice of work

A constructed warehouse case starts with two scanners compared by nominal scan speed. Dispatch staff explain that the consequential difficulty is correcting identifiers before the vehicle leaves. The supplier first asks how that work is performed and which sample would expose a wrong identifier. This develops a previously omitted criterion. It does not establish that any scanner prevents the error.

The buyer already values dependable support. The supplier makes the proposed response, its scope and available provider inspectable and asks whether the same meaning will be used by the purchasing group. This strengthens an existing justified criterion. A competing device is faster in a demonstration; whether that matters depends on the actual dispatch work, including correction and downtime. When the buyer has already tested speed and found both devices adequate, another pitch about speed contributes nothing.

A separate requirement now defeats the proposed arrangement: the warehouse needs replacement consumables within twelve hours, while the supplier's replenishment takes forty-eight. The customer explains that this is a protected continuity condition. Raising the importance of price or calling forty-eight hours “fast” does not resolve it. A proposed local stock becomes worth examining because the need concerns usable availability, rather than every delivery's travel time.

Operations returns a conditional stock-and-replenishment arrangement with the included demand, reorder trigger, responsibility and storage conditions. The customer must supply suitable storage and an assigned stock check. Compare this whole option with an alternative provider that can deliver within twelve hours and with retaining the current system. If the customer can and chooses to supply the stock work, the option can proceed to actual commitments. If storage is unavailable, the arrangement remains unavailable: retain the other alternatives. Clarification selected a different provision question; the resulting proposal did not create its own prerequisites.

#### MKT.4:5.4 - Two allowed concessions exceed the whole support capacity

In a constructed negotiation, a provider seeks €12,000 for a base implementation using twelve specialist hours. Its representative may offer the base down to €10,500, but the supplied economic comparison puts the acceptable lower boundary at €10,800 under the base conditions. The authorized minimum is therefore not the working lower boundary. The proposed customer use and available alternatives supply reasons to discuss the opening; they do not guarantee acceptance.

The customer asks for operator training and data migration. Each takes six additional hours from the same specialist. Twenty hours are actually allocated for the whole implementation in the required window. Each addition fits alone at eighteen hours; both need twenty-four. A list of permitted concessions would conceal that incompatibility.

The provider qualifies two complete packages: training with customer-supplied prepared data, or migration with an already qualified customer trainer. Price, economic acceptability, schedule and responsibilities are checked for each package; the base price boundary is not silently reused for changed work. The customer has no qualified trainer, but its data owner can supply the prepared data. The parties therefore compare the training package, priced at an accepted €11,400, with other suppliers and deferral. The customer authorizes that package and reserves operator time; the provider commits the eighteen hours. These are stipulated decisions, not an estimate of market willingness to pay.

If the data owner then cannot supply the data, retain the explained training need and reopen the package. Adding the six migration hours exceeds the allocated capacity. Obtain another qualified contribution, agree different timing or scope, or defer. Neither the customer's enthusiasm nor the seller's discount authority supplies those hours.

### MKT.4:6 - Bias-Annotation

A conversion target can encourage treating informed refusal as failure or hiding customer effort in a low initial price. Inspect whether the proposed improvement changes the customer's ability to choose and obtain a useful contribution, rather than only their likelihood of proceeding. Likewise, one enthusiastic contact can bias the account of organisational readiness; recover the other decisive conditions.

### MKT.4:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The current decision, relevant participants and attainable alternatives are known or explicitly limited.
- Misunderstanding, unsuitability, unavailable authority or resources, transition difficulty and operational failure receive distinct responses.
- The selected criterion work follows the customer's actual grounds; changed meaning or importance does not waive a protected condition.
- Negotiable packages distinguish aspiration, discretion and acceptability, and their contributions remain jointly available.
- Consequential terms, effort, timing and limits are understandable before the action that relies on them.
- A trial has a question, bounded arrangement, end and honest continuation or refusal.
- The selected action is attainable, and its actual result and agreed content reach the delivery recipient.
- Changed conditions reopen the dependent choice without erasing unaffected work or existing obligations.

### MKT.4:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Interpret every delay as a need for more persuasion. | Identify the actual unanswered condition and its competent owner. |
| Make a trial easy to enter by hiding the work needed to use or end it. | Explain the preparation, support, end and next decision with the offer. |
| Optimise a checkout while the offer promises an unavailable service. | Repair or reduce the proposal before improving acquisition. |
| Require every customer to traverse every sales stage. | Use the live prerequisites and skip a question already answered sufficiently. |

### MKT.4:9 - Consequences

The provider can distinguish a useful acquisition from mere progression through a sales system. The customer can receive a clearer comparison and a feasible transition, including a legitimate decision not to proceed. Coordination may slow an apparent quick sale while avoiding an unsupported commitment; ordinary adequate purchases remain light.

### MKT.4:10 - Architectural Rationale

Buying is work in its own right. Its result and prerequisites differ from performing the activity for which a product is acquired. This method therefore connects understanding, choice, commitment, transition and operational completion while permitting different orders and sufficient early stops.

ECO.3/5 explain the exchange and its terms; the supplying practices establish their contributions. The marketing method keeps those results usable by the actual deciding people. It does not take over contracting authority, technical delivery or the customer's subsequent use.

### MKT.4:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For understanding a stalled choice, **adapt** the [Christensen Institute's current JTBD account](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/): recover circumstances and transition rather than infer the decision from product attributes alone. This changes §§4.1–4.4. The serious lighter alternative is repairing a known operational obstacle directly; retain it when the obstacle is already established. Narrative inquiry costs more and is justified when the explanation could change the offer or transition. The source's case stories are not causal estimates of this method's effect. Reopen the explanation when contrasting choices defeat it.

For making a choice attainable, **adopt** ECO.3/5's developed comparisons of separate plans, terms and actual alternatives. Compared with treating every delay as friction, this adds explicit attention to contributions and protections that a step serves. The gain is a usable conditional choice; the cost is coordinating answers from people who may disagree or be unavailable. Legal validity of the proposed terms requires its own competent result.

The criterion repertoire in §4.3.1 adapts Rackham's historical *Major Account Sales Strategy*, Chapters 4–5. It preserves the outer choice of criterion work and the four ways to address a crucial unmet criterion; it changes the author's commercial selection pressure by requiring the customer's relevant grounds and attainable provision. Compared with an unstructured list of product strengths, it explains which work a comparison needs. That preparation is unnecessary when the criteria and available choice are already sufficient.

Chapter 7's range preparation and differently valued concessions inform §4.3.2. [Harvard's Program on Negotiation](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/batna/manage-the-tension-between-claiming-and-creating-value-nb/) distinguishes the attainable alternative, reservation boundary and aspiration. The receiving method also separates authority and joint provision. This adds preparation and supplier coordination; use ordinary fixed terms when they suffice. A sequence of decreasing concessions may signal a boundary, but is not evidence of that boundary or a universally effective tactic. Reopen a package when a relevant alternative, discretion, cost or supply condition changes.

### MKT.4:12 - Relations

[MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) supplies the coherent proposal and receives misunderstanding or unsuitable scope. [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) investigates an unresolved choice question. [MKT.1](#mkt1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-customers) receives a recurring difference that changes target selection.

MKT.6 addresses help in obtaining the useful result after or alongside acquisition; MKT.7 addresses continuation and ending relationships. MKT.11 coordinates promises and delivery, and MKT.14 can encompass several such choices within a programme of work with named organisations. One participant's progress does not advance every opportunity in that programme.

### MKT.4:End


## MKT.5 - Develop and Align a Customer Experience Concept

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.5:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when the way customers encounter a product or service is inconsistent with the intended relationship or promise, or when several contributors need a shared concept for a new experience. Examples include an offer of independent use that requires repeated staff intervention, a reassuring message followed by an unexplained wait, or a useful service made exhausting by its arrangements.

The result is an explicit experience concept connected to feasible conditions of use, communication, environment, interfaces and people's work, together with a way to discover whether the intended response occurs. An experience concept describes the character of the experience sought in the customer's situation, such as calm independent completion with attainable help. The provider can arrange contributing conditions; the customer's felt response remains a separate result to investigate.

The intended reader can describe the customer's activity and obtain design and operating contributions. A broad experience programme is unnecessary when a local known defect has a sufficient remedy and no consequential connection changes. Correcting a wrong opening time can remain an ordinary correction. Specialist design remains with the responsible practice.

### MKT.5:2 - Problem

Teams often replace an experience concept with “make customers happy,” a drawing of contacts, or a list of interface improvements. None determines what kind of experience is useful in the person's wider activity or whether the conditions can produce a coherent encounter. A polished interface can coexist with contradictory terms, unavailable assistance or incentives that defeat the promise.

Develop a specific concept from the customer's context, connect it to the whole relevant arrangement, and resolve contradictions through the responsible contributors. Separate the intended conditions, their actual performance, the person's response and the usefulness of the result.

### MKT.5:3 - Forces

| Tension | Required distinction |
| --- | --- |
| The provider needs a coherent direction; customers differ. | A concept for a stated situation, with relevant variation, rather than a universal desired feeling. |
| Memorable experiences can attract attention; ordinary use may need little attention. | Appropriate intensity and effort rather than constant stimulation. |
| Several organisations shape an encounter. | Conditions this provider can change, conditions it can coordinate and conditions outside its influence. |
| A local improvement can move effort elsewhere. | The consequence for the customer's activity and the other contributors. |
| Positive feelings, useful outcomes and satisfaction can accompany each other. | Separate questions and observations for each claim. |

### MKT.5:4 - Solution

#### MKT.5:4.1 - Recover the customer's wider activity and relevant expectations

Start before the provider's first contact. Ask what the person is trying to accomplish, what other activities compete for attention, what preparation and other services are involved, and what happens after this contribution. Recover the conditions that could change the concept: urgency, access, prior experience, uncertainty, the need for independence or help, and the consequences of failure. Investigate these as practical differences, not demographic stereotypes.

Use qualified existing evidence where it is sufficient. [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) can help resolve a consequential unknown about activity, expectations or experience. Specify whose account, which occasion and what time of recall it concerns. A survey after a whole service can answer a different question from a report during one encounter; neither automatically reconstructs the other.

Choose the reach needed for this decision. Include the other people, providers and customer work that can defeat the proposed experience. Do not attempt to map a person's whole life when only a few connections matter. Retain differences between a first encounter and familiar repeated use when they change what explanation or help is needed.

#### MKT.5:4.2 - Construct and compare concrete experience concepts

Describe the intended character of the encounter in ordinary terms and explain its value in the receiving activity. “Calm independent completion, with a visible way to obtain help” selects a different arrangement from “guided exploration with frequent discussion.” Both are more useful for design than “excellent experience.” An ordinary reliable transaction can be the appropriate concept; exceptional intensity is not an improvement by itself.

Construct at least the materially different alternatives that the unresolved choice needs. For each, describe a plausible encounter from the customer's position: what they would notice, understand, do, wait for, rely on and encounter if something went wrong. This short account exposes the conditions the concept requires. It also distinguishes a desired experience from a list of brand attributes or a provider's internal process.

Compare the alternatives against the customer's relevant expectations, useful result, effort and tolerable uncertainty, together with the provider's ability and constraints. Decide which differences warrant distinct arrangements. A single concept can allow both self-service and requested help; it cannot honestly promise independence if every case must finish through a telephone call. If alternatives remain uncertain, choose a bounded comparison that can change the decision rather than declaring one idea universally preferable.

#### MKT.5:4.3 - Connect the concept to conditions throughout the encounter

Translate the selected concept into the conditions needed to make the proposed encounter possible. Work from the customer's account in §4.2 and examine the connected contributions:

| Contribution | Construction question |
| --- | --- |
| Product or service result | What must actually work for the person, and what remains their own work? |
| Physical and digital environment | What access, privacy, orientation and resources does this encounter require? |
| Message and promise | What expectations are created before and during use, including qualifications and timing? |
| Interface and information | What can the person perceive, understand, control and recover from at the needed moment? |
| People's actions and support | Who helps, decides, responds or resolves an exception, under which conditions? |
| Organisation and partners | What assignment, capacity, incentives and partner contributions make those actions sustainable? |

For each consequential condition, distinguish an existing capability from a proposed change. Name who can supply or decide it and what result is needed. [SYSE.2](../Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/SYSTEMS-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#syse2---develop-linked-use-and-system-concepts) develops compatible use and system concepts; [SYSE.8](../Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/SYSTEMS-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#syse8---develop-an-integrated-offering-and-provider-concept-for-using-an-engineered-result) joins an offering with its actual or intended providers. They can return a feasible arrangement or a missing contribution, not merely a more attractive description.

Give the responsible practice a concrete request. “Enable the customer to save an incomplete application and resume it with its previous answers” is a design request with a recognizable use. “Be more empathetic” does not establish the interface, staff ability or available time. Operations must arrange performance; HCD can develop a needed capability; organisation change must obtain the relevant assignment and enabling conditions. Marketing coordinates the experience question and consumes those results.

#### MKT.5:4.4 - Find contradictions and select a feasible repair

Walk through the proposed encounter under an ordinary condition and an action-changing variation. Compare what each part leads the customer to expect with what the next part actually permits. Look for incompatible demands: a message invites speed while an irreversible choice needs consideration, the interface offers help outside support hours, or an operator is rewarded for ending a call before resolving the issue.

Trace a contradiction to the condition that produces it. Compare feasible repairs, including changing the promise, changing the product or operation, narrowing the circumstances served, or offering a different form of help. Include effects on other customers, staff work and already made commitments. Moving waiting time from a queue into repeated customer calls is not automatically an improvement.

Obtain agreement from those who can make the required changes, and update the affected promises and conditions of the encounter to match those changes. A concept can remain a useful proposal while a provider contribution is unresolved, but it must not be advertised as an available experience. If a limited arrangement can be supplied now, state its limits and preserve the wider concept as a separate possibility. MKT.11 governs coordination when several promises and contributors must be reconciled.

#### MKT.5:4.5 - Test the arrangement and investigate the response separately

Select a proportionate use of the arrangement that can expose its live uncertainty. A walkthrough can reveal a missing action or contradiction; a prototype can test understanding and interaction; actual service performance can reveal waiting, support and coordination failures. State which conditions are represented and which remain simulated. A script or prototype does not establish that an operating team can sustain the service.

Observe whether the planned conditions occurred and what the customer experienced. Ask questions appropriate to the concept without supplying the desired answer. For calm independent completion, relevant observations might include successful completion without imposed assistance, points where help was sought, and the person's account of confidence or anxiety at specified moments. Completion alone cannot establish calmness; reported calmness cannot establish a correct result.

Keep usefulness, effort, experience and later evaluation distinct where they matter. A demanding training session may be useful without being pleasant throughout. A pleasant contact may fail to solve the problem. If a causal claim about the intervention is needed, MKT.10 addresses that question; a satisfied participant after a trial is not sufficient evidence of causation or broad suitability.

#### MKT.5:4.6 - Revise the concept or its conditions from the result

Compare the intended encounter with the observed arrangement and response. If the conditions were not supplied, obtain or revise them before blaming the concept. If the arrangement worked but the intended response did not occur, investigate the context or assumption linking the conditions to that response. If the desired response occurred while the useful result failed, revise the contribution rather than treating the experience measure as success.

Return the selected concept, the connected feasible conditions, material limits, the observations and the next change. Reopen the construction when a new audience, channel, provider, access condition or operating constraint alters the encounter. Retain sufficient existing work when the change is local. A journey map through MKT.8 can help locate the relevant connection, but the repair remains a change in actual conditions or in the warranted promise.

### MKT.5:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.5:5.1 - Calm self-service with an unavoidable telephone call

Consider a constructed registration service used by working adults, often outside daytime support hours. Its message promises calm independent registration. The form can be saved, but final submission requires an operator's telephone call. Automated reminders say “Act now” even when no deadline is near. Operators are measured mainly by call duration and tend to close calls before resolving unusual cases.

The team first recovers the wider activity: registrants are arranging participation around work and need to know whether they have completed the required step. Two candidate concepts are considered: independent completion for ordinary cases with requested help, and scheduled guided registration. The first fits the ordinary situation if eligibility can be resolved without a mandatory call; the second remains useful for cases requiring assistance.

The selected arrangement lets an ordinary eligible person submit and receive a clear confirmation. Cases needing a decision show the unresolved matter and an attainable response time. Reminders name a real deadline when one exists; otherwise they explain the incomplete step without invented urgency. Help explains its available hours. Operations obtains responsibility and time for resolving exceptions, while the relevant manager changes the incentive that defeated that work. Engineering confirms which eligibility decisions the interface can support.

The team checks the connected encounter with an ordinary case and one requiring a decision. It separately observes the availability of confirmation, the actual handling of the exception, and the participants' accounts of uncertainty. A participant might still feel anxious because the programme affects an important career choice; correct registration does not place that response under the provider's control. MKT.12 can investigate a remaining consequential uncertainty without promising that the provider will eliminate all anxiety.

If support capacity later falls, a displayed same-day answer becomes unsupported. The team must restore the contribution, narrow the cases accepted or offer an honest later response. Editing the page to sound more reassuring would leave the contradiction intact. If only one displayed opening time was wrong and the arrangement remains adequate, correcting that time is sufficient.

#### MKT.5:5.2 - Useful effort need not feel effortless

A professional course aims for supported practice with candid feedback. Learners must do difficult exercises; promising an effortless transformation conflicts with the contribution. The experience concept can instead make the challenge understandable, the feedback usable and help attainable. HCD owns the learning construction and the evidence of capability. Marketing can improve the encounter and explain its demands without equating comfort, course completion and professional competence.

### MKT.5:6 - Bias-Annotation

Providers readily notice the contacts they own and the survey answers they can count. Start from the receiving activity to expose external conditions and the customer's own effort. Avoid assuming that the most intense, novel or immediately pleasant response is the most useful one. Ask whose experience the chosen evidence represents and what a different moment of observation could change.

### MKT.5:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The concept names an intelligible kind of experience for a concrete situation and practical purpose.
- Its conditions connect the relevant product, environment, messages, interfaces, people and organisation.
- Existing capabilities, proposed changes and conditions outside the provider's influence are distinguished.
- An ordinary case and a consequential variation expose incompatible expectations or actions.
- Responsible contributors can supply the selected changes, or the remaining limits are explicit.
- Supplied conditions, felt response, useful result and evaluation are judged at their actual scope.

### MKT.5:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Use “delight the customer” as the complete concept. | Describe the intended encounter and why its character helps this activity. |
| Improve one interface while keeping a contradictory operation. | Trace and change the contributing conditions together. |
| Treat a drawing of contacts as an implemented service. | Obtain the product, people, capacity and coordination that the encounter requires. |
| Read a satisfaction score as direct observation of every contact. | Match the evidence to the person, occasion, timing and question. |
| Promise a feeling that depends on the person's wider circumstances. | Promise the supported contribution and investigate the response. |

### MKT.5:9 - Consequences

A shared concept can guide coherent changes across contributors and reveal when the honest promise must become narrower. It can also make ordinary reliable service a deliberate choice. The work costs more than a local cosmetic correction and cannot remove all variability in experience. Its value is the better connection between the intended encounter, feasible conditions and evidence of the result.

### MKT.5:10 - Architectural Rationale

Customer experience is wider than the provider's interface and is produced in a person's encounters within a broader situation. A provider therefore needs both constructive design of contributing conditions and inquiry into the response. Keeping these contributions distinct makes correction possible: a missing condition, a mistaken expectation and a different felt response do not call for the same remedy.

The concept joins contributions without taking over their professional methods. It can select a direction for engineering, operations and learning work, while their actual results determine what can be promised and supplied.

### MKT.5:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For constructing a whole experience concept, **adapt** the historical Customer Experience Management treatment in [Schmitt, Experience Marketing, §5.3, pp. 88–90](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/5678/experience_marketing.pdf). Its move from wider customer context to a concept and its expression in products, environments, communications and organisational conditions supplies more construction than a contact list. The cross-contributor work is warranted when those conditions interact; a local sufficient repair is cheaper when they do not.

For the meaning and limits of that construction, **adopt** the distinctions in [Becker and Jaakkola, 2020, Premises 1, 2 and 4](https://pure.hanken.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/42844202/2020_Becker_Jaakkola.pdf). They distinguish the customer's response from stimuli and evaluation, place experience beyond firm-owned contacts, and limit provider control to contributing conditions. This later synthesis corrects an overextended interpretation of experience management. Neither source proves that this concept will cause a particular response in a new setting. Reconsider the concept when observed response or changing conditions defeat its grounds.

For a feasible offering and providers, **adopt** the SYSE.2 and SYSE.8 constructions used in §4.3. They expose what a coherent experience description leaves undelivered. The trade-off is explicit design and coordination effort rather than assuming that aligned wording creates an aligned service.

### MKT.5:12 - Relations

MKT.2 supplies and receives the offer and its implications. MKT.6 develops the useful result and the customer's contributions; MKT.7 handles continuation or recovery. MKT.8 can represent relevant encounters for a decision, MKT.11 coordinates contributors and promises, and MKT.12 supplies evidence about context and response. MKT.10 addresses a causal or resource conclusion when needed.

SYSE supplies linked use, system and provider concepts. Operations supplies actual performance and exception handling; HCD supplies needed capability; organisation change supplies enabling arrangements. A service description does not by itself establish delivery or the customer's response.

### MKT.5:End


## MKT.6 - Jointly Shape a Useful Result and Help Achieve It

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.6:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when the useful result of a proposed contribution is unclear or contested, or when an acquired product or service is not yet producing that result. The work can begin before an offer is made: the people involved must discover what is worth pursuing and what each can contribute. It also applies when changed circumstances make a previously agreed result unsuitable.

The result is an agreed, bounded useful outcome or a clearly limited contribution, connected to feasible customer and provider work, help and a way to reconsider the arrangement. A product supplied, an account activated, attendance completed and a useful result achieved are different events. This method connects them where a supported connection exists.

The intended reader knows the subject contribution and can involve the people whose goals, authority and work matter. This method does not confer competence to set another person's life goals, provide specialist counselling or perform all the customer work. When a known customer requests a suitable ordinary contribution and its use is settled, supply that contribution; a programme of joint goal discovery would add unnecessary effort.

### MKT.6:2 - Problem

A provider can assume that purchase reveals the customer's goal and that product use proves success. Yet the purchaser may seek a lower fee, the user less repeated work, and the manager more dependable completion. The proposed service may contribute to one of these while increasing another person's burden. A customer may also seek a wider change that no single supplier can deliver.

Construct a meaningful receiving result with the relevant people before turning it into a promise. Connect the agreed contribution to access, the customer's own work, capability, assistance and other providers. Use actual progress and changed goals to continue, revise, narrow or end the arrangement.

### MKT.6:3 - Forces

| Tension | What must remain explicit |
| --- | --- |
| Agreement helps coordination; participants can have different goals. | The limited common result and any remaining distinct interests. |
| A broad ambition gives direction; the supplier controls only part of its achievement. | The contribution that can be promised and the work or conditions it depends on. |
| Early specificity helps action; the goal can become clearer through use. | A usable present agreement and the condition for reopening it. |
| Guidance can help the customer; collaboration has a cost and requires willingness. | A proportionate form of participation or a narrower offer. |
| Product activity is visible; useful change may be delayed or occur elsewhere. | Evidence of the useful change at the relevant time, sufficient for the receiving decision. |

### MKT.6:4 - Solution

#### MKT.6:4.1 - Recover goals, roles and the activity in which a result would matter

Identify who will do the work, receive its effects, decide, pay and authorize access. Ask what each is trying to accomplish, what currently happens, what difficulty matters and what would make a change worthwhile. Recover a concrete occasion or work situation so that an attractive abstract goal can be related to actual activity. Reuse an adequate existing account; involve additional people only where their conditions could change the proposal.

Distinguish a broad ambition, a nearer useful result and the supplier's possible contribution. A career change can motivate learning; demonstrating a particular capability can be a nearer result; a course can supply instruction and practice. The course is neither the whole career change nor evidence that the capability has been obtained.

Do not force the answers into one indicator. Record a disagreement in ordinary terms and locate its practical consequence. A purchaser's fee limit and a user's need for help may constrain different parts of the same proposal. If the people with necessary authority or knowledge are unavailable, determine whether a smaller contribution can be agreed; otherwise return the missing decision or evidence instead of inventing their assent.

#### MKT.6:4.2 - Construct worthwhile alternatives before selecting a promise

Use the recovered situation to describe a worthwhile problem and candidate contribution. [FPF C.40.PC](../../FPF-Spec.md#c40pc---construct-a-worthwhile-problem-and-proposed-contribution) supplies that construction when the connection is missing. Start from the present activity and ask which change could make the desired result more attainable. Include changes to the customer's work and other contributors, not only different packages of the provider's existing product.

Build materially different directions when the choice requires them. For each, explain what would change from the present situation, how that change contributes to the receiving result, who must do what, which supports it needs and what remains outside. [FPF C.40.DC](../../FPF-Spec.md#c40dc---construct-directions-with-support-configurations) develops a direction together with those supports and permits comparison on a common basis. Merely listing a basic, premium and enterprise price does not supply different ways of achieving the result.

Compare the directions against the important goals and constraints. Include leaving an adequate current arrangement in place. Identify the reason to prefer a direction, the conditions that could defeat it and any conflict it leaves unresolved. A feasible limited result can be valuable without reconciling every participant's ambition. A proposal that depends on an unavailable contribution must become conditional, be narrowed, or return to its responsible practice for construction.

#### MKT.6:4.3 - Agree what will count as useful and what is actually promised

State the receiving result so that the people involved can recognize its achievement or a meaningful shortfall. Specify the subject, circumstances and useful difference; choose an observation appropriate to that difference. Recover a relevant starting condition when comparison with the present matters. The result may require a qualitative judgement, a direct work result or several observations rather than a single number.

For example, “the operator can prepare the agreed report from approved inputs without re-entering the same fields” identifies an activity and change. It does not guarantee that the report's underlying data are correct, that preparation time falls by a particular amount or that managers use it well. Add such claims only with the required contribution and evidence. A system login would establish much less than the proposed result.

Agree the provider contribution, customer and partner work, necessary access and support, timing, costs or consideration, and what happens when a required condition is absent. Ask the relevant people to explain the arrangement and their next action in their own terms. Resolve incompatible interpretations before making the promise. [MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) can then express the supported offer, and [MKT.4](#mkt4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible) can arrange its feasible acquisition.

If the customer does not want this form of collaboration, offer a suitable narrower contribution with clear limits or end the proposal. Consent to a purchase does not authorize an open-ended programme of goal intervention. An ordinary well-specified purchase may already contain enough agreement for use.

#### MKT.6:4.4 - Join access, capability, actual work and help

Construct the route from the acquired contribution to the receiving result. Work through a representative use: what must be available, what the customer does, what the supplier does, what a partner provides, and where a decision or exception can stop progress. Give each consequential gap a concrete request and an appropriate receiving practice.

| Gap | Needed contribution |
| --- | --- |
| The required product or service does not exist. | Engineering develops a feasible contribution and the conditions of its supply. |
| A required enabling condition is unavailable: materials, system access, working time or a decision from an authorized person. | The responsible authority or organisation obtains the specific enabling condition. |
| A person cannot yet perform the required activity. | HCD develops the needed capability with suitable practice and evidence of performance. |
| A known action or service is not being carried out. | Operations arranges the actual work, performer and continuation. |
| Several people's work conflicts or is unassigned. | Organisation change and the responsible managers establish compatible roles and conditions. |
| The useful result or its grounds remain unknown. | The relevant participants resolve the choice, or MKT.12 qualifies the needed inquiry. |

Specify help around the actual point of difficulty. Identify how the customer can recognize the need, reach competent assistance and continue or stop while waiting. A help article can explain an action; it does not establish that the user can perform it, has permission or has time. Escalating every problem to training can hide missing design, access or capacity.

Set the effort at a level the parties can sustain. Preserve the customer's own necessary work and the supplier's obligations. Making the supplier silently perform all practice can produce a finished artifact while leaving the customer's intended capability unchanged. Conversely, assigning an unavailable supplier contribution to the customer by calling it “engagement” does not repair the service.

#### MKT.6:4.5 - Continue from evidence of progress and the current goal

Choose observation and contact points where a result could change the next move: first meaningful use, a known difficult step, a relevant work cycle or a reported exception. The timing follows the activity rather than a universal schedule of compulsory meetings. Observe whether the agreed contributions occurred, what changed in the receiving activity and how the participants now judge its usefulness.

Distinguish failure to supply a condition from inability to perform, a mistaken contribution and a changed goal. If access was never obtained, more reminders to use the product are unlikely to help. If access and competent use occurred but the result is unsuitable, reopen the contribution or goal. If the result is useful but maintaining it requires unsupported work, change the arrangement before scaling it.

Use [OPS.6](OPERATIONS-MANAGEMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#ops6---continue-cases-and-handle-exceptions) when a concrete case needs an executable continuation or a precise unmet condition. Use a progress record as evidence only for the claims supported by its observations and source. Use MKT.10 only when the decision depends on the intervention's causal contribution or costs; a useful observed outcome does not establish that the provider alone caused it.

#### MKT.6:4.6 - Reframe, narrow or close the contribution when conditions change

When the goal changes, return to the affected connection. Reconsider the contribution, support and promise, not automatically every earlier inquiry. Obtain agreement for changed work and limits. A newly important result can call for another provider or a different customer group; return those consequences to MKT.1/2/5/11 as appropriate.

Conclude with the useful result achieved and its evidence, a feasible next contribution, an explicitly limited result, or a justified pause or ending. Name remaining work and obligations so that a completed supplier deliverable does not imply that the wider goal has been fulfilled. MKT.7 handles the relationship decision. A good ending may be the correct result when the provider's contribution is complete or no longer appropriate.

### MKT.6:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.6:5.1 - Three goals behind one reporting request

In a constructed case, a purchaser at Sever asks for a cheaper reporting tool, an operator wants to stop repeated entry, and a director wants dependable reporting before a meeting. The existing report is usable but preparation is laborious. The provider could sell access to a tool immediately, yet access alone would not settle which result the offer should achieve.

The participants compare three directions: retain the current tool and improve the agreed input procedure; run an assisted file-mapping trial; or commission continuous integration with the required access and support. The third promises a broader change but needs a contribution that is not available for the next cycle. The first costs less preparation but leaves repeated entry. The assisted trial can investigate whether one supported mapping removes that work for an agreed report.

They agree on a limited result for the next cycle: the operator prepares that report from approved inputs without re-entering the same mapped fields. The purchaser's spending limit remains a constraint. The director accepts that the trial does not yet promise dependable reporting for every subsequent meeting. The operator supplies one approved sample and participates in the mapping session; the provider supplies the supported mapping and assistance. Data validity and the authority to use the sample remain separate necessary conditions.

Before the trial, the operator can explain which inputs are permitted and what the session should produce. During use, the team observes the mapping, the operator's report preparation and the remaining manual work. Successful login is not their result. If the operator does not know how to distinguish the current approved input from an obsolete version, that precise capability needs instruction and a performance check; a general product tour would miss the difficulty.

Suppose the customer later changes the required report and no longer needs the original mapping. The team first establishes the new receiving result and whether the existing contribution can support it. More reminders to use the old mapping would not restore usefulness. If the changed request requires unavailable integration, the provider can offer another bounded contribution or help end the trial with its completed work and limits clear.

#### MKT.6:5.2 - A course inside a professional change

A person approaches a course provider with “I want a different profession.” The provider helps distinguish possible receiving results: explore the work before committing, acquire a particular capability, or obtain help with a job search. Instruction, the learner's own practice, employment opportunities and recruitment are different contributions. Their comparison may lead to a small exploratory activity before an expensive programme.

If they agree on developing a capability, HCD supplies the learning method and evidence of performance. The provider arranges access, practice and suitable help; the learner supplies the agreed effort. Completion of the course does not establish employment or the whole life change. If the person seeks only a known lesson, the provider can supply it without requiring disclosure or reconstruction of broader personal goals.

### MKT.6:6 - Bias-Annotation

Providers can define success by the activity their product records or by the result they already sell. Customers can also state an ambitious goal before discovering its cost or required work. Compare the proposed contribution with actual alternatives and ask what would make it no longer useful. Preserve disagreement long enough to affect the proposal instead of relabelling it as insufficient commitment.

### MKT.6:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Relevant goals, roles and action-changing differences are recovered before a definite promise depends on them.
- The selected direction explains how a contribution can change the receiving activity and what supports it.
- The agreed result has appropriate recognition conditions and does not silently stand for the whole broad ambition.
- Provider, customer and partner work, access, capability and help are feasible or explicitly unresolved.
- Actual progress and usefulness are distinguished from purchase, activity counts and supplied artifacts.
- Changed goals can revise the contribution, promise and relationship without forcing continued use.

### MKT.6:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Translate every customer goal into the product's usage metric. | Recover the receiving activity and an observation of the useful difference. |
| Sell first and assume all parties want the same outcome. | Discover consequential differences and agree a limited supported contribution. |
| Assign every stalled use to customer motivation. | Check access, design, capability, actual supply and the current goal. |
| Present a list of suppliers as a complete support arrangement. | Obtain each necessary contribution and connect it to the relevant work. |
| Keep a completed or unsuitable programme active to preserve renewal. | Reconsider the useful next contribution and permit a good ending. |

### MKT.6:9 - Consequences

The parties can make a more accurate promise and direct help to the actual missing contribution. They may discover that a smaller offer, another provider or no purchase is appropriate. Joint construction takes willingness, time and relevant knowledge; it should be proportionate to the uncertainty. Agreement improves coordination but does not prove future achievement or eliminate conflicting interests.

### MKT.6:10 - Architectural Rationale

Useful results arise in the customer's activity through several contributions, including the customer's own work. A marketing relationship can help identify and connect those contributions, but it cannot make them exist through an outcome statement. Keeping goal formation before the promise allows the offer itself to change when its original premise was mistaken.

The same method supports later revision because useful goals and feasible supports can change independently of a product's availability. Acquisition, learning, operating work and relationship continuation therefore remain distinct contributions with explicit receiving uses.

### MKT.6:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For relating an offer to broader and nearer customer goals, **adapt** [Becker, Jaakkola and Halinen, 2020, §§6–7](https://pure.hanken.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/42844094/2020_Becker_et_al..pdf). The selected contribution places customer activity, several providers and the person's own work inside the account of goal pursuit. It improves a provider-only sequence, while its particular empirical setting does not establish one universal hierarchy of goals or authorize intervention in another person's life.

For joint goal identification and operationalization, **adapt** the Goal framing treatment in [Customer success management, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-025-01121-5). The selected qualitative B2B account, weighted toward SaaS settings, supports asking how customer and provider identify and revise goals together. Its assumptions about willingness to collaborate limit the transfer; performance effects and universal applicability are not established by that account. A narrow agreed service is preferable when wider collaboration is unnecessary or unwanted.

For the missing construction before a promise, **adopt** the C.40.PC and C.40.DC contributions and join them with the actual capability and operating work. They make the problem, alternative direction and supports inspectable instead of treating “agree on success” as a complete instruction. Reopen the selected direction when the customer's goal, required work or available support changes.

### MKT.6:12 - Relations

MKT.1 receives changed differences between customer situations; MKT.2 expresses the agreed contribution; MKT.4 supports choice and acquisition; MKT.5 aligns the encounter. MKT.7 handles useful continuation or ending, MKT.11 coordinates promises and contributors, and MKT.12 supplies evidence for a live unknown. MKT.13 can investigate whether the resulting commercial work is repeatable.

C.40.PC and C.40.DC supply worthwhile problems and development directions with supports; Strategy can relate a selected direction to capabilities and initiatives when the work is strategic. HCD, Operations and organisation change supply actual learning, performance and enabling conditions.

### MKT.6:End


## MKT.7 - Continue, Restore or End a Customer Relationship

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.7:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when the next useful contribution in a customer relationship is uncertain, an expected continuation does not occur, or a failure requires recovery. It covers continuing a suitable arrangement, changing it, pausing, restoring a damaged contribution and ending the relationship or a particular part of it.

The result is a justified, permissible next contribution or ending, agreed to the extent the parties' decisions require, with responsibilities, remaining obligations and evidence of what actually happened. Relationship continuation is not the same as another sale. A completed one-off contribution can end well; a continuing relationship can contain a long interval without a purchase.

The intended reader can recover the customer's current situation, commitments and relevant performance, and can obtain decisions from the responsible people. A known ordinary renewal under unchanged conditions can use its existing arrangement. A fully completed transaction with no unresolved obligation does not automatically need a retention programme. This method does not determine legal remedies or override the customer's choice about further participation.

### MKT.7:2 - Problem

Teams often treat every absence of purchase as departure and every departure as a failure to persuade. They can then send unsuitable offers, confuse a service remedy with a new sale, or seek renewal after the contribution has ceased to be useful. Conversely, closing a sales opportunity can hide unfinished support or other commitments.

Recover what the relationship is for now, distinguish the reason for the uncertain continuation and select the corresponding work. Consider the prospective consequences without using past acquisition effort as a reason to impose further activity. Preserve duties that remain after a new sale or service period ends.

### MKT.7:3 - Forces

| Tension | What must remain distinct |
| --- | --- |
| The provider values continuity; the customer's need can end or change. | Useful continuation and renewal pursued for its own sake. |
| A complaint can reveal a failure or a changed expectation. | Restoring a promised contribution and negotiating a different one. |
| Commercial history informs the decision; past expenditure cannot be undone. | Historical economics and the consequences of today's alternatives. |
| Several relations can involve the same people. | Distinguish paid service, voluntary cooperation and community participation by the agreements or rules governing each. |
| A relationship can end while obligations remain. | Future optional work, unfinished commitments and the actual completion of closure. |

### MKT.7:4 - Solution

#### MKT.7:4.1 - Identify the actual relationship and the event needing a decision

Name the contribution, customer situation and period concerned. Identify the people receiving the result, deciding continuation, paying and providing the work. Recover what is currently agreed, which contributions have occurred, what remains open, and which contact or next action is appropriate under the existing arrangement.

Describe the event without prematurely interpreting it: a service period is ending, a report was not delivered, the customer declined an invitation, an expected purchase did not occur, or the customer requested a pause. Then determine whether the event is actually unusual for this relationship. A quarterly need cannot be judged by a monthly repurchase expectation. A contact changing jobs can interrupt communication without changing the organisation's need.

Use [Account for Customer and Product Economics Over Time (MA.8)](MANAGEMENT-ACCOUNTING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md) when timing, cohorts or future economics affect the decision. Preserve who was eligible for renewal, the observation period and the meaning of nonpurchase. Do not infer an individual customer's reason from a population retention rate.

#### MKT.7:4.2 - Recover current usefulness and the action-changing cause

Ask what useful result the customer still seeks, whether the contribution is helping, what changed and what continuation the customer wants. Combine the customer's account with relevant performance and commitment evidence. Distinguish an observation from an interpretation: an unused feature may be unnecessary, difficult, inaccessible or no longer suited to the goal. Use [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) for a consequential unknown that existing information cannot settle.

Select the needed depth. An explicit request to end an optional service can settle that decision without a compulsory interview. A serious ambiguous service failure may need competent investigation before a remedy is promised. A known scheduling gap can need only a later agreed contact. Ask only for information that can change a legitimate next action.

Separate the following working situations, allowing more than one where the evidence supports them:

| Situation | Work it calls for |
| --- | --- |
| The contribution remains suitable and continuation is wanted. | Confirm the next scope, conditions, timing and available supply. |
| The contribution is useful but a temporary condition blocks it. | Arrange a feasible pause or limited continuation and a meaningful return condition. |
| The customer's goal or situation has changed. | Reconsider the result and offer through MKT.6 or MKT.2. |
| A promised contribution failed. | Restore what can be restored and resolve the remaining consequences through the responsible operation and authority. |
| The work is complete, unsuitable or no longer wanted. | End the relevant part clearly and fulfil remaining obligations. |

If the cause is unknown, return that uncertainty rather than selecting a persuasive response by default. Several paths can proceed together: an old failure can require resolution even when the customer declines all future work.

#### MKT.7:4.3 - Construct the relevant continuation, recovery or ending

For continuation, connect the current useful result to the next feasible contribution. Confirm the needed customer work, provider capacity, support, access, terms and time. A familiar name for the service does not establish that its conditions are unchanged. Let a changed contribution reopen the offer and its dependent promises.

For a pause, identify what actually pauses and what continues: access, reserved capacity, communication, charges or support can have different arrangements. State the return condition, such as a resumed work cycle or a customer request, and who may initiate it. Avoid turning a pause into repeated unsolicited invitations. If an indefinite pause cannot be supplied, explain the feasible alternatives instead of silently retaining an unavailable arrangement.

For recovery, first identify the failed commitment and the affected result. Distinguish an immediately attainable correction, remaining harm or inconvenience, and any question that needs a competent decision. Arrange the actual restorative work and communicate its supported scope and timing. [OPS.6](OPERATIONS-MANAGEMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#ops6---continue-cases-and-handle-exceptions) can supply a concrete continuation with performer, conditions, evidence and fallback, or return the exact unmet condition. An apology or concession may matter but does not perform a missing service.

Do not make restoration conditional on buying more when the restoration is already owed under the applicable arrangement. A new useful proposal can be considered separately after its conditions are clear. If the original result cannot be restored, obtain the appropriate decision about an alternative or remedy instead of promising a fictional repair. Legal or specialist questions go to their competent source.

For ending, distinguish the part that ends from work that must still be completed. Arrange any required handover, return or export, final communication, access change, support and other outstanding contributions under the governing terms. Identify who performs each necessary action and what will establish completion. A closed customer record does not itself return working material, stop a charge or fulfil a support commitment.

#### MKT.7:4.4 - Compare prospective consequences and respect different relations

Compare only the alternatives that are appropriate and feasible. Consider the customer's useful result and burden, the provider's ability and incremental effort, future receipts or costs at their relevant times, and the remaining commitments under every alternative. Use MA.8 to keep the chosen horizon, dated flows, later obligations and uncertainty visible. A forecast of future activity is conditional, not an observed lifetime value.

Keep past acquisition expenditure in the historical account without making it a new avoidable cost. Conversely, do not remove a prior support obligation from the ending alternative merely because no future sale occurs. These distinctions can change whether continuation is feasible or worthwhile for the provider; they do not override a customer's decision to decline it.

When the same people also cooperate in a community or another shared activity, use [ECO.8](../ECONOMIC-REASONING-AND-COORDINATION-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#eco8---join-direct-cooperation-with-a-wider-economic-order) to preserve the relations and their authority. A volunteer's willingness to help does not amend a paid service obligation, and purchasing a course does not oblige someone to participate in a community. Choose the actual agreement or rule that governs each contribution instead of using personal loyalty to blur the boundary.

#### MKT.7:4.5 - Agree and perform the next action

Present the relevant alternatives, material conditions and practical consequences in language the customer can use. Make the selected action and any refusal or deferral effective through the applicable means. Obtain a decision from the person entitled to make it; a user who reports a difficulty may not be the person who can renew an organisational contract.

Arrange the selected work with its responsible people, attainable timing and fallback. Tell the customer what will happen, what they need to do, what remains uncertain and how to obtain a relevant update. The amount of communication follows the actual work; a simple confirmed cancellation does not need a series of retention conversations.

After performance, establish what changed. Recover direct evidence of the completed contribution, restored result, effective pause or ending and any obligation still open. Ask about the customer's resulting situation when it matters and is appropriate. Do not infer restored trust from a completed ticket or treat acceptance of a refund as a request to resume the service.

#### MKT.7:4.6 - Return the result and improve the affected arrangement

Return the relationship's current practical condition: what continues or ends, what was fulfilled, what remains to be done and what event could justify another decision. Keep unknown reasons unknown. A legitimate refusal or a completed useful result can close the present work without predicting whether the person will ever return.

When evidence reveals a repeated mismatch, send its substance to the owning method. MKT.2 can narrow a misleading offer, MKT.5 can repair contradictory experience conditions, MKT.6 can reconsider the useful result, and MKT.11 can restore the contributor arrangement. Population-level economics or a causal claim about a retention intervention needs its own adequate comparison through MA.8 or MKT.10. One recovered relationship does not establish that a general programme works.

### MKT.7:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.7:5.1 - A completed trial, a pause and a failed continuation

Consider a constructed assisted reporting trial at Vector. The agreed mapping and supported report preparation are complete. The customer will next need this report in a later work cycle and declines an immediate continuation. A monthly sales view marks the absence of a new purchase as loss; the actual relationship gives no monthly renewal expectation.

The provider asks the limited question that can change the next action: whether a later contact about the next reporting cycle would be useful. The customer agrees to one contact near that cycle. The parties clarify that no current session or paid reservation continues. Work already owed under the trial, including the agreed handover, still has to finish. The completed trial, temporary absence of need and permissible later invitation are separate facts.

For an illustrative comparison over the next service period, suppose continuation would bring 300 monetary units of receipts and require payments of 190 for delivery and 40 for new support. Ending would require a payment of 20 for closure work. A payment of 15 for an old support commitment remains under both alternatives. With these amounts falling within the period and no other flows assumed, the forecast net cash flows are 55 for continuation and −35 for ending, a difference of 90. Earlier acquisition expenditure of 600 belongs in the historical account and is not added again to either future alternative. These assumptions help the provider assess a possible offer; they do not create current need or authorize charging the customer during the pause.

When the customer later chooses a new period, a provider error makes the agreed mapping unavailable. A new sales offer does not repair that failure. The provider identifies the failed contribution, obtains an attainable restoration from Operations and communicates its timing. If restoration cannot support the customer's actual reporting deadline, the responsible people must consider a feasible alternative or remedy under the agreement. The original task remains unresolved until that work or its agreed disposition occurs.

Suppose the customer chooses to end after the incident. The provider completes the required handover and outstanding support, makes the ending effective and records what actually occurred. The customer can decline a new offer while still receiving the owed contribution. A closed sales record or an apology alone would not establish this result.

#### MKT.7:5.2 - A community member who also buys a service

A learning community member purchases a separate mentoring service. They later stop the paid service but want to continue voluntary discussions. The provider identifies the paid sessions and any remaining obligation separately from community participation. Neither continued membership nor friendly cooperation supplies consent to further charges. The community's own participation rules still apply to its activities. If the member also leaves the community, each relevant relation can end on its own terms.

### MKT.7:6 - Bias-Annotation

Past effort can make a team overvalue continuation; a visible complaint can make it offer an expedient concession without repairing the failure. Recover the useful result and actual commitment before selecting a response. Preserve the denominator and timing of retention claims, and avoid interpreting a quiet customer through the stories supplied by the most vocal ones.

### MKT.7:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The relationship, contribution, people and period are specific enough to interpret the event.
- Current usefulness, wanted continuation, failed supply and changed goals remain distinguishable.
- The selected action has a feasible performer, conditions, timing and appropriate authority.
- Future consequences preserve dated costs and receipts, uncertainty and obligations under each alternative.
- Customer choice and separate paid or voluntary relations are respected in the actual arrangement.
- Evidence establishes the performed continuation, recovery, pause or ending and names any unfinished obligation.

### MKT.7:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Label any gap in purchasing as churn. | Recover the actual need, eligibility and expected timing. |
| Offer another sale before resolving a failed commitment. | Perform the owed restoration or obtain an appropriate remedy decision. |
| Count past acquisition expense as a cost that renewal can now avoid. | Compare the prospective alternatives while retaining the historical account separately. |
| Remove all obligations when a sales record is closed. | Identify and complete the duties that survive the ending. |
| Use community belonging or friendship to pressure a paid continuation. | Keep the relations, permissions and contributions distinct. |

### MKT.7:9 - Consequences

Continuation can follow actual usefulness, and recovery can address the failed contribution rather than merely its appearance. An ending can protect a useful completed result and clarify remaining work. The method may reduce immediate renewals and still improve the appropriateness of the relationship. It cannot guarantee restored trust, future purchase or the profitability of a general retention programme.

### MKT.7:10 - Architectural Rationale

A customer relationship contains contributions over time, changing circumstances and obligations that need not coincide with a sales period. Its continuation therefore requires an account of current purpose, actual work and permissible future contributions.

Restoration and renewal can be connected but answer different questions. The former addresses a failed commitment; the latter concerns a possible next contribution. Keeping them distinct allows the customer to end future work without making the provider's unfinished work disappear.

### MKT.7:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For a continuing useful result, **adapt** the joint goal identification and revision in [Customer success management, 2025, Goal framing](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-025-01121-5). It supports asking what the relationship should now contribute instead of treating use or renewal as the whole outcome. The qualitative B2B and predominantly SaaS basis, including willingness to collaborate, limits transfer. It does not establish a universal retention effect or a complete remedy method.

For temporal and economic judgement, **adopt** MA.8's distinction between observed customer or product histories, eligible continuation, conditional forecasts and prospective alternatives. It improves a single lifetime-value label when later support or closure work changes the decision. Its additional accounting is unnecessary for an ordinary settled ending whose economics do not affect the action.

For multiple relations between the same people, **adopt** ECO.8's separation of direct cooperation, exchange and wider rules. For actual recovery, use OPS.6's executable continuation and evidence. Together these contributions supply boundaries and working moves that a persuasive renewal script omits. Reconsider the arrangement when the goal, governing commitment, operating ability or relevant time horizon changes.

### MKT.7:12 - Relations

MKT.2 can change the offer, MKT.4 can make the selected continuation or refusal effective, MKT.5 can repair the encounter, and MKT.6 can reshape the useful result. MKT.11 coordinates required contributors, MKT.12 investigates a consequential unknown, and MKT.10 evaluates a claimed intervention effect or resource difference. MKT.14 can distinguish the continuation of several opportunities inside one organisation.

MA.8 supplies the temporal economic account; ECO.8 preserves different forms of cooperation and exchange; Operations supplies actual restorative and closing work. A marketing decision or updated customer record does not establish those results.

### MKT.7:End


## MKT.8 - Construct a Journey Map for a Specific Decision

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.8:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when understanding an activity across several contacts, people or periods could change a marketing decision. A customer may understand an offer but fail to obtain access, encounter conflicting promises from two providers, or return to an earlier question after beginning use. The map helps locate the connection that matters and choose an attainable change or a precise inquiry.

A journey map is a selective representation of the relevant activity and encounters, including their order, conditions and possible continuations. Its result is useful when someone can act on the represented difference. It can describe an observed history, compare possible future experiences or support a proposed intervention; keep these uses distinguishable.

The intended reader knows the activity and can obtain accounts from the relevant participants and providers. A known local defect with a sufficient direct remedy does not require a map. The method does not make the marketer responsible for a person's whole life, learning, employment or activity with other organisations.

### MKT.8:2 - Problem

A familiar stage diagram can hide the very difference that matters: the purchaser is not the user, contacts occur outside the provider's channels, several conditions coexist, or the customer returns and changes direction. A map can also mix what happened with what the team hopes will happen. An arrow then appears to explain a transition whose enabling work is absent.

Build the representation from a decision and qualified accounts of the relevant activity. Keep actors, observations, interpretations and proposed work recoverable. Use the map to obtain a change in the actual arrangement or an answer to the unresolved question.

### MKT.8:3 - Forces

| Tension | What the map must retain |
| --- | --- |
| A concise view is easier to use; omitted work can determine the result. | The connections that can change the decision and the consequential unknowns. |
| A shared account helps coordination; people's histories differ. | Differences that defeat a common intervention. |
| Sequence is visible; causation and feasible transition need more. | The conditions and actual work connecting events. |
| Providers can improve some conditions and only observe others. | Control, negotiated influence and conditions outside their reach. |
| A plan gives direction; customers can take another route. | Possible return, refusal, pause and alternative continuation. |

### MKT.8:4 - Solution

#### MKT.8:4.1 - Begin with the decision the map should change

Name the useful result at issue, whose activity receives it and which decision can change that activity. For example: should the provider change an invitation, obtain an access contribution, redesign the support handover or stop an unsuitable offer? Determine who will use the answer and what difference would lead them to another action.

Choose a sufficient reach in time and participants. Start far enough before the apparent problem to include its prerequisites, and continue far enough afterward to see the receiving use. Include a third-party approval, a customer's preparation or a later support need when it can change the decision. Stop expanding where an added contact cannot affect the present choice.

Select the intended use of the representation. A history asks what occurred. A possible route asks what could occur under stated conditions. A plan asks which actions someone will undertake. These accounts can be compared, but do not let the proposed route fill gaps in the historical evidence.

#### MKT.8:4.2 - Recover activity, encounters and consequential differences

Use sufficient existing accounts first. When evidence is missing, [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) supplies the decision-specific inquiry. Ask relevant participants about concrete occasions and connect their accounts to available work or contact evidence. Preserve who reported what, when, and what remains uncertain.

Recover the customer's own actions as well as encounters with the provider. Ask what the person was trying to accomplish, what they used, who else contributed, what had to become available and what happened when a condition failed. An activity need not produce a contact visible to the firm. Advice from a colleague or an internal budget decision can matter more than another visit to a page.

Distinguish the people doing, choosing, paying, approving and receiving the result. A purchaser's approval can occur while a user's access is still absent. For example, having the prerequisite skills, having access to instruction and wanting to buy can occur in different combinations. Do not assign a single stage to the whole customer when that would conceal the needed condition.

Compare more than one history when the planned intervention depends on a common route. Group histories only when their differences leave the proposed intervention suitable. Keep a separate branch or a narrower population when another person's circumstances change the work required. An account assembled from several people is a synthesis, not the observed history of one typical person.

#### MKT.8:4.3 - Choose a representation that exposes the live connection

Use the smallest form that makes the decision understandable. A short story can recover a sequence; a table can compare actors, conditions and evidence; a diagram can show alternative continuations or overlapping work. A graphical template is useful only if it preserves the distinctions needed here.

Make the meaning of the representation explicit. Identify the actors or activities represented by rows or nodes, what an arrow means, and how a reader can distinguish observed, reported, inferred and proposed content. An arrow for time order does not assert a cause; an arrow for a planned handover does not establish that the receiving team can perform it.

At each relevant connection, retain the following content in whatever form serves the reader:

- the activity or encounter and the participant concerned;
- the useful result or condition sought there;
- what is known about what occurred, with its relevant source and limits;
- the prerequisite, response or contribution needed for a continuation; and
- the party able to change that condition, or the fact that it lies outside the provider's control.

Add reported experience or evaluation when the decision concerns it. Keep these separate from an observer's account of performance. A long wait can be observed; whether it felt reassuring, frustrating or acceptable needs an appropriate account from the person. If recollection concerns the whole service, do not distribute that answer across individual contacts as though each had been measured.

#### MKT.8:4.4 - Check the proposed explanation at the consequential connections

Read the representation from the receiving activity, not only along the provider's preferred sequence. Look for an unexplained transition, missing participant, external contribution, simultaneous condition or return to an earlier question. Compare the picture with the underlying accounts and ask the relevant participants to correct consequential errors where possible.

Challenge the connection that would justify the intended intervention. If the map suggests that a customer stopped after a message, ask what establishes the reason and what else changed. Temporal proximity can direct an inquiry without identifying a causal effect. If an arrow implies a handover, recover what is transferred, who receives it, what they need and what happens if they cannot act.

Distinguish a missing observation from evidence that an event did not occur. A provider's record can omit contact with another firm or the customer's own work. Where that unknown can change the intervention, obtain the needed answer or qualify the decision. Filling the gap with a standard funnel stage would conceal it.

When the representation is about possible futures, test whether each proposed continuation has the needed contributions. Include an ordinary case and an action-changing variation, such as absent access, a changed goal or unavailable support. Retain a feasible current arrangement among alternatives. A route that requires a new service returns a construction question to MKT.9 and its engineering suppliers.

#### MKT.8:4.5 - Select an intervention, inquiry or justified non-change

Locate a change the responsible people can actually make. Describe the failed connection and compare suitable alternatives: revise the explanation, change an interface, obtain an approval, arrange help, alter supply, narrow the offer or leave an adequate arrangement in place. Include the customer's effort and the burden moved to other contributors.

Name the receiving method and result. [MKT.3](#mkt3---reach-a-suitable-audience-and-obtain-a-meaningful-response) can change communication and its response path; [MKT.4](#mkt4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible) can make a choice or transition feasible; [MKT.5](#mkt5---develop-and-align-a-customer-experience-concept) can align the experience conditions; [MKT.6](#mkt6---jointly-shape-a-useful-result-and-help-achieve-it) can reshape the useful result and its support. MKT.11 coordinates the required contributions. Use the located problem and its supporting evidence to make the needed change with the selected method and responsible contributors.

If a consequential cause or condition remains unknown, return a focused question instead of an intervention presented as a known remedy. If the decision requires a causal or resource comparison, MKT.10 develops it. An adequate map can also support retaining the existing arrangement when the apparent defect disappears under a better account.

#### MKT.8:4.6 - Revise from actual use without rewriting the past

After a selected change, obtain evidence of the altered arrangement and the receiving result at appropriate times. Compare it with the intended continuation. Keep the earlier history and the new observation distinguishable; a revised plan does not change what the earlier customer encountered.

Update only the reach affected by new evidence, a different customer situation or changed provider ability. State what the map can now support and what remains outside. Retire a view that no longer serves a decision rather than maintaining every contact indefinitely.

### MKT.8:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.8:5.1 - An apparently abandoned trial

In a constructed reporting-service case, a dashboard shows that Sever's purchaser read an invitation but no trial was booked. The team considers sending another invitation. Its live decision is whether to change communication or resolve a different condition before inviting again.

The provider reconstructs the relevant interval from the purchaser, operator and available correspondence. The purchaser understood the assisted scope and forwarded it internally. The operator found a suitable sample but could not obtain the required permission in time. A colleague recommended waiting for the next reporting period. That advice occurred outside the provider's channels.

| Relevant part of the account | Kind and grounds | Consequence for the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invitation received and forwarded | Correspondence supports these actions. | Another copy of the same explanation may add little. |
| Operator prepared a candidate sample | The operator reports the preparation; the sample's suitability is separately checked. | The operator's preparation and the purchaser's contact with the provider were separate actions. |
| Permission remained unavailable before the session window | The responsible authority confirms the unresolved condition. | The provider cannot offer that trial as immediately feasible. |
| Colleague advised waiting | Purchaser's account of an external encounter. | The observed pause is not evidence that the offer was misunderstood. |
| Later approved sample and session | Proposed continuation, not part of the observed history. | Its feasibility depends on actual permission and available support. |

The provider clarifies the permission question and offers a later session if the necessary contributions become available. MKT.4 handles the practical transition; MKT.11 obtains a support answer. If the purchaser instead confirms that unattended integration is essential, the receiving problem changes to offer suitability through MKT.2; resolving the sample permission would no longer rescue this proposal.

The provider records whether the later trial actually occurs and what its result establishes. It does not label the earlier pause as a completed trial or treat this one account as a universal route for all customers.

#### MKT.8:5.2 - A learner's activity exceeds a supplier's course

A learner combines a course, workplace practice, advice from colleagues and a later change of project. A provider wants to decide whether to add a practice consultation. The relevant map includes the work opportunity and the learner's own attempts because they determine whether consultation can help. It does not turn the course sequence into the person's whole learning history. HCD supplies the learning intervention; if the actual obstacle is absence of suitable work, another lesson may be the wrong contribution.

### MKT.8:6 - Bias-Annotation

Provider records favour visible contacts and successful completions. Teams can also make a synthesized persona unusually cooperative. Seek the action-changing work outside those records and preserve refusals, returns and unresolved conditions. Use a different history to challenge a claimed common route where that difference could alter the intervention.

### MKT.8:7 - Conformance Checklist

- A named decision determines the map's people, time reach and useful detail.
- Observed history, interpretation, possible continuation and planned work remain distinguishable.
- Different actors, customer work, relevant external encounters and unknowns are represented where they affect the decision.
- Arrows and grouping have explicit meanings that do not silently assert cause or control.
- A selected change has a responsible contributor and an attainable receiving result, or the missing question is returned.
- Later observations revise the applicable account without rewriting earlier events.

### MKT.8:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Fill a standard funnel before finding the problem. | Start from the decision and retain the activity that could change it. |
| Treat several people's accounts as one observed customer. | Mark the synthesis and preserve consequential differences. |
| Turn a missing record into proof that nothing happened. | Preserve the unknown and obtain a needed external account. |
| Infer a cause from the order of boxes. | Establish the needed causal basis through MKT.10 or return a narrower explanation. |
| Announce an improved journey after redrawing its map. | Obtain the actual changes and evidence of their receiving use. |

### MKT.8:9 - Consequences

A decision can use a more accurate account of the customer's activity and locate the right contributor. The map can also reveal that the provider cannot control the relevant condition. Recovery and maintenance cost effort; the representation is worthwhile only while it helps a live decision. Selectivity remains necessary, so its limits should be visible to later users.

### MKT.8:10 - Architectural Rationale

A journey representation connects accounts of activity to a particular use. History, possible route and plan answer different questions, while the customer remains able to choose and act outside the provider's arrangement. The method therefore joins representation with a receiving decision and actual specialist work rather than making one diagram govern every practice.

### MKT.8:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For the reach of the account, **adopt** the broader contextual view in [Becker and Jaakkola, 2020, Premise 2](https://pure.hanken.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/42844202/2020_Becker_Jaakkola.pdf), together with the distributed activity discussed in [the journey source](https://ailev.livejournal.com/1740317.html). These contributions challenge provider-only contact sequences. They do not prohibit all advance planning or establish that a pictured sequence causes a result.

For turning a representation into useful work, **adapt** the journey-representing and subsequent action connection in [Customer experience orientation, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-024-01031-y). The present method makes the receiving decision, evidence distinctions and actual intervention explicit. A simple sufficient account is cheaper when no wider connection changes the decision. Reopen the map when a changed participant, condition or goal defeats its intended use.

### MKT.8:12 - Relations

MKT.12 supplies qualified accounts; MKT.1 distinguishes customer situations; MKT.2 supplies the proposed offer. MKT.3 receives communication questions, MKT.4 choice questions, MKT.5 questions about experience conditions, and MKT.6 questions about the useful result and help. MKT.7 receives continuation questions, MKT.9 questions about the offer variant, and MKT.11 provision questions. MKT.10 handles causal contribution and cost, while MKT.14 can connect several opportunities within an organisation.

A map can locate the needed learning, engineering and operating contributions. Obtain and check their results through the responsible practices.

### MKT.8:End


## MKT.9 - Select an Offer or Help for a Particular Customer

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.9:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a known offer or form of help must be matched to a particular customer's circumstances, or when that comparison reveals that no suitable variant exists. Examples include choosing supported access rather than an unsupported self-service option, adapting a service to a changed schedule, or finding that the requested combination needs new engineering and staff preparation.

The result is a suitable feasible proposal with recoverable grounds, material conditions and a possible next action, or a precise request to construct a missing contribution. Personalization changes the proposal or help in response to relevant differences. Addressing a person by name does not by itself perform that work.

The intended reader understands the service or can obtain a competent account of it and its availability. A direct request for a known suitable variant can be fulfilled without constructing a detailed customer profile. The method does not require collecting every available attribute or making every customer a separate product project.

### MKT.9:2 - Problem

A team can personalize the expression while leaving the offered service unsuitable. It can also select a plausible option from stale or irrelevant data, or promise a combination whose required work conflicts. Automated ranking makes a selection repeatable but can repeat the same unsupported assumption at greater scale.

Connect a particular customer's useful result and consequential conditions to actual variants and their supply. Gather only the information that can alter that comparison. Preserve a way to correct the grounds, decline the proposal or return an absent contribution to its proper constructor.

### MKT.9:3 - Forces

| Tension | What the practitioner must preserve |
| --- | --- |
| More information can improve a choice; collection has cost and limits. | The attributes and permissions needed for this decision. |
| Standard variants are economical; customers differ. | The differences that a shared variant can tolerate and those requiring another contribution. |
| A combination can be attractive; its work can be incompatible. | Capability, timing, access and combined resource demand. |
| Automated selection can be fast; its grounds can be wrong or stale. | A qualified selection rule, its limits and a correction path. |
| A provider can suggest a useful option; the customer chooses. | The reason, material conditions and effective refusal. |

### MKT.9:4 - Solution

#### MKT.9:4.1 - Name the decision and the differences that can change it

Recover the useful result, the person or group receiving it and the immediate decision. Distinguish selecting an offer, selecting help for an acquired service and constructing a new service. The relevant information differs: a purchaser's spending limit may matter to the first decision, while access and present ability may determine the second.

Ask what difference would change the suitable action. Start with the required use, available time, access, necessary ability, support, material constraints and preferences that affect the result. For each proposed attribute, explain which alternative it can select or exclude. Omit an attribute whose value cannot change the decision. A broad market group can provide a useful starting hypothesis through [MKT.1](#mkt1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-customers); it does not settle the individual's conditions.

When the result itself is unclear, use [MKT.6](#mkt6---jointly-shape-a-useful-result-and-help-achieve-it) to obtain an agreed contribution before choosing its variant. When sufficient conditions are already known, proceed without asking the customer to repeat them.

#### MKT.9:4.2 - Qualify the information used for this choice

Recover the source, subject and currentness of the information. Distinguish something the customer stated from an inferred preference, a recorded action or an assumption supplied by a model. Confirm an action-changing condition when the present decision cannot safely rely on its existing basis. A person's earlier attendance does not establish current availability; a click does not establish that they can perform the required work.

Use information under its applicable access and use conditions. Make consequential information correctable by the person or other appropriate source. An organisation's purchaser, user and access authority can supply different answers; do not merge their preferences or permissions into one profile.

If a needed condition is unknown, choose the least elaborate adequate continuation: ask a focused question, offer a clearly conditional option, use an available suitable default, or stop the dependent selection. A default is suitable only when its consequences remain acceptable under the unresolved difference. More data collection is warranted when its answer can change the proposal enough to justify the burden.

#### MKT.9:4.3 - Compare the actually available variants

Recover the existing variants as complete contributions: what each supplies, the useful result it can support, required customer work, access and ability, timing, support, terms and available supply. [MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) supplies this offer content. Keep proposed variants separate from ones the provider can presently deliver.

First exclude a variant that fails a necessary condition. Then compare the remaining alternatives by the customer's relevant reasons for preference and the full burden of obtaining the result. Distinguish a favourable feature from a prerequisite: extra guidance can be optional convenience for an experienced user and necessary support for someone unable to proceed alone.

Compare combinations as well as individual options when the proposal joins several contributions. Check that the output of one is suitable for the next, that the same person or resource is not promised to incompatible work, and that the combined arrangement preserves support and failure handling. A set of individually available services is not necessarily available as one simultaneous package.

Return a supported selection with its grounds and conditions, or identify the exact unmet condition. If a ready variant suffices, arrange its use. If none does, decide whether an altered time or narrower contribution is useful before requesting new construction.

#### MKT.9:4.4 - Obtain a missing service configuration when selection is insufficient

Give [SYSE.8](../Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/SYSTEMS-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#syse8---develop-an-integrated-offering-and-provider-concept-for-using-an-engineered-result) the receiving activity, needed result, differences between customers, constraints and candidate provider contributions. Ask for a feasible offering and provider arrangement. Where several methods or allocations must be combined, use [FPF C.32.MWA.PP](../../FPF-Spec.md#c32mwapp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-provision-arrangements) to compare their actual relationships, simultaneous work and burdens.

For a reusable service, these suppliers must develop the following connected construction:

1. Identify contributions that can be reused by their supplied results, required inputs and conditions. Do not divide the service solely by existing department names.
2. Construct materially different combinations that can obtain the receiving result. Keep the current adequate arrangement where one exists. Explain necessary order, compatible outputs, shared resources and failure or return conditions.
3. Obtain the needed subject methods, competent people, assignments, access and support. Distinguish work that can be scheduled in advance from new requests, and work that can be interrupted from work that must finish before another action starts.
4. Return available variants with their conditions of promise and execution, or the precise contribution still missing. Test consequential compatibility in an ordinary use and a revealing variation before relying on an untried combination.

For recurring service construction, retain useful alternatives with the conditions that made them feasible, conditional or rejected. Retrieve relevant earlier configurations when developing another proposal, then recheck their fit and the reasons for the earlier decision. Use them to generate and refine options; a changed result or condition may require a configuration outside the stored set.

A service-module description states a reusable contribution's supplied result, required work and conditions. Developing that contribution may involve systems, people and several methods; calling it a module does not settle their composition. A list of options becomes usable only when the needed contributions and combinations can actually be supplied.

Marketing supplies the customer question and chooses among supported proposals. Engineering constructs the arrangement; HCD obtains needed capability; organisation change establishes assignments and enabling conditions; Operations performs the actual service. MKT.11 aligns their promises and returns any limit that changes the offer. Writing a personalized explanation can express the resulting service but cannot create it.

#### MKT.9:4.5 - Present a usable choice and arrange the selected help

Explain why the proposal fits the stated situation, what it requires and which consequential conditions remain open. Give the customer a proportionate way to correct the grounds, choose another suitable option or decline. If a preference was inferred, present it as revisable rather than as a fact about the person.

Make the next action feasible through [MKT.4](#mkt4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible) and the actual receiving operation. A promise of assisted use needs available assistance; a modified timetable needs a real place in that timetable. When help is selected during use, show the person how to obtain it and what can happen while waiting.

If software supports the selection, specify the operation it is meant to perform and the same result conditions. Check representative cases, an action-changing missing or wrong input, and an incompatible combination. The software may rank suitable variants, explain them or execute an authorized action; these are different functions with different failure consequences. A generated description should refer only to supported contributions. Obtain a competent decision when the system cannot resolve a necessary condition within its assigned authority.

#### MKT.9:4.6 - Learn whether the match worked and revise the affected part

Obtain evidence that answers the question that justified the personalization. A relevant reply can establish understanding or a preference; completed access can establish a feasible transition; successful use and later benefit require their own evidence. Distinguish the quality of the selected variant from the accuracy of the information and the actual performance of the service.

When a match fails, repair the affected connection. Correct mistaken data, change the selection rule, obtain missing support, alter the variant or reopen the useful result. Do not infer that a customer segment is wrong from one failed supply case. Use MKT.12 for a consequential unknown and MKT.10 for a claimed causal gain from personalization.

Return the selected contribution or unresolved need, the grounds and limits of the choice, and the actual next result. Reuse a sufficient shared variant; create another only when a consequential difference warrants the extra construction and maintenance.

### MKT.9:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.9:5.1 - A personalized package that cannot be performed

In a constructed event-service case, a customer requests ordinary room preparation and then adds a short-notice move to another room. The needed result is a usable room at the agreed time. The provider has descriptions of both services, and a recommendation system proposes the combined package from the customer's request.

The receiving team checks the actual conditions. Only one available worker can perform the required equipment check, and that check cannot be interrupted safely under the supplied operating rule. The same worker cannot simultaneously prepare the replacement room. A customer profile or more persuasive confirmation would not remove the conflict.

The team compares three attainable directions: retain the original room and complete the check; move later after the current work is finished; or obtain another appropriately prepared worker and the required access. The third remains conditional until that contribution is confirmed. The customer prefers a later move if it preserves a ready room; MKT.4 establishes the changed agreement and Operations schedules the supported work.

If repeated requests justify a reusable combined service, SYSE.8 and C.32.MWA.PP develop it with the customer-facing result, permitted combinations, relevant worker capabilities, timing and interruption conditions. HCD and the responsible managers obtain any new capability and assignment. The recommendation system then receives the available variants and their actual constraints. A test with the sole competent worker already occupied should exclude the impossible immediate combination or return a request for another provider.

The team retains the rejected immediate combination with its timing and capability grounds. On a later request it retrieves that reasoning, rechecks inputs and access, and generates options under the new conditions. If a qualified partner can now supply an already prepared room, the team also compares that new option rather than searching only among the earlier three.

When a second prepared worker later becomes available with the required access, the immediate alternative may become feasible. The new selection follows that changed contribution rather than changing the customer's presumed personality. The case establishes a possible arrangement under stated conditions, not that all service modules can be combined freely.

#### MKT.9:5.2 - A sufficient ready option

A customer asks to collect a standard item at an available time and confirms the only condition that affects access. The provider offers that ready option and arranges collection. Further profiling would not improve this choice. If access later changes, the provider reopens that condition and offers an attainable alternative.

### MKT.9:6 - Bias-Annotation

Visible attributes are easy to collect and can be mistaken for useful selection grounds. The option with the largest expected sale can also displace a smaller suitable contribution. Ask which customer result each difference changes and whether the proposed variant can actually be supplied. Keep an inferred preference correctable instead of allowing repeated recommendations to turn it into an unquestioned fact.

### MKT.9:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The decision and action-changing customer differences are explicit.
- Information retains its subject, source, currentness, use conditions and material uncertainty.
- Selected variants satisfy necessary conditions and have a supported reason for preference.
- Combined work preserves compatibility, competent people, access, support and timing.
- An absent service receives a concrete construction request and remains conditional until supplied.
- The customer can correct the grounds, choose or decline, and obtain the selected next action.
- Feedback distinguishes mistaken matching, failed supply and changed usefulness.

### MKT.9:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Change the greeting while keeping an unsuitable service. | Match the actual contribution and its conditions to the receiving result. |
| Infer ability or permission from a click. | Obtain the particular condition needed for the selected action. |
| Offer every listed service at the same time. | Compare combined work and shared resources before making the promise. |
| Treat a generated option as an available service. | Obtain its actual provider contributions or state the missing construction. |
| Collect an exhaustive profile for a settled ordinary request. | Use the sufficient existing conditions and proceed. |

### MKT.9:9 - Consequences

Customers can receive proposals and help better suited to their circumstances, while the provider can reuse adequate service variants. The work exposes when individualization requires real engineering or preparation. Data qualification, variant maintenance and exceptions have costs; unnecessary personalization can make an ordinary service slower or harder to understand.

### MKT.9:10 - Architectural Rationale

Selecting a fitting contribution and constructing a missing one are different problems. Connecting them explicitly prevents a choice mechanism from hiding absent supply and prevents every small customer difference from starting a new design project. The same distinction applies whether a person or software assists the selection.

### MKT.9:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For reusable individualized services, **adapt** the historical modular-service construction in [Pine and Gilmore, Welcome to the Experience Economy, Mass Customize Your Offerings](https://strategichorizons.com/wp-content/uploads/HealthForumJournal0110WelcometotheExperienceEconomy.pdf). Its nonclinical support-service example connects reusable contributions, rules of combination and prepared workers, including interruption conditions. This is more useful here than personalization of wording alone; its broader transformation and effectiveness claims are not needed for this construction.

For feasible combinations, **adopt** SYSE.8's construction of an offering and the provider arrangement needed to deliver it, and C.32.MWA.PP's comparison of methods, work and simultaneous burdens. They develop the conditions that a catalogue leaves unspecified. Use a ready suitable variant when available; reopen construction when a new result, incompatibility, scarce capability or changed interruption condition defeats the old arrangement.

For subsequent configuration work, **adapt** [Hunke, Satzger and Tuunanen (2024)](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000174859/154981710): preserve and retrieve design reasoning while testing its relevance to new alternatives. Their qualitative evaluation concerns analytics-based services and also found uncritical copying by novices. Reuse here informs construction without restricting it to stored choices.

### MKT.9:12 - Relations

MKT.1 supplies relevant customer differences, MKT.2 the offer, MKT.6 the useful result and needed help, and MKT.8 the relevant activity connection. MKT.4 makes the choice and transition feasible; MKT.11 aligns contributors and promises; MKT.12 supplies missing evidence. MKT.10 evaluates a claimed intervention effect and MKT.14 can decide which preparation is shared across organisations.

SYSE.8 and C.32.MWA.PP construct the missing offering and work arrangement. HCD, organisation change and Operations supply capability, effective assignments and actual service. A selection result retains the limits of those contributions.

### MKT.9:End


## MKT.10 - Evaluate the Contribution and Cost of a Marketing Intervention

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.10:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a decision to continue, change, expand or stop marketing work depends on what that work contributes and what it consumes. Examples include choosing between invitations, deciding whether a supported trial improves customer results, or reconsidering a channel whose attributed revenue looks attractive but whose incremental contribution is unknown.

The result is an answer bounded by a particular intervention, alternative, population, outcome and period, with the relevant costs and uncertainty. The answer may support a decision, delimit a smaller trial, or show which missing evidence prevents the claimed conclusion.

The intended reader can name the business decision and engage the people who supply the observations, causal analysis and financial account. A simple count may be sufficient to describe completed work. Use the causal parts only when the decision or claim depends on what the intervention changes; an ordinary record of what happened does not require an experiment. Specialist statistical identification and estimation remain with the people competent to supply them.

### MKT.10:2 - Problem

A dashboard can show impressions, replies, purchases, satisfaction and expenditure without explaining whether an intervention helped. People who receive more support may have harder problems; customers already more likely to achieve the outcome may select themselves into a trial; demand may rise during the campaign for another reason. Assigning revenue to the most recent contact describes a rule of attribution, not the result under an alternative contact policy.

Begin from the decision and its contrast. Define observations that retain their meaning, obtain an adequate comparison when a causal conclusion is needed, and combine that result with the whole economic and operational consequence. Choose the action in light of what the answer establishes and what a mistaken choice would cost.

### MKT.10:3 - Forces

| Tension | What the evaluation must preserve |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate responses are easier to observe than useful results. | The distinction between exposure, response, acquisition, performance and benefit. |
| Customers and staff choose whom to contact or help. | Selection and other explanations of observed differences. |
| A decision is needed before all long-term effects are visible. | A bounded horizon, material delayed consequences and stated uncertainty. |
| More detailed data can improve a comparison while increasing burden. | The smallest adequate observation and permitted use of its information. |
| A positive effect can consume scarce support or arrive after bills are due. | Complete incremental work, dated flows and feasible delivery. |

### MKT.10:4 - Solution

#### MKT.10:4.1 - State the decision and the result that would change it

Name the actual alternatives: retain the existing invitation, add guided help, use another channel, reduce volume, or stop the intervention. Keep the useful customer result and the provider's decision visible. An increase in replies can answer a reach question while remaining insufficient for an expansion justified by better customer performance.

Specify whose result matters, what would count, when it must occur and what downside can defeat the action. Include different participants when their outcomes differ: the purchaser can save effort while the user acquires extra work. [MKT.6](#mkt6---jointly-shape-a-useful-result-and-help-achieve-it) supplies the agreed result; [MKT.5](#mkt5---develop-and-align-a-customer-experience-concept) supplies the intended experience conditions. Neither supplies an observed effect by itself.

Determine the kind of answer required. “How many trials were completed?” is descriptive. “Which customers are likely to request help?” is predictive. “How many additional useful completions would this invitation produce compared with the current invitation?” is causal. These questions can use related data but require different grounds. A descriptive answer may be enough to stop an infeasible service even when its causal benefit remains unresolved.

Choose a practically consequential difference before interpreting a favourable fluctuation. Its basis can be the value of the result, the burden of the intervention and the consequences of a mistaken decision. Use a justified local threshold or a comparison of alternatives; do not invent a universal minimum response rate.

#### MKT.10:4.2 - Make each observation interpretable

Define the observation unit, eligible population, period, source and counting rule. For a response rate, say who had an opportunity to respond and what response counts. Distinguish unique people, organisations, opportunities and events. Several purchases by one organisation need not be several independent observations; a missing report is not necessarily a failed result.

Match the measure to the claimed outcome. A completed form can establish a submitted request. It cannot by itself establish informed choice, successful work or a favourable experience. Ask for the additional observation only when the decision depends on it. Where a customer reports an experience, preserve the question, timing, scale and respondent; an ordinal rating does not automatically support arithmetic differences, monetary conversion or comparison with another questionnaire.

Check how the data are produced. A new reminder may change who answers a survey, a new interface may change what gets logged, and better records may increase the apparent number of problems. Account for these changes before interpreting a trend. Retain consequential uncertainty about missing observations and selective follow-up.

Agree how much observation is needed and how to obtain it without defeating the intervention or its receiving service. Use existing adequate records; ask for a limited observation when it can resolve the decision. [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) supplies a focused inquiry where the unresolved issue is what the customer's work or answer means.

#### MKT.10:4.3 - Construct the causal contrast and its credible comparison

When contribution means a causal effect, define the intervention and comparison as ways of acting, including intensity, timing, support and consequential variants. Specify the target population, outcome and horizon. An offer of help and actual use of that help are different interventions. An invitation sent to one team may also affect another team through shared staff or shared information.

Use [FPF C.28.CM, Construct and Challenge a Causal Model](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/FPF-Spec.md#c28cm---construct-and-challenge-a-causal-model) to explain why the contrast could change the outcome and what else could produce the observations. Recover common causes, selection, intermediate results and feedback that change the inference. A journey diagram or chronological sequence is not a causal model merely because arrows connect its events.

Then obtain an identification answer through [Identify an Intervention Effect from Available Data (MMP.15)](https://github.com/ailev/FPF/blob/main/DPFs/Foundational%20Thinking%20DPF%20Suite/MATHEMATICAL-MODELING-PRACTICE-DPF.md#mmp15---identify-an-intervention-effect-from-available-data) when this requires specialist analysis. The request names the contrast and available data, including how participants entered them. The supplier returns which effect the data can identify, under which assumptions, or a useful bound or reason it remains unidentified. More accurate fitting cannot repair an effect the data do not identify.

Select a feasible comparison suited to the question. Random allocation can make groups comparable for the effect of the assigned intervention if allocation, follow-up and interference conditions are adequate. It does not automatically identify the effect of actual use among self-selected users. Observational comparisons require defensible assumptions about relevant differences, usable variation and measurement. A before-and-after comparison requires a reason that other changes do not explain the result. Asking for “a control group” without these conditions supplies no causal answer.

Design the comparison with its operators. Avoid allocating a promise the service cannot fulfil or depriving people of an existing commitment. Arrange follow-up, exception handling and a stopping decision if the work becomes infeasible. If the credible comparison is unavailable, return the supported descriptive result and the unresolved causal question. A bounded action under uncertainty can still be chosen explicitly.

#### MKT.10:4.4 - Estimate the supported effect and challenge its use

Give the identified question and qualified observations to an adequate estimation method. Retain the estimated magnitude, uncertainty and consequential assumptions. Statistical precision addresses variability under the method; it does not remove selection, measurement error, missing follow-up or a wrong causal model.

Check the conclusions that matter for the decision. Could plausible missing outcomes reverse it? Does a result disappear when an unsupported comparison is removed? Does an average conceal a group for whom the intervention is impractical or harmful? A subgroup claim needs adequate grounds; dividing the same small dataset repeatedly does not create them.

Keep discovery and confirmation distinct when many messages, channels or outcomes were examined. A selected favourable result may justify a new bounded comparison rather than immediate expansion. If a decision permits repeated inspection, agree how the resulting uncertainty will be handled with the analyst instead of repeatedly stopping at the first attractive number.

Before applying the result elsewhere, compare the new population, baseline service, intervention and support conditions. A trial with spare specialist capacity may not describe a larger programme that queues every customer. Establish whether the earlier estimate applies under those conditions, or obtain a new comparison. A precise result for the wrong use remains an inadequate answer.

#### MKT.10:4.5 - Compare the whole contribution with its burden

Use [Account for Customer and Product Economics Over Time (MA.8)](MANAGEMENT-ACCOUNTING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md) when the decision depends on economic consequences. Preserve the target unit and horizon, distinguish historical costs from costs changed by the future alternative, and place receipts and payments at their relevant dates. Count acquisition, preparation, adaptation, delivery, support, recovery and remaining obligations where they change the comparison.

A customer's purchase value is not automatically incremental profit. Ask what revenue and expense would differ under the alternatives, which contributions would have happened anyway, and whether later work has been included. Avoid counting the same additional outcome under several channels or counting both its estimated monetary benefit and the same benefit inside a customer-value total.

Combine uncertainty honestly. An uncertain causal effect and an uncertain margin produce an uncertain economic conclusion. Show the threshold at which the choice changes or compare credible ranges when a point estimate would conceal the issue. The largest expected contribution can still be infeasible because of cash timing, scarce staff or obligations to current customers. [MKT.11](#mkt11---align-promises-partners-and-execution) supplies actual delivery conditions; its missing capacity is not repaired by a favourable average return.

Use nonmonetary consequences in their own terms where no adequate conversion exists. A client's ability to complete important work, burden and experience can affect the decision without a fabricated price. State whose objective is served and what trade-off the decision maker accepts.

#### MKT.10:4.6 - Return the decision and the conditions for revisiting it

Return the compared alternatives, what the observations establish, the causal conclusion if supported, the relevant burden and the decision that follows. State what remains uncertain and why that uncertainty permits or limits the action. Continue, change, run a smaller informative comparison, defer expansion or stop according to this answer.

Send the consequence to the receiving work. MKT.3 can change the invitation; MKT.5 or MKT.6 can change the proposed contribution; MKT.11 can change the supply arrangement; MKT.13 can bound deployment of the commercial model. Keep a needed action and its responsible recipient explicit rather than returning an uninterpreted dashboard.

Reopen the answer when the intervention, population, measurement, service capacity, economic conditions or a material causal assumption changes. Keep the original result with its scope; a later disappointing outcome does not justify rewriting what the earlier comparison actually observed.

### MKT.10:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.10:5.1 - A promising invitation with an uncertain useful effect

A provider wants to add guided onboarding to an invitation. The decision concerns another comparable group of 1,000 eligible customers. The useful outcome is verified completion of a specified task within 14 days, not clicking the invitation. Existing commitments remain available under both alternatives, and the provider has checked that the additional help can be delivered without reducing that service.

In a constructed trial, 2,000 eligible customers are randomly assigned equally to the new and current invitations. Assume complete follow-up, no material spillover and the same stated outcome rule. There are 120 useful completions among 1,000 assigned the new invitation and 100 among 1,000 assigned the current invitation. The estimated effect of the assigned invitation is 12% minus 10%, or 2 percentage points. Comparing only people who used the help would answer another question and would lose the original allocation's simple comparability.

For this illustration, an approximate 95% confidence interval uses the difference plus or minus 1.96 times its standard error. With independent binary outcomes, that standard error is the square root of (0.12 × 0.88 / 1,000 + 0.10 × 0.90 / 1,000). The interval is approximately −0.7 to 4.7 percentage points. The positive point estimate is compatible with both a small loss and a worthwhile gain. It does not establish that the intervention improves outcomes. The interval also depends on the stated trial and estimation conditions; it says nothing about an omitted delayed outcome.

Suppose a qualified prospective account values each additional useful completion at 40 monetary units of contribution after its associated delivery expense, and another campaign for 1,000 customers would require 500 units of additional fixed preparation and help capacity. Assume these flows occur within the decision's funded period. The point estimate gives 20 additional completions, a contribution of 800 and a net increment of 300. Roughly carrying the effect interval through this simplified account gives about −800 to 1,400, even before uncertainty in the value per completion. A positive expected increment alone is therefore a weak basis for an expensive irreversible expansion.

The decision maker chooses another affordable bounded comparison because the unresolved effect can change the decision. If even the favourable plausible effect could not cover the prospective burden, stopping would be more useful than collecting more data. If customers independently need the help to receive an existing promise, that obligation must be supplied regardless of this marketing experiment.

The next proposed group includes organisations with restrictive data access and would exhaust specialist capacity. It is no longer the same deployment condition. MKT.11 establishes a feasible service variant before its benefit is tested; multiplying the earlier 2% by the new audience would conceal the changed intervention.

#### MKT.10:5.2 - More support accompanies more failures

An observational report shows that customers receiving many support calls fail more often. Staff explain that they contact customers after detecting serious difficulties. The report may identify a high-burden group, but it does not establish that calls cause failure. The causal model retains initial difficulty and the timing of support; the analyst considers whether available observations permit an adequate contrast. If they do not, the provider can improve the handover and record the needed conditions while withholding a causal effect claim.

### MKT.10:6 - Bias-Annotation

Attribution systems, selective follow-up and incentives to show campaign success favour visible positive results. Customers who are easy to observe can differ from those who leave silently. The person commissioning an evaluation may prefer a precise answer even when the real uncertainty concerns identification. Preserve alternative explanations, missing outcomes and the possibility that no additional intervention is warranted.

### MKT.10:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The answer serves a named decision and preserves its alternatives, population, outcome and period.
- Observation rules distinguish the events and participants that affect interpretation.
- A causal claim has an explicit intervention contrast and an adequate identification basis; description or prediction is not silently promoted to effect.
- Estimation uncertainty and consequential causal or measurement limitations remain visible.
- The whole incremental burden, dated economic consequences and actual capacity qualify the decision.
- The receiving work can act on the conclusion and recognise a change that requires revisiting it.

### MKT.10:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Anti-pattern | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Crediting the last contact with every subsequent purchase. | Distinguish the attribution rule from the causal contrast needed for the decision. |
| Calling a survey score the customer's achieved result. | Observe the claimed outcome or narrow the conclusion to the reported experience. |
| Fitting more variables until the answer looks persuasive. | Obtain the causal model and identification grounds before interpreting estimates. |
| Declaring success from a positive point estimate alone. | Compare meaningful magnitudes, uncertainty and the cost of the wrong action. |
| Expanding a supported pilot into an unsupported service queue. | Re-establish the intervention and capacity conditions for the proposed population. |

### MKT.10:9 - Consequences

The evaluation becomes useful for an actual action and limits claims that the data cannot support. It can reveal that a small descriptive answer is sufficient or that an expensive causal analysis would not change the decision. Establishing a causal effect or applying its estimate to a new population, for example, may require additional observations, specialist work or a trial; unresolved uncertainty can legitimately limit expansion.

### MKT.10:10 - Architectural Rationale

Marketing chooses the intervention question and uses its answer. Causal modeling supplies the explanatory structure, mathematical modeling supplies identification and estimation, and management accounting supplies the economic comparison. Keeping these contributions explicit allows a practical decision without turning a dashboard, statistical fit or revenue attribution into proof of effectiveness.

### MKT.10:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For observational marketing models, **adopt** the distinction in [Google Meridian's causal-inference guidance](https://developers.google.com/meridian/docs/causal-inference/about-mmm-causal-inference-methodology) between model fit and causal grounds: alternative well-fitting models can imply different returns, and consequential assumptions need domain justification. This supports bounded interpretation rather than selection by predictive accuracy alone.

For the construction of an answer, **adopt** C.28.CM and MMP.15's developed separation of causal contrast, assumptions, identification and estimation, then join their result to MA.8's prospective account. This adds work when an action depends on an effect; an adequate ordinary description remains sufficient for a descriptive question.

### MKT.10:12 - Relations

MKT.3/5/6/7/9/11/14 supply interventions and receiving decisions. MKT.8 can locate an observed difficulty but does not identify its cause. MKT.12 can qualify the customer's situation and the meaning of an outcome. MKT.13 uses an effect estimate only within its actual deployment conditions.

C.28.CM supplies the causal model, MMP.15 the identification answer and appropriate mathematical modeling the estimate. MA.8 supplies customer and product economics over time. A missing specialist result returns as a bounded unresolved contribution, not as an invented precise number.

### MKT.10:End


## MKT.11 - Align Promises, Partners and Execution

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.11:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a customer promise depends on several people, teams or organisations, or when customer evidence reveals a mismatch between a promise and actual delivery. Examples include a consultation advertised without available specialists, a trial that needs another organisation's access decision, or a service improvement that staff understand but lack the time or authority to perform.

The result is a supported promise joined to effective contributor arrangements and an executable continuation, or a precise limit requiring the promise to change. It includes what happens when a contribution fails and who receives the customer evidence that can change the arrangement.

The intended reader can recover the offer and engage the people responsible for its contributions. A settled ordinary offer with sufficient current supply can use that arrangement directly. This method does not replace specialist engineering, operations, organisational design or the authority to commit another party.

### MKT.11:2 - Problem

Separate teams can each perform a local task while the customer receives an incompatible whole. Marketing describes a result, a partner provides only part of it, and support inherits a problem it was never equipped to solve. Recording a responsible person or sending feedback does not establish the missing contribution.

Trace the promise to the actual work and conditions that make it attainable. Obtain the relevant provider decisions and enabling contributions, resolve conflicts in the whole arrangement and return the supported scope to every expression of the offer. When evidence calls for change, connect it to a responsible decision and performed work.

### MKT.11:3 - Forces

| Tension | What the arrangement must preserve |
| --- | --- |
| A simple customer promise can require many contributions. | The dependencies that determine whether the whole result is attainable. |
| Teams have local goals and authority. | Effective decisions and commitments without treating a meeting as consent by every provider. |
| A provider can be capable but unavailable at the needed time. | Capability, access, assignment and capacity as separate conditions. |
| New experience insight can justify change while current service must continue. | The complete burden of both and a feasible way to recover. |
| Flexibility helps exceptions; unrestricted promises can defeat the service. | Bounded discretion, actual support and a known return when limits are reached. |

### MKT.11:4 - Solution

#### MKT.11:4.1 - Recover the whole promise and the conditions it implies

Take the offer, relevant communication and customer's understood result. Identify what is promised, to whom, when and under which conditions. Include access, support, customer work and later obligations where they change the result. [MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) supplies the offer; [MKT.5](#mkt5---develop-and-align-a-customer-experience-concept) can reveal conflicting experience conditions; [MKT.6](#mkt6---jointly-shape-a-useful-result-and-help-achieve-it) supplies the agreed useful contribution.

Read the promise from a concrete use. What would have to happen for the customer to receive the supported result? Recover the required work and contributions, including those performed by the customer or another provider. Include what must happen before a later action can begin and what must remain available while it occurs.

Keep a proposed new offer distinguishable from an existing commitment. A proposal can be narrowed before agreement; an existing obligation requires its applicable change or remedy decision. If the whole service arrangement is missing, obtain its construction through MKT.9 and SYSE.8 before treating a list of willing contributors as sufficient.

#### MKT.11:4.2 - Obtain a real supply answer for each consequential contribution

Ask the responsible contributor what result can be supplied, under which inputs, capability, access, timing, volume, terms and support conditions. Identify the person or organisation that can make the commitment. Separate a possible contribution, a decision to arrange it, an effective arrangement and work already performed.

For a small known service, this can be a short confirmation of the changed condition. For a new or disputed arrangement, recover the needed relations explicitly. [OCE.6](ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce6---establish-holder-assignments-and-enabling-relations-for-organization-change) establishes actual assignments and their enabling conditions: a named person still needs suitable capability, available time, permission, access and the authority relevant to the work. Obtain missing conditions from their responsible sources rather than assigning them by implication.

Use a competent operating answer for the supported service volume and response conditions. An apparently free calendar is not evidence that a team can handle variable arrivals, exceptions or a required service level. If the provider cannot yet answer, keep the dependent promise conditional or choose a smaller contribution whose supply is known.

Do not assume the customer's work merely because it appears in the offer. Obtain the needed participation, preparation and decisions in a form appropriate to the relationship. A payer's approval may not provide a user's time or an access authority's permission.

#### MKT.11:4.3 - Join the contributions and resolve the whole conflict

Check the connections from the receiving result through the actual work. For every consequential handover, establish what the receiving party obtains, what makes it usable, who notices an absent or unsuitable result and what continuation follows. Include responsibility for helping the customer when the failure spans teams.

Compare the combined demand at the times and resources that can constrain the promise. Count preparation, customer-specific work, coordination, help, exception handling and recovery as well as the visible delivery. Keep unlike resources separate. Two teams' total hours do not establish availability of the one specialist needed at a particular moment.

When contributions conflict, compare feasible alternatives: change scope or time, choose another provider, obtain a missing capability or access condition, change the arrangement, or decline the unsupported promise. Use [FPF C.32.MWA.PP](../../FPF-Spec.md#c32mwapp---construct-and-compare-method-performance-and-provision-arrangements) when the choice concerns incompatible combinations of methods, work or allocation. Use the returned comparison where it is sufficient instead of repeating it as another marketing exercise.

Obtain the decisions that make the selected arrangement effective. Then align the offer, invitation, customer-facing interface and staff explanation with its actual scope. A contradictory old invitation can continue to create expectations after the internal plan changes; revise the affected expression and address already made commitments through their applicable terms.

#### MKT.11:4.4 - Connect customer insight to a bounded change in work

When a customer account, journey inquiry or observed failure exposes a mismatch, state the specific condition that should change and the receiving result it is meant to improve. Give the people who can act the relevant evidence and its limits. They must be able to distinguish a reported experience, a failed operation and an inferred cause because these can require different changes.

Choose the level of change that the result needs. A known exception can be handled within current authority; a repeated missing contribution may require a changed assignment, resource allocation, staff capability, product feature or provider agreement. Obtain that contribution through its responsible practice. A general instruction to care more does not supply any of them.

Where staff need discretion, establish what they may decide, within which scope and resource conditions, and where they return a case outside that scope. Obtain the knowledge and capability needed to exercise that discretion. Where a recurring cross-team decision is needed, arrange a usable means for the responsible people to receive the evidence and resolve it. A new standing committee is unnecessary when an existing decision and communication route can do the work.

Give the change actual time, support and an attainable receiving operation. [OCE.11](ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce11---coordinate-organization-change-work-with-continuing-service) coordinates preparation, learning, trial, recovery and hand-back with continuing service. If the total burden is not feasible, reduce or reschedule the change, obtain support or return the decision. Do not finance the new promise with unacknowledged work by staff who must still serve current customers.

After the change, recover what was implemented, what occurred in use and whether the relevant customer result changed. Use that distinction to retain, revise or reverse the arrangement. Repeated useful changes can alter the organisation's ordinary methods, assignments and support; the responsible practices must establish those changes. The insight record alone does not institutionalize them.

#### MKT.11:4.5 - Arrange the actual continuation and failure response

Before a dependent customer action begins, confirm the necessary contributions for that case and interval. Use [OPS.6](OPERATIONS-MANAGEMENT-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#ops6---continue-cases-and-handle-exceptions) to obtain the executable continuation with a performer, effective conditions, expected result, stop or fallback, and a return to continuing operation. A sufficient existing result can be reused while its conditions hold.

Make failure handling intelligible to the people who encounter it. Identify what the customer can do, who receives an unresolved case, what temporary alternative is available and what cannot honestly be promised. A customer should not have to discover which of two providers owns an internal disagreement before obtaining the agreed response.

Observe performance where a difference changes action. A provider's delayed input, a lost permission or an exhausted support interval can invalidate a dependent continuation. Pause or revise that branch and obtain the changed input; preserve independent useful work where its conditions remain valid. MKT.7 handles restoration or ending when the relationship decision is affected.

#### MKT.11:4.6 - Return the supported promise and maintain its dependencies

Return what can now be promised and performed, its material conditions, the actual continuation and any unresolved contribution. Communicate changes to the people maintaining the offer and arranging work. Keep a condition visible for as long as another contribution relies on it. When a team's task finishes, identify which obligations remain under the applicable terms, who must fulfil them and which receiving contributions depend on them. Include obligations retained by the supplying team as well as those of receiving teams.

Review the arrangement when a provider, customer requirement, support capability or relevant demand changes. Reuse current sufficient confirmations. A recurring mismatch can reopen the service construction or the commercial model through MKT.9/13; a changed scale of work with organisations can reopen MKT.14. The result remains an account of supported contributions, not a claim that the entire customer's outcome is under provider control.

### MKT.11:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.11:5.1 - An evening registration promise with daytime support

In a constructed registration service, a page promises that every submitted application will receive a same-day decision. Ordinary cases can be completed automatically, but exceptions require a qualified operator. The operator works during the day, while many customers apply in the evening. An automated receipt message is being interpreted as confirmation of acceptance.

The team recovers the whole result: customers need to know whether registration is complete or what remains unresolved. Engineering supplies which cases can be decided automatically. Operations supplies the supported exception hours and response conditions. The responsible manager identifies who may decide exceptions and obtains the necessary assignment and access; staff availability by itself does not confer that authority.

The team compares narrower next-working-day decisions for exceptions, additional supported evening coverage, and reducing the conditions that require manual decisions through product changes. The first is presently feasible. The page and receipt then distinguish a received application from a completed registration, explain the remaining condition and give the attainable response time. The changed explanation follows the actual service arrangement.

Customer accounts later show that the exception timing still prevents some useful participation. That evidence goes to the people who can alter the product, allocation or offer. They select a bounded trial of an additional supported decision window. OCE.11 accounts for training, supervision and current daytime commitments, rather than counting only the new customer-facing hours. The trial separately records the supplied window, actual decisions and customers' accounts of whether the timing helped.

If the qualified substitute becomes unavailable, the trial's dependent promise must be withdrawn or rescheduled before new customers rely on it. Already submitted cases receive the applicable continuation or remedy. Publishing a reassuring message without restoring a qualified performer would leave the missing contribution intact.

#### MKT.11:5.2 - Permission governed by the customer's organisation

A reporting trial needs a sample supplied under the customer's access rules. The provider can supply a session and mapping assistance, but it cannot grant the customer organisation's permission. It obtains a precise return from the relevant authority, keeps the trial conditional while permission is pending and arranges a later session if the condition becomes effective. The customer's payment or the salesperson's commitment cannot substitute for that separate contribution.

### MKT.11:6 - Bias-Annotation

People can interpret willingness as capacity and the presence of an owner as evidence that the work is enabled. Marketing may also favour the broadest promise while Operations favours the easiest local service. Compare each position with the customer's receiving result and the full burden of the arrangement. Preserve a qualified refusal from a provider instead of translating it into a communication problem.

### MKT.11:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The promise and its material implications are recoverable from an actual customer use.
- Each consequential contribution has a supported result, provider and effective conditions.
- Capability, assignment, access, authority, timing and actual performance are distinguished where they affect the work.
- Handover and simultaneous-demand conflicts are resolved or returned as explicit limits.
- Customer insight reaches a responsible decision, enabled change and observation of its result.
- Failure handling and remaining obligations are usable by the customer and contributors.
- Dependent expressions and future starts change when the supported promise changes.

### MKT.11:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Name an owner and assume they can deliver. | Obtain the specific assignment, capability, access and resources the work requires. |
| Add several locally feasible promises without checking their combination. | Compare their shared demand and receiving conditions. |
| Send customer feedback with no one able to act on it. | Connect the evidence to the responsible decision and enabling contribution. |
| Count only visible delivery in a new-service trial. | Include preparation, learning, support, recovery and continuing work. |
| Keep a public promise after its provider contribution fails. | Restore supply or revise the promise and handle affected commitments. |

### MKT.11:9 - Consequences

Customers can receive a more coherent contribution, and missing supply becomes a concrete repair or a smaller honest promise. Cross-team work and maintained dependencies cost effort; existing adequate arrangements should be reused. The method can expose that no feasible combination supports the proposed offer, which is a useful result before additional commitments are made.

### MKT.11:10 - Architectural Rationale

An offer can depend on several separately governed contributions. Marketing connects their results to the promise, while their responsible practices establish the capability, authority and performance. This division makes a missing contribution actionable and prevents an attractive description from being treated as implementation.

Customer evidence becomes practically useful when it informs a decision to retain or change the work. The connection may require a local remedy or changes to ordinary resources and assignments; its scale follows the problem rather than a universal transformation programme.

### MKT.11:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For carrying experience knowledge into work, **adapt** the organisational-learning question in [Customer experience orientation, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-024-01031-y). Its nine-case managerial account connects insight with discretion, resources and organisational support; outcome effects remain propositions requiring further testing. This sharpens §4.4 without making every customer issue require a new organisation-wide programme.

For actual enabling contributions, **adopt** OCE.6's establishment of effective assignments and its separate checks of effective access and the performer's required capability. OCE.11 coordinates change with continuing service, and OPS.6 supplies an executable continuation. These developed constructions resolve the gap left by naming responsible departments or circulating feedback. C.32.MWA.PP supplies a needed comparison of incompatible work arrangements. A current sufficient result is cheaper to reuse; reopen only dependencies defeated by a changed promise, provider, customer condition or burden.

### MKT.11:12 - Relations

MKT.2/3/5/6/9 supply promises and receiving uses and consume the resulting supply limits. MKT.4 makes the supported transition feasible; MKT.7 handles relationship recovery or ending; MKT.8 locates consequential connections; MKT.12 supplies qualified customer evidence. MKT.10 evaluates a claimed intervention effect, MKT.13 tests repeatability, and MKT.14 coordinates common and individual work with selected organisations.

SYSE constructs the offering and provider arrangement; C.32.MWA.PP compares how methods are related and whether their performance creates conflicts; OCE establishes assignments and workable change; HCD supplies capability; Operations performs the service. Their results retain their own conditions when used in a marketing promise.

### MKT.11:End


## MKT.12 - Investigate a Customer or Market Question for a Decision

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.12:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when a product, offer or customer decision depends on something you do not yet understand about people's work, choice, use or experience. Perhaps customers praise a service but stop using it, apparently similar buyers choose differently, or a team cannot decide which difficulty to address. Start by stating the decision and the answer that could change it.

The useful result is an answer with a stated reach, or a precisely bounded unanswered question, delivered to someone who can use it. That can come from existing information, ordinary information gathering, or a deliberately designed inquiry. A new survey is not the default result.

The intended reader understands the product or service and can arrange a competent conversation, observation and basic analysis. A specialised sampling, measurement or causal claim needs the corresponding expertise. This pattern supplies the marketing question and the connections between inquiry and action; it does not confer interviewing skill or access to private material.

Use an existing sufficient answer directly. Repair an already established service error through the responsible operating practice. If the decision concerns the effect caused by an intervention, qualify that causal question separately; a customer's account of an experience can inform it without answering it.

### MKT.12:2 - Problem

An organisation can collect abundant feedback without learning what its next decision requires. A buyer's stated preference may concern acquisition rather than use. A score after delivery may omit a difficulty that appears later. A striking interview may reveal a possible explanation while leaving its prevalence unknown. Combining these observations into one customer metric loses the question each can answer.

The remedy is to construct the inquiry backward from a receiving decision, then forward from obtainable observations to a warranted answer. Keep the work being studied, the person reporting it, the observation and the inference distinct.

### MKT.12:3 - Forces

| Tension | Consequence for the inquiry |
| --- | --- |
| A decision needs an answer soon; the full situation may be extensive. | Seek the smallest answer that can distinguish the live actions, and state what it leaves unresolved. |
| People can describe their situations; memory, incentives and question wording affect their reports. | Keep an attributed report separate from an observed event and an inferred explanation. |
| Common measures permit comparison; unlike meanings make the comparison misleading. | Establish a comparable activity, role, period and interpretation before aggregating. |
| Broad coverage costs attention and access. | Include a contrasting or missing case when it can change the conclusion, rather than collecting everything available. |
| Evidence must reach action; the investigator may not own that action. | Arrange the recipient and decision before collecting more data. |

### MKT.12:4 - Solution

#### MKT.12:4.1 - Define the receiving question and try the existing answer

Name who must decide, what alternatives remain live, when the decision matters and what answer would change it. Replace “understand our customers” with, for example, “decide whether the next offer needs an easier acquisition route or a different way of performing the work”. Ask what observation would distinguish those alternatives. If no plausible answer changes the move, revise the question or stop collecting.

Inspect available service records, previous inquiries, current customer accounts and relevant external sources. Check their subject, period and conditions before reuse. An old answer about individual buyers may not answer a current question about organisations with a separate access authority. Conversely, age alone does not defeat an answer whose conditions still hold.

Use sufficient information. When a missing fact can be obtained by a known suitable operation, obtain that fact and check its meaning. For a surviving non-routine knowledge question, take the question and existing grounds to [RMP.1](../RESEARCH-METHOD-PRACTICE-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#rmp1--decide-whether-a-question-needs-research-and-when-to-stop). Its qualification can return an existing answer, adjacent work, a missing-input hold or a research brief. Calling an activity customer research does not determine which result is appropriate.

#### MKT.12:4.2 - Choose the object, people and kind of answer

Distinguish the person performing the work, selecting a solution, paying, permitting access and living with its effects. They may be one person or several. Name the relevant episode or continuing activity as well as the people. “The company wants less effort” hides whose effort and which decision can establish a commitment.

Choose the inquiry from the unresolved distinction:

| Question that can change the decision | Material and inquiry | Permitted first result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which results of an understood activity are poorly served? | Observe and discuss performance with its executors; establish desired results, then compare their importance and present attainment where comparable measurement is possible. | Candidate improvements and differences between customers; use §4.3. |
| Why did someone change, decline or remain with an alternative? | Reconstruct an actual choice episode, including circumstances, considered alternatives and the difficulty of transition. | An explanatory hypothesis about that choice and a question for contrast; use §4.4. |
| What happens at a contact, during continued use or around a new possibility? | Select observation, conversation and measures over the appropriate interval, with a named recipient. | A bounded account of experience or a new possibility; use §4.5. |
| How widespread is an established difference? | Define the population and a suitable sampling and measurement design. | A population claim with its uncertainty, if the design warrants it. |
| Did our intervention produce the change? | Distinguish the intervention, outcome, comparison and competing explanations; obtain the causal design needed for that claim. | A bounded causal conclusion, or a descriptive result when attribution remains unsupported. |

These routes can inform each other. A choice history can expose the wrong activity boundary; an outcome comparison can reveal a difference worth exploring in a new conversation. The result of one route becomes a question or input for the other, not automatic evidence for its conclusion.

#### MKT.12:4.3 - Investigate results of performing the work

Start with an executor and an activity described independently of a preferred product. Ask the person to walk through a concrete attempt: what started it, what they did, what they tried to achieve, what made success difficult and what happened afterward. Observe the relevant work or inspect its permitted traces where that can challenge the account. Separate a requested feature from the result it is expected to improve.

Use the attempt to sketch what must be accomplished for the activity to succeed. Start with its central work, then recover the preparation and completion it depends on. Ask what each part achieves and whether it is merely a feature of the present solution. "Open spreadsheet A" may serve "establish which source values are valid"; another solution can perform that purpose differently. A small map or list helps retain these relations. Keep the boundary tied to the decision instead of expanding into everything the person does.

Challenge the sketch with a materially different attempt, including an interrupted or unsuccessful one when it could expose a missing part. Examine handovers and combinations of tools that the initial account treated as incidental. Ask what was omitted, repeated or repaired and what outcome mattered there. Test a proposed addition against an actual attempt or relevant participant; a suggested possibility alone does not establish an unmet need. If the addition describes another activity, either revise the inquiry's boundary explicitly or leave it outside this answer.

Turn a vague desire into a result that can be interpreted consistently. “Make reporting easy” might conceal time spent locating missing entries, risk of sending an incomplete report, or effort correcting discrepancies. State the direction of improvement, the thing affected and the relevant circumstances. Ask participants to explain the wording in their own situation; split an item when people are rating different things. A product-independent statement helps compare solutions, but it may still omit an important concern.

Check coverage before ranking the surfaced results. For each relevant part of the activity, determine whether the inquiry has a usable account of success, an unresolved question or a reason it cannot affect this decision. Ask what a plausible missing outcome could change: the target group, proposed contribution or next inquiry. Pursue the smallest missing observation when it could alter that choice. If it cannot be obtained, return the affected choice as unresolved or narrow the recommendation to what the evidence supports. When the remaining uncertainty cannot change the bounded next move, stop at that scope; more interviews are not required merely to lengthen the list. Existing sufficient material can supply this comparison without a new mapping exercise.

Once meanings and coverage support the intended comparison, compare importance and present attainment for the people and work under consideration. Keep the two questions distinct. A result can be important and already adequately attained, or poorly attained but irrelevant to the current decision. Inspect combinations within and across cases before using an aggregate. A priority score is a decision aid under its assumptions; it does not prove demand, causal benefit or a stable segment.

Return candidate target results, the activity and contrasting attempts they cover, the relevant differences between executors and their evidence. Identify any unresolved omission that can change the receiving decision and the smaller answer needed to settle it. [MKT.1](#mkt1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-customers) uses this to qualify customer groups; [MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) uses it to construct an offer. If the activity or meaning of a measure changes, revisit that part of the comparison before carrying forward its ranking.

#### MKT.12:4.4 - Reconstruct a choice and the transition it involved

Select an actual episode rather than a hypothetical favourite. Ask for the sequence from the earlier situation through first concern, search, comparison, decision and what followed. Where did the person first consider a change? What was happening then? Which alternatives were genuinely available? What made one acceptable and another difficult? Preserve uncertainty about dates or reasons instead of silently completing a smooth story.

Use concrete episodes and permitted documents to probe the account. “We chose it because it was modern” may lead to a more useful account of an upcoming deadline, a trusted colleague's advice, a procurement restriction or fear of losing a valued way of working. Functional, emotional and social considerations can coexist. Keep each attributed to the participant and supported at the strength available; do not diagnose motives that the inquiry cannot establish.

Include a contrasting case when it can defeat the explanation: someone who stayed, delayed, chose another supplier or did not complete a transition. Compare the circumstances that differ, not only the words people use. A possible explanation of several episodes can guide an offer or acquisition change. Estimating how often it applies needs additional grounds.

[MKT.4](#mkt4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible) consumes the actual transition difficulty. If the episode shows that the person seeks a different result altogether, return to the problem and offer rather than treating every objection as a barrier to remove.

#### MKT.12:4.5 - Match the observation window to the experience question

For a specific transaction, collect information close enough to recover the relevant event and send an actionable failure to the operating recipient. For a continuing relationship, observe use and changes over a period that can reveal the issue; a pleasant purchase does not settle later usefulness. For a new possibility, use a bounded exploration whose observations can change the proposed contribution. These are different choices of cadence and recipient, not obligatory successive phases.

Select the combination of observation, conversation and measures from what each can expose. A log may show that an action stopped without showing why. A conversation may explain a perceived difficulty without measuring its frequency. A rating records a response under particular wording and conditions. State what each contributes before joining them.

Schedule inquiry around the decision and the participant's burden. Combine requests when their meanings remain clear; stop repetitive questioning when it no longer changes the answer. Give urgent operational failures their own response rather than making them wait for a periodic research report.

#### MKT.12:4.6 - Obtain and interpret enough evidence

Choose cases that can distinguish the live explanations, including access failures or non-users when their absence would bias the answer. An initial purposive set can discover differences. A population estimate needs a defensible population and sampling design; a convenient set of willing customers does not acquire representativeness by growing larger.

Arrange permitted access, understandable participation and competent inquiry. Pilot consequential questions on a small attainable case to find ambiguity before wider collection. Keep the question wording, relevant context and source of observations recoverable in the ordinary working material. Retain only information needed for the stated use under the applicable conditions. Obtain specialist advice where access, rights or a statistical claim depends on it.

During analysis, separate observed events, participant reports and your inferences. Compare cases and examine contrary observations. Ask whether a difference could come from the way information was obtained: selective response, changed wording, interviewer prompting, a different period or an incentive to give a favourable answer. A plausible explanation is useful as a hypothesis when it guides the next discriminating move; it need not be inflated into a general law.

#### MKT.12:4.7 - Return an answer that changes the work

Deliver the answer in the recipient's decision terms: what was learned, about whom and under which conditions; which alternative it supports or defeats; what remains unknown; and what action or smaller inquiry is now warranted. Show the evidence needed to challenge a consequential inference. A concise note can suffice; no particular report format makes the inference valid.

The answer can support proceeding, changing the offer, repairing supply, retaining an existing choice or stopping. If the receiver cannot act, resolve the missing recipient or authority instead of maintaining an unused measurement programme. Reopen only the affected question when customer work, roles, circumstances, measurement meaning or the decision changes.

### MKT.12:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.12:5.1 - Positive onboarding scores, little subsequent use

Consider a stipulated supplier of a file-transfer service. The team wants to choose between simplifying its purchase form and changing the service. A support record already shows that one customer cannot obtain an access approval. That case goes to the responsible access and service participants; another satisfaction survey cannot supply the approval.

For the remaining question, the investigator follows permitted reporting attempts with an operator and reconstructs two contrasting buying episodes. Operators in both cases want less repeated entry. One purchaser can approve a short trial; the other needs a purchasing window and is reluctant to replace a familiar review process. The first observation concerns execution, the second set concerns acquisition and transition. They do not support one undifferentiated “easy to use” score.

The investigator also checks the work preceding entry against a month-end attempt with corrected source values. "Copy the spreadsheet" had hidden a preparation task: establish which values remain valid. The operator and the correction record confirm that a later revision can make an apparently complete report wrong. The additional desired result is reducing the chance of using a superseded value. It could change the service choice, so the inquiry adds that question rather than ranking duplicate-entry effort alone. The delivery owner must now establish whether the proposed operation can recognise a revised source or requires a separate customer check.

The returned answer is bounded: retain reduced entry and valid source selection as offer hypotheses, provide a separate acquisition route for the purchasing window, and investigate whether the familiar review can be retained. It does not estimate the market proportions of these cases. MKT.1 can now keep the source-validation and purchasing conditions visible, MKT.2 can construct a trial around those unresolved contributions, and MKT.4 can make its next decision feasible.

#### MKT.12:5.2 - Change the decision and the needed inquiry

Now management asks whether the trial reduced total reporting time. The earlier accounts do not identify that causal effect: workload or another process change may have altered the result. Define the comparison and competing explanations before choosing an evaluation design. Existing observations remain useful for constructing that question; they are not upgraded into its answer.

In a learning-service case, the paying employer's reason for enrolling staff can be studied through the same question discipline. Whether each learner can subsequently perform the work needs an HCD task and observation under stated assistance conditions. A favourable buying story cannot supply that capability judgement.

### MKT.12:6 - Bias-Annotation

Easy-to-reach customers, recent complaints and vivid successful transitions can dominate the account. Look for the case whose absence could reverse the decision. A supplier can also prefer the question its existing dashboard can answer. Begin with the decision before deciding which available measure to use.

An AI-generated synthesis can help find passages or propose contrasts. Keep its claims connected to obtainable source material and inspect meaning, disagreement and omitted cases. Fluency supplies no missing observation.

### MKT.12:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The receiving decision, live alternatives and answer condition are recoverable.
- Existing sufficiency, ordinary information gathering and non-routine inquiry remain distinct possible outcomes.
- The selected people, activity, period and evidence can answer the declared question at the strength claimed. Outcome comparison includes a challenge for omitted parts of the activity and a decision-relative sufficiency or unresolved return.
- Result investigation, choice history, experience observation and causal evaluation are not silently substituted for each other.
- Contrary evidence, relevant missing cases, participant burden and conditions of data use can change the inference or next action.
- A named recipient receives the answer, its limits and an actionable continuation or stop.

### MKT.12:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Misuse | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Ask what features people want, then call the list their underlying work. | Recover the actual attempt and expected result before deciding which feature could contribute. |
| Explain a choice from the first answer to “why?”. | Reconstruct the episode and compare an action-changing contrast. |
| Add more post-transaction ratings to explain long-term non-use. | Choose an observation window that includes use and the relevant outside conditions. |
| Turn a few interviews into a market percentage or intervention effect. | Keep the explanatory hypothesis; obtain the distinct design required for the stronger conclusion. |

### MKT.12:9 - Consequences

The team can obtain a useful answer without automatically commissioning a study. When inquiry is needed, its effort goes toward a distinction that changes work. This can also reveal that no answer is presently obtainable within the access, competence or time available. Preserve that limit in the decision rather than reporting certainty because a questionnaire was completed.

### MKT.12:10 - Architectural Rationale

The decision-to-evidence-to-action connection belongs together because an otherwise competent data-collection operation can answer the wrong question. Different inquiry routes remain available because their units and warranted conclusions differ. They can be composed through explicit new questions without a universal sequence or a single meaning of “job”.

A full research method, specialist measurement or causal design remains with its supplier. The local contribution is selecting and interpreting those results for a marketing decision, including the sufficient-answer stop and the return to a changed offer or service.

### MKT.12:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For choosing an inquiry, the selected line is question-specific construction with explicit limits. **Adapt** [Strategyn's public ODI account, steps 1–5](https://strategyn.com/outcome-driven-innovation/) for comparing results of an identified activity, and the [Christensen Institute's current JTBD account](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/) for reconstructing choice in circumstances. They are useful rival entries for different questions, not interchangeable definitions. This changes §§4.2–4.4. The linked [needs explanation](https://strategyn.com/outcome-driven-innovation/understanding-customer-needs/) and [job-map construction](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/customer-centered-innovation-map/) inform the coverage challenge in §4.3; its stopping rule remains relative to the receiving decision. Supplier success claims, permanent outcome stability and population-wide causation are not adopted. Reopen the choice when the activity or explanation no longer fits the observed cases.

For cadence and receiving action, **adapt** [Meyer and Schwager, 2007, pp. 6–11](https://zimmer.csufresno.edu/~sasanr/Teaching-Material/MIS/SIS/understanding-customer-experience.pdf): transaction, relationship and exploratory questions call for different arrangements. This historical construction remains useful in §4.5 because a uniform survey misses their different time spans. Keep the later [Becker and Jaakkola, 2020, premises 1, 2 and 4](https://pure.hanken.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/42844202/2020_Becker_Jaakkola.pdf) distinction between provider conditions and a customer's response. The added design effort is justified when that distinction changes the decision; adequate ordinary information is the lighter alternative. These sources do not establish the effectiveness of this combined method.

### MKT.12:12 - Relations

[MKT.1](#mkt1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-customers) consumes role, circumstance and group differences. [MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) consumes grounds for a proposed contribution and comparison. [MKT.4](#mkt4---make-choice-and-acquisition-meaningful-and-feasible) consumes the actual acquisition difficulty. Their failed assumptions can each reopen a bounded inquiry here.

RMP.1 qualifies whether a non-routine research question remains. MKT.10 owns the marketing question about an intervention's contribution; MKT.13 owns the commercial repeatability decision. An experience observation can inform both while retaining its original evidence limits.

### MKT.12:End


## MKT.13 - Test Commercial Model Repeatability and Deployment Limits

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.13:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when deciding which part of a commercial way of working can be repeated or expanded, and which part still needs discovery, repair or a bounded trial. Typical situations include a successful founder-led sale, a pilot dependent on unusual help, a new channel with uncertain purchasing behaviour, or a proposed expansion whose receipts arrive after the next acquisition payments.

The result is a bounded deployment decision: what can be offered, to whom, through which means and at what volume under supported delivery and economic conditions. It also identifies the significant unresolved premise and the work needed to settle it, change the model or stop.

The intended reader can describe the customer contribution and coordinate customer inquiry, delivery and economic analysis. An established arrangement continuing within unchanged supported limits need not repeat discovery. A small viable business need not seek rapid growth; repeatability is useful whenever recurring promises depend on ordinary provision, including deliberately limited volume.

### MKT.13:2 - Problem

A prototype, enthusiastic interview or exceptional sale may establish something useful while leaving most of the commercial arrangement untested. The founder may supply reputation, custom work or access that ordinary staff cannot reproduce. Increasing promotion can then multiply unresolved delivery problems rather than a viable service.

Treat the model as connected claims about obtaining customers, supplying a useful result and sustaining that supply. Examine the consequential uncertain claim under conditions relevant to the intended deployment. Use the resulting evidence to retain, change or bound the arrangement, preserving ordinary work and existing commitments during the search.

### MKT.13:3 - Forces

| Tension | What the decision must preserve |
| --- | --- |
| Early work needs personal initiative and adaptation. | The difference between useful discovery and the conditions of ordinary repeated provision. |
| Evidence is uneven across customers, channels and model components. | The scope of each supported claim instead of one overall label of validation. |
| A larger deployment can reveal problems but also multiply obligations. | A useful bounded increment with a way to stop or recover. |
| Search and current service need different decisions and support. | A workable organisation of both, including their shared scarce contributors. |
| Future margin can be positive while present cash is insufficient. | Timing, capacity, continuing obligations and actual funding. |

### MKT.13:4 - Solution

#### MKT.13:4.1 - Recover the whole commercial proposition and the intended deployment

Explain the proposed customer contribution, actual alternatives, audience, way of reaching and acquiring customers, customer work, provision, support and exchange. [MKT.1](#mkt1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-customers) and [MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) supply the qualified problem and offer. Include partners, specialised work and later obligations when they are needed for the result.

State the deployment decision: repeat the present service next month, employ another team, enter another segment, use a new channel, or increase volume. Define the proposed period and service conditions. Without these, a model can be called repeatable while every repetition changes the essential offer or consumes exceptional help.

Distinguish the claims and their present support. A working artifact may support technical feasibility; a customer conversation may clarify a problem; an actual choice may support willingness to obtain a specified contribution on particular terms. None alone establishes the full combination of acquisition, delivery, benefit and sustained economics. Keep uncertainties connected to their consequence rather than treating every box in a business-model diagram as equally urgent.

Use [Relate Capabilities, Dependencies, Business Models and Initiatives (STR.8)](STRATEGY-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#str8---relate-capabilities-dependencies-business-models-and-initiatives) to explain how the contribution would be obtained and sustained. Its result exposes a missing method, capability, access, arrangement or funding condition at the right subject. A market problem need not be repaired with a product feature, nor an access gap with another interview.

#### MKT.13:4.2 - Select the uncertainty that can change the next commitment

Ask which unsupported claim could defeat the proposed deployment and what alternative explanation fits the evidence. Customers may like the demonstration but lack a usable purchase route. The channel may reach interested users but no payer. A price may cover visible execution while omitting preparation and support. A pilot may work only because the founder silently completes customers' work.

Choose the next inquiry or trial by its ability to distinguish the consequential possibilities at reasonable cost. State what would make the proposed commitment acceptable, smaller, different or unavailable. A fixed number of interviews, a minimum conversion percentage or a prototype milestone is useful only when justified for this decision.

If the issue is the meaning of the customer's situation, use [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision). If it is a concrete choice and transition, use MKT.4 with real conditions. If the decision needs an intervention effect, use [MKT.10](#mkt10---evaluate-the-contribution-and-cost-of-a-marketing-intervention). If the missing contribution is the service itself, request the relevant engineering result through MKT.9 and MKT.11. Keep these different questions distinct even when one trial informs several of them.

Use the smallest adequate means of inquiry. A sample output, terms of a limited service or a manual trial may answer a purchasing or use question more directly than a finished product. Conversely, a simulation that conceals the crucial operational difficulty may be inadequate. The artifact serves the question; its availability does not establish that the question has been answered.

#### MKT.13:4.3 - Make search possible alongside ordinary work

Arrange the trial as real work. Obtain the needed people, time, inputs, permitted access, technical support and authority to change the proposal. Explain what current service must continue and how failures or premature commitments will be handled. Use [Coordinate Change Work with Continuing Service (OCE.11)](ORGANIZATION-CHANGE-ENGINEERING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#oce11---coordinate-organization-change-work-with-continuing-service) to reconcile the whole burden, including learning, supervision, temporary duplication, exception handling and the eventual handover.

Compare a few feasible ways to organise the search: a bounded effort within the current team, a separately supported small team, or a qualified external contributor. Compare how each arrangement would answer the selected question and obtain the same useful customer result. Also compare effects on continuing work, access to scarce specialists and freedom to revise assumptions; identify who will receive the result. Separation is useful when it resolves a real conflict; it is not an automatic remedy for uncertainty.

Give the responsible people a legitimate way to return disconfirming evidence and change or stop the proposal. A team measured only by immediate sales may conceal evidence that the offer is unsuitable. A search team rewarded only for prototypes may leave acquisition and ordinary delivery unexplored. Select expectations appropriate to the actual question without exempting the work from cost, commitments or accountable decisions.

Name the receiving work before the trial finishes. The operating team needs more than a favourable presentation: it needs a usable offer, performance conditions, remaining exceptions, training or access, and the support obligations it would inherit. The search can produce a useful negative answer or a smaller complete service; neither should be converted into a promise of the original scale.

#### MKT.13:4.4 - Observe a complete attempt under relevant conditions

Follow a real attempt far enough to answer the selected question. For an ordinary provision claim, follow suitable customer selection, contact, choice, agreed terms, inputs, performance, use and support. Preserve where the work stops and why. An inquiry about purchase alone need not wait for years of later use, but its conclusion must remain about purchase.

Record material assistance and deviations. Identify whether the founder supplied a personal introduction, unusual discount, manual repair, omitted invoice or unplanned night work. These can be legitimate ways to learn; their cost and availability must enter the proposed repeatable arrangement if it still depends on them. Remove or supply the dependency before claiming ordinary provision without it.

Compare attempts across the differences relevant to deployment. Repetition by the same expert for similar friendly customers may leave another team or channel untested. Select cases that can expose a consequential variation rather than merely accumulating successes. Preserve refusals and ineligible cases so that the number of suitable opportunities is not inflated.

Return the observed result and its limit. Distinguish “this attempt succeeded”, “the ordinary team repeated this bounded service under these conditions”, and “the proposed larger deployment is supported”. A few successful attempts can justify a limited next commitment under uncertainty; they need not establish a stable population rate or unlimited scale.

#### MKT.13:4.5 - Test whether the proposed repetition can be sustained

Join the customer result to acquisition, complete provision, continued use where relevant, and the means of funding the next period. Use [Account for Customer and Product Economics Over Time (MA.8)](MANAGEMENT-ACCOUNTING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md) for the population, dated flows, future obligations and prospective alternatives. Retain expenses that the pilot concealed, including founder time valued on an appropriate basis and the cost of replacing exceptional assistance.

If continuation depends on a growing base, use STR.8's period account: define who is active, reconcile entries and exits, explain the source and delay of the next contributions, and locate the receipts that could fund them. Repeated visits are not new customers; referrals are not acquisitions until the relevant condition is met. A lifetime contribution forecast and a count of invitations do not finance next month's work.

Check shared capacity at the proposed volume. Preparation may be reusable while qualification, adaptation, support or exception handling still grows with each customer. Several individually feasible promises can jointly exceed a specialist's available hours. Compare retaining the current volume, narrowing the service, pacing entry, adding supported capacity or obtaining a contribution externally. A sufficient total of hours does not resolve an impossible simultaneous assignment.

Separate an economically attractive model from a currently feasible commitment. Unavailable funds, ineffective access or an unprepared receiving team can limit deployment even when expected contribution is positive. Return the precise missing condition and allow independent useful work to continue under its existing limits.

#### MKT.13:4.6 - Decide what to deploy and what to learn next

Return one coherent decision: continue the bounded arrangement, expand a supported part, revise a consequential claim, perform another discriminating attempt, pause the dependent commitment or stop. Identify the customer and offer scope, channel, volume, support, economic conditions and receiving team that qualify the decision.

Keep the unresolved part separate without making it disappear. A file-based supported trial can be offered while continuous integration remains an engineering question, provided the offer makes the difference clear. A new segment reopens the affected assumptions; it does not erase evidence about the old one.

Arrange observation and response at the limits that matter. New exceptions, acquisition costs, delayed payment, support queues or changed customer work can invalidate the next repetition. Return to the responsible method with that change. Stable adequate provision can continue without performing the whole discovery sequence again.

### MKT.13:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.13:5.1 - Three founder sales do not yet define the ordinary service

In this constructed case, a founder sells analysis trials to three acquaintances. All pay and receive useful reports. Each trial also consumes substantial undocumented night work by the founder to repair the input data. A proposal to advertise broadly treats the three sales as proof of a repeatable model.

The team reconstructs the result and discovers two consequential claims: suitable customers can choose the offer without the founder's personal relationship, and ordinary staff can deliver it from stated inputs within the available burden. It narrows the trial to a supported batch-file analysis with explicit input conditions. Continuous integration remains outside this offer because no adequate service contribution has been obtained.

Six independently reached organisations are considered. Two can supply the permitted input and choose the limited trial; two need continuous integration; two cannot yet meet the input conditions. This is useful qualification, not a general conversion-rate estimate. MKT.2 keeps the offer narrow, MKT.4 makes its terms and transition usable, and MKT.11 obtains the analyst's actual allocation and support arrangement.

The ordinary analyst completed both eligible trials with the agreed support and no founder repair. Execution and customer support take four hours per trial against a six-hour planning allowance; separate qualification and preparation work is also recorded. These two completed trials do not establish that every new data source, operator or volume will behave likewise.

For the next month, the analyst's supported envelope is 48 hours. Current commitments consume 14 hours, shared preparation and qualification for the proposed trials require six, and four hours remain protected for unexpected current-service support. Each trial retains the six-hour planning allowance for execution and ordinary trial support; the two observed four-hour executions do not replace that allowance.

| Demand on the same analyst in the next month | Four trials | Five trials |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current commitments | 14 hours | 14 hours |
| Shared preparation and qualification | 6 hours | 6 hours |
| Protected current-service support | 4 hours | 4 hours |
| Trial execution and ordinary trial support | 4 × 6 = 24 hours | 5 × 6 = 30 hours |
| Total against the 48-hour envelope | 48 hours | 54 hours |

Four trials fit this local burden comparison; a fifth exceeds the envelope by six hours. In this constructed case, the other contributors, calendar compatibility and cash conditions have separately adequate supply answers. Their unlike resources are not added to the analyst's hours. The responsible decision maker therefore permits at most four compatible trials with explicit review of exceptions. The calculation supports that bounded commitment, not a statistically proven population rate or scalable demand.

If compulsory trial preparation increases by three hours, four trials would require 51 hours. Three would require 45 while preserving the protected four hours. The team must reduce or reschedule the new work, or obtain additional supported capacity, before promising the fourth trial. Broad promotion waits because it could create unsupported integration expectations and exceed the receiving team's capacity.

When a new customer needs a different input repair, the team does not restore hidden founder work. It either obtains that service contribution and its cost, changes the offer, or declines the incompatible trial. The original batch-file result remains valid within its conditions.

#### MKT.13:5.2 - Positive future contribution arrives too late

For an independent constructed expansion, acquiring and beginning service for each of 100 customers requires 12 monetary units now. A qualified forecast gives 15 units per customer of receipts net of later provision and support payments, collected after 60 days. The first group has separate funding. A proposed second group would begin after 30 days using only the first group's receipts, with no other available cash after existing obligations.

The prospective three-unit contribution per customer does not fund the second group at day 30. The team compares a later start, a feasible change in payment terms and separately obtained funding. Each alternative must preserve the offer, customer feasibility and current obligations. Until one is supported, deployment stays within the first group's funded limit. Another enthusiastic interview cannot settle this timing gap.

#### MKT.13:5.3 - A ready artifact changes what remains to be learned

An AI-assisted team can produce an impressive prototype immediately. That artifact may be useful for explaining an offer, but its existence does not establish customer need, purchasing feasibility, the team's ability to operate the service or its economics. The team returns to the consequential untested claim and chooses an appropriate inquiry or trial. It keeps the artifact where useful and changes the means of learning when the artifact no longer discriminates the relevant possibilities.

### MKT.13:6 - Bias-Annotation

Founder reputation, selective pilot recruitment and hidden exceptional effort favour early success. Growth incentives can treat a viable small arrangement as failure, while fear of uncertainty can prevent a justified limited deployment. Preserve the actual purpose, disconfirming attempts and the distinction between evidence of a result and willingness to take a bounded risk.

### MKT.13:7 - Conformance Checklist

- The proposed repetition names the customer contribution, audience, acquisition means, provision, period and volume.
- The next inquiry addresses a significant uncertainty whose answer can change a commitment.
- Search has adequate support and preserves continuing work and existing promises.
- Observed attempts retain material assistance, refusals, exceptions and their actual evidential scope.
- The sustaining account includes complete burden, shared capacity, dated flows and remaining obligations.
- The decision states what may proceed, what remains unresolved and which change requires reconsideration.

### MKT.13:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Anti-pattern | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Treating a finished prototype as validation of the whole business. | Recover the customer, acquisition, provision and sustaining claims that remain untested. |
| Counting founder heroics as ordinary delivery. | Make the exceptional contribution explicit and obtain a repeatable provision condition. |
| Launching more promotion to solve a missing service. | Construct or bound the service before multiplying its promises. |
| Separating a search team without support or a receiver. | Compare organisational alternatives against the actual inquiry, current work and handover. |
| Financing next month with receipts due later. | Place flows at their dates and obtain a feasible timing or funding arrangement. |

### MKT.13:9 - Consequences

Deployment can advance in useful supported portions while unresolved assumptions remain visible. The method can prevent spending on acquisition that multiplies an unworkable promise and can protect a viable limited business from an unjustified growth requirement. It also requires honest accounting of assistance, unsuccessful attempts and the work of transferring a result to ordinary provision.

### MKT.13:10 - Architectural Rationale

Repeatability concerns a connected commercial arrangement. Customer inquiry, offer construction, service engineering, organisation and financial accounting retain their distinct results. MKT.13 joins them for a deployment decision and detects when a change invalidates that decision. It does not replace their specialist constructions or impose a universal sequence on every organisation.

### MKT.13:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For search before expansion, **adapt** the historical [Blank and Dorf Customer Development Manifesto](https://growthorientedsustainableentrepreneurship.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/en-customer-development-manifesto.pdf): a consequential business-model assumption needs discriminating evidence, and ordinary repeated acquisition and provision differ from an exceptional sale. The present method bounds deployment without adopting a universal growth requirement or fixed trial quota.

For changed learning conditions, **adapt** [Blank's 2026 account of AI-generated initial products](https://steveblank.com/2026/09/25/ai-killed-the-mvp-long-live-the-iup/): easy artifact production weakens the inference from an artifact to prior learning or competence. Retain the substantive question and choose a means that can answer it. Renaming the artifact alone supplies no new method.

STR.8, MA.8 and OCE.11 supply developed sustaining, economic and organisational comparisons. Use them where the corresponding condition can change deployment; an adequate existing professional answer can be reused within its scope.

### MKT.13:12 - Relations

MKT.1/2/3/4/6/7/9/11 supply the commercial proposition and its actual continuation. MKT.12 resolves a focused customer or market uncertainty; MKT.10 supplies a bounded intervention evaluation. MKT.14 may organise repeated work with named customer organisations while using this method to qualify a proposed expansion.

Strategy selects the wider direction and commitment; service and method engineering construct missing provision; organisation and operations obtain and perform the work; management accounting qualifies the temporal economics. A new customer promise requires their actual contributions where the model depends on them.

### MKT.13:End


## MKT.14 - Develop and Change a Programme for Selected Customer Organisations

> **Type:** Marketing Method pattern
> **Status:** Stable

### MKT.14:1 - Problem frame

Use this pattern when commercial work with named customer organisations requires a continuing programme: choosing where to invest attention, joining several opportunities, deciding which preparation can be shared, and maintaining feasible separate promises. Examples include a broad programme with one important organisation, a limited programme for one customer result, or repeated work with a justified group of organisations.

The wider family is often called account-based marketing. This pattern governs the construction and revision of the programme: its selected organisations, useful results, individual and shared work, resource commitment and continuation. The result is an executable programme, or a bounded programme proposal with a precise missing contribution. Its contacts and communications serve that whole.

The intended reader can obtain qualified customer knowledge, engage the relevant commercial and delivery participants, and compare the complete work. An ordinary direct request for a ready suitable offer can be fulfilled without a separate programme. This method does not itself construct a specialised service, confer authority to represent a customer, or develop an entire CRM or advertising system.

### MKT.14:2 - Problem

A list of attractive organisations and personalised messages can consume considerable effort without a coherent customer contribution or receiving team. Conversely, several similar opportunities can repeat expensive preparation unnecessarily. Calling them one segment does not establish that the common material, promised result or service conditions fit them all.

Construct the programme from qualified opportunities and attainable results. Choose how broad the work within each organisation should be and which work can be shared across organisations. Develop the common contribution and its local adaptations, compare the whole burden, arrange actual continuation and revise the programme when its grounds change.

### MKT.14:3 - Forces

| Tension | What the programme must preserve |
| --- | --- |
| A large customer can contain several independent opportunities. | Distinct purposes, buying groups, permissions, promises and results. |
| Reusable preparation can reduce effort while hiding consequential differences. | A justified common basis and explicit local adaptations and exceptions. |
| More contacts can improve access while consuming scarce delivery capacity. | The useful outcome and complete burden of responding and supplying it. |
| Individual teams own parts of the work. | A feasible whole with effective contributors and separate customer commitments. |
| A programme needs continuity while customers and conditions change. | A reason to retain, change, separate or finish each part. |

### MKT.14:4 - Solution

#### MKT.14:4.1 - Select organisations and recover the actual opportunities

Begin with the commercial purpose and the useful customer contribution that could justify a programme. Use [MKT.1](#mkt1---qualify-a-market-problem-and-differences-between-customers) and [MKT.12](#mkt12---investigate-a-customer-or-market-question-for-a-decision) to qualify the problem, relevant differences and uncertainty. Select named organisations because the contribution and feasible relationship warrant attention, not simply because they are large or visible.

Within each organisation, distinguish the opportunities that can proceed or fail independently. Recover the working group, affected users, purchaser or sponsor, the intended useful result and the material conditions of choice and delivery. Two departments may share an employer yet need different products, permissions or decisions. One person's interest does not establish the organisation's agreement or every department's need.

Keep the organisation, its representatives, the commercial opportunity and its record distinct. A customer record can help preserve knowledge, but it neither grants authority nor makes several opportunities one. Use only enough structure to maintain the differences that change action: a small programme may need a short opportunity table and connected explanation.

State why each opportunity is included, which condition would exclude it and what further inquiry could change that choice. Consider the option of serving an ordinary request directly or leaving a weak opportunity outside the programme. Selection commits scarce attention; it is not merely a descriptive classification.

#### MKT.14:4.2 - Choose the breadth and degree of shared work

Choose two related things: the breadth of the useful result within an organisation and the justified common work across organisations. A long joint change can contain several linked results; a limited programme can resolve one decision. Either may use reusable preparation where its grounds apply.

| Way of working | When it can be useful | What must be constructed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broad individual programme | Several related opportunities require a continuing joint change and coordinated investment. | Their common purpose, dependencies, separate results and sequence of supported commitments. |
| Limited individual programme | One organisation or department needs a particular result under distinctive conditions. | The sufficient inquiry, contribution, participants and continuation for that result. |
| Shared work for a justified group | Named organisations have sufficiently similar work, useful contribution and application conditions. | A transferable common basis, local adaptation rules, separate promises and exception handling. |
| Repeatable work with a wider named population | Reliable organisation and opportunity information can support prepared choices and receiving work at the intended volume. | The information, action variants, selection conditions, performers and maintenance needed for repeated execution. |

These are alternatives to compare, not mandatory stages. Wider repeatable work may include several groups and individual exceptions; it need not be fully automated. Broad individual work can finish with a small remaining problem that needs only a limited programme. A shared group can dissolve when its common basis no longer holds.

Test the proposed commonality against the actual result. Similar sector or company size may help locate candidates but is insufficient when one needs batch-file analysis and another needs unavailable continuous integration. Ask which preparation transfers, what must change locally, and whether those changes still make sharing worthwhile. Preserve evidence from each consequential difference; information about one client does not automatically apply to the others.

#### MKT.14:4.3 - Construct the reusable contribution and its permitted adaptations

Recover what can be prepared once: a supported explanation, demonstration, method of diagnosing fit, a reusable service contribution, or preparation of the participating teams. Specify the result, conditions and audience that make it transferable. Remove identifying customer information unless its use is permitted and appropriate for the receiving purpose.

Then name what remains local. Customer work, input samples, permitted uses, benefit, price, timing, responsible participants and stopping conditions may differ even when the demonstration is shared. Build an adaptation that preserves these differences instead of hiding them in a nominally personalised greeting.

Use [MKT.2](#mkt2---construct-and-test-an-offer-and-its-positioning) and [MKT.6](#mkt6---jointly-shape-a-useful-result-and-help-achieve-it) to obtain the supported offer and useful result. [MKT.9](#mkt9---select-an-offer-or-help-for-a-particular-customer) selects a suitable variant or obtains the missing service construction. Shared preparation cannot supply an absent technical or organisational contribution.

Prepare the people and means that will use the common contribution. They need to recognise where it fits, adapt it, answer the relevant questions and return an exception they cannot resolve. Account for learning, rehearsal, access and later updates. A reusable file on a shared drive does not establish any of these results.

#### MKT.14:4.4 - Join each opportunity to its next meaningful action

For every live opportunity, identify the next useful result and who can act on it. One customer may need to understand whether the offer fits; another may already have chosen it and need an input permission. A common sign of interest can therefore lead to different actions. Keep simultaneously true conditions visible instead of placing the entire organisation at one funnel stage.

Use MKT.3 for a suitable contact and meaningful response, MKT.4 for choice and acquisition, MKT.6 for obtaining the useful result, and MKT.7 for continuation, restoration or ending. The programme relates these contributions across opportunities; it does not replace them with a count of meetings.

Obtain effective contributors through [MKT.11](#mkt11---align-promises-partners-and-execution). Agree how a response reaches the person who can continue it, what information that person needs, and what happens if the promised work is unavailable. Reconcile calendars, specialist access and overlapping contacts from different teams. A marketing interaction should not create a promise the receiving sales or service team cannot support.

When software selects or performs actions, specify the needed information, selection conditions, permitted action and receiving result. Check behaviour when information is missing, stale or contradictory and when an exception exceeds the prepared variants. The programme can use automation only to the extent that those conditions and its actual receiving work are supported.

#### MKT.14:4.5 - Compare the complete programme and obtain its resources

Compare alternatives against the same useful results and period. Include research, qualification, common preparation, adaptation, team learning, contacts, coordination, provision, support, exceptions, updates and closure where required. Reuse may lower one component while increasing another. Compare a shared programme with the complete individual alternative, not with an individual alternative that omits its receiving work.

Check the contributors' actual available capacity and the timing of simultaneous work. Use OCE.11 to preserve continuing service while preparing or changing the programme. A total within the available hours does not establish compatible calendars, capability, access or cash. Use [STR.8](STRATEGY-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md#str8---relate-capabilities-dependencies-business-models-and-initiatives) for the sustaining contribution and [MA.8](MANAGEMENT-ACCOUNTING-PRINCIPLES-FRAMEWORK.md) for dated economic flows, shared resources and obligations.

Select the smallest complete supported programme. If demand exceeds available capacity, reduce the agreed scope, sequence the work differently, obtain a missing contribution or change the included opportunities. Preserve the useful result and communicate the revised promise. Cheaper message production does not resolve an unavailable specialist or an impossible service.

Obtain the actual resource and contributor decisions within the decision makers' authority. A calculated proposal remains a proposal until those conditions hold. Return precisely which part can start and which part depends on an unresolved contribution.

#### MKT.14:4.6 - Use the programme and learn at the receiving decisions

Keep enough current information to act: the opportunity, supported useful result, present conditions, performed work, response, next action and receiving person. Preserve the reason for a stop or delay. Do not translate silence into refusal or a first purchase into achieved benefit without the relevant observation.

Provide different receiving answers where their decisions differ. The person continuing a sale needs the specific customer's situation and next feasible action. The programme owner needs grounds to change inclusion, common preparation or resource commitment. The service provider needs the actual promise, inputs, timing and capacity conditions. One report may support several of these, but one engagement score cannot answer them all.

Compare the observed result and whole burden with the proposed programme. Use [MKT.10](#mkt10---evaluate-the-contribution-and-cost-of-a-marketing-intervention) when the decision requires the programme's causal contribution; descriptive progress is sufficient for some operational adjustments. Use [MKT.13](#mkt13---test-commercial-model-repeatability-and-deployment-limits) before expanding a model whose ordinary repeatability or sustaining conditions remain uncertain.

#### MKT.14:4.7 - Revise, separate or finish the affected work

Reconsider the affected connection when a customer's goal, participants, data permission, available service, economics or provider capacity changes. Keep sound common preparation where its conditions remain valid; separate an exception when preserving it inside the group would distort the offer or burden the other work.

Admit a new organisation only after checking its contribution, permitted use, necessary adaptation and complete additional burden. A larger list is not itself an improvement. When repeated exceptions remove the advantage of sharing, compare an individual continuation or a newly justified group.

End completed or no-longer-useful opportunities after settling their remaining commitments or arranging how the parties still obliged will fulfil them. A broad relationship can continue while one opportunity ends; an individual success does not automatically advance another opportunity in the same organisation. Preserve reusable learning.

### MKT.14:5 - Archetypal Grounding

#### MKT.14:5.1 - Four opportunities in three organisations

In this constructed case, a provider considers Sever, Vector and Luch. Inquiry with the relevant participants and sample work reveals four opportunities. Sever's first department and Vector can use a supported batch-file analysis trial. Sever's second department and Luch require continuous integration that the provider cannot yet supply. The desired two-week programme result is two supported limited trials and two reasoned decisions about the missing integration contribution.

The first two opportunities share a useful basis: compatible permitted input formats, a limited batch result and a return condition: a rejected sample must be replaced by a permitted compatible one before scheduling. Their data-use permissions and commitments remain separate. The other two receive a scoped engineering question and a time for returning the answer; they do not receive an invitation that promises the unavailable integration. A trial for Sever's first department says nothing by itself about the second department's opportunity.

The provider can reuse an anonymised demonstration, the explanation of its observation conditions, and preparation of the two customer-facing teams. Each customer retains its own sample, permission, agreed result, price, responsible participants and stopping condition. MKT.9 supplies the actual trial variant; MKT.11 joins it to the receiving analysts and support.

The team compares complete alternatives for the same result. Forty additional person-hours are available over the two-week window after continuing service has been accounted for. The following numbers are illustrative planning inputs, not an empirical claim about account-based marketing.

| Required work for the same programme result | Individual preparation | Shared preparation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scenario, material, trial explanation and preparation of the client-facing teams | 2 × 8 = 16 hours | 10 hours |
| Qualification of three organisations and the integration exceptions | 9 hours | 9 hours |
| Adaptation, performance and support of two trials | 2 × 6 = 12 hours | 12 hours |
| Coordination, handover and updates through the window | 7 hours | 9 hours |
| Total | 44 hours | 40 hours |

Sharing saves six hours of preparation while adding two hours of coordination, a net saving of four. The individual alternative needs four more hours or a revised window. The shared alternative reaches the available ceiling without reserve. It can be chosen only after the relevant people confirm compatible timing, adequate capability, effective permissions and actual allocation. The hours comparison establishes neither profitability nor customer benefit; MA.8 and the trials answer their respective questions.

Suppose an additional mandatory exception would require eight hours. The shared alternative now needs 48 hours. The team reduces scope by agreement, reschedules or obtains another qualified contribution before making the affected promise. Automatically generating cheaper emails does not remove the missing eight hours of actual work.

Other changes reopen different parts. If Vector still wants the limited trial but requires different customer-local preparation, obtain the feasible preparation conditions and rebuild the complete programme comparison. If Vector instead refuses the service method or its required data use, reopen the opportunity and offer before deciding whether a trial remains possible. If Luch later obtains the missing integration contribution, it can return for selection and capacity qualification; availability alone does not put it into the current two trials. If one trial succeeds, the other opportunities retain their own evidence and continuation.

#### MKT.14:5.2 - Repeatable actions with different receiving work

A wider programme maintains reliable information about named organisations and prepares three actions: clarify suitability, arrange an already chosen trial, or return a delivery exception. A new interest signal is a reason to inspect the opportunity, not proof of need or purchase intent. Staff use the current customer's work and conditions to choose the action. Where the relevant information is absent, they obtain it or stop the dependent action. Expansion requires capacity for all three continuations and for keeping the information current, not only capacity to send more messages.

#### MKT.14:5.3 - An educational community and an employer are different participants

A learning community may help an employer explore a commercial offer for a particular work result. That programme concerns the employer's opportunity and agreed contributions. A learner's development and voluntary participation in the community retain their own purposes and conditions. Common educational material can be reused where appropriate, but participation does not imply purchasing authority or consent to an employer programme. HCD and Community Management supply their actual results; MKT.14 joins only the commercial work that has a justified place.

### MKT.14:6 - Bias-Annotation

Prestigious names, easy contacts and visible engagement can displace less conspicuous but more suitable opportunities. Pressure to standardise can conceal exceptions; pressure to personalise can repeat work that has a sound common basis. Compare attainable customer results and complete burden, preserving local refusals and constraints even when they make the apparent programme smaller.

### MKT.14:7 - Conformance Checklist

- Named organisations and separately actionable opportunities have qualified inclusion grounds and useful results.
- The breadth of individual work and the common basis across organisations are justified separately.
- Shared preparation has stated application conditions, local adaptations, permitted information use and prepared performers.
- Every promised continuation has an effective receiver and adequate delivery conditions.
- Alternatives preserve the same required results and include complete burden, timing, economics and current obligations where consequential.
- Observed progress supports the receiving decisions without treating contact as purchase or purchase as benefit.
- Changes, exceptions and endings revise the affected opportunity and preserve other valid results.

### MKT.14:8 - Common Anti-Patterns and How to Avoid Them

| Anti-pattern | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Treating one organisation as one buyer with one state. | Separate opportunities, participants and simultaneous conditions that change action. |
| Calling a sector list a justified programme. | Establish the common useful contribution and actual application conditions. |
| Sharing a message while repeating or omitting all preparation. | Construct the reusable work, local adaptations and prepared receiving teams. |
| Comparing communication costs while omitting service and exceptions. | Compare complete alternatives for the same result and period. |
| Expanding automation before responses can be handled. | Obtain the information, action variants and capacity for the actual continuations. |

### MKT.14:9 - Consequences

The programme can reuse work without erasing separate customer purposes or promises. Its owner can choose, revise and finish opportunities on substantive grounds and see when apparent economies disappear under exceptions or support demand. More explicit preparation and coordination may be required, and a justified result may be a smaller programme or direct individual service.

### MKT.14:10 - Architectural Rationale

The programme has a distinct whole: selected organisations, related opportunities, shared and individual work, resources and continuing decisions. Offer construction, personal selection and contributor alignment each supply necessary results but do not themselves choose that whole. Keeping their outputs explicit preserves specialised competence while making the programme constructible and revisable.

### MKT.14:11 - SoTA-Echoing

For a bounded result within one organisation, **adapt** [Burgess's Scenario ABM](https://inflexiongroup.com/resources/what-is-scenario-abm/): relate the chosen outcome, preparation and available team effort. For shared work, **adapt** [Segment ABM](https://inflexiongroup.com/resources/how-is-segment-abm-used-today/): justify the group's common basis while retaining separate continuations. For broader repeated work, **adapt** [Programmatic ABM](https://inflexiongroup.com/resources/how-is-programmatic-abm-used-today/): prepare relevant organisation information, differentiated actions and their receiving work. These distinctions help choose a way of working; reported engagement is not evidence of causal benefit.

STR.8, MA.8 and OCE.11 strengthen the comparison by supplying sustaining dependencies, temporal economics and change alongside continuing service. Their use prevents a choice based only on message volume or reuse of a document.

### MKT.14:12 - Relations

MKT.1/12 supply qualified customer differences and inquiry; MKT.2/6 supply the useful contribution and offer; MKT.3/9 supply suitable contact and help; MKT.11 supplies effective contributors. MKT.4/6/7 preserve acquisition, use, continuation and ending as different results. MKT.10/13 qualify effect and deployment where those questions govern the programme.

Strategy and management accounting supply the wider sustaining and economic decisions. Service and method engineering construct missing provision; organisation and operations obtain and perform it. HCD and Community Management retain learning and voluntary cooperation when the same people also participate in a commercial programme.

### MKT.14:End

# Cross-pattern applications

These are constructed working cases. They develop connections and consequential alternatives; their figures and outcomes are assumptions for reasoning, not observations of a business. Reuse the methods after recovering the conditions of the actual case.



## MKT.Application:1 - APP-MKT-SERVICE - Construct a service when a suitable variant is missing



### MKT.Application:1.1 - Recover the customer's result and the provider's constraint

A training provider has agreed to support a practical exercise in a booked room. The customer now requests an immediate move to another room. MKT.6 clarifies the intended benefit: participants need a quieter setting for their exercise. MKT.1 distinguishes the people affected and their authority: the learner needs workable conditions, the organiser can agree the schedule, and the venue controls room access.

MKT.9 compares available variants against that result. The only worker prepared to move and check the equipment is performing a check that cannot safely be interrupted. The requested move and continuing the check are separately possible, but cannot both rely on that worker at the same time. A catalogue entry saying “room change available” does not resolve this conflict.



### MKT.Application:1.2 - Obtain a construction and compare whole alternatives

The provider asks SYSE.8 for a service arrangement that preserves the needed learning activity and equipment conditions. Its request names the required room conditions, equipment readiness, timing, access and ongoing obligation. C.32.MWA.PP compares the methods and actual overlapping performances. The returned alternatives must identify the contributions that make each combination workable.

| Alternative | What must be supplied | Consequence for the offer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continue in the original room with an adequate local adjustment | Evidence that the adjustment permits the intended exercise; agreed use of that room. | Preserve the original schedule only if the learning activity remains viable. |
| Move after the equipment check | Room access, the worker's later availability, time to move and check, and a workable later learning sequence. | Obtain agreement to the changed schedule; retain consequences for participants who cannot stay. |
| Use another prepared worker or an already prepared partner room | Actual capability, access, equipment readiness and compatible assignments, including any coordination and cost. | Offer immediate continuation only after those conditions are supplied. |

Use HCD for a missing human capability or a changed learning arrangement, OCE for assignments and access, and Operations for admission and service continuation. An available worker without the relevant preparation is not an equivalent replacement. A suitably prepared partner room may be a new solution even if earlier configurations did not include it. Reuse prior reasoning to find relevant options and recheck it under the new conditions.



### MKT.Application:1.3 - Turn the returned arrangement into a truthful choice

Assume the later move is supplied, while immediate continuation remains unavailable. MKT.11 reconciles the changed service with existing commitments and names who will inform participants and perform the move. MKT.2 revises the relevant offer conditions. MKT.4 lets the customer compare the supported later move with an agreed deferral or cancellation. Offer an adjustment in the original room only if its learning conditions have also been established. The choice includes the changed time, assistance and venue access conditions.

MKT.8 is useful only if the schedule change has unclear consequences across participant activities. Its map can expose, for example, a learner's fixed departure and the point at which feedback would arrive. That result may require another HCD arrangement. When the local choice and its consequences are already understood, omit the map.

After the service, MKT.6 asks whether the intended useful result was obtained under the actual conditions. MKT.7 handles an unfinished obligation or necessary restoration. If the organisation later claims that the revised arrangement causes better outcomes, MKT.10 frames that additional question; this successful construction does not itself answer it.

The practical change is an offer grounded in the whole service. The combination is revisited if the room becomes inaccessible, preparation fails or the worker receives another incompatible obligation. Unaffected customer knowledge and learning content remain usable.



## MKT.Application:2 - APP-MKT-MEDIA - Keep a supportable offer through a changing intermediary

Use this application when a platform, publisher or other intermediary changes who encounters an offer, how it is expressed, what the provider can control or what the returned observations mean. A fixed invitation may become a family of assembled advertisements; a new placement may lack the observation or correction the original plan required. The useful result is a supported arrangement for the encounter and its receiving work, with a way to revise or stop the affected part.

The reader knows the offer and can obtain competent media, information-use and service contributions. Platform operations and applicable permissions come from their actual providers. A requested quotation through an adequate agreed route can use MKT.3 directly. Nothing here requires anonymous advertising to become a programme for named customer organisations under MKT.14.



### MKT.Application:2.1 - Start from the offer and the work that can receive a response

Take MKT.2's supported contribution and MKT.3's intended audience, occasion and useful next response. MKT.11 supplies the receiving work and its limits. Distinguish a request for information, a suitable trial application and a purchase; the next person must know which result arrives and what to do with it. Set the attempt's period and attainable volume from that work.

Consider a constructed assisted batch-reporting trial. The provider can map one approved sample with an operator's participation; it cannot supply unattended integration. A useful first response establishes whether the sample and operator can be available. In a fixed professional newsletter, the stipulated participation terms permit the placement, the publisher supplies the agreed expression and a named person receives replies. The invitation keeps assistance and the limited sample visible and asks for a suitability conversation. It does not request working files in a public form.

The initial arrangement is sufficient if those conditions hold. A different channel must preserve the supported contribution and receiving work, even when it changes the message's form or makes distribution cheaper.



### MKT.Application:2.2 - Obtain the intermediary's actual contribution

Ask the media practitioner to establish who selects the audience or context, combines or generates material, controls its placement, receives payment, reports events and can change or stop the exposure. Obtain the supported formats, information uses, available restrictions and important limits for this account and region. Compare only routes capable of serving the intended occasion. A fixed paid placement, an auction and organic visibility can require different work and supply different observations.

The professional return must connect a proposed setting with what it actually permits. A selectable objective is not a guarantee of the customer's later result. A preview does not establish that every future rendering can be examined. If a needed fact is unavailable, retain its effect on the proposal instead of assuming control.

For a programmatic purchase, [IAB Tech Lab's seller and supply-chain explanation](https://iabtechlab.com/sellers-json/) supplies identification and verification of sellers and intermediaries in its specified setting. Use that answer for the inventory being purchased. It does not establish that the intended person will notice or benefit from the offer. A closed platform may supply a different account of its intermediary work.



### MKT.Application:2.3 - Construct the permitted family of meanings

When material can be combined, judge the combinations the arrangement permits, not just each acceptable element or one favourable preview. Identify what can appear together, what can be omitted and what can be generated beyond the supplied elements. Group combinations by a difference that could change the inferred offer: assistance, scope, price, timing, expected result or required customer work. Apply MKT.3:4.3's meaning comparison to each consequential group.

In the reporting case, a heading promising a ready report, an image of automatic synchronisation and an optional sentence explaining specialist assistance can each seem reasonable in their original setting. A rendering that omits the sentence can imply unattended work. Correct information on a later page does not undo the expectation already created by that rendering.

Locate the condition where it can affect the initial interpretation. Revise an ambiguous element; separate incompatible material where the platform permits it; or use a format that keeps the necessary claim and condition together. Ask the operator to demonstrate the actual constraint rather than write an instruction that the platform cannot enforce. Recheck the retained family's meaning after that change. Where ambiguity can change the proposal, obtain an intended recipient's interpretation without supplying the expected answer first.

If generation or omission prevents a sufficient bound on the promise, choose a more controllable format or another route, narrow the supported claim where truthful, or decline that arrangement. Monitoring after exposure can reveal a failure, but cannot establish beforehand that uncontrolled expressions preserve the offer. The required assurance depends on the promise and consequences; this is not a demand to prove every possible human interpretation.

[Yandex's combinatorial-ad instructions](https://m.yandex.ru/support/direct/ru/unified-performance-campaign/create-comb-ad) illustrate the technical supplier: elements are assembled into advertisements, with preview and element-based reporting operations. The instructions do not establish arbitrary controls over combinations or complete observation of future meanings. Confirm the controls needed by this offer in the selected format. In the constructed case, assume the operator cannot guarantee retention of the assistance condition in the proposed combination format. The team retains a supplied fixed placement whose agreed expression preserves that condition. This is a change of arrangement, not an attempt to improve an unsupported advertisement's small print.



### MKT.Application:2.4 - Connect observable events to the receiving decision

For each event used to choose or optimise placement, establish what produces it, its unit, available denominator, period and information conditions. A submitted form can register a request while leaving suitability, informed choice and useful use unknown. Give the receiving person the separate question and means to establish those results. Use permitted information needed for that purpose; a stronger optimisation signal does not itself authorise another use of customer records.

A platform may offer a proxy for the desired result, a delayed signal or no suitable observation. Compare a qualified proxy with a separate receiving observation, a simpler arrangement or a narrower question. MKT.10 supplies causal and full-cost comparison when required; MKT.12 supplies a missing inquiry. Neither produces an unavailable platform segment.

For example, [Google's Ads and AI Overviews explanation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16297775?hl=en) currently provides no placement-only targeting, opt-out or segmented reporting for ads within AI Overviews. A plan to buy only that placement and compare its isolated conversion rate therefore cannot be performed on this basis. Compare a supported broader campaign, another route or a different question. Organic visibility does not supply the missing paid-placement observation. Recheck the supplier's current conditions before use; this example asserts neither availability in every market nor better commercial results.



### MKT.Application:2.5 - Make exposure compatible with actual provision

Connect the displayed invitation, response operation and receiving person before relying on distribution. Try the path as the recipient encounters it. Confirm needed access, usable information, response time and capacity, including an exception. Obtain a supported means to reduce or stop new exposure when that continuation fails. Where a selective stop is unavailable, compare the broader stop and its lost useful contacts with another arrangement. Existing enquiries and commitments retain their applicable response or remedy.

Suppose the reporting provider has ten specialist-hours after existing obligations: two for common preparation, three for each of two trials including learning help, and two for exception support. Compatible customer windows and separate media and sales allocations are stipulated. Before commitments are made, the available specialist time falls to seven hours. One trial with the same preparation and reserve fits; two do not. MKT.11 obtains a later interval, another qualified provider or a supported one-trial offer. The media operator adjusts exposure or the invitation to that returned capacity. It is not enough to change an internal calendar while advertisements still invite reliance on two immediately available trials.

If two trials were already agreed, reducing new invitations does not discharge them. Obtain the applicable revised arrangement or remedy with the affected parties and include its work. A customer withdrawing external-data permission instead changes the dependent sample operation: obtain a permitted adequate input or a supported local arrangement, or defer that trial. Keep the established customer question and unrelated permitted work.



### MKT.Application:2.6 - Compare filtering and mistaken blocking from the affected purposes

A receiving organisation can filter commercial messages to protect attention and information conditions. In the reporting case, suppose a purchaser has requested the supported trial quotation, but the filter blocks it. Delivery failure establishes neither a refusal by the purchaser nor permission for the sender to circumvent the filter.

Use D.1–D.3 to recover the purchaser's need, the protection sought by the recipient organisation, the sender's contribution, other recipients' burdens and who can change the arrangement. Through D.4 compare retaining the block, disabling protection broadly, a narrowly authorised route for this requested quotation, or another agreed means. Ask the responsible operator what each option actually permits and requires.

Assume an existing authenticated portal can deliver the requested quotation without exposing unrelated messages, and both parties can use it within the needed period. Under the stipulated concerns for obtaining this requested answer and protecting other attention, that route preserves both purposes better than broad disabling or losing the answer. The recipient supplies the permission and the operator the access; the sender's commercial benefit supplies neither. Keep any added effort visible.

Now suppose the purchaser cannot access the portal and the authorised operator is unavailable before the deadline. The preferred route is no longer supplied. Compare another actually agreed means, a changed deadline or the unresolved decision; do not keep crediting the unavailable portal with its former result. If the value conflict or authority remains unresolved, return that question through D.4. D.5 is relevant to a separately claimed filtering or bias assurance. One blocked requested message neither establishes an overall filter error rate nor justifies abandoning protection.



### MKT.Application:2.7 - Revise the failed connection and preserve the useful remainder

Return a changed platform rendering rule to the permitted family and expression; a changed event definition to interpretation; an unsupported promise to offer and provision; and missing response access to its operator. If the cause is unknown and matters, investigate that question instead of assigning every silence to the message.

Follow dependencies in both directions. A narrower service can require changed material, feed, destination page and staff explanation. A platform that drops a previously retained qualifier can require another format before further exposure. Retain adequate customer evidence, supported offers and already obtained use results; repeat only the work whose conditions changed.

A useful reminder is: recover the receiving result, obtain the intermediary's real powers and limits, construct supportable permitted expressions, connect observations to the right decisions, supply the response, and revise the affected connection. The steps above explain those joins. They can be revisited independently, and an already adequate arrangement need not be reconstructed merely because a new channel is available.



## MKT.Application:3 - APP-MKT-ORGANISATIONS - Turn a programme comparison into actual customer work



### MKT.Application:3.1 - Keep the programme's unit and result stable

Use MKT.14's case of Sever, Vector and Luch. There are three organisations and four opportunities: two can use a limited batch-file trial; two need continuous integration that is presently unavailable. The intended result for the two-week period is two supported trials plus two reasoned decisions about the missing integration. Comparing four meetings with that result would change the question.

MKT.12 asks what must be known to select the next action in each opportunity. MKT.1 recovers users, decision holders, payer, data conditions and relevant workflow. A permitted representative sample and a conversation about use can qualify a batch trial; a request for continuous integration returns a construction question to engineering. Interest from one Sever department establishes no readiness in the other.

MKT.6 helps the trial participants state the useful result and what they must contribute to obtain it. MKT.2 then constructs an offer and positioning against their actual alternatives. The common demonstration can explain a shared contribution. State explicitly that continuous integration is unavailable when explaining that demonstration.



### MKT.Application:3.2 - Compare the complete programme before promising its parts

MKT.14 compares separately prepared programmes with justified common preparation. Its worked account includes research and qualification, materials and preparation of the people doing the work, adaptation, trial execution, support, coordination and updates. The two alternatives require 44 and 40 person-hours for the same stated result. Forty hours are available in addition to existing service obligations. The common arrangement fits that total with no reserve; calendars, capability and cash remain separate conditions.

The programme owner uses that result with MKT.11 to obtain the actual contributions and receiving work. Sales receives the next action for each opportunity. Trial providers receive the accepted input, support and timing conditions. The programme owner receives the comparison, resource commitments and facts that reopen it.

MKT.3 can now invite an appropriate response. For a qualified batch trial, that may be confirmation of an agreed sample and time. For an integration-dependent opportunity, it may be information needed to decide whether constructing that integration is worthwhile. Sending the same purchase invitation to both would conceal the reason their actions differ.



### MKT.Application:3.3 - Perform the supported continuations and revise the whole when needed

MKT.4 makes each trial choice understandable and executable. It retains local consent, price, preparation, access and the receiving performer. MKT.9 selects the applicable variant. During delivery, MKT.6 checks the useful result and required help, while MKT.7 handles continued work or ending. The shared demonstration is prepared once; confidential samples and individual commitments remain separate.

Suppose Vector still wants the limited trial but requires different customer-local preparation. The team obtains feasible preparation conditions and rebuilds the complete programme comparison. If Vector instead refuses the service method or its required data use, reopen the opportunity and offer before deciding whether a trial remains possible. In a separate variation of the original 40-hour programme, a compulsory exception adds eight hours, so the programme needs 48. The owner must obtain support, reduce the result or change its timing before making the additional promise. If automation reduces preparation, recalculate the whole programme, including its setup, checking and coordination costs. If continuous integration becomes available for Luch, qualify and compare the revised programme before adding a third trial.

MKT.13 asks whether the ordinary team can repeat this work at the proposed later scale. It uses observed preparation, exceptions and support, not merely the founder's successful intervention. STR.8 and MA.8 retain the actual business contribution and dated financial alternatives. A planned positive contribution does not pay an earlier supplier bill.

MKT.10 is needed if a decision depends on the incremental effect of the common programme. Two completed trials alone describe what happened; they do not identify what the alternative would have produced. Retain the outcome, unit, comparison conditions and uncertainty appropriate to that question. A bounded programme can finish usefully without making that stronger claim.

This application connects qualification, offer, execution and revision around MKT.14's full construction. Its ordinary alternative is a ready, independent enquiry: respond through the direct methods without creating a shared programme whose coordination adds no useful result.



## MKT.Application:4 - APP-MKT-ACCOUNT - Carry a customer's changing question from first contact into useful work

Use this application when work with one organisation spans several people, meetings and decisions, and a locally successful conversation leaves the next contribution unclear. The result sought is a supported customer choice and useful receiving work, including a justified refusal or ending. Use MKT.4 directly for a settled ordinary purchase; a single adequate contact needs no account programme.

The [Reference explanation of understanding, agreement and ensuing work (Engineering.Reference:21)](../Engineering%20DPF%20Suite/ENGINEERING-DPF-SUITE-REFERENCE.md#engineeringreference21---they-understand-or-agree-why-does-the-needed-work-still-not-follow) develops the shared distinction between explanation, judgement, contribution and performance. This application adds how to prepare and revise the commercial work across encounters, carry its case through other people, and connect criterion work and negotiation to implementation. Constituent methods remain independently usable.

A working reminder is:

> Find the present question and the people who can answer it → prepare a useful next contribution → develop and carry the grounds for choice → compare or change the offering → settle supported terms → help the result become useful → retain what holds and reopen what changed.

The phrases recall the connections below. They are not compulsory buying stages. One conversation can clarify criteria while exposing an implementation concern; the operator and purchaser can be answering different questions. Return to a question only when its answer is missing or has changed.



### MKT.Application:4.1 - Prepare an encounter that can change the work

Start from the customer's actual situation and the result still missing. Use a recent episode, current comparison or failed attempt to establish the question. Keep a reported problem, a possible explanation and a warranted reason for action distinct. Reuse sufficient knowledge before asking the customer for it again.

Set a concrete attainable objective for the encounter: obtain an introduction to the problem owner, clarify a consequential criterion, obtain permission for a sample, or agree who will prepare a proposed trial. “Build the relationship” does not say what the other person can use or what changed. The proposed objective must allow a useful correction, refusal or different next action.

Prepare from the question backward. Recover facts available elsewhere; identify the relationship, consequence or alternative that needs the participant's knowledge; prepare questions and suitable material for that relationship. For a consequence, trace the proposed connection before asserting a benefit: which extra work follows from this difficulty, for whom, under what conditions? Ask what would defeat the explanation as well as what supports it. MKT.12 supplies needed inquiry and MKT.1 the qualified customer situation; a domain expert supplies a missing technical or operating account.

After the encounter, compare the actual return with the objective. An introduction received, a criterion clarified and a demonstration requested are different results. Preserve the new fact and its limits, identify who will use it, and prepare the next contribution from that changed state. A cordial meeting can leave the objective unmet; an unwelcome correction can settle it usefully. What happens between meetings also matters: a colleague can reject the case, a provider can withdraw support, or the customer can propose a better trial after reflection.



### MKT.Application:4.2 - Obtain access and let the case travel

Consider a constructed warehouse that loses dispatch time while correcting identifiers. A technically interested engineer offers a scanner demonstration. That contact provides access and technical knowledge; it does not establish the dispatch problem or authority to change the work.

The supplier asks for an introduction to dispatch staff and their supervisor. Before that meeting it learns enough about the identifier flow to ask useful questions. Dispatch describes a recent missed departure; the supervisor identifies which correction work mattered. The supplier checks the connection before counting benefits. Recorded downtime is not automatically saved cash, and the same loss described by two people is not two losses.

The next contribution is a case the supervisor can discuss with the data owner and purchaser. It explains the receiving result, alternatives, expected burden, grounds and unresolved question. Ask the supervisor to explain it in their own terms and raise the questions their colleagues are likely to ask. Repair a missing explanation through EXD.4. If instructional preparation is actually needed, EXD.5 develops a generated explanation and correction; rehearsal is not a required sales ritual or evidence that colleagues will agree.

An internal sponsor's willingness is distinct from ability to explain and authority to decide. Identify whose judgement, resources or permission the next step needs. When no one can settle the linked questions alone, propose a joint discussion or decision group to the customer's responsible owner. State the decisions and contributions it would connect; that owner determines its remit and participation. A committee can remove otherwise indefinite waiting, but an existing sufficient decision route is cheaper.

Avoid making continuity depend on one person where a consequential change is plausible. With the customer's agreement, connect the other relevant participants and leave them a usable account. Do not contact everyone indiscriminately. If the sponsor leaves, recover who now owns the work and whether the case and authority still apply. The departure neither erases established technical facts nor transfers the old authority to the most receptive replacement. MKT.14 keeps separate opportunities within the organisation distinct.



### MKT.Application:4.3 - Turn grounds for action into a usable comparison

Once the warehouse accepts a reason to examine change, more emphasis on the old difficulty does not determine which option to choose. Compare retaining the current arrangement, improving identifier preparation, obtaining a reporting or correction service, and a supported scanner arrangement. MKT.2 makes the actual contribution and exclusions clear; MKT.4 connects them to choice.

An experienced data owner may begin with interface and data-use criteria. Enter through how they judge adequacy, then relate that judgement to dispatch work. Another participant may still need to establish why any change is worthwhile. Carry the relevant answer to each, rather than assigning one stage to the whole organisation.

Use MKT.4:4.3.1 to choose criterion work. Derive an omitted condition from a qualified need; make an important fulfilled condition and its evidence usable; investigate an overlooked consideration; or address an important unmet condition through changed importance, meaning, an acceptable sacrifice or different provision. These choices have different inputs and results. The warehouse examples in MKT.4:5.3 explain why a continuity minimum selects provision work rather than a more persuasive price comparison.

When a property such as reliability is hard for the customer to judge, define what it means in this work, refine it into inspectable distinctions and return to its justified importance. Obtain relevant evidence about the mechanism and conditions. General criticism of a competitor's technology supplies no comparative conclusion. A technically supported causal explanation can be useful even when brand labels are not. Send the qualified explanation with the case when it passes to another decision group.

Compare vulnerability through importance, the customer's view of this offer and its view of alternatives. A competitor's improved model can erase an advantage without changing the customer's need. A faster device can outperform another and still fail the receiving workflow. Reopen the affected comparison when new evidence changes fit; retain unaffected criteria and facts. If a different arrangement is proposed, request its actual engineering and operating contributions before inviting reliance.



### MKT.Application:4.4 - Investigate a concern and prepare an attainable agreement

A preferred option can still expose someone to an unacceptable consequence. The size of the decision for that person, its visibility, unfamiliar technology, replacing an established supplier or the reputation of an alternative can justify earlier inquiry. They establish neither a hidden motive nor a universal probability of hesitation.

Look for a consequential discrepancy to discuss: an apparently settled question returns, a previously available decision maker stops meeting, or a needed fact no longer reaches the group. Ask what changed and listen to the answer. The first concern can be the complete problem, a tentative way of raising it, or a symptom of another difficulty. Clarify with the person; do not impose the most commercially convenient interpretation.

In the warehouse, the purchaser asks for a discount after expressing preference for the scanners. The supplier asks which part of the proposed cost or exposure is unacceptable. The purchaser explains that an unsuccessful start would interrupt dispatch and leave operators without help. A cheaper unsupported launch does not answer that concern. The participants compare a supported bounded trial, a smaller start, another provider and deferral.

A grounded recommendation can reduce unnecessary search when it names the reasons, limits and serious alternatives and leaves the customer the decision. Use PSDA.14 for that contribution. A decision-changing unknown remains a reason to inquire. A trial is useful only if its question, inputs, available help and end can support the later choice.

If scope, price, timing or support can usefully change, use MKT.4:4.3.2 to prepare and revise the proposal. Carry the criteria into that preparation: which contribution matters to this customer, which reciprocal contribution is available, and which package each party can accept and perform? The training-and-data packages in MKT.4:5.4 show how this changes a conversation about discount into a feasible exchange. ECO.3/5 supply the exchange comparison; the required specialist and operating answers remain with their providers.

Raise a genuine impossibility when discovered. A data restriction or unavailable service cannot wait for a later supposed negotiation stage. Conversely, a known suitable purchase with accepted fixed terms needs no invented bargaining phase. Before treating a package as agreed, resolve important ambiguities, obtain the relevant decisions and return its conditions to those who will deliver and use it.



### MKT.Application:4.5 - Make implementation a receiving part of the agreement

Preparation for useful use often begins before the order. Include the operators' knowledge, actual inputs, permitted data, preparation time, learning, support and a workable response to failure in the proposed arrangement. Customer participation supplies contributions and correction; it is not a device for making the customer feel responsible for a deficient product.

In the warehouse, the trial must show that approved identifiers pass through the selected dispatch work with an available response to errors. It needs both a technically suitable configuration and people who can perform the relevant work with the permitted help. MKT.6 constructs this receiving result. MKT.11 obtains the provider's answer about support. OCE.6 addresses a missing assignment, access or authority; OCE.11 reconciles setup, practice and recovery with continuing dispatch when their demands conflict.

Use the actual shared allocation before making the promise. If an expert is needed for both the trial and an existing obligation at the same time, two separate statements of feasibility do not establish the joint arrangement. Change timing, scope or provision, or defer. The Reference's purchase case develops this conflict and the selective return when support is lost.

Early difficulty needs a discriminating response. A wrong identifier can arise from source data, an unavailable correction rule, access, a defective configuration or a person's performance. Obtain the responsible contribution. When the limitation is a person's way of performing the operation, HCD.3 locates it and HCD.9 supplies a focused attempt, feedback, correction and retry. Useful help early in implementation can prevent a small difficulty from defeating use; it does not prove a universal motivation curve or durable independent capability.

Observe what the receiving dispatch work actually obtains. A valid scan, an installed device, a confident operator and an on-time useful dispatch answer different questions. MKT.6 returns an inadequate contribution to its owner and preserves sufficient results. Early enthusiasm may justify keeping a conversation open; it does not establish a reliable reference case. Ask permission before using another customer's account or contact, and keep any reported benefit within its observed conditions.



### MKT.Application:4.6 - Choose continuation and reopen only the affected work

MKT.7 compares supported continuation, restoration, pause and ending against present usefulness and outstanding commitments. A new sale requires a newly worthwhile contribution. Owed support remains owed even if the customer declines expansion.

Suppose the agreed scanner installation works, yet improved identifier preparation elsewhere removes the reason to expand it. Reconsider the benefit and alternatives; more persuasion or training would repeat a settled question. The warehouse can end optional expansion while existing commitments are fulfilled.

Other changes select different returns. A departing sponsor changes access and authority; a newly competitive device changes the comparison; unavailable storage changes the proposed consumables arrangement; lost support changes the start or restoration; failure to perform with adequate inputs changes the capability question. Preserve the other warranted answers. Several preparations can continue together when their inputs and shared resources permit.

This application adapts the historical construction in Neil Rackham's *Major Account Sales Strategy* (1989), especially Chapters 2–9: preparation and revision between encounters, the internal passage of a case, criterion strategies, concerns, negotiation and implementation. Rackham's commercial objective includes winning and expanding accounts. Here the receiving result also permits declining an unsuitable purchase and ending optional work; selectively intensifying dissatisfaction or redefining criteria to favour the seller is not adopted as sufficient justification. The cases explain a construction under stated conditions, not its empirical superiority. The related MKT.4 source comparison qualifies the criterion and negotiation contributions; the Reference and supplying methods develop the other connected operations.



## MKT.Application:5 - APP-MKT-LEARNING - Connect a learning offer with personal development and voluntary exchange



### MKT.Application:5.1 - Recover the different purposes before building one arrangement

An analyst encounters a discussion of modelling practice in an independent professional community. She tries one comparison on her own work, asks a question, pauses consideration of a paid course, obtains a useful explanation and later considers a programme. Her employer wants more dependable analysis; she wants to make better modelling choices without surrendering every evening. The provider needs a sustainable offer. Community participants want useful exchange and a maintained practice. These purposes can cooperate, but none stands in for the others.

MKT.12 investigates the decision that the provider actually faces: which difficulty can a proposed programme help this audience address, and under what conditions? MKT.1 distinguishes the payer, learner, intended user of later work and holder of study time. MKT.6 helps formulate a useful result without turning the employer's chosen sales or productivity measure into the learner's personal goal. For this case, the proposed human contribution is to choose and justify an appropriate model on a later task under stated available help.

The early useful explanation can finish a community exchange without a purchase. A pause may mean that the analyst has enough help, lacks time or is comparing alternatives. Use MKT.8 only when knowing the connections among those activities changes a decision. Its map retains the outside discussion and the analyst's own work; absence from the provider's records is not absence of activity.



### MKT.Application:5.2 - Obtain the learning product and the personal programme

The provider gives HCD.28 the later-work family, intended human contribution, candidate audience, subject material and service limits. HCD.28 distinguishes a missing subject method from missing teaching or help. It returns a connected route with meaningful entry differences, learning tasks, explanation, practice, feedback, later observation and provider conditions. When the needed judgement remains implicit, use Method Engineering to recover it, retaining an adequate demonstration or supervised contribution where verbal explanation is insufficient. If the required subject contribution is unavailable, narrow or defer that part of the learning promise while it is obtained.

Assume the subject basis is available. The product offers a supported individual route and a route containing selected shared episodes. HCD.2 compares those variants for this analyst using her actual preparation, later work, available time and protected contributions. A population description does not determine that personal choice. HCD.20 compares individual and group arrangements; HCD.21 establishes compatible participants only if interaction is useful. HCD.22 supplies a shared task whose individual contributions matter.

Choose the interaction from the missing learning operation. HCD.23 can arrange explanation and comparison of model choices; HCD.24 can develop evaluation and revision; HCD.25 can connect project comparison to the person's own use. HCD.26 helps regulate shared learning when actual work diverges from intention, while HCD.27 connects personal preparation and recovery with shared timing. The offer need not include every group method. The supported individual route remains a serious alternative when it better supplies the required work.

MKT.2 consumes the returned learning and service construction to form its promise and positioning. MKT.4 makes the choice meaningful: entry actions, later work, required participation, help, schedule, price, limitations and a possible refusal must be understandable. A paid place supplies neither personal preparation nor the employer's release of work time. If that release is missing, offer a supported later or differently scheduled route where adequate, or defer.



### MKT.Application:5.3 - Keep community participation workable alongside the offer

Community Management owns the useful exchanges and their conditions. CB.2 arranges the first exchange; CB.3 makes entry and changes of participation workable; CB.5 obtains and returns useful contributions. Someone who later shares a modelling example can contribute as a practitioner without being assigned recruitment work. CB.14 maintains a reusable repertoire when participants want that shared result.

CB.9 supplies the provision and agreed terms for community work. Suppose a sponsor offers to fund the discussion space in exchange for identifiable sales leads. That condition changes participant expectations and permitted information use. Resource holders and the people authorised to make the community decision use CB.9/13 to agree any changed terms with affected participants. A possible result is separate voluntary discussion and an explicitly chosen commercial contact route; another is different sponsorship terms or declining the funding. A marketing programme cannot accept the obligation on participants' behalf.

MKT.14 applies only if the provider constructs a commercial programme for selected employers or other customer organisations. It can share justified preparation while preserving their separate opportunities and promises. It does not make the whole community one account, determine learners' personal programmes or claim every helpful exchange as commercial progress.



### MKT.Application:5.4 - Let a condition of the whole change the constituent action

Consider one worked provision conflict. In a particular week, a prepared facilitator has ten hours available after existing obligations. The promised learning route requires two hours of preparation and six hours of feedback during the period in which learners can revise. Accepted community support requires the remaining two hours. Preparing, holding and following up a proposed employer-sales meeting needs another two hours from the same person. The combined twelve hours exceed the ten supplied; all figures are constructed assumptions, not standard workload rates.

MKT.11 returns this conflict to the responsible owners before promising the meeting. OCE and Operations establish what work can actually move or receive another capable person. HCD.28 checks the learning consequence of delayed or reduced feedback. CB.9 obtains agreement for any change to accepted community support. The programme can add a qualified contributor, move a meeting that can still achieve its purpose, reduce a promise by agreement or decline the added work.

Suppose the employer agrees to a later meeting and its preparation and follow-up, and the responsible owner supplies the required two hours from the facilitator's following week. The current week's ten hours remain allocated to the accepted learning and community work. The later meeting remains conditional on its own access and support being available.

The conflict can also be temporal. Two available hours after learners' revision deadline cannot supply feedback needed before it. During a shared explanation, withholding the answer long enough for the analyst to make a model choice may be part of learning, while offering the answer immediately may be good customer support in another situation. Recover the intended contribution before substituting the action. An AI answer can help with work; when the human must acquire the judgement, the arrangement must still obtain that person's choice, explanation, correction and later performance under the declared help conditions.



### MKT.Application:5.5 - Judge and maintain the distinct results

MKT.6 asks whether the agreed useful result and enabling help are being realised; HCD judges the actual human contribution, transfer and retention under its evidence conditions. Community Management considers useful exchange, participation and burden. MKT.10 investigates a marketing intervention's effect only if the receiving decision requires it. MKT.7 handles a commercial continuation, restoration or ending while the learning and community arrangements keep their own decisions.

After the course, a useful new modelling contribution may enter the community repertoire with permission and suitable explanation. A person may instead stop participating, continue privately or need further development. The provider's publication, sale and satisfaction result do not establish the learner's capability; the learner's capability does not establish a causal effect of the course. Keep the separate evidence and use each where it can change an actual decision.

The application returns separate learning, community and commercial arrangements with the conditions each must satisfy. It can preserve useful community participation without purchase, a personal learning route without group work, and a commercial employer programme while preserving the analyst's chosen learning purpose and the community's agreed participation terms. Reopen only the affected connections when preparation, support, terms or intended work changes.
